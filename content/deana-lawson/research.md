# Deana Lawson — research note

## Scope and evidence

This chapter studies twelve publicly displayed, web-sized JPEG renditions from
David Kordansky Gallery's Deana Lawson featured-works page. Titles, dates,
media, credit, direct display links, and an image-specific four-factor fair-use
rationale are recorded in [sources.md](sources.md). The selection runs from
*Sharon* (2007) to *Cardeidra* (2023), and includes the pigment-print works,
the collaged photograph *Nation*, the hologram *Torus*, and the displayed work
identified as an appropriated image.

## Documented method and medium

The gallery record identifies most selected works as pigment prints; it identifies
*Nation* as a collaged photograph and *Torus* as a hologram. Its biographical
text describes Lawson's portraits as meticulously staged and attentive to the
relation between figures and domestic space. That supports this chapter's
description of pose, rooms, props, and surrounding fields as constructed visual
relations. It does not establish a universal meaning for an individual gesture,
object, or expression, so the readings remain with visible placement and
framing.

## What is not documented

The available records do not provide a camera body, lens, focal length, aperture,
shutter speed, ISO, film stock, digital-capture workflow, lighting setup,
post-production process, paper, printer, or edition-specific print date for these
individual works. The ingested display JPEGs contain no capture EXIF. The chapter
therefore treats the stated media as documented and treats camera settings as
unknown; it does not infer them from flash-like illumination, shallow space, or
the appearance of a web reproduction.

## Analysis limits

The analyzer measured the supplied display files, not original prints or the
hologram. It found the darkest mean luminance in *Cowboys* (0.097) and the
highest edge density in *The Garden, Gemena, DR Congo* (0.256). Its strong
symmetry and horizon suggestions often arose from frames, tonal shifts, display
edges, blinds, or dark room fields rather than a literal horizon or a camera
axis. The finished overlays retain only visually verified figure groups, room
boundaries, material contours, the one measured hologram axis, and explicitly
named display edges. Pixel values and palette descriptions are properties of
these accessible JPEGs, not claims about Lawson's intended print colour or
density.
