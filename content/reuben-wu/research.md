# Reuben Wu — research notes

## Record and scope

The supplied source record identifies twelve artist-portfolio photographs from
*Lux Noctis*, *Aeroglyphs*, *Siren*, and *The Sea of Ice*. Wu’s account dates
the beginning of *Lux Noctis* to 2015. His biography describes the decisive
2014 night shoot at Trona Pinnacles and explains that the series developed from
powerful lights attached to drones; it describes *Aeroglyphs* as drone flight
paths captured through long exposures. The artist’s project pages identify
drone-mounted lighting in *Lux Noctis*, controlled drone paths and long
exposure in *Aeroglyphs*, and drone-mounted laser light responding to waves
through long exposure in *Siren*. See [source notes](sources.md).

The source record does not establish a common camera body, lens, exposure
setting, film stock, print process, edition, or post-production workflow for
these twelve frames. The supplied public JPEGs retain no camera EXIF in the
ingest manifest. This chapter therefore reports documented project methods and
visible effects, rather than assigning unverified settings or printing methods
to individual photographs.

## Method and period

Wu (born 1975) came to dedicated landscape photography after a 2011 Svalbard
trip, according to his biography. The 2014 accidental illumination by truck
headlights supplied the premise for the subsequent *Lux Noctis* work. The
artist describes using drones as movable light sources: in *Lux Noctis*, they
illuminate remote terrain; in *Aeroglyphs*, their programmed flights become
the visible light drawing; in *Siren*, movement of water shapes the light’s
form. These are documented process claims, not deductions from appearance.

The selection makes a clear progression inside that approach. The earlier
night landscapes give artificial illumination the task of revealing rock, ice,
and water. *Aeroglyphs* turns the trace itself into a temporary geometric form,
while *Siren* allows the trace to become less regular at the shoreline. The
analyzer’s measurements are used only to describe the supplied frames’ palette,
tonal distribution, and spatial marks; they are not evidence of how the artist
made any particular exposure.

## Printing and settings

No print process or camera settings are documented for these files. No claim
about exposure duration is made for an individual plate beyond the artist’s
project-level description of long exposure in *Aeroglyphs* and *Siren*.
