# Yagazie Emezi — research note

## Scope and evidence

This chapter studies twelve public display files from Yagazie Emezi’s
*#EndSARS Protests* (2020). The source record identifies each as *Untitled,
from “#EndSARS Protests,” 2020* and links the artist’s public project archive;
it also records MoMA’s presentation of the series in *New Photography 2023*.
The photographs are treated here as a selected sequence of protest images, not
as a substitute for the artist’s archive, exhibition, or licensed
reproductions. Image-specific provenance, copyright credit, and the fair-use
rationale are in [sources.md](sources.md).

## Process, camera, and print information

The supplied source record identifies the artist, series, year, exhibition
context, and a copyright credit, but it does not document camera body, lens,
focal length, aperture, shutter speed, ISO, sensor, lighting setup, film stock,
negative format, or print process for these individual frames. The ingested
display JPEGs contain no capture EXIF. No claim is therefore made here about
camera settings, digital workflow, or printing method.

The chapter confines itself to visible decisions: close and wide framing,
crowd density, raised hands and placards, architectural thresholds, vehicles,
road markings, and the relation of a figure to surrounding bodies or open
space. The source files are 1,599 × 1,066px web display copies and are used for
criticism and composition teaching only.

## Analysis limits

The analyzer reliably measured several useful structural lines and tonal
relationships, but a detected horizon or vanishing point is not automatically a
literal horizon or a claim about political meaning. The retained overlays were
visually checked against the photographed features: signs, arms, bridge cables,
road edges, vehicles, crowd bands, and figures. Palette and tonal statistics
describe the display files rather than a definitive print colorimetry.
