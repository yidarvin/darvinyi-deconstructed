# Mitch Epstein — research note

## Scope and documented context

The source record identifies the first seven photographs as works from Mitch Epstein's *American Power*, a project begun in 2003. Yancey Richardson describes the series as an examination of how energy is produced and consumed in the United States, relating government, corporations, mass consumption, and the American landscape. The source record also connects the later photographs to Epstein's land-focused practice, including resistance, encampment, environmental aftermath, and the relation between nature and human society. Titles, dates, collection records, credits, routes, and image-specific fair-use analyses are retained in [sources.md](sources.md).

The chapter uses twelve complete-frame public display renditions from Whitney, Smithsonian American Art Museum, SFMOMA, the National Gallery of Art, and Yancey Richardson. They are contemporary copyrighted photographs. Each is used under the documented, image-specific fair-use rationale for nonprofit criticism, scholarship, and transformative composition teaching; these web-sized files are not substitutes for prints, editions, collection records, or licensed reproductions.

## Technique, material, and settings

The consulted source records do not establish a camera, lens, focal length, aperture, shutter speed, ISO, film stock, negative format, digital workflow, print process, paper, edition, or individual capture settings for these works. Two supplied display JPEGs carry embedded Hasselblad metadata dated 2013, later than the pictured works; it is file-level metadata rather than a documented capture record and is not used as evidence about Epstein's process. The chapter therefore does not infer equipment or printing method.

The formal discussion is limited to visible full-frame structure and deterministic measurements of the ingested web renditions: normalized overlay geometry, tonal percentiles, palette clusters, and edge density. Those measurements describe the supplied files, not an original print.

## Era and source limits

The selection spans *American Power* photographs made 2004–2008 and later images dated 2017–2019. The series context supports a reading of energy infrastructure as inseparable from ordinary civic, domestic, and ecological settings. It does not license a technical account of how any one photograph was exposed or printed. That distinction matters: the chapter reads the supplied frames and preserves the historical and rights record rather than filling gaps with plausible photographic lore.
