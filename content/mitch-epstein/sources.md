# Mitch Epstein — source shortlist

This selection follows the broad systems in Epstein's landscape practice: *American Power* (energy production and its civic surroundings), then the more recent photographs of resistance and contested land.  The first series was made between 2003 and 2008 at fossil-fuel, hydroelectric, wind, solar, and other energy sites; Yancey Richardson describes the later work as examining the relation between nature and human society. All twelve acquired files are publicly served, complete-frame display images from collection records or the artist's representing gallery. They are copyrighted contemporary photographs, so the use basis is **fair-use**, not open licence.

**Shared fair-use context:** Each image below is a deliberately web-sized, credited copy for nonprofit criticism, scholarship, and transformative composition teaching. The chapter needs the complete published frame—not a detail—to analyze the relation between infrastructure, people, weather, and terrain. Each file is linked to its authoritative collection or gallery page and is not a substitute for the photograph, a collection record, or a licensed reproduction. The individual entries record the four-factor application and the source-specific credit/copyright notice.

## Research context

Yancey Richardson states that Epstein began *American Power* in 2003 to examine how energy is produced and consumed in the United States, and describes the photographs as considering government, corporations, mass consumption, and the American landscape: https://www.yanceyrichardson.com/artists/mitch-epstein. The National Gallery of Art's 2026 acquisition announcement likewise identifies *American Power* as an examination of energy production and environmental consequence, and connects it with *New York Arbor*, *Rocks and Clouds*, *Old Growth*, and *Property Rights*: https://www.nga.gov/press/national-gallery-art-receives-major-collection-works-american-photographer-mitch-epstein. This shortlist uses *American Power*'s infrastructure images as its spine, then extends the chapter's composition argument to photographs of land defense and environmental aftermath.
