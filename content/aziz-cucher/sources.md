# Aziz + Cucher — source shortlist

This selection concentrates on the three early, decisive bodies of work in which Aziz + Cucher make digital alteration legible as an alteration of visual identity itself: the sealed portraits of *Dystopia* (1994–95), skin-clad devices of *Chimeras* (1998), and anatomized rooms of *Interiors* (1999–2001). The ten JPEGs were downloaded directly from the artists’ public archive, which supplies the titles, series, media, and public display images. Every file opens successfully, preserves the complete published image, and exceeds the project’s 640px long-edge floor.

**Rights basis:** **fair-use** for every selection. The registry identifies this corpus as copyrighted; the official archive identifies the artists as Aziz + Cucher but does not publish an open licence or a separate rightsholder notice on these image entries. Each assessment below therefore credits Aziz + Cucher, treats the work as highly creative, uses a complete-frame web-sized display copy only for nonprofit criticism, scholarship, and transformative composition teaching, links to the artists’ archive, and explains why the copy does not replace a work, collection record, or licensed reproduction.

## Research context

SFMOMA describes *Dystopia* as digitally altered portraits in which the removal of eyes, nose, or mouth makes personal identity obsolete, and situates the artists’ broader practice at the junction of technology and the human body: https://www.sfmoma.org/artist/Aziz_Cucher/. The artists’ archive dates the series as *Dystopia* (1994–95), *Chimeras* (1998), and *Interiors* (1999–2001), and identifies the selected works’ print media: https://www.azizcucher.net/work. Wexner Center likewise describes *Interiors* and *Chimera* as digitally manipulated work using rooms and found objects wrapped in human skin: https://wexarts.org/public-programs/aziz-cucher.
