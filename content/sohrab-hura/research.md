# Sohrab Hura — research note

## Scope and documented practice

Sohrab Hura (b. 1981) is a New Delhi–based photographer and filmmaker. This chapter follows publicly displayed works dated 2008–2020 from *Life is Elsewhere*, *Look It’s Getting Sunny Outside!!!*, *Pati*, *Snow*, *The Coast*, and *The Levee*; their individual records, credits, and use rationales are preserved in [sources.md](sources.md). The sequence is not a survey of a fixed style. It moves between close domestic pictures, landscape, animal portraits, and a time-based work while returning to proximity, partial knowledge, direct light, and unsettled relations between people and place.

Hura has described a practice that tests and rebuilds its own methods rather than treating documentary as a neutral category. His interview on *Snow* describes an interlocking practice across still image, moving image, text, sound, and sculpture; the [Huis Marseille exhibition context](https://huismarseille.nl/en/exhibitions/sohrab-hura/) likewise places *The Coast* and *Snow* within that expanded practice. Those sources support readings of ambiguity and sequencing, but not claims that a single plate exhausts a project’s subject or meaning.

## Materials, technique, and settings

Experimenter lists the selected works from *The Levee*, *Snow*, *The Coast*, *Look It’s Getting Sunny Outside!!!*, and *Life is Elsewhere* as archival pigment prints. Its records give editions and dimensions, rather than capture metadata; the image-specific entries are linked in [sources.md](sources.md). For *The Levee* specifically, the [Cincinnati Art Museum](https://www.cincinnatiartmuseum.org/thelevee) documents an 83-picture series made along the lower Mississippi River in 2016, while published scholarship describes its quiet, shallow or closed planes and its use of a medium-format film camera. That medium-format fact applies to the suite, not automatically to every other project here.

*Pati* requires a different material description: the Experimenter record presents it as a single-channel video with two-channel audio, and [MoMA](https://www.moma.org/magazine/articles/1160) identifies it as black-and-white video with sound. The chapter’s supplied file is therefore discussed as a still from a time-based work, not as a photographic print.

No consulted source establishes a camera maker or model, lens, focal length, aperture, shutter speed, ISO, film stock, flash unit, exposure mode, or post-production recipe for these individual images. The ingested JPEG manifest contains no usable capture EXIF. Descriptions of hard flash, exposure, black-and-white rendering, or wide-angle proximity in the chapter are visible properties of the supplied display files, not inferred camera settings.

## Era and access limits

The chapter’s images span 2008–2020, with Hura’s early domestic work alongside later projects on Kashmir, the Mississippi, the Indian coast, and Central India. *The Coast* is a long-term project that deliberately changes image pairings and withholds a stable contextual account; [Magnum’s project interview](https://www.magnumphotos.com/theory-and-practice/the-coast-sohrab-hura/) is useful context for that instability. Every image here is a credited, web-sized Experimenter display rendition used under the per-image fair-use rationale in [sources.md](sources.md); it is not a substitute for a print, video work, book, exhibition, or licensed reproduction.
