David Alekhuogie's photographs make an image answer to other images, supports, and sites. A house façade can turn a gathered group into a compressed public scene; a small printed insert can interrupt an otherwise empty field; a hinge, record sleeve, package band, or exposed timber frame can be as decisive as a depicted figure. The selected works move between photographs of place and photographs of constructed photographic objects. In both cases, the frame is not a neutral window: it is a way of binding attention, memory, material, and circulation. [Source notes](../../content/david-alekhuogie/sources.md)

## place held in a frame

<Plate slug="david-alekhuogie" id="01-birth-home" label="Birth Home, 2013" caption="The porch opening and hedge hold a gathered tour group at the threshold of the house." />

*Birth Home* gives the crowd a precise architectural address. The overlay's porch opening is not an invented inner frame: the columns and eaves genuinely contain the group, while the hedge makes a low lateral limit across the scene. The broad anchor names the cluster rather than pretending one person is the subject. This is a nearly monochrome web rendition, whose measured palette runs from #151515 to #cecece, with a tonal span from p05 0.035 to p95 0.804. That value range lets the bodies register as a dense event between dark shrubs and the pale façade.

<Plate slug="david-alekhuogie" id="02-ebenezer" label="Ebenezer, 2013" caption="A farmhouse, distant church, and rising field divide the painted landscape into unequal intervals of attention." />

In *Ebenezer*, a small farmhouse at left and a distant church at right do not balance the image evenly; the long rising field makes their separation active. The thirds grid is a measuring device rather than a claim that the work follows a rigid rule. The overlay traces the visible field sweep and keeps the two buildings distinct in scale. The normalized palette uses ochre #bdad7f, olive-brown #766634, and pale beige #d3cab2; its low measured edge density of 0.008 helps the broad painted land read as an extended pause between the structures. The High Museum identifies both this and *Birth Home* as 2013 pigmented inkjet prints; the image analysis concerns the available display rendition, not its original print color.

## photographs become objects

<Plate slug="david-alekhuogie" id="03-constructions-01" label="Constructions, image 1 of 15, undated" caption="A wooden frame makes the blue wall and small poster into a nested picture-space." />

The blue support field of *Constructions* image 1 is not mere backdrop. It surrounds a wood-framed interior in which a poster, bed, and tabletop take on a compressed, staged relation. The table's rear edge sets a modest horizontal measure, while the poster figures become the local point of concentration. The palette's saturated blue #087bc0 and lighter #50a5db dominate against wood and small orange accents; p05/p95 of 0.231/0.941 explains the strong separation of wall, frame, and poster. The work makes framing feel cumulative: an image is mounted within an object, itself photographed within a larger blue field.

<Plate slug="david-alekhuogie" id="04-constructions-02" label="Constructions, image 2 of 15, undated" caption="A raised hand and treeline occupy a small color inset beneath a moon in a black film-strip field." />

Image 2 reverses that density. Most of the frame is black, punctuated by a small color inset and a single bright moon. The overlay deliberately refuses the analyzer's tempting but meaningless convergence from film-border marks: its rectangle, moon ellipse, hand path, and treeline stay on visible features. With mean luminance 0.041 and edge density 0.249, this is the darkest and most granular normalized file in the selection. The raised hand becomes consequential because it rises out of a miniature landscape rather than out of a continuous scene.

<Plate slug="david-alekhuogie" id="05-constructions-03" label="Constructions, image 3 of 15, undated" caption="The central hinge divides two photographic fields while exposed wood keeps the construction visibly object-like." />

The two panels in image 3 are linked by a real hinge, not a symbolic axis. Its vertical line makes the left and right photographic fields comparable without making them equivalent. The top enclosure edges describe the skewed housing that holds both pictures; wood grain, hinges, and blank board remain visible instead of disappearing behind the imagery. A palette of pale gray #e8e6e3, wood beige #cdc5b2, and charcoal #242932 supports that alternation between support and photograph. The low 0.052 edge density keeps the construction legible as a few large relationships rather than an undifferentiated collage.

<Plate slug="david-alekhuogie" id="06-constructions-04" label="Constructions, image 4 of 15, undated" caption="A record sleeve, taped note, portrait, and color-control strip turn a face into a layered surface." />

In image 4, the black record sleeve is an enclosing field, but it is continually interrupted: the portrait is only partial, a taped note crosses it, and a color-control strip turns reproduction into a visible procedure. The overlay names each bounded component rather than treating the image as a conventional portrait. Its unusually broad p05/p95 span of 0.090/0.957 agrees with the collision of white card, black sleeve, warm skin, and orange package color. The facts of the pictured construction are visible; the source record does not authorize a more specific account of how the original work was physically made.

<Plate slug="david-alekhuogie" id="07-constructions-05" label="Constructions, image 5 of 15, undated" caption="A yellow sleeve, vertical pipe, stair trim, and timer dial make a utility room into overlapping frames." />

Image 5 makes a domestic utility space feel diagrammatic without making it orderly. The yellow record sleeve is a bright rectangle on the left; the pipe interrupts the center; the rising stair trim points toward a timer dial at right. These are visible contours, so the overlay uses interpretive paths rather than claiming a false vanishing point. The normalized file has the group’s widest tonal spread, p05 0.012 to p95 0.937, with yellow #f8e05c set against dark #13121d. Bright sleeve and dark equipment pull the eye in opposite directions, leaving the pipe to mediate the frame.

<Plate slug="david-alekhuogie" id="08-constructions-06" label="Constructions, image 6 of 15, undated" caption="A potted plant and translucent strip divide the timber-framed work into pictured and material zones." />

The timber frame in image 6 holds a deliberately uneven interior. A small potted plant sits low at left, while the translucent strip takes over the right side, separating a blurred figure and plant imagery from a visibly material screen. The overlay's vertical seam names that division without pretending it is a landscape horizon. This high-key normalized reproduction has mean luminance 0.852 and a p95 of 0.992; black #1d1b0f and plant-green/brown accents become especially forceful against the pale field. The brightness makes the strip's opacity and the pot's weight easy to compare.

<Plate slug="david-alekhuogie" id="09-constructions-07" label="Constructions, image 7 of 15, undated" caption="Package bands and printed faces wrap around a central plywood block inside a timber enclosure." />

Image 7 turns packaging into a spatial skin. The full timber enclosure gives the object a stable outer limit, but the upper package band slides across it and the centered plywood block interrupts the printed material. The small ellipse around a child's face is a local citation of the imagery, not a claim that the face governs the entire object. In the normalized palette, red #e1391f rises sharply out of pale gray and black; p05/p95 of 0.024/0.953 preserve that abrupt pressure. The construction makes repetition and obstruction occur at the same time.

<Plate slug="david-alekhuogie" id="10-constructions-08" label="Constructions, image 8 of 15, undated" caption="A window grid and tiny figurine resist the large downward sweep of the orange garment." />

The orange garment in image 8 is a broad descending mass, but the work does not let it become a single uncomplicated subject. The window grid contains a diagonal shadow at left; the small figurine fixes the lower edge; blue tape breaks the orange drape at several points. The overlay treats those as counterweights inside the displayed work’s outer frame. Orange #f5a05a and #ee6020 dominate the measured palette, while the low 0.037 edge density makes the dark shadow and small figurine unusually legible. The contrast is structural, not an inferred account of lighting or camera settings.

## density beside reserve

<Plate slug="david-alekhuogie" id="11-negative-positive-01" label="Negative_Positive, image 1 of 11, undated" caption="An exact split puts a dense black panel beside a sparse blue-marked field without making the halves mirror images." />

*Negative_Positive* image 1 stages its key decision at the join. The dense black panel and sparse blue-marked field occupy comparable rectangles, yet their visual weight is radically unequal. The overlay uses the literal diptych split, the two panel bounds, and a lower blue gesture; it avoids calling the arrangement symmetrical simply because the support is divided. The file’s p05/p95 of 0.055/0.976 and palette of pale #f5f2f0 against dark #1d1512 make the opposition almost tonal before the marks are read as imagery. Reserve becomes an active material, not an empty remainder.

<Plate slug="david-alekhuogie" id="12-negative-positive-02" label="Negative_Positive, image 2 of 11, undated" caption="Leaning orange and blue-marked panels use the gallery floor to make uneven balance visible." />

The second *Negative_Positive* image moves that imbalance into the room. The orange panel leans and bears a dense collage; the blue-marked panel stands beside it; a pale third support stays further right. The overlay names the two principal bounds and holds a broad anchor on the orange collage, where the eye sees packing, tape, and printed fragments accumulate. The palette repeats orange #f38639, beige #d1cbc4, and dark #221f21, while the 0.020 edge density keeps the panels distinct from their pale gallery setting. Rather than supplying a neat final symmetry, the photograph leaves support, floor, and image in negotiated balance.

## Exercises

<ExerciseCard n="01" title="Make the support do work">
Photograph an image inside a frame, sleeve, window, or box. Keep enough of the support visible that it changes the meaning of the picture rather than merely decorating it.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="02" title="Use a seam as a decision">
Place two image fields side by side. Let a hinge, edge, overlap, or strip make their relation visible, then test whether the seam creates comparison, interruption, or concealment.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="03" title="Balance density with reserve">
Build one crowded surface and place it next to a calmer field. Make the empty-looking space carry an equal compositional job: scale, pause, direction, or contrast.
</ExerciseCard>
