# David Alekhuogie — research notes

## Scope of the source record

The selected material combines two dated 2013 works in the High Museum of Art collection with ten numbered public portfolio images. The High Museum identifies *Birth Home* (accession 2018.11) and *Ebenezer* (accession 2018.12) as David Alekhuogie works from 2013. The artist's portfolio identifies the remaining images only as numbered entries from *Constructions* (images 1–8 of 15) and *Negative_Positive* (images 1–2 of 11); it does not supply individual dates, media, or locations for those images. Titles, credits, source links, and the per-image fair-use rationale are retained in [sources.md](sources.md).

## Process, print, and context

The High Museum records *Birth Home* as a 30 × 40 in pigmented inkjet print and *Ebenezer* as a 24 × 30 in pigmented inkjet print. That medium is documented only for those two collection works. MoMA's 2020 discussion describes Alekhuogie's use of diptychs, triptychs, and photographs stitched like quilts; it also documents a separate project in which he re-photographed printed images placed at meaningful Los Angeles sites. This supports discussing montage, repetition, and remade photographic surfaces as elements of his wider practice, but it does not establish the production method of every portfolio image selected here. A 2016 interview similarly describes a move from documentary photography toward painting, sculpture, and material experiment; its comments about commercial material are context, not proof of the material in any individual shown work.

## Technical limits

None of the twelve ingested public display JPEGs retains camera EXIF. The source record does not provide camera body, lens, focal length, aperture, shutter speed, film stock, sensitivity, lighting setup, or a uniform print process for this selection. Accordingly, the chapter does not infer capture settings or make unverified production claims. Its palette, tonal-range, and edge-density observations are deterministic measurements of the normalized web reproductions, including photographs of installed or constructed works; they are not measurements of original prints.
