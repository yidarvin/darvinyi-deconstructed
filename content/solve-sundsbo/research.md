# Sølve Sundsbø — research note

## Scope and sources

Sølve Sundsbø (born 1970) is a Norway-born, London-based photographer and
filmmaker. His official archive describes a practice across still and moving
image that brings traditional techniques into contact with newer technology;
it specifically identifies 3-D scanning and hand-painted retouching as parts
of the wider practice. A 2013 *Vogue Italia* interview similarly describes
experiments with hand-painted retouching, X-ray photography, and 3-D scanning,
and places his move to London in 1995 after which he assisted Nick Knight for
four years. These are practice-level facts, not a claim that any particular
method was used for every plate here. [Official archive](https://www.solvesundsbo.com/),
[Vogue Italia interview](https://www.vogue.it/fotografia/interviste/2013/10/25/solve-sundsbo),
[National Portrait Gallery record](https://www.npg.org.uk/collections/search/person/mp121678/slve-sundsb).

The sourced sequence is a set of credited, web-sized fair-use reproductions
documented plate by plate in [sources.md](sources.md). It runs from the archive
labelled *Dazed and Confused* image of 1999 through *Vogue Nippon* (2011),
*Out of Order* (A/W 2017), and early-2020s magazine work. It is therefore a
selected route through a long editorial practice, not a catalogue raisonné.

## Technique and visual method

The source evidence supports an account of Sundsbø as an image-maker who makes
fashion bodies operate as graphic material: a close crop can turn a face into
a wet, reflective surface; a translucent sheet can make a portrait feel like
a screen; a reticulated contour can make a figure read like a diagram. Across
the selection, the durable compositional facts are visible rather than
speculative: off-frame gaze, cut-off bodies, enclosing dancers, mirrored eye
fields, flat colour masses, and foreground layers that interrupt easy depth.

The archive's documented scanning and retouching context helps explain why
these photographs often resist a simple distinction between photograph,
illustration, and design. It must not, however, be converted into undocumented
production history for individual images. The chapter therefore attributes
specific claims only to the visible supplied reproduction and its measured
overlay, and reserves statements about scanning, X-ray, and hand-painted
retouching for Sundsbø's practice as a whole.

## Era and publication context

The sources identify the supplied plates as editorial and fashion-project
images: *Dazed and Confused* (1999); *Vogue Nippon* (November 2011); *Out of
Order* (A/W 2017); *Beauty Papers* 3 (S/S 2017); *Vogue Italia* 795 (November
2016); *The Surreal World* for *V Magazine* 133 (Winter 2021); *Face Off* for
*V Man* 45 (Winter 2020–Spring 2021); plus the archive-linked *V Magazine*
135 and *Perfect* 2 selections. Where the present source record does not
secure a date or issue, the chapter keeps the identification cautious rather
than inferring it from page publication or embedded file metadata.

## Printing and capture settings

No source used here records a print process, edition size, camera body, lens,
focal length, aperture, shutter speed, sensitivity, lighting plan, film stock,
or a complete digital-production workflow for any individual selected work.
The ingested files retain assorted JPEG EXIF fields, but those delivered-file
fields are not treated as a verified work catalogue or as historical evidence
for the finished editorial image. The chapter consequently makes no
work-specific capture or printing claim. Its tonal statistics and palette
descriptions refer only to the normalized web reproductions held in this
repository.
