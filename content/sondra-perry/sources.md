# Sondra Perry — source shortlist

This source set follows Perry’s transformation of screen-based images into physical viewing situations: bicycle and rowing-machine workstations, chroma-key rooms, sculptural monitors, and print/light-box formats. The eleven files below were retrieved directly from the publicly accessible Bridget Donahue archive’s web display endpoints, then opened successfully. Nine have an 840px long edge. The two 600px-long-edge files are retained as rare, compositionally essential exceptions: each still clearly shows the full workstation/print structure needed for analysis, and no larger public file was served by the archive endpoint.

**Rights basis:** **fair-use** for every selection. These are copyrighted contemporary works by Sondra Perry; the archive does not offer an open licence, and its work pages identify the artworks and, where applicable, image credits. Each use is limited to a web-sized, whole-frame public display copy for nonprofit criticism, scholarship, and transformative composition teaching. The complete published view is necessary to examine the relation between image, screen, apparatus, room, and viewer; a crop would remove that argument. The chapter credits and links to the archive record and does not substitute for the artwork, installation, collection record, or a licensed reproduction.

## Research context

MoMA’s artist record lists *Double Quadruple Etcetera Etcetera I & II*, *Resident Evil*, *Historic Jamestowne: Share in the Discovery and Take Several Seats*, *Wet and Wavy Looks—Typhon coming on for a Three Monitor Workstation*, *Graft and Ash for a Three Monitor Workstation*, and *netherrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrr* as works online: https://www.moma.org/artists/48584-sondra-perry. The Bridget Donahue archive supplies the work-specific media/installation descriptions used above. Mudam’s collection text for *IT’S IN THE GAME* explains Perry’s combination of family photographs, a basketball avatar, and chroma-key blue as an inquiry into authorship, representation, and cultural bias: https://www.mudam.com/collection/sondra-perry.
