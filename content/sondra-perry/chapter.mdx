# Sondra Perry: a screen can reorganize the room

Sondra Perry’s screen works do not treat photography as a window that opens
onto a stable world. Across projections, light boxes, lenticular fields,
workstations, and a mounted print, image, apparatus, and exhibition room keep
exchanging roles. A wall can become a chroma-key ground; a monitor can become a
face; a bike or rowing machine can turn looking into a bodily circuit. These
eleven web-sized archive views are used for composition criticism under the
documented fair-use rationale in the [source notes](../../content/sondra-perry/sources.md).

## the room is part of the image

<Plate slug="sondra-perry" id="01-a-terrible-thing" label="A Terrible Thing, 2019" caption="A projected field is boxed by gallery architecture while ceiling and floor edges converge into the display." />

In *A Terrible Thing*, the projection is not a detachable screen at the far end
of the room. The overlay’s two architectural rakes meet at the measured room
convergence, so bench, ceiling, floor, and projected field cooperate to make
viewing a spatial encounter. The web image’s modest edge density (0.082) and
gray-green palette, from #262928 to #b5bec1, keep that deep shell legible
without claiming that these JPEG values reproduce the installation’s light.

<Plate slug="sondra-perry" id="02-flesh-graft-ash-1" label="flesh, graft, and ash #1, 2020" caption="Three light boxes make a central face answer two flanking illuminated panels." />

The documented light boxes of *flesh, graft, and ash #1* organize their own
sequence. The central panel axis and face anchor make the middle panel a point
of address, but the flanking panels prevent it from settling into a conventional
single portrait. A compressed p05–p95 range of 0.443–0.780 and low edge density
(0.0115) make illumination and interval, not visual clutter, the work’s rhythm.

<Plate slug="sondra-perry" id="03-ocean-modifier" label="Ocean Modifer, 2020" caption="A thin lenticular band distributes visual weight across a wide, dark gallery field." />

*Ocean Modifer* asks for lateral reading. The overlay frames the long
lenticular band and marks the measured divide through its changing field;
attention moves across the work rather than resolving at one centred subject.
The dark JPEG average (0.275) and low p05 (0.094) make its brown, mauve, and
cream strip feel suspended in a much larger surrounding void. A still view can
only teach this broad layout, not simulate the lenticular change Perry built
into the work.

<Plate slug="sondra-perry" id="04-typhoon-coming-on" label="Typhoon coming on, 2018" caption="A receding gallery corridor and projection edge turn an image field into engulfing room-scale pressure." />

In *Typhoon coming on*, the significant frame is the room-scale projection wall.
The measured convergence sits at the leftward opening of the corridor while the
right projection edge sweeps back toward the viewer; this makes the luminous
field read as something entered rather than simply watched. The middle-gray
palette and p05–p95 span of 0.306–0.843 preserve gradation across an otherwise
turbulent visual event.

## blue is a system, not a backdrop

<Plate slug="sondra-perry" id="05-its-in-the-game" label="IT’S IN THE GAME ’17 - Full Installation, 2018" caption="Chroma-key blue makes wall, projected screen, and open floor share one artificial ground." />

The archive’s installation view of *IT’S IN THE GAME* makes chroma-key blue a
structural condition. A small projected rectangle interrupts a broad blue room,
while the room’s measured divide and off-centre balance refuse a neutral
gallery backdrop. The analyzer’s palette is led by #0226a1 and #10113e, and its
p05 of 0.059 preserves the force of that blue field against the pale floor.
Mudam’s collection account connects the work’s family photographs, basketball
avatar, and blue ground to questions of representation and cultural bias;
composition here shows how that ground organizes the encounter rather than
reducing the work to its color.

<Plate slug="sondra-perry" id="06-title-tk-2" label="Title TK 2, 2018" caption="A monitor and splayed support legs turn a utilitarian apparatus into a centred, figure-like structure." />

*Title TK 2* concentrates a large blue surround around one spare apparatus.
The wall-floor join stabilizes the monitor’s horizontal bar; below it, the
splayed support lines converge at the base like legs that cannot quite become a
body. The phi grid is the analyzer’s better fit for this frame, and the image’s
0.064 edge density makes those few supports more decisive than decoration.

<Plate slug="sondra-perry" id="07-eclogue-inhabitability" label="Eclogue for [in]HABITABILITY, 2017–2019" caption="Checkered floor and machine arm bring a converted backhoe into the depth of a corridor-like gallery." />

The converted backhoe in *Eclogue for [in]HABITABILITY* becomes a viewing
machine because it occupies a stage of real depth. The checkered floor and a
rightward machine arm both pull toward the corridor convergence, while the
central aisle axis keeps the apparatus from dissolving into texture. This is
the chapter’s densest file (edge density 0.109); its browns, reds, and pale
walls separate machine, floor, and gallery without inventing a camera
perspective beyond the documentation view.

## bodies operate the display

<Plate slug="sondra-perry" id="08-ffff-four" label="fffffffffffoooooooooooouuuuuuuuuuurrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrr, 2017" caption="A blue arc encloses the bicycle workstation, binding a body-scale apparatus to the display field." />

The bicycle workstation in *fffffffffffoooooooooooouuuuuuuuuuurrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrr*
does not sit politely beside its screens. Its measured anchor lies within the
apparatus, and the blue arc becomes an enclosing contour that makes hardware,
exercise, and view share a single figure-like footprint. The relatively pale
room (mean 0.533) is punctured by blue #146fe5 and dark equipment, so the
workstation reads as an active interruption rather than a specimen.

<Plate slug="sondra-perry" id="09-acrylic-gel-full-set" label="Acrylic Gel Full Set and Post Holy Land Experience Target Photo Studio Print, 2016" caption="A mounted print frames a portrait cluster inside a larger wall of white surround." />

This vertical archive view makes the object’s mounting part of its syntax. The
print frame, central axis, and portrait-cluster anchor distinguish a pictured
group from the physical object that contains it; the white wall is not empty,
but the condition that lets the framed surface declare itself. Its wide JPEG
range (p05 0.145 to p95 0.918) supports that separation. The record identifies
the work as a print, but does not document a fuller process, so no printing
recipe is assigned here.

<Plate slug="sondra-perry" id="10-wet-and-wavy-looks" label="Wet and Wavy Looks - Typhon coming on for a Three Monitor Workstation, 2016" caption="A red rowing machine punctures a blue room and redirects attention toward a three-screen workstation." />

In *Wet and Wavy Looks*, the rowing machine is a red directional event inside a
nearly monochrome blue enclosure. The machine anchor lies low in the frame,
while room convergence and a slightly tilted wall-floor join make the screens
feel like the destination of exertion rather than detached displays. This is
the darkest view in the set (mean 0.197; p05 0.0039), so the blue palette
#012199/#0032c0 and the red apparatus have unusually strong separation.

<Plate slug="sondra-perry" id="11-graft-and-ash" label="Graft and Ash for a Three Monitor Workstation, 2016" caption="A vertical bicycle workstation links three monitors and a body-scale apparatus into a single feedback loop." />

The tall view of *Graft and Ash* gives the workstation near-portrait scale.
The monitor cluster holds the measured anchor above the bicycle apparatus, and
the floor threshold keeps the object grounded while an axis joins display and
machine. At mean luminance 0.231, blue #262f99 and near-black #07040a compress
the room around those small screens. The low-resolution source remains here
because the full bicycle-screen relation is still legible; its documented
exception is described in the source notes.

## Exercises

<ExerciseCard n="01" title="Let the room enter the frame">
Photograph a screen, projection, or image object in a room. Make one version
that crops tightly to the display and another that keeps floor, wall, and one
architectural edge. Compare whether the room becomes a neutral container or a
directional part of the image.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="02" title="Use one color as a system">
Build a scene around one dominant color, then interrupt it with one small
screen, tool, or body. Keep the interruption small enough that the field still
sets the terms of attention. Mark only the division and shape that explain the
relationship.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="03" title="Make apparatus carry a gesture">
Photograph a machine, stand, cable, or monitor so its support lines lead toward
or away from a display. Decide whether it reads as equipment, a figure, or a
barrier, and annotate only the line or axis that causes that reading.
</ExerciseCard>
