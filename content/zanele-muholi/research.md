# Zanele Muholi — research note

## Scope and evidence

This chapter studies eleven web-sized collection display images: seven portraits
from *Faces & Phases* and four self-portraits from *Somnyama Ngonyama (Hail the
Dark Lioness)*. Titles, dates, credits, collection records, and the
image-specific fair-use rationale are preserved in [sources.md](sources.md).
The local files are display JPEGs rather than substitutes for prints or licensed
reproductions.

## Series, medium, and visual method

The selected *Faces & Phases* photographs date from 2010–14. Art Institute of
Chicago object records identify the selected works as gelatin silver prints and
describe the series as a collaborative, serial portrait archive of Black queer
and trans people. Near-frontal pose, clothing, and deliberately varied
backgrounds establish a shared vertical format without making sitters generic.

The 2015–16 *Somnyama Ngonyama* selections are also recorded as gelatin silver
prints by SFMOMA. Their visible method is self-staging through costume-like or
found materials, direct gaze, high contrast, and framing devices such as curtains
and doorways. This chapter limits its claims to visible construction—silhouette,
highlight, contour, and frame—rather than inferring a private motive from a prop
or expression.

## Documented limits

The sources support titles, dates, series membership, collection credit,
copyright notice, and print medium. They do not document camera body, lens,
focal length, aperture, shutter speed, ISO, film stock, exposure, lighting setup,
or darkroom recipe. Ingestion found output-file metadata only, not capture EXIF.
The chapter therefore discusses framing, posture, tonal range, and costume as
visible decisions, never as evidence of unrecorded settings.

## Analysis limits

The pixel analyzer sometimes proposed a horizon in a curtain, clothing edge, or
tonal change, and vanishing points in textile or headwear detail. The overlays
retain only features verified by visual inspection: faces, arms, garment and
shadow contours, curtain folds, doorways, and costume frames. No texture is
treated as perspective merely because it produces converging lines.
