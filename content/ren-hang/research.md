# Ren Hang: research notes

## Evidence base

This chapter studies eleven credited public web-display JPEGs supplied by Blindspot Gallery for the Estate of Ren Hang. The selected works are dated 2010 to 2014. They are used here under the image-specific fair-use rationale documented in [sources.md](sources.md), not under an open licence.

## What the records support

Blindspot Gallery describes Ren Hang's work as carefully choreographed arrangements of bodies, colour, animals, and plants. In this selection, the visible direct or hard-flash effect is central to the pictures' surface: it flattens depth while retaining contour, makes dark animals and flowers read as sharply placed interruptions, and makes skin and pale walls behave as clean fields. The records support discussion of cropping, repetition, bodily interlocking, figure-ground contrast, rhythmic grouping, and abstraction through scale.

The sample moves from *Untitled 50* (2010) and *Untitled 16* (2011) to a concentration of 2012 works, then *Untitled 66* (2014). It is a selected study, not a career survey.

## Production information

The available records do not identify camera body, lens, aperture, shutter speed, sensitivity, film stock, capture format, lighting setup, digital processing workflow, original print medium, paper, printer, edition, or print date. The eleven ingested web JPEGs contain no EXIF. Descriptions of flash in this chapter concern visible image effects and source notes, not inferred exposure settings or undocumented production facts.

## Measured web-file observations

The analyzer measures the supplied display JPEGs, not original prints. The soft body field in `03-untitled-46` has very low measured edge density (0.0033); `07-untitled-01` is similarly low (0.0036). `06-untitled-66` is the highest-edge-density file in the set (0.0707), consistent with its branches, profile, and horse contour. The brightest mean-luminance file is `10-untitled-45` (0.7767), narrowly ahead of `05-untitled-54` (0.7764); `06-untitled-66` is darkest (0.4335). The measured palettes range from pale gray-blue and skin tones to the green-black of the lotus image and the red-brown accents of painted hands.
