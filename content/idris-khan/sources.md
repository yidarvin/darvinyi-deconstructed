# Idris Khan — source shortlist

This set follows Khan’s central photographic strategy: scanning an entire sequence, archive, score, or image set and allowing repeated marks to become a new, simultaneous image. Eight complete-frame, publicly displayed JPEG renditions were retrieved from collection, gallery, and auction records and opened successfully. They range from 950px to 1302px on the long edge (except the 1200px-long Government Art Collection files), and are deliberately web-scale rather than print-production files.

**Rights basis:** **fair-use** for every selection. These are contemporary copyrighted works; no open-licence or public-domain rendition was located. Each assessment is specific to the cited published rendition: this nonprofit book uses a credited, record-linked, web-sized copy for transformative criticism, scholarship, and composition teaching; it needs the complete frame to analyze Khan’s accumulation and spatial organization; and it neither replaces an original, an editioned print, the source record, nor a licensed reproduction.

## Shortlist

### 01 — *every…William Turner Postcard from Tate Britain* (2004)

- **Why it matters:** Layered Turner postcards turn landscape into a low-contrast atmospheric field, where the broad horizontal bands and almost erased tree line make the collapse of many pictures visible as one image.
- **Source:** https://fraenkelgallery.com/exhibitions/idris-khan (Fraenkel Gallery, exhibition record and public display rendition: https://i0.wp.com/fraenkelgallery.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/03/IK-Turner.jpg?w=1024&quality=90&strip=all&ssl=1)
- **Rights / fair-use assessment:** Idris Khan; the gallery identifies the work as a 2004 digital C-print; © Idris Khan (rights notice confirmed on Government Art Collection records for Khan works). **Factor 1:** transformative teaching analyzes the layered horizon and tonal compression. **Factor 2:** a highly creative contemporary photograph. **Factor 3:** the full landscape frame is necessary to read its dissolved horizon, foreground, and vertical remnants. **Factor 4:** this 1024 × 740px gallery display copy is credited and record-linked, and cannot substitute for the mounted print or a licensed file.
- **File:** `raw/idris-khan/01-every-william-turner-postcard.jpg` (1024 × 740px)

### 02 — *every…Nicholas Nixon’s Brown Sisters* (2004)

- **Why it matters:** The four sisters’ repeated portrait sequence becomes a hovering, unstable group; faces and bodies fuse while the familiar horizontal family-portrait format remains just legible.
- **Source:** https://fraenkelgallery.com/exhibitions/idris-khan (Fraenkel Gallery, exhibition record and public display rendition: https://i0.wp.com/fraenkelgallery.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/03/Idris_Khan_001.jpg?w=1024&quality=90&strip=all&ssl=1)

## Research context

The National Gallery of Art records three additional core Khan works—*The Creation* (2009), *Houses of Parliament, London* (2012), and *Every…Bernd and Hilla Becher Spherical Type Gasholder* (2004)—but expressly marks its media as unavailable for download: https://www.nga.gov/artists/35267-idris-khan. The Guggenheim’s collection record for *Homage to Bernd Becher* (2007) confirms the water-tower work’s importance in the same serial-photographic line: https://www.guggenheim.org/artwork/21637. These records, together with the full-frame public display files above, establish a canonical arc from early appropriated photographic sequences to scores, industrial typologies, and later print-based accumulations.
