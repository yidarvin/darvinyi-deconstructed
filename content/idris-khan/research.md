# Idris Khan — research

Scope: technique, dates, printing, and camera information for the eight web-scale public display renditions documented in `sources.md`. The source records concern the finished works; the normalized JPEGs are analytical derivatives, not evidence of original capture or printing conditions beyond what those records state.

## Era and method

This selection runs from 2004 to 2019. The 2004 works re-compose Turner postcards, Nicholas Nixon's *Brown Sisters*, and the Bechers' industrial typologies; *Bach… Six Suites for the Solo Cello* dates from 2006. The later *Lost Happiness* and *Blue Rhythms* date from 2019. Across those bodies of work, Khan scans or layers an existing sequence, archive, score, or set of marks until repetition reads as a single simultaneous image. The source notes establish this sequence-based strategy and the individual titles; they do not make this selection a complete survey of his practice.

## Process and printing

The source records identify *every…William Turner Postcard from Tate Britain* as a 2004 digital C-print; *every…Nicholas Nixon’s Brown Sisters* as a 2004 digital C-print mounted on aluminum; and the Bach work as a 2006 Lambda digital C-print. The Government Art Collection identifies *Every… Bernd and Hiller Becher Gasholder* (2008) as a lithograph. The Phillips record identifies the prison-type gasholder as a 2004 chromogenic print, while Sotheby’s identifies the spherical-gasholder work as a 2004 pigment print. Sean Kelly Gallery identifies *Lost Happiness* as a 2019 digital C-print and *Blue Rhythms* as gesso and oil stick on eighteen black-and-white fibre prints.

Those catalogued media must not be flattened into one process. The visible JPEG palette, density, and edge measurements in this chapter describe the supplied web reproductions after ingestion, not a claim about a particular paper, ink, scanner, or edition beyond the cited records.

## Camera settings

No source in the packet documents a camera body, lens, focal length, aperture, shutter speed, sensitivity, or exposure for these individual works. That absence is especially material here: Khan’s finished pictures derive from scanned or layered prior materials, so familiar camera-setting language would misdescribe the operative method. `manifest.json` contains no original-camera EXIF. No setting is inferred or invented.

## Rights and source route

Every image is used under the image-specific fair-use rationale in `sources.md`. The sources identify the artists, media, and public display records; the complete, web-sized rendition is necessary here to teach Khan’s accumulation, format, and spatial organization. These analytical copies do not substitute for an editioned print, collection record, auction record, or licensed reproduction.
