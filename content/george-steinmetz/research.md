# George Steinmetz — research note

## What the supplied records establish

The ten selected photographs span source assets dated 1997–2013; the two National Geographic plates are identified by their 2008 and 2013 publication contexts. The set therefore represents a late-1990s-through-2010s aerial practice, not a single dated project. The supplied National Geographic profile describes Steinmetz's preferred working altitude as roughly 100–500 feet and calls the result “street photography from the sky.” That supports describing a low aerial vantage that joins broad pattern to human activity. It does not establish the altitude of any particular frame.

The source records credit every image to George Steinmetz. They identify publisher display copies used here as individually documented, credited, web-sized fair uses for criticism and composition teaching; they do not document print editions, paper, ink, or other output process for this selection.

## Technique visible in the selected frames

The recurring method is an aerial change of scale: animal groups, workers, a road, a plane shadow, or a dense settlement remain visually specific while surrounding land reads as a large field. The sources specifically support the following readings: repeated animals in open shallows; a plane shadow in a mineral field; a black-water curve against pale sand; pepper piles, workers, and a tractor; smoke over conversion to farmland; modular salt pits; an oil road punctuated by buildings; and rooftops that read as tessellation and circulation.

## Technical limits

No supplied source establishes camera body, lens, focal length, aperture, shutter speed, ISO, capture medium, aircraft type, capture format, or printing process. The ingested publisher JPEGs contain no usable EXIF camera metadata. Their pixel dimensions are display-copy dimensions, not original-negative, capture, or print dimensions. The chapter consequently describes only documented aerial practice, the source dates/publication contexts, and visible or measured compositional structure.
