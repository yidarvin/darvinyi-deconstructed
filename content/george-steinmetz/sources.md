# George Steinmetz — source shortlist

This ten-image set introduces George Steinmetz’s low-altitude aerial practice through frames where human systems and landforms become readable as both abstract pattern and lived terrain. Eight works are from Condé Nast Traveler’s freely accessible 2017 Steinmetz gallery; its captions identify the locations, credit Steinmetz, and serve public display JPEGs. Two further works come from National Geographic’s freely accessible 2014 profile, whose captions and image credits identify Steinmetz. No open-licence or public-domain rendition was located for these contemporary works.

**Rights basis:** **fair-use** for every selection. The photographs are copyrighted; Condé Nast Traveler’s display captions credit George Steinmetz, and National Geographic identifies him as photographer while displaying the notice “Please be respectful of copyright. Unauthorized use is prohibited.” The following are individual, limited assessments, not a claim of blanket permission. This nonprofit chapter uses credited, page-linked, web-sized copies for transformative criticism, scholarship, and composition teaching. Each complete frame is needed to analyze its spatial organization; none substitutes for the artist’s prints, books, the publisher’s record, or a licensed reproduction.

## Research context

National Geographic describes Steinmetz’s preferred working altitude as roughly 100–500 feet and calls the result “street photography from the sky”; its profile explains how this vantage joins large pattern to human activity. Condé Nast Traveler’s gallery likewise identifies the locations and credits the eight selected photographs to Steinmetz. Together, these sources support a chapter built around the ethical tension and compositional power of seeing extraction, agriculture, water, wildlife, and settlement as one continuous aerial field.
