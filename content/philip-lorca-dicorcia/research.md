# Philip-Lorca diCorcia — research notes

## Scope and evidence

The supplied, credited museum display files span the late-1970s domestic photographs (*Hartford* and *New Haven*), *Salonika* (1980), *Hustlers* (1990–92), travel and street pictures from 1995–2000, and *Hannah* (2004). MoMA's artist context, recorded in [sources.md](sources.md), describes diCorcia's combination of real people and places with arranged objects, lighting, and framing; it identifies the late-1970s friends-and-family photographs, *Hustlers*, and later street work as key bodies of work. That supports an account of staged-looking encounters across different settings, not a claim that every pictured element was arranged.

## Process, printing, and settings

The Art Institute of Chicago identifies *Havana* and *Ike Cole, 38 Years Old, Los Angeles, California, $25* as chromogenic prints. The supplied SFMOMA source records establish titles, dates, artist credit, copyright notice, and collection-display renditions for the other eight works, but do not in this source set establish a print process for each one.

None of the ten ingested web-display JPEGs contains camera EXIF. The available sources do not document a camera body, lens, film stock, focal length, aperture, shutter speed, ISO, lighting diagram, or post-production workflow for these individual photographs. The chapter therefore treats evident pools of light, dark framing, and selective focus as visible compositional effects, not as proof of particular equipment or exposure settings.

## Measured display-file observations

The analyzer describes very different tonal fields in the supplied reproductions. *Hannah* is the darkest (mean luminance 0.127; p05/p95 0.063/0.220), while *Havana* has the widest bright range (0.114–0.933) and *Tokyo* the highest measured edge density (0.111). *Salonika* is also dense (0.121), consistent with its rebar lattice, whereas *New Haven* is unusually spare (0.010). Palette representatives run from near-black `#0b0f15` in *Naples* and `#171415` in *Hartford* (1979) to the blue-green room colors of *Hartford* (1978) and the pale street values in *Havana*. These measurements describe the normalized museum display JPEGs, not original-print color or density.

## Method for this chapter

The overlays name only visible structures: courtyard threshold, pantry, rebar screen, tub rim, crossing, roofline, head, pole-stage cables, street recession, market façade, and car roof. They deliberately omit detector proposals that do not make a truthful photographic claim, including several apparent vanishing points and symmetry axes. The chapter uses the source record for titles, dates, bodies of work, artist credit, and print media where explicitly supplied; it uses the analysis files for claims about the reproduced frame's geometry and tonal distribution.
