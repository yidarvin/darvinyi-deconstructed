# Jason Salavon — research

Scope: technique, date, medium, and camera information for the twelve public
display renditions recorded in `sources.md`. Several of those renditions are
installation or presentation views. Measurements made after ingestion describe
those supplied JPEGs, never an edition, a source image set, or a contributing
photograph.

## Era and method

The selection spans 2002–2016, but it is not a single story of photographic
averaging. Salavon uses artist-authored software, statistical composites,
sampled film frames, virtual reconstructions, data visualization, palette
systems, image-search results, and procedural patterning. The official archive
describes *Golem (Printer)* (2002) as one of 100,000 abstract paintings made by
an automaton program; the supplied image documents the printer/display setup,
not an averaged printer portrait. *114 Homes for Sale, Dallas/Ft. Worth
Metroplex* (2002) mean-averages realtor images in regional median-price bands.

The 2004 *Emblem (Apocalypse Now)* is a digital C-print whose sampled film
frames flow outward in concentric rings. *100 Special Moments* (2004), also a
digital C-print, combines one hundred internet commemorative photographs using
both mean and median operations. *Still Life at the Speed of Sunrise* (2005) is
an 80-minute, continuously changing synthetic video made with custom software
and shown on an industrial LCD; its still frame must not be described as a
photographic aggregate.

The later works shift the operative system rather than simply extending an
average. *The Loop, Chicago, 1848–2007* (2007) is a unique three-lightbox work
with digital C-prints, built as a virtual, semi-transparent reconstruction.
*American Varietal (US Population, by County, 1790–2000)* (2009) translates
county-population data into a three-dimensional form. *Baroque Painting*
(2010) uses a 1,024-colour palette derived from Rubens paintings and orders
colour by saturation and frequency. The 2012 *<Color> Wheel* and *Good and
Evil ’12* are archival inkjets assembled through image-search systems: colour
terms structure the former, positive and negative terms the latter.
*Automatic Pattern for You* (2016) is an archival inkjet on paper; the archive
describes 300 unique 15 × 15 inch prints that transform pattern photographs
into painterly abstractions.

## Process, printing, and source boundaries

The documented media differ: digital C-print for *Homes*, *Emblem*, and *100
Special Moments*; custom software and industrial LCD for *Still Life*; three
lightboxes/digital C-prints for *The Loop*; and archival inkjet for *Baroque
Painting*, *<Color> Wheel*, *Good and Evil ’12*, and *Automatic Pattern for
You*. The supplied display files do not establish a single paper, ink, screen,
edition, or installation process for every plate. They are web-scale critical
references used under the itemized fair-use rationale in `sources.md`.

## Camera settings

No source in the packet documents a camera body, lens, focal length, aperture,
shutter speed, ISO, film, sensor, or exposure for these finished works. The
only retained EXIF in `manifest.json` appears on the supplied presentation
rendition for *Automatic Pattern for You*; it identifies an iPhone 6s capture
of that rendition, not the artist's production settings. No camera setting is
inferred for any work.
