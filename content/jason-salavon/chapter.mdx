Jason Salavon makes pictures out of systems that usually remain offstage: a program's
output, a stack of realtor photographs, sampled film frames, a virtual city, a data
set, a search query, or a pattern-recognition rule. The twelve supplied files are
public web renditions, often installation documentation rather than straight-on
artwork files. Their overlays therefore distinguish the pictured presentation from
the work's internal image structure. Source routes, credits, and item-specific
fair-use reasoning are in the [source notes](../../content/jason-salavon/sources.md).

## a system can make a picture before it makes a scene

<Plate slug="jason-salavon" id="01-golem-printer" label="*Golem (Printer)*, 2002 (installation view)" caption="The printer, roll, and successive generated images turn software output into a physical sequence." />

The axis and paper-roll edges make the supplied installation view feel almost like a
portrait of a machine, but *Golem* is not an averaged printer image: the archive
describes an automaton program that generated abstract paintings. The proof keeps its
claim at the level of the displayed apparatus. Its p05–p95 tonal range is 0.098–1.000,
with 0.0245 edge density; gray `#a5a5a0`, green-gray `#4d5548`, and near-black
`#212220` leave the bright printed colour fields as interruptions in a workshop-like
space.

<Plate slug="jason-salavon" id="02-homes-dallas-fort-worth" label="*114 Homes for Sale, Dallas/Ft. Worth Metroplex*, 2002" caption="The mean-averaged realtor images leave a centered, hazy suburban field." />

For *Homes*, the operation is explicitly statistical: realtor photographs are mean
averaged in regional median-price bands. The symmetric axis does not claim that any
single house is centered; it names the supplied composite's broad balance. The low
lawn band and tiny caption keep the field answerable to its print presentation. At
p05 0.325 and p95 1.000, pale blue `#8eacb8`, green `#546043`, and white `#fcfdfc`
produce a soft landscape with only 0.0067 edge density.

<Plate slug="jason-salavon" id="03-emblem-apocalypse-now" label="*Emblem (Apocalypse Now)*, 2004" caption="Sampled film frames are organized as a concentric disc rather than read as a film still." />

The two circles and vertical axis identify a radial order, not a vanishing point.
The archive describes frames sampled in time and arranged in outward-flowing
concentric rings. The rendition is correspondingly dense, at 0.1261 edge density, with a
near-black p05 of 0.051 and white p95 of 1.000; white `#fefefe`, brown-black
`#1c130e`, and umber `#352920` make the rings pulse between light and dark.

## aggregation is only one of several operations

<Plate slug="jason-salavon" id="04-100-special-moments-newlyweds" label="*100 Special Moments (Newlyweds)*, 2004" caption="Paired face contours and opposed dress and suit masses survive the mean-and-median composite." />

The archive identifies one hundred online commemorative photographs and both mean
and median operations. Here the overlay avoids pretending that soft forms are crisp
faces: it marks only paired face contour, a pale dress mass, and a dark suit mass.
The web rendition has no detected hard edge density, and its p05–p95 range of
0.122–0.847 carries brown `#60534b`, near-black `#2b272b`, and pale pink `#d9cecc`.
The visible couple is an effect of the composite, not evidence about any contributor.

<Plate slug="jason-salavon" id="05-100-special-moments-little-leaguer" label="*100 Special Moments (Little Leaguer)*, 2004" caption="A cap, face mass, and elongated player silhouette persist inside an exceptionally soft field." />

The same mean-and-median method gives this image a different convention to retain:
the centered youth-sports pose. The two ellipses and interpretive pose axis keep the
claim deliberately broad rather than inventing a sharp body edge. Its compressed p05–p95 range,
0.337–0.541, and zero detected edge density explain why olive-brown `#736450`,
`#806f5b`, and `#635646` read as one continuous atmosphere.

<Plate slug="jason-salavon" id="06-still-life-speed-of-sunrise" label="*Still Life at the Speed of Sunrise*, 2005 (display rendition)" caption="A generated pitcher and two vessels sit inside the pictured video field." />

This is the necessary break in any averaging story. *Still Life at the Speed of
Sunrise* is a completely synthetic, continuously changing 3D animation shown on an
industrial LCD. The overlay names the documented video field, pitcher, and right
vessel, not a photographic accumulation. Black `#1c1a0c`, brown `#372f26`, and pale
`#faf2ea` span p05 0.043 to p95 0.984, while 0.0230 edge density lets the luminous
objects appear unusually solid.

<Plate slug="jason-salavon" id="07-the-loop-chicago" label="*The Loop, Chicago, 1848–2007*, 2007 (installation view)" caption="Three lightboxes step laterally through a virtually reconstructed city." />

The three frames are properties of the supplied installation view, but they make the
work's lateral rhythm legible. The archive describes a modeled Chicago made with
semi-transparent textured rectangles and then virtually photographed; it is not a
historical image index. The web rendition ranges from p05 0.165 to p95 0.820, with
0.0463 edge density and beige `#9a9185`, pale `#c8c6d3`, and gray `#67636d` helping
the transparent architecture hold together across the panels.

<Plate slug="jason-salavon" id="08-american-varietal-us-population" label="*American Varietal (US Population, by County, 1790–2000)*, 2009 (installation view)" caption="The mural's panel grid contains alternating diagonals and a shared lower rail." />

The archive identifies a Census installation mapping 3,171 counties and their
1790–2010 population-growth curves. The overlay treats its mural frame, lower rail,
and coloured diagonal flow as visible facts of this documentation, not as a literal
map of population density. The saturated panel field reaches 0.0674 edge density;
blue-gray `#424752`, near-black `#1e2024`, and slate `#6e656f` sit between p05 0.086
and p95 0.898.

## ordering systems make their own compositions

<Plate slug="jason-salavon" id="09-baroque-painting" label="*Baroque Painting*, 2010" caption="Nested squares funnel the palette system toward a tiny terminal centre." />

The archive describes a 1,024-colour palette derived from Rubens paintings, ordered
by saturation and frequency. This is not an averaged or blurred baroque painting.
The two nested frames, horizontal and vertical axes, and terminal square show how the
optical image is built by repeated enclosure. It is also the sharpest rendition in
this selection: 0.2092 edge density, p05 0.047, p95 0.996, and a palette moving from
burnt brown `#4e3016` through `#725331` to black-brown `#1b110b`.

<Plate slug="jason-salavon" id="10-color-wheel" label="*Color Wheel*, 2012 (installation view)" caption="Thousands of colour-term search results form a ring around a deliberately empty centre." />

The overlay follows the mounted print, perimeter, and central void; it rejects the
analyzer's tempting but false perspective lines. The archive specifies thousands of
Bing image-search results for colour terms. The screenshot-like rendition remains
highly articulated at 0.1413 edge density, with p05 0.216 and p95 0.980; near-white
`#eef0f1`, gray `#cdcdcb`, and warm `#796e5e` are measurements of this web view, not
claims about every thumbnail in the wheel.

<Plate slug="jason-salavon" id="11-good-and-evil-12" label="*Good and Evil ’12*, 2012 (installation view)" caption="Two framed sunburst fields make a query-based diptych, not a statement about the moral value of its source images." />

The paired panels derive from roughly 25,000 thumbnails associated with one hundred
positive and one hundred negative words. The overlay therefore marks the displayed
diptych and shared baseline rather than assigning moral content to its colours or
textures. The web rendition has p05 0.184, p95 0.988, and 0.1278 edge density;
`#d9d5d4`, `#f7f5f3`, and `#a79d97` describe the photographed gallery presentation.

<Plate slug="jason-salavon" id="12-automatic-pattern-for-you" label="*Automatic Pattern for You*, 2016 (installation view)" caption="Eight one-off prints align into a regular four-by-two display field." />

*Automatic Pattern for You* transforms pattern photographs such as rugs and tiles
into painterly abstractions, and the archive documents 300 unique one-off prints.
The image is an installation view: the overlay names the eight-work array and its
two row rhythms, not a single internally averaged pattern. p05 0.271 and p95 0.969,
with 0.0356 edge density and pale `#cac9c5`, gray-green `#a0a59d`, and taupe
`#7d7870`, keep the diverse samples visually united without making them identical.

## Exercises

<ExerciseCard n="01" title="Make the operation visible">
Choose a set of related images, a source list, or a small data series. Before making
anything, write down the operation: average, rank, sample, sort, repeat, or transform.
Make a result whose structure lets a viewer infer the operation without mistaking it
for a neutral photograph.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="02" title="Separate work from documentation">
Photograph or collect a presentation view of a picture, screen, or installation.
Mark which lines belong to the artwork and which belong to its frame, wall, caption,
or camera viewpoint. Compose a second image that makes that distinction productive.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="03" title="Test a query as a composition">
Gather a small set of search results using two different terms. Arrange them by one
visible rule, colour, order, word, or radial position, then ask what the layout reveals
about the query and what it cannot prove about the images it returned.
</ExerciseCard>
