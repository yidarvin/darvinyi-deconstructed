# Sofia Crespo: biology after the camera

Sofia Crespo's *Neural Zoo* (2018–2022) makes an encounter with an animal feel simultaneously familiar and unreliable. The artist identifies convolutional neural networks as the medium; these published images are not records of photographed specimens, but constructed images that use photographic surface, focus, texture, and framing to borrow the authority of natural history. In this ten-work selection, a body can first appear as a coral field, a specimen, a swarm, or a portrait, then begin to unravel. The artist-published web renditions are used here for criticism, scholarship, and composition teaching under the documented fair-use assessments in the [source notes](../../content/sofia-crespo/sources.md).

## a body can be a field

<Plate slug="sofia-crespo" id="01-opening-the-oceans-heart" label="Opening: The Ocean’s Heart, from Neural Zoo, 2018–2022" caption="A coral field rises diagonally toward a bright knot, then releases the eye into black water." />

*Opening: The Ocean’s Heart* does not put a creature cleanly in front of an environment. Its overlay follows a branching ascent from the lower-left field toward a coral knot at x0.367, y0.197, then marks a radiating junction that gathers nearby diagonals. The frame is dark but busy, with mean luminance 0.3345 and edge density 0.1517; near-black #0e090a, red-brown #8d4f3b, and pale #c7bbb0 let black gaps become as active as the colored growth.

<Plate slug="sofia-crespo" id="02-assemblage" label="Assemblage, from Neural Zoo, 2018–2022" caption="A loose central silhouette makes the square read as a creature assembled from translucent, competing parts." />

In *Assemblage*, the suggested body is held by a near-central vertical axis at x0.488, but the outline never becomes a stable anatomical boundary. The large ellipse identifies the aggregate rather than pretending that its limbs resolve cleanly; the lower bright mass at x0.664, y0.773 pulls recognition down through the body. A low mean luminance of 0.1938, high edge density of 0.1657, and black, blue-grey, pale pink, and amber palette turn the square into a dark stage for local flashes of material.

<Plate slug="sofia-crespo" id="03-the-lovers" label="The Lovers, from Neural Zoo, 2018–2022" caption="A tall black field turns an overlapping oval form into an unstable, specimen-like portrait." />

The unusually tall frame in *The Lovers* gives the object room to become a presence. Its central axis and enclosing ellipse make a single encapsulated body legible, while the upper luminous fold at x0.522, y0.211 prevents it from settling into a merely symmetrical diagram. With mean luminance 0.1720 and p95 only 0.6627, the dark surround is not empty space; it is the pressure that makes each amber and pale tissue edge read.

## taxonomy without a specimen

<Plate slug="sofia-crespo" id="04-courage" label="Courage, from Neural Zoo, 2018–2022" caption="A descending coil organizes a crowded field of green scales around a single bright edge flare." />

*Courage* places its force in a descending coil rather than a centered face. The overlay follows the snake-like chain through the frame and marks the bright flare at its upper-right edge; that flare keeps the layered greens from becoming one uniform texture. The analyzer finds a broad tonal span (p05 0.0039, p95 0.7137) and high edge density (0.1712), which agrees with the picture's simultaneous density and sharp local hierarchy.

<Plate slug="sofia-crespo" id="07-bug" label="Bug, from Neural Zoo, 2018–2022" caption="A nearly bilateral beetle body is held against black while detached limbs pressure its specimen axis." />

In *Bug*, the centered vertical axis at x0.500 makes the tall body read immediately as a mounted specimen. The enclosing ellipse states that primary mass, while the anchor on the right-side limb calls attention to a fragment that tries to break the symmetry. The image is the darkest in this selection by mean luminance (0.1115); brown #31271b, ochre #a7936d, and greenish body flashes emerge slowly from the black ground instead of describing a continuous natural habitat.

<Plate slug="sofia-crespo" id="08-taboo" label="Taboo, from Neural Zoo, 2018–2022" caption="A crowded bat mass crosses a black surround, making every overlapping muzzle, ear, and eye feel exposed." />

The overlay for *Taboo* contains the overlapping bat mass and traces a visible diagonal contour through it, while the measured horizontal light-to-dark break at y0.465 keeps the lower faces from floating free. The drawing remains an argument about visible overlap, not a claim that the animals are a real colony. At mean luminance 0.2092 and edge density 0.1769, the close crop has enough tonal information to make the black interval at left feel like a second, silent body.

## distributed attention

<Plate slug="sofia-crespo" id="05-soft-sea-of-awareness" label="Soft Sea of Awareness, from Neural Zoo, 2018–2022" caption="A translucent central cluster shares the frame with isolated jellyfish, so center and periphery continually exchange weight." />

*Soft Sea of Awareness* reverses the specimen logic. Its central translucent cluster is the largest form, but the upper jellyfish at x0.134, y0.038 and scattered surrounding bells keep attention circulating around it. The vertical balance at x0.613 is deliberately approximate: it shows how a broad, soft mass can hold a frame without requiring rigid symmetry. The very high measured edge density (0.1995) comes from fine internal tentacles and membranes, not from a hard-edged subject.

<Plate slug="sofia-crespo" id="06-delicate-orchestration" label="Delicate Orchestration, from Neural Zoo, 2018–2022" caption="A loose moth column and a diagonal chain of scattered wings distribute attention across a bright field." />

The white field of *Delicate Orchestration* makes negative space part of the rhythm. The overlay's column at x0.438 and its path through scattered moths describe how attention travels from a lower cluster to the upper-right outliers without implying a literal flight trajectory. This is the set's brightest image (mean luminance 0.7345) and its least edge-dense (0.0510); beige #cbc7ba, brown #966d3b, and the sparse dark marks let small shifts of scale carry the composition.

## portrait as an unstable system

<Plate slug="sofia-crespo" id="09-realisation" label="Realisation, from Neural Zoo, 2018–2022" caption="A nearly centered parrot-like silhouette is disrupted by a white chest flare and stacked, contradictory feather planes." />

*Realisation* borrows the frontal force of a bird portrait but does not grant it one coherent anatomy. The portrait-balance axis at x0.463 and the large silhouette make the figure available at a glance; the anchor on the white chest flare and the dense feather planes postpone any easy identification. Its palette moves from black #060605 through yellow #d4a320 and green-blue #a7c0b8 to red #cf3511, while an edge density of 0.1891 keeps that color event materially fractured.

<Plate slug="sofia-crespo" id="10-free-will" label="Free Will, from Neural Zoo, 2018–2022" caption="A frontal anatomical figure is held in a central oval while smaller partial bodies turn the black surround into a taxonomy." />

In *Free Will*, the vertical anatomy line and enclosing oval make a large frontal body the chapter's clearest citation of a scientific plate. Yet the surrounding partial figures and the stacked organs deny it the stability of an atlas illustration. The scoreable symmetry axis sits at x0.438, but the visible anatomy continues to spill across it. High edge density (0.2041) and a palette of near-black #070609, flesh #a57a6b, pale #cfc0b6, and blue #3b5b8d make the body feel less like a single object than a crowded catalogue.

## Exercises

<ExerciseCard n="01" title="Make recognition arrive late">
Photograph or arrange one object so its overall silhouette reads quickly but its material identity remains uncertain. Use overlap, an unusual crop, or mixed textures; do not solve the ambiguity with a descriptive background.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="02" title="Build a taxonomy from fragments">
Make one frontal composition from three or more related fragments. Give it a central axis, then let one fragment break the apparent order so the image remains a system rather than an inventory.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="03" title="Let the surround do work">
Place a detailed subject against a large quiet field. Test whether a small bright edge, isolated secondary form, or negative-space break can keep the field active without adding a conventional background.
</ExerciseCard>
