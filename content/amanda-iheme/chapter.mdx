# Amanda Iheme: buildings keep company with the city

Amanda Iheme’s *The Way of Life* does not separate historic architecture from traffic, vendors, weather, signage, repairs, and people passing it. The four project cycles documented in the source record–*Death*, *Stagnation*, *Preservation*, and *Restoration*–supply a vocabulary, but these photographs make their argument through interval: an arcade above umbrellas, an empty court between wings, a tiled wall beside a pedestrian route, an entry opening onto a stair. [Source notes](../../content/amanda-iheme/sources.md)

## the street changes the scale of a facade

<Plate slug="amanda-iheme" id="01-casa-de-fernandez-death-01" label="Casa de Fernandez–Death–01, 2015/2019" caption="A repeated arcade remains legible above taxis, umbrellas, pedestrians, and a hard band of market activity." />

The roofline makes a long, nearly level bar above a smaller rhythm of arches. The market canopy band cuts across the building’s base, so the facade cannot sit apart as an object. The central market figure makes that collision of architectural and street scales clear. A p05–p95 span of 0.035–0.965 and palette including pale gray #ebecec, dark brown #231d18, and yellow #e3a318 separate sky, recesses, and vehicles.

<Plate slug="amanda-iheme" id="02-casa-de-fernandez-death-02" label="Casa de Fernandez–Death–02, 2015/2019" caption="Curb lines and flanking buildings steer a raised view into a packed market corridor." />

The second view makes distance concrete. The left curb line and measured street recession pull from foreground umbrellas toward small traffic at center, while tall newer buildings and low commercial fronts press in from opposite sides. Blue #84adde, charcoal #242425, and orange #d1844a make the roadway an open channel through competing verticals.

<Plate slug="amanda-iheme" id="03-casa-de-fernandez-death-03" label="Casa de Fernandez–Death–03, 2015/2019" caption="Foreground umbrellas establish a near field before the market thins into a distant street." />

The street threshold divides the image at y0.345, but the foreground market cluster refuses to become a neutral lower border. Umbrella tops, parcels, and shoppers take up most of the picture before the road begins to recede. The roof-edge line follows a visible structure toward the measured convergence rather than treating every patterned object as a leading line.

<Plate slug="amanda-iheme" id="04-casa-de-fernandez-death-04" label="Casa de Fernandez–Death–04, 2015/2019" caption="A weathered wall forms a near screen beside a narrow market street and a diagonally crossing utility cable." />

The facade plane occupies almost half the image as a shallow, scarred screen; the usable scene is squeezed into the left passage. A utility-cable diagonal and receding street make that opening active, while the balcony rail checks their movement. Its p05–p95 values of 0.133–0.937 keep bright street and shaded wall materially distinct.

## order can hold an empty court

<Plate slug="amanda-iheme" id="05-old-secretariat-stagnation-01" label="Old Secretariat–Stagnation–01, 2018" caption="A clock tower holds an empty courtyard between a deep right wing and distant high-rises." />

The Old Secretariat photographs make stillness structural rather than merely atmospheric. A clock tower sits inside a narrow tower bay, with rooflines extending away on both sides. The analyzer’s convergence falls near the left building edge, so the courtyard remains a space entered from the left and held from the right. Pale blue #aabbd3 and dark #23221f make sky and recesses do much of that separation.

<Plate slug="amanda-iheme" id="06-old-secretariat-stagnation-02" label="Old Secretariat–Stagnation–02, 2018" caption="A shallow courtyard sweep gives a long historic facade width without making it static." />

The wings spread across the format. A sloping main roofline and broad courtyard sweep keep the central pavilion from becoming a rigid centerpiece; the eye has to travel laterally before it returns. Mean luminance 0.540 and edge density 0.041 describe an open, legible field where small architectural differences can register.

<Plate slug="amanda-iheme" id="07-old-secretariat-stagnation-03" label="Old Secretariat–Stagnation–03, 2018" caption="A tower interrupts a long roofline while a balcony edge leads across the otherwise unoccupied court." />

The tower’s axis gives one vertical answer to the long roofline, but the balcony edge directs attention diagonally into the court. The overlay separates those jobs: the axis establishes a standing order and the line produces a route. With edge density 0.025, repeated openings and their intervals carry more weight than fine surface detail.

<Plate slug="amanda-iheme" id="08-old-secretariat-stagnation-04" label="Old Secretariat–Stagnation–04, 2018" caption="An entry arch contains a stair and dark inner rooms, turning the threshold into a layered interior." />

The interior is built from enclosure. The entry arch forms an internal frame, echoed by the closed dark doors at the outer edges. The small doorway anchor at left and the stair at right resist a single central subject: one route stays blocked and another climbs. Mean luminance 0.386 and gray-brown palette #918f8c, #241b14, and #463d37 sustain the difference between threshold and recess.

## preservation is a way of framing passage

<Plate slug="amanda-iheme" id="09-waterhouse-preservation-01" label="Waterhouse–Preservation–01, 2018" caption="A tiled facade, narrow route, and foliage opening draw the pedestrian street into the building’s frame." />

The first Waterhouse view takes an oblique route along the building rather than stopping at its facade. The building edge descends beside the paved threshold, and the measured vanishing point lies where the walkway joins the street. A dark canopy opening at upper left keeps the exposed wall from reading as pure surface. Pale #f3f0eb, tan #c5bcad, and dark #18160d make the white edge feel both luminous and substantial.

<Plate slug="amanda-iheme" id="10-waterhouse-preservation-03" label="Waterhouse–Preservation–03, 2018" caption="The long tiled wall, curb turn, and a small street vendor make a corner a measured public passage." />

Repeating arched windows establish a rhythm gradually broken by the big corner pier and the street beyond. The cornice is an actual visible diagonal, not a generic perspective cue; it draws across the facade while the curb turns toward the vendor. Mean luminance 0.585 and beige, gray, and near-black palette let the tile grid remain active without overwhelming larger divisions.

<Plate slug="amanda-iheme" id="11-waterhouse-preservation-05" label="Waterhouse–Preservation–05, 2018" caption="A grille and open doorway make paired arches answer one another across a tiled wall." />

This close view makes preservation a matter of unequal openings. The nearly frontal paired arches share an axis, yet the left is dense with ironwork and the right opens into darkness. The low, gently sloping wall-and-ground boundary prevents the patterned tile field from becoming a static diagram. Its high edge density of 0.175 is concentrated in grille and tile rather than spread uniformly across the frame.

<Plate slug="amanda-iheme" id="12-jaekel-house-restoration-01" label="Jaekel House–Restoration–01, 2017" caption="A low viewpoint turns roof, columns, louvered bands, and open windows into a tall, symmetrical facade." />

The Jaekel House view looks upward, giving the central gable axis a precise structural role. The roof edge provides a low, nearly level cap; beneath it, columns and louvered bands repeat across the facade, while the right roof support supplies a measured imbalance. A p05–p95 range of 0.067–0.992 separates nearly white sky from dark undersides and warm brown structure without claiming anything about exposure settings.

## Exercises

<ExerciseCard n="01" title="Make architecture share the frame">
Photograph a facade with the activity that crosses its base. Compare an isolated building with a frame in which signs, vehicles, umbrellas, or pedestrians cover part of it. Keep the version where use and time become visible.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="02" title="Use a threshold as a sequence">
Photograph a doorway, arch, or passage containing at least two depths. Mark only the enclosing frame and one path or anchor inside it; retain the marks that show how the eye enters and stays.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="03" title="Let repetition admit interruption">
Photograph repeated windows, columns, or bays with a small break: a person, open door, vendor, repair, or hanging object. Decide whether the break changes the structure or merely decorates it.
</ExerciseCard>
