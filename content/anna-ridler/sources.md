# Anna Ridler — source shortlist

This set follows Ridler’s photographic dataset work into the generative moving-image works it enabled. The twelve files were retrieved from the public display files on [Anna Ridler’s official project archive](https://annaridler.com/works), converted only from their served format to JPEG for local ingest, and opened successfully. All are 768px or larger on the long edge. The archive identifies its author as Anna Ridler; its project pages identify *Snapshots: Orchids* as a collaboration with Sofia Crespo. No open licence or public-domain statement is supplied for these contemporary works.

**Rights basis:** **fair-use** for every selection. These are public, web-sized display copies of copyrighted contemporary artworks, used in this nonprofit book for transformative criticism, scholarship, and composition teaching—not decorative replacement. Each entry below records the specific four-factor assessment. The complete published frame is retained because the argument concerns a dataset grid, screen sequence, installation scale, or the formal instability of a generated image; a crop would omit that relation. The chapter credits and links to the artist’s record and cannot substitute for a physical installation, video work, original, or licensed reproduction.

## Research context

Ridler’s own [works overview](https://annaridler.com/works) describes a practice concerned with how classification and machine vision decide what can be seen. The *Myriad* page states that the hand-labeled tulip photographs became the dataset for both *Mosaic Virus* works; the [V&A’s collection note](https://www.vam.ac.uk/info/collection-selection-boxes-artificial-intelligence) independently identifies selected photographs from *Myriad (Tulips)*, 2018, in its collection (museum no. E.78-2025). Together these sources support a chapter organized around how photographic repetition, annotation, and projection change the way an image is composed and read.
