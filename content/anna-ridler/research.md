# Anna Ridler — research notes

## Documented practice and chronology

Anna Ridler’s project pages describe a practice that makes classification visible rather than treating it as neutral background. *Myriad (Tulips)* (2018) is the photographic source project: its individual, hand-labeled tulip photographs accumulate as a physical installation. The artist’s *Myriad* page identifies that image set as the dataset for *Mosaic Virus 2018* and *Mosaic Virus 2019*. Those later works are generative moving-image/installations, represented here by supplied documentation views and web stills. *Broken Flowers (variations)* (2022) uses DALL·E-generated floral imagery; *Snapshots: Orchids* (2023) is credited by the artist as a collaboration with Sofia Crespo.

The V&A collection note independently records selected photographs from *Myriad (Tulips)*, 2018 (museum no. E.78-2025). It supports describing *Myriad* as a photographic artwork and dataset, but does not establish a camera, film stock, lens, print paper, editioning method, or the technical settings of the later generated works.

## Media, making, and what is not documented

The supplied primary sources support these distinctions:

- *Myriad (Tulips)* is a photographic, hand-labeled tulip installation; the relevant formal facts are serial prints, handwritten annotation, and a large accumulated grid.
- *Mosaic Virus 2018* and *Mosaic Virus 2019* are described by the artist as works made from the *Myriad* dataset. The supplied images show generated tulip states on screens and in installation views. They do not document model architecture, training duration, generation parameters, display calibration, or a conventional photographic print process.
- *Broken Flowers (variations)* is identified on the artist’s page as DALL·E-generated. The supplied composite is treated as the published artwork image, not evidence for a camera process.
- *Snapshots: Orchids* is a Ridler–Crespo collaboration. Its source image visibly organizes many Polaroid-like modules into one composite, but the provided record does not supply camera or printing specifications.

## File metadata

The ingest manifest preserves limited EXIF from source documentation files. The 2018 *Mosaic Virus* installation documentation (`05-mosaic-virus-2018-installation.jpg`) reports Apple iPhone 6 metadata: 4.15 mm focal length, f/2.2, 1/33 s, ISO 64, timestamp 2018-10-28 12:36:16. This describes that documentation file only—not the making of the screen work, its tulip source photographs, or the generative process. Other retained records provide timestamps only, or no EXIF. The chapter therefore does not infer camera settings or print technique beyond this narrow file-metadata fact.
