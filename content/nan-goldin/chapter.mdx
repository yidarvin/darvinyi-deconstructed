Nan Goldin's photographs make closeness structural. This six-work selection moves from a projected social sequence into cars, beds, a face at near distance, and a window held against daylight. The overlays do not convert those relations into neutral diagrams: they name the repeated devices that make the pictures feel contingent yet tightly organized: an enclosing frame, a pressure line, a body-sized oval, a bright opening, or a grid that turns separate images into one rhythm. [Source notes](../../content/nan-goldin/sources.md)

## sequence, enclosure, and proximity

<Plate slug="nan-goldin" id="01-the-ballad-of-sexual-dependency" label="The Ballad of Sexual Dependency, 1979/2001" caption="A bright projected rectangle gathers a dark room of viewers, making spectatorship and sequence part of the image's structure." />

The display record for *The Ballad of Sexual Dependency* documents a 720-slide installation with programmed soundtrack, rather than a single autonomous photograph. Here, the projected frame at upper right supplies the light and the subject anchor, while the low bench rhythm distributes the seated audience across the darkness. The analyzer's thirds grid is the closer fit; its very low p05–p95 range (0.004–0.294), dense 0.2858 edge field, and near-black-to-beige palette make projection feel like a small, warm event inside a room that resists description.

<Plate slug="nan-goldin" id="02-kim-and-mark-in-the-red-car" label="Kim and Mark in the Red Car, Newton, MA, 1987" caption="The roofline, windshield edge, and continuous red seat make two leaning bodies read as one confined interior." />

In *Kim and Mark in the Red Car*, the phi grid is the measured best fit, but the image's force comes from enclosure rather than an abstract proportion. The roofline arches over the pair, the windshield edge cuts through the bright opening, and the oval marks the grouped figure mass without pretending the two bodies are one person. The palette moves through rust #863a24, dark wine #401d1e, muted pink #b1745f, and blown window white #e2e2e4. With edge density only 0.0413, the red upholstery and the large zones of light do more compositional work than fine detail.

<Plate slug="nan-goldin" id="03-self-portrait" label="Self-Portrait, 1978/95" caption="Sixteen dye-bleach prints become a single field whose row divisions turn recurring rooms, bodies, and colors into an uneven rhythm." />

The museum identifies *Self-Portrait* as sixteen silver dye-bleach prints, and the overlay treats the displayed sixteen-part arrangement as the image's primary structure. The panel field and two horizontal dividers make each scene comparable without flattening their differences; the anchored upper light is a small point of recurrence among rooms, bodies, and faces. Phi is again the analyzer's best fit. Its p05 0.067 and p95 0.780, 0.1145 edge density, and palette of dark olive #211a10, teal #5d8071, pale gray-green #bcbda8, and orange #a57238 explain why color changes from panel to panel without dissolving the whole into visual noise.

## bodies held in a shallow room

<Plate slug="nan-goldin" id="04-j-and-richard-chicago" label="J. and Richard, Chicago, 1977" caption="A low bed edge, heavy headboard, and a contour around the resting figure hold two differently directed bodies in one shallow room." />

*J. and Richard, Chicago* makes separation visible without giving either person a cleanly independent stage. The measured line at y0.579 catches the bed-edge break, and the dark headboard boxes the left side before the body contour travels toward the pillow. The resting figure's oval makes the near body a horizontal weight; the seated figure is left exposed against a blue-gray field. The phi grid fits the analyzer better than thirds. Its p05–p95 span of 0.106–0.792 and restrained 0.0274 edge density keep the pale body, yellow bedspread, and dark furniture in a few large tonal planes.

<Plate slug="nan-goldin" id="05-suzanne-crying-nyc" label="Suzanne Crying, NYC, 1985" caption="A narrow face axis and two downward facial falls turn a close portrait into a field of pressure rather than detached observation." />

The image gives almost no reserve space. The centered axis and enclosing ellipse make the face's scale explicit, while the two diagonal facial falls follow the hair and face into the lowered gaze. The thirds grid is the measured fit, but its white dashed lines remain secondary to the cropped, warm face. The value range rises from p05 0.110 to p95 0.714; #211a20, #4a2623, #9d5931, #e29470, and #f2cfc1 supply a compressed dark-to-skin palette. A moderate 0.0676 edge density means exposure, texture, and the tear are legible without the background competing for attention.

<Plate slug="nan-goldin" id="06-clarence-in-the-window-with-gun" label="Clarence in the Window with Gun, 1989" caption="The window opening and a centered silhouetted figure make the bright exterior and dark interior press against each other." />

*Clarence in the Window with Gun* turns a window into a rigid counterframe. The overlay follows its opening, central vertical, sill break, and the two arms that stretch across it. The third-grid fit gives the tall image stable intervals while the silhouetted body interrupts them. The p05–p95 range of 0.118–0.890 is the broadest in this selection, and the palette of #22201e, #eadcd1, #d5b5a5, #6d5845, and #a58573 describes the conflict between interior near-black, white shirt, and pale exterior. Its 0.0524 edge density leaves the window's large value contrast to carry the tension.

## Exercises

<ExerciseCard n="01" title="Let an enclosure do the framing">
Photograph two people inside a car, doorway, or other tight container. Keep at least one hard architectural edge in the frame and let it decide how close the figures appear.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="02" title="Build a sequence, not a set of singles">
Make four to sixteen small photographs in connected rooms or over one evening. Place them in a grid and notice which repetitions of color, distance, or posture make the separate frames read as one visual sentence.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="03" title="Use a bright opening against a dark room">
Place a figure before a window or projection, then expose for the relation between silhouette and opening. Keep the surrounding dark space active enough that the figure is framed rather than simply cut out.
</ExerciseCard>
