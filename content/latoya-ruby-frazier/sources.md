# LaToya Ruby Frazier — sourced photographs

These six selected works are publicly displayed museum collection images of
copyrighted photographs. They are retained as web-sized reference copies for a
criticism and scholarship chapter, not as open-licence files. Every item below
has its own fair-use assessment: the use is transformative composition teaching
and criticism; the complete frame is needed to analyze the photograph; the
local copy is limited to a web-sized display image; and the chapter will credit
and link to the holding institution rather than replace the work, its museum
record, or a licensed reproduction.

1. **The Last Cruze** (2019) — `01-the-last-cruze.jpg` (843 × 562). The
   installation’s grid of individual worker portraits makes serial sequencing,
   repetition, and collective scale legible in one frame. Source: [Art
   Institute of Chicago, 264953](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/264953/the-last-cruze),
   retrieved from the museum’s public IIIF delivery service; credit: Partial and
   promised gift of Rennie Collection; The Alyce and Edwin DeCosta and the
   Walter E. Heller Foundation Endowment Fund; Major Acquisitions Centennial
   Fund; copyright notice: © LaToya Ruby Frazier, courtesy of the artist and
   Gladstone Gallery. **Use basis: fair-use.** The installation view is used to
   teach how its complete field turns many portraits into a collective argument;
   the 843px copy is web-sized and, with credit and a direct collection link,
   does not substitute for the installation, catalogue, licensed reproduction,
   or museum record.

2. **Huxtables, Mom, and Me**, from *The Notion of Family* (2008) —
   `02-huxtables-mom-and-me.jpg` (850 × 669). The mirror makes a frame inside
   the room and places the artist and her mother in one deliberately split
   space, a clear lesson in reflection, staging, and relational portraiture.
   Source: [Institute of Contemporary Art/Boston](https://www.icaboston.org/art/latoya-ruby-frazier/huxtables-mom-and-me-series-notion-family/),
   public collection display image; credit: Institute of Contemporary
   Art/Boston, anonymous promised gift; courtesy the artist and Michel Rein,
   Paris/Brussels; copyright notice: © LaToya Ruby Frazier. **Use basis:
   fair-use.** This is transformative criticism and scholarship; the complete
   mirror composition is necessary to teach nested framing and separated shared
   space; the 850px display copy is web-sized; attribution and the collection
   link do not replace the print, licensed reproduction, or museum record.

3. **Momme** (2008) — `03-momme.jpg` (1200 × 971). The tightly conjoined
   mother-and-daughter profiles create a shallow, almost fused pictorial plane,
   making overlap and conflicting sightlines the chapter’s most concentrated
   study in collaborative portraiture. Source: [Haggerty Museum of Art,
   2009.12.3](https://haggerty.zetcom.net/en/collection/item/6339/), public
   collection display image; credit: Museum purchase with funds from Mrs.
   Martha W. Smith by exchange; rightsholder: LaToya Ruby Frazier (the museum
   identifies her as artist; no separate copyright-year notice is supplied).
   **Use basis: fair-use.** The purpose is transformative composition
   scholarship; the whole frame is essential to analyze profile overlap and
   eye-line; the 1200px museum display copy is web-sized; credit and the object
   link do not substitute for a print, licence, or collection record.

4. **Mom and Me** (2008) — `04-mom-and-me.jpg` (750 × 467). The wide,
   horizontal domestic scene distributes two bodies across a room so that
   distance, furniture, and negative space become part of the relationship.
   Source: [Portland Museum of Art collection](https://www.portlandmuseum.org/collection/),
   public collection display image for accession 2023.2.1; credit: Museum
   purchase with support from Friends of the Collection; copyright notice: ©
   LaToya Ruby Frazier. The source supplied an AVIF web display image, preserved
   locally as a JPEG without cropping or visual alteration. **Use basis:
   fair-use.** It supports transformative criticism of distance and domestic
   framing; the full 750px web-sized frame is needed for that analysis; and
   credit plus the collection link do not replace the print, licensed image, or
   collection record.

5. **Mom Making an Image of Me**, from *The Notion of Family* (2008) —
   `05-mom-making-an-image-of-me.jpg` (850 × 820). The mirror-within-mirror
   arrangement turns the act of photographing into the subject, allowing the
   chapter to trace authorship through reflected frames. Source: [Institute of
   Contemporary Art/Boston](https://www.icaboston.org/art/latoya-ruby-frazier/mom-making-image-me-series-notion-family/),
   public collection display image; credit: Institute of Contemporary

   frame is necessary to analyze reflection and co-authorship; only an 850px
   display copy is retained; attribution and the museum link leave the original,
   licence, and record non-substitutable.

6. **Momme Portrait Series (Floral Comforter)**, from *The Notion of Family*
   (2008) — `07-momme-portrait-floral-comforter.jpg` (850 × 631). Two direct
   gazes and the floral field test how repeated pattern can bind a paired
   portrait while keeping each sitter distinct. Source: [Institute of
   Contemporary Art/Boston](https://www.icaboston.org/art/latoya-ruby-frazier/momme-portrait-series-floral-comforter-series-notion-family/),

## Coverage note

The six-image set exceeds the readiness minimum of four while avoiding a
redundant group of copyrighted display images. It spans Frazier’s intimate,
collaborative *The Notion of Family* portraits and the later collective
installation *The Last Cruze*: mirror structures, conjoined profiles, domestic
negative space, patterned grounds, and serial installation layout. All local
files were opened and dimension-checked after acquisition. No selected work is
public domain or offered as an open-licence image.
