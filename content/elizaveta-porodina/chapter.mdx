Elizaveta Porodina’s portraits make the fashion figure behave less like a stable sitter than like a force moving through an altered surface. Across this sourced selection, reflection, haze, saturated colour, costume, and pose repeatedly prevent a clean separation of body from setting. The portfolio record supports placing these editorial and commission images in late-2010s and early-2020s practice; it does not establish how individual distortions or colour treatments were made. The overlays therefore name only visible hinges: glass, a hat edge, an arm, a shoreline, and a headband, rather than attributing an effect to a lens or to post-production. ## a portrait can become a surface

<Plate slug="elizaveta-porodina" id="01-dazed-chanel-01" label="Dazed - Chanel Beauty, frame 01, n.d." caption="Ribbed glass repeats one face and torso across a wavering vertical surface." />

In the first *Dazed - Chanel Beauty* frame, the portrait is distributed rather than simply doubled. The oval holds the face behind rippled glass; the traced glass strip and repeated body band show why the figure cannot settle into a single outline. The web rendition is dark and restrained (mean luminance 0.396, p05 0.090, p95 0.725), with black-purple #221b21, muted green #627c63, and skin-toned #caa490 pressing against one another. Its low edge density of 0.0479 gives the glass pattern unusual authority: every interruption reads as a deliberate deformation.

<Plate slug="elizaveta-porodina" id="02-dazed-chanel-03" label="Dazed - Chanel Beauty, frame 03, n.d." caption="A hard hat edge and a right-facing profile organize an otherwise open orange field." />

The companion portrait works by containment. A glossy, black cap makes a strong enclosing mass; the short profile path sends attention through the eye and nose, while the hat edge counters it from the left. Orange, gold, and deep black dominate the supplied JPEG (#d17c31, #f1bb2d, #111418). The range from p05 0.020 to p95 0.776 is broad, but the edge density is only 0.0299, so the image reads as a few large, nearly cut-out colour blocks instead of a deep space.

<Plate slug="elizaveta-porodina" id="03-vogue-russia-creativity-03" label="Vogue Russia Creativity Issue 2021, frame 03" caption="A raised arm and the body’s S-curve keep a striped figure active inside a hot, hazed field." />

The 2021 *Vogue Russia Creativity Issue* image turns pose into a rhythmic line. The raised arm arches over the face, and the body turns through an S-curve before dissolving into the orange lower field. Those overlays do not claim a conventional stage horizon; they keep the reading with the actual figure. This is the group’s sparsest measured frame, at edge density 0.0127. Orange #f9a405 and #ea6d09, red #c31f1a, and deep maroon #541109 establish a narrow set of colour pressures while the body remains legible by silhouette.

## colour makes the pose unstable

<Plate slug="elizaveta-porodina" id="04-carolina-herrera-vanity-fair-04" label="Carolina Herrera × Vanity Fair US, frame 04, n.d." caption="An extended arm and a triangular yellow skirt cross two differently coloured picture fields." />

In the *Carolina Herrera × Vanity Fair US* frame, the figure is a bridge. The long arm cuts upward through the cool-to-warm split, and the skirt’s broad triangular perimeter pulls the composition back down. The eye reads yellow #ebc737 against red #bd5337 and blue-grey #708ba2 as much as it reads anatomy. A mean luminance of 0.535 and extremely low edge density of 0.0068 explain the clarity: large colour regions do almost all the structural work.

<Plate slug="elizaveta-porodina" id="05-sixteen-journal-06" label="Sixteen Journal, frame 06, n.d." caption="A reclining profile and a monumental foreground head exchange scale across a crowded shallow field." />

*Sixteen Journal* lets the model’s face remain small while the sculpted foreground head becomes a second, much larger presence. The neck-and-shoulder line is the hinge between them, not a false perspective route. The monochrome file has the widest tonal spread in this selection (p05 0.118, p95 0.984), and the grayscale palette runs from #292929 through #c4c4c4 to #efefef, keeping the two heads distinct. Edge density 0.0317 is enough to articulate the contact but not enough to release either form into descriptive detail.

<Plate slug="elizaveta-porodina" id="06-vogue-china-lina-04" label="Vogue China (Lina), frame 04, n.d." caption="A bent leg and profile-to-torso contour gather the seated body into one dark-blue mass." />

The *Vogue China (Lina)* frame makes a seated figure feel less posed than folded. The overlay’s bent-leg path and profile-to-torso contour collect the blue garment into a compact mass against lavender and yellow ground. The palette supports that compression: lavender #d1b4ca, dark teal #0b3033, and yellow #e8ac49 dominate an image with an edge density of only 0.0080. That scarcity of sharp structure makes colour boundaries, rather than drawn outlines, do the separating.

## masks, frames, and a direct look

<Plate slug="elizaveta-porodina" id="07-pan-and-the-dream-08" label="Pan and the Dream × Alice Hualice, frame 08, n.d." caption="Hands and hair mask the face while the arms make a shallow triangle above the plinth." />

Here, hands do not merely cover a face: they turn it into an unstable mask within a larger hair-and-body mass. The arm triangle and the low plinth edge make the pose read as sculptural without inventing deep space behind it. Pale lilac #d5c9d6 and peach #e6b795 surround darker blue-grey #7e88a5 and brown #38372f. With mean luminance 0.604 and edge density 0.0093, the image depends on soft changes in value and colour rather than crisp contour.

<Plate slug="elizaveta-porodina" id="08-vogue-china-cover-04" label="Vogue China Cover × Zoom, frame 04, n.d." caption="One eye anchors the close portrait while a necklace loop returns the gaze to the lower field." />

The *Vogue China Cover × Zoom* portrait has a clear attention relay: the left eye is the measured subject anchor, then the face-to-neck contour drops to the necklace loop. That loop prevents the split red-and-blue portrait from being a simple facial close-up; jewellery becomes a circular counterweight. The rendition is bright (mean 0.586, p95 0.882) but lightly edged at 0.0153. Red #e36b6b, pale blue #c5d7f6, green #304730, and blue #497783 divide the face into broad chromatic planes.

<Plate slug="elizaveta-porodina" id="09-vogue-us-beluga-whale-05" label="Vogue US × Beluga Whale Sanctuary, frame 05, n.d." caption="A face above the low shoreline is held between a dark cliff, a pale water field, and an incoming wave." />

The *Vogue US × Beluga Whale Sanctuary* photograph takes Porodina’s portrait logic outside the studio-like field. The face is a small anchor near the top; below it, the distant shoreline and incoming wave make the exposed water a set of layered intervals. It has the broadest tonal contrast in the group: p05 0.020 and p95 0.984, with near-black #061313, pale #d1dad4, and white #f9f9f9. Yet its edge density is only 0.0091, so the drama remains in the division of cliff, water, and figure rather than surface detail.

## the face is an enclosure

<Plate slug="elizaveta-porodina" id="10-numero-netherlands-06" label="Numéro Netherlands Cover, frame 06, n.d." caption="A face sits on a mirror axis while repeated arms fan outward through a foggy vertical field." />

The *Numéro Netherlands* frame exaggerates frontal address into something almost emblematic. The face sits on the measured vertical mirror axis; paired arm paths fan out from it and make the figure appear to multiply. Its grayscale palette (#1c1c1c through #dddddd) and edge density of 0.0060 are remarkably spare. The lack of small detail makes the arm repetitions and the dark dress read as a single graphic architecture.

<Plate slug="elizaveta-porodina" id="11-the-cut-chloe-sevigny-03" label="The Cut × Chloë Sevigny × Zoom, frame 03, n.d." caption="A turned face and the oversized yellow hat form two compressed portrait masses." />

The *Cut* portrait makes the hat a second portrait mass. The large yellow oval crowds the turned face, and the traced inner edge keeps the accessory from becoming background decoration. Warm beige #c7b297, blue-black #273a3a, pale blue #b0c0e1, and ochre #9a8a6a make a measured, muted field; mean luminance is 0.530 and edge density 0.0181. The face reads in tension with material rather than as an unmediated likeness.

<Plate slug="elizaveta-porodina" id="12-selected-works-60" label="Selected Works, frame 60, n.d." caption="An illuminated headband encloses a softened face around a deliberately near-central portrait axis." />

The final frame reduces the visual argument to face, light, and an enclosing band. The headband path makes a literal halo-like perimeter, while the vertical axis describes the near-frontal address without denying the asymmetric blur. Its values are forceful: mean 0.449, p05 0.016, p95 0.867, while the edge density stays at 0.0207. Black #141414, white #d1d1d1, and intermediate greys make softness feel constructed rather than merely unfocused.

## Exercises

<ExerciseCard n="01" title="Turn a portrait into a surface">
Photograph a face through ribbed glass, translucent fabric, water, or another
material that visibly redistributes it. Keep one feature legible, then let the
surface interrupt the rest. Decide whether the interruption makes the figure
multiply, flatten, or disappear.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="02" title="Make colour carry the pose">
Stage a figure across two or three large colour fields. Use one limb, garment,
or prop to cross their boundary, and make a version with no visible background
detail. Check whether the colour split changes how the pose feels before you add
any texture.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="03" title="Give the face a second frame">
Make a close portrait in which a hat, collar, hand, necklace, or headband forms
an enclosure around the face. Keep the accessory visibly structural: it should
redirect attention through the frame rather than simply decorating the sitter.
</ExerciseCard>
