# Jake Elwes — build research

## Scope and record

This chapter is grounded in the twelve public display files and the project-page
records documented in [sources.md](sources.md).  They establish a sequence of
works and views: *CUSP* (2019); *Zizi — Queering the Dataset* (2019–ongoing),
including the 2020 Gazelli Art House render, the 2021 Onassis Foundation
installation, and the 2022 Zabludowicz Collection view; and *The Zizi Show*
(2020–23), including the 2023 V&A presentation.  The source record identifies
*CUSP* as generated-bird and Essex-marsh material and identifies the *Zizi*
works through their portrait screens, dataset framing, and performance/display
formats.  It does not support claims about a proprietary model, training
parameters, or fabrication process beyond those public descriptions.

## Technique, display, and photographic method

The recurring technique visible in the supplied record is an exchange between
machine-produced or manipulated portrait imagery and the physical conditions of
display.  In the *Zizi* materials, small portrait units are printed, projected,
or shown on individual screens; the Athens and Zabludowicz views make spacing,
supports, trees, stairs, and audience-scale part of the work's composition.  In
*CUSP*, a bird image is shown on a bounded rectangular plane against an Essex
marsh; the supplied working view visibly includes the operator, light, and
screen.  *The Zizi Show* changes the unit from a portrait tile into a temporal
performance: a bank of displays, a projected trio, and a montage of Lilly
SnatchDragon make sequence and scale visible.

The supplied material is therefore best read as a mixed photographic and
screen-based practice.  The chapter treats the project images, renders, and
installation photographs as distinct kinds of records; it does not flatten an
installation view into a direct still from the moving work.

## Print, camera, and metadata limits

The source record does not give a print process, paper, edition, projector
specification, camera body, lens, exposure, or colour workflow for the artworks
themselves.  It credits Toby Elwes for the *CUSP* camera work; the Athens
installation image to Stelios Tzetzias; the Zabludowicz image to Tim Bowditch;
and the V&A overview to Thomas Adank and Gibson Thornley Architects.  The two
V&A stills retain the V&A credit specified in the source record.

The ingested web files retain limited embedded EXIF for a few *documentation*
files only: the Zabludowicz view carries Canon EOS 5D Mark III metadata (21 mm,
1/50 s, f/11, ISO 1000); the *CUSP* working view carries iPhone 7 Plus metadata
(6.6 mm, 1/50 s, f/2.8, ISO 20); and the two V&A stills carry Canon EOS R5 C
metadata (16 mm, 1/80 s, f/5.6 ISO 3200 and f/4.5 ISO 2500 respectively).  These
describe the delivered documentation files, not the generation, rendering, or
performance pipeline of Elwes's artworks.  All remaining ingested files expose
no usable capture EXIF.

## Analysis boundary

Tonal percentiles, palette swatches, edge density, horizons, symmetry, and
convergence in the chapter are deterministic measurements of the supplied
web-sized renditions.  They make no claim about the colour calibration of an
installation, a print, or a live performance.
