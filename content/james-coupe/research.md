# James Coupe — research notes

## Scope and sources

James Coupe (b. 1975) is a British-born, Seattle-based artist whose work joins video, installation, internet and emerging media. His public biography describes projects involving real-time public surveillance, interactive deepfakes, and work with Amazon Mechanical Turk workers; it frames these systems through classification, automation, labor, data, and affect. This chapter follows the documented 2012–20 sequence from surveillance panoramas through crowd/profile systems and platform labor to deepfake and commissioned-painting projects. The project-specific claims below come from the artist's work records unless otherwise noted; links and image-specific rights rationales are retained in [sources.md](sources.md).

## Method and media

These are not documentary photographs made by one camera practice or a body of photographic prints. They are public web renditions of video works, monitor installations, installation documentation, and gallery documentation. The records do not document a general camera model, lens, exposure practice, color pipeline, or print process for Coupe's artworks, so the chapter does not infer any.

*Panoptic Panorama #1* (2012) used five central cameras and computers that filtered movement to assemble five wall screens from recently detected "empty" intervals. *Panoptic Panorama #2* (2013) similarly used five cameras and screens; the artist describes face selection, age/gender profiling, Facebook-status subtitles, OpenFrameworks/OpenCV computer vision, and a Java/MySQL narrative system. The resulting visual question is temporal discontinuity inside an apparent panorama, rather than classical single-point perspective.

For *Sanctum* (2013, with Juan Pampin), the artist describes cameras that tracked approaching people, age/gender classification, an eighteen-monitor façade, and social-media narratives; the Henry Art Gallery confirms the 2013–15 façade installation and its profiling/social-media relation. *Swarm* (2013) used four stereo cameras to profile and locate visitors, selecting and compositing groups without occlusion. Their screen arrays turn classification into spatial distribution.

*General Intellect* (2015) drew on Mechanical Turk workers' hourly one-minute recordings, captions, and metadata; Coupe describes Python and OpenFrameworks systems that created tasks, paid workers, and displayed uploads. *Watchtower* (2017) used a four-storey wooden structure, sixteen monitors, and a Python/Raspberry Pi/MySQL system to process thousands of worker-made videos. Both works make a screen's support and a labor system part of the image rather than background context.

For *I am not a robot* (2019), the artist lists metal, electronics, video, computers, and the International Personality Item Pool; he describes a worker cage with a multiple-choice personality test. The *Warriors* installations (2020) use cameras, iPads, computers, customized Deepfakes, and AI classifiers. Coupe states that *GAN* classifies gender-related markers, *War* demographic/economic/occupational markers, and *Sixty Thousand Soldiers* groups faces into gangs through comparable markers; he also notes that swaps vary in speed and resolution and are not always seamless. ICP independently documents Face++, ImageNet, deepfakes, and opt-in visitor participation in the *Warriors* installation.

*The Chinese Rooms* (2020) comprises ten oil-on-canvas works made through Instapainting.com with named participating painters. Coupe relates the project to Searle's Chinese Room and positions the paintings between selfie and the artist-at-work pose. Its gallery photograph documents the series rather than a photographic print by Coupe.

## File metadata

The manifest retains EXIF only from three supplied documentation files. The *Panoptic Panorama #1* file reports an Apple iPhone 4, 3.85 mm focal length, 1/15 s, f/2.8, ISO 320; *I am not a robot* reports a Hasselblad H6D with HC 100 lens, 100 mm, 1/15 s, f/5.6, ISO 200; *The Chinese Rooms* gallery view reports an iPhone X, 4 mm, 1/30 s, f/1.8, ISO 32. These are metadata of the published documentation files, not production specifications for the artworks or evidence of a uniform photographic method.

## Visual approach

Coupe repeatedly makes the apparatus legible: a monitor seam, a façade grid, a cage, a screenshot border, a close face, or a gallery procession stops an image system from seeming neutral. This chapter treats those supports as composition. Analyzer statistics describe only the supplied web renditions; palette and tonal measurements are not claims about the original installations' calibrated display or exhibition lighting.
