# Thomas Struth — source shortlist

This set spans the compositional shifts that make Struth’s work especially useful for this chapter: centralized city description, posed social relations, museum spectatorship, dense nonhierarchical landscape, and the later pictures of complex technological systems. All eleven files are the artist’s own public website’s JPEG display renditions, downloaded directly without authentication or a technical workaround and checked as opening JPEGs. The page supplies a full-frame 1,000px rendition for nine works and 1,000px on the long edge for the two vertical works; each is above the project’s 640px quality floor.

**Rights basis:** **fair-use** for every selection. Thomas Struth’s official website publicly displays the works, while MoMA and the Met collection records expressly identify his works as © Thomas Struth; no open licence or public-domain reproduction was found. Each entry is a separate, limited assessment: this nonprofit book uses a credited, official, web-sized copy for transformative criticism, scholarship, and composition teaching; the complete frame is needed to analyze its organization; and the linked display rendition does not replace the artwork, museum record, or a licensed reproduction.

## Research context

The official archive, *Thomas Struth Photographs 1978–2010*, describes the *New Pictures from Paradise* series as a move toward dense detail and away from the selection process used for the museum photographs: https://www.thomasstruth32.com/bigsize/photographs/new_pictures_from_paradise/index.html. MoMA’s collection record for *Shinjuku (Skyscrapers), Tokyo* identifies the city photographs’ postwar urban focus and carries the copyright notice © Thomas Struth: https://www.moma.org/collection/works/49485. The Met’s *United Nations Plaza, Midtown East, New York* record identifies the centralized viewpoint and comparative approach behind the early street photographs: https://www.metmuseum.org/en/art/collection/search/284785.
