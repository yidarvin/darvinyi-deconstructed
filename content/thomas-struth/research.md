# Thomas Struth — research note

## What the source record establishes

The eleven supplied works span 1978–2023 and move between city streets, family
portraits, museum visitors, natural density, industrial manufacture, scientific
interiors, and institutional enclosures. The Metropolitan Museum describes the
early city pictures as a centralized, comparative approach to urban architecture,
informed by the Bechers' teaching; its record for *United Nations Plaza, Midtown
East, New York* is supporting context, not a claim about every work here. MoMA
identifies the related 1970s–80s city work as black-and-white, often unpopulated
views of postwar cities. For the supplied 1978 street image, MoMA's collection
title is *Grand Street at Crosby Street, New York/Soho*; `sources.md` retains the
shorter title used by the artist-site file.

The official archive says the first eight *New Pictures from Paradise* were made
in the Daintree rainforest in 1998 and describes the series as a more intuitive,
dense field in which viewing, rather than a ranked subject, becomes central. It
identifies various works in that series as chromogenic prints, but the supplied
sources do not document an exact process, size, or edition for *Paradise 02*. The
other selected works are identified by title and date on the artist's public site;
this record supports the chapter's observations of their visible structures, not a
universal account of their capture or printing methods.

## Printing and technical limits

MoMA records the 1978 street work as a gelatin-silver print, printed in 1989. That
is work-specific evidence and is not generalized to the family, museum, landscape,
or later technological pictures. The supplied source record does not reliably
establish camera body, lens, focal length, aperture, shutter speed, ISO,
film/sensor format, lighting plan, or work-specific printing settings for this
selection. The JPEG metadata is derivative-file metadata: notably, the 1978 and
1987 images carry Kodak scanner tags, so it cannot be treated as capture data.

## Rights note

Every image is the artist website's publicly displayed, web-sized rendition and
is used under the image-specific fair-use rationale in [sources.md](sources.md):
credited, linked copies are necessary for transformative criticism and composition
teaching, while the display files do not substitute for a print, licensed
reproduction, or the artist's own site.
