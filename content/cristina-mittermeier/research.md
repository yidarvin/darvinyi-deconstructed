# Cristina Mittermeier — research note

## Scope and evidence

This chapter studies twelve public, artist-hosted display photographs made from
2009 to 2023 in Ethiopia, Ghana, Galápagos, the Bahamas, Hawaiʻi, French
Polynesia, the Dominican Republic, Canada, Madagascar, and Antarctica. Titles,
locations, dates, image URLs, rights credit, and the image-specific fair-use
rationales are preserved in [sources.md](sources.md). The local files are
normalized web display JPEGs, not print scans or substitutes for licensed
reproductions.

The source note’s National Geographic and SeaLegacy references identify
Mittermeier as a marine-biologist-turned conservation photographer and a
SeaLegacy co-founder. They support the chapter’s broad context—photographs of
ocean life, coastal communities, and climate-linked environments—but not
unrecorded production details for individual frames.

## Working method visible in this selection

The selection moves between close portraiture, long-distance seascapes,
underwater encounters, and overhead land views. Across those modes, the visible
method is relational: a person, animal, boat, or moving body is given enough
surrounding space to be read with its water, ice, shoreline, or cultivated
terrain. This is a formal observation about frame, scale, and tonal separation,
not an inference about an individual subject’s private experience.

Several frames make that relation through a strong horizontal division: the
boat in *Alone*, the flamingo field in *All That Glitters*, the whale in *Leap*,
and the distant shore in *Ta’kaiya*. Others trade the horizon for a directional
contour: rain and boat rim in *Pantaneiros*, the stingray tail in *Alone
Together*, a fish current in *Red Curtain*, and the turtle-facing school in
*The Visitor*. *Rice Paddy I* is the exception that makes landscape itself a
network of seams rather than a backdrop.

## Medium and technical limits

The supplied sources establish dates, locations, titles, artist credit, and
the conservation-photography context. They do not document camera body, lens,
focal length, aperture, shutter speed, ISO, underwater housing, lighting,
post-production, or print process for these individual photographs. Ingestion
found no usable capture EXIF in the downloaded display JPEGs. The chapter
therefore does not infer settings or printing method from appearance; it limits
itself to visible framing, tonal range, palette, overlap, and directional
structure.

## Analysis limits

The composition analyzer detected strong anchors in several small or
high-contrast subjects and reliable horizontal transitions in a few seascapes.
It also proposed false perspective points and horizons in underwater texture,
fish, and terrain. Those artifact-prone proposals were not carried into the
overlays. The resulting diagrams retain only visually checked subject anchors,
real water/shore/ice transitions, and plainly visible contours such as arms,
tails, paddles, field seams, and groups of animals.
