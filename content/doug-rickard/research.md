# Doug Rickard — research note

## What the supplied sources establish

The seven selected pictures belong to the period of Doug Rickard's *A New
American Picture*. Their titles date the Google Street View source frames to
2008–09; the Helena-West Helena and #82.948842 Detroit works also carry 2010
work dates. MoMA's *New Photography 2011* material describes Rickard's method
as composing from Google Street View on a computer screen, then rephotographing
and cropping the results panoramically. The source record documents the series'
concerns with poverty, racial equity, online imagery, and privacy. These are
screen-mediated rephotographs, not direct street photographs. [Source notes](sources.md)

## Printing and technical limits

MoMA's collection record identifies *#82.948842, Detroit, MI. 2009* as an
inkjet print. A Davidson College collection note identifies
*#34.546147, Helena-West Helena, AR. 2008, 2010* as an archival pigment print.
Those are work-specific descriptions and should not be generalized to all seven
plates. The supplied record does not establish a camera body, lens, focal
length, aperture, shutter speed, ISO, screen/display type, or the particular
rephotographic setup for any plate; the ingested web JPEGs contain no camera
EXIF. The analysis therefore stays with the documented workflow, visible frame
structure, and measured tone/palette rather than invented capture settings.

The available MoMA exhibition renditions are 480–483 pixels on the long edge.
That public-display scale makes their deliberate low-detail, screen-mediated
appearance discussable, but it is not evidence of the resolution of an original
print.
