Doug Rickard's *A New American Picture* asks what a photographic frame does after
the camera has already been somewhere else. The source record describes a
deliberate chain: Google Street View is composed on a computer screen, then
rephotographed and panoramically cropped. The resulting blur and compressed
detail make scale, distance, and the viewer's social position unusually explicit.
These are screen-mediated rephotographs, not direct street photographs.

## distance as a structure

<Plate slug="doug-rickard" id="01-29942566-new-orleans-2008-2009" label="#29.942566, New Orleans, LA. 2008, 2009" caption="A sloping road edge and a small group of walkers hold the broad street and heavy sky in one precarious scale." />

In New Orleans, the street-to-sky break sits at y0.472, while the road edge rises

<Plate slug="doug-rickard" id="02-32700542-dallas-2009" label="#32.700542, Dallas, TX. 2009" caption="A near utility pole, its long shadow, and a white dog turn an ordinary verge into a sharply pointed foreground encounter." />

Dallas refuses the conventional deep vista. A utility pole enters at left; its

#94947d and muted green #b3b49e make the small white dog an interruption rather

## displaced anchors

<Plate slug="doug-rickard" id="03-34546147-helena-west-helena-2008-2010" label="#34.546147, Helena-West Helena, AR. 2008, 2010" caption="A setting sun at the left edge and a teal house at the right make a low winter field into a stretched, two-point balance." />

Helena-West Helena refuses the center: the setting sun is held at the far left and
the teal house at the right, with bare trees supplying a thin vertical rhythm. The
low ground band at y0.625 gives the pairing a base. Mean luminance is 0.715, p95
reaches 0.996, and edge density is only 0.027. Pale #f5f9f3 and #cfe0e1 make the
sky nearly blank; green-gray #566364 makes the house a durable color event.

<Plate slug="doug-rickard" id="04-83016417-detroit-2009" label="#83.016417, Detroit, MI. 2009" caption="Storefronts, a receding curb, and a cropped pale-blue car make the street read as stacked, interrupted planes." />

In Detroit, the storefront band at y0.353 separates signage and façades from
sidewalk and road. The curb moves from lower left toward the middle, then the car
arrives too close to become a complete object. The crop turns a passing vehicle
into a hard foreground block. The tonal range is 0.129–0.890 and edge density
0.090; blue-gray #708291 and red-brown #8a4f3c keep pavement, metal, and painted
frontage in friction.

<Plate slug="doug-rickard" id="05-120074209-fresno-2009" label="#120.074209, Fresno, CA. 2009" caption="A parked pickup and a small motorcycle make two quiet scale anchors above the long curb and below a low domestic roofline." />

Fresno makes stillness active. The curb crosses toward the right, while a pickup
at left and motorcycle near the middle provide unequal anchors. The roofline at
y0.115 keeps the picture shallow: there is no dramatic escape into distance, only
a broad observed frontage. Its p05 is 0.078, p95 0.800, and edge density 0.126.
Violet-gray #7f7482, dark #1a121e, and pale #dbd0ef separate its low-resolution
values.

## a road made public

<Plate slug="doug-rickard" id="06-82948842-detroit-2009-2010" label="#82.948842, Detroit, MI. 2009, 2010" caption="Road edges and lane markings converge behind three pedestrians, making their cross-frame movement the image's central interruption." />

The second Detroit frame supplies the clearest axial proposition. Road edges pull
toward a central street axis, but three pedestrians cross it rather than follow it.
Their grouped anchor sits at y0.703, so lane markings are read through bodies
before poles and storefronts. Edge density is only 0.048 despite a 0.224–0.867
tonal range. Beige #c8c1b6 and #dbd7c9 widen the road; #482e3b and #77676a make
the pedestrians and commercial interruptions hold their ground.

<Plate slug="doug-rickard" id="08-40805716-new-york-city-2009" label="#40.805716, New York City, NY. 2009" caption="An entering passerby, a waiting figure, and a storefront band make the crop feel like a newly claimed encounter rather than a transparent record." />

New York City makes cropping visible as authorship. An entering passerby is
clipped at lower left while a waiting figure is isolated against the storefront.
The sidewalk band at y0.607 connects them without making them share a scene; the
curb arc reinforces their separation beneath a firm storefront block. Mean
luminance is 0.495, p05–p95 is 0.098–0.827, and edge density 0.070. Brown-black
#2c2321, faded yellow #d6ccad, and red-brown #594d43 keep the figures separate
in a compressed urban field.

## Exercises

<ExerciseCard n="01" title="Make a small figure set the scale">
Photograph a broad street or open lot from far enough away that one person is
small. Keep a curb, road edge, or wall long enough to describe distance, then
compare a figure placed against that line with one isolated against blank ground.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="02" title="Use a crop as a decision">
Photograph a storefront or roadside scene with two people at different distances.
Make one crop that leaves a nearby body incomplete at an edge and another that
includes it fully. Notice which makes the frame an authored boundary.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="03" title="Build a crossing against recession">
Find road markings or curbs that lead away. Wait for a person to cross that
movement rather than walk along it. The successful frame makes the body interrupt
an existing directional system instead of merely decorate the street.
</ExerciseCard>
