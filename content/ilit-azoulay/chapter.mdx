Ilit Azoulay’s *No Thing Dies* makes the archive legible by refusing its normal
order. The 2017 project is documented as twenty photomontages made after a
2014–17 investigation of the Israel Museum’s archives and storerooms. Here,
photographed objects, rooms, labels, display furniture, and voids do not resolve
into a continuous place; they are held together by deliberate black-edged
compartments. The overlays trace those visible compartments rather than treating
the analyzer’s strongest tonal seam as a literal horizon. [Source notes](../../content/ilit-azoulay/sources.md)

## the vitrine as a grammar

<Plate slug="ilit-azoulay" id="01-under-the-surface" label="Vitrine No. 1, 2017" caption="Nested black edges turn shelves, an archive corridor, stairs, and figures into a vertical cabinet." />

In *Under the surface only the mereness of things could be seen*, the outer vitrine,
lower compartment, and shelf break make the picture read by enclosure before it
reads by object. The analyzer records a wide tonal span (p05 0.090, p95 1.000) and
a brown-to-white palette (#2a271a, #665b42, #cab69a, #f8f8f8); the bright open
fields therefore make the black framing look like a device for joining incompatible
scales rather than a neutral border.

<Plate slug="ilit-azoulay" id="02-dreamtime" label="Vitrine No. 2, 2017" caption="A glass cabinet and a descending stair complicate the central statue’s apparent dominance." />

*Dreamtime* sets a statue, a small glass cabinet, a piled field of objects, and the
right-hand stairs into one interrupted route. The tree-and-figure anchor catches a
high-saliency interruption at the upper right, while the stair descent counteracts
the central figure. Its near-black #0f0e08, warm #574d31, and white #fafafa palette
and p05/p95 of 0.004/1.000 make the montage’s cuts especially emphatic.

<Plate slug="ilit-azoulay" id="03-new-head" label="Vitrine No. 3, 2017" caption="A barred portal and an enclosed side room stack incompatible artifact scales without smoothing the joins." />

The gridded portal in *New head* is a literal screen across the montage: stained
glass, figures, a column, and the yellow curtained side room remain separate even
when their edges overlap. The analyzer’s high edge density (0.096, the largest in
this group) accords with that insistence on seams; #f1f0ef, #28231c, #5a513d, and
#8d825e keep the collage bright but structurally busy.

<Plate slug="ilit-azoulay" id="04-true-story" label="Vitrine No. 4, 2017" caption="A yellow rail links a microscope window and blue tile panel while a gilded animal interrupts the route." />

In *(Take, for instance, this) true story*, the yellow rail is the unusually simple
line that lets three otherwise incompatible views share a level. The instrument
window and blue tile panel behave as nested display surfaces; the gilded-animal
anchor disrupts their alignment. The measured palette moves from #2c2a25 through
#656254 and #9e967d to #f6f6f6, with an edge density of 0.063, so color accents
matter more than a conventional illusion of depth.

<Plate slug="ilit-azoulay" id="05-neither-dream-nor-riddle" label="Vitrine No. 5, 2017" caption="Blind panel, shadowed tray, and lower doorway make a small green figure travel across separate rooms." />

*Neither dream nor riddle* uses three compartmental clauses rather than a unified
room: the blind panel with its vertical slats, the shadowed tray, and a lower
doorway. The green figure is a small but sharp anchor at their boundary. The
display image is bright at its upper end (p95 1.000), yet #201d17 and #605748
preserve a dark counterweight; its 0.039 edge density lets those large blocks carry
the composition.

## archive fields and blank space

<Plate slug="ilit-azoulay" id="06-everything-stood-still" label="Vitrine No. 6, 2017" caption="A central light box, lower display, and shelf seam suspend fragments around a conspicuous white void." />

*It is impossible to explain how suddenly everything stood still* makes a blank
light box nearly as important as the packaged object, blue glass, and lower display.
The shelf seam keeps those fragments in a vertical sequence without inventing a
single interior. Its mean luminance is 0.671, the highest of these ten ingested
plates, and #f5f5f4, #b0b5a7, and #2a281f show how a pale field preserves a few dark
weights.

<Plate slug="ilit-azoulay" id="07-return-of-things" label="Vitrine No. 9, 2017" caption="A crisp vitrine divide places a catalog-like object wall over a monumental gold-backed animal field." />

In *The return of things that are no more*, the horizontal vitrine divide does not
describe a natural horizon: it is a constructed seam between two contradictory
scales. Above it, the object wall has the spacing of a catalog; below it, an animal
and textile turn gold ground into a monumental field. The measured palette
(#2c2118, #6f5c49, #d9ccb2, #fafafa) and p05/p95 of 0.078/0.980 make that cut read
as a change of register, not atmosphere.

<Plate slug="ilit-azoulay" id="08-place-they-occupied" label="Vitrine No. 12+13, 2017" caption="Three room-boxes make doors, puppets, storage furniture, and white gaps operate as a broken panorama." />

The horizontal *For unknown reasons, the place they occupied is empty* is a useful
exception to the chapter’s vertical rhythm. Its left room, center room, and upper
cabinet never share a believable floor; instead, their black frames make the wide
white gaps active. The analyzer finds an edge density of 0.074 and a tonal span from
0.063 to 1.000, while #2a190f, #5b4331, #7c6c59, and #fcfcfb turn absence into a
structural material.

<Plate slug="ilit-azoulay" id="09-contemplating" label="Vitrine No. 15, 2017" caption="Document field, portrait window, and specimen tray build a provisional filing system around pottery fragments." />

*Contemplating, what did he see?* stages documents, a portrait, a pottery tray, and
a lower artifact panel as separate evidentiary surfaces. The overlay does not force
their disparate frames into symmetry; it identifies the temporary filing system
they form. This is the lowest-edge-density plate (0.030), and its high mean
luminance (0.704) lets warm #ca975e and dark #373025 puncture a mostly pale field.

<Plate slug="ilit-azoulay" id="10-glass-storage" label="Vitrine No. 20, 2017" caption="Clock vignette, storage bay, blue mural, and dark lower room build an intentionally collision-prone archive." />

*All sorts of sights went wrong like a great catastrophe in a glass storage* makes
overlap the governing logic. A clock vignette interrupts the blue mural; a storage
bay continues beneath it; the lower dark room then resets the scale again. Its
#0f619e blue is unique within the ten analyzer palettes, meeting #1f1b14,
#6b5d47, and #f0efee across a p05/p95 range of 0.047/1.000. The resulting color
collision reinforces the work’s refusal to let a museum storehouse become a tidy
system.

## Exercises

<ExerciseCard n="01" title="Make a cabinet before a scene">
Photograph or collect three unrelated fragments, then use two rectangular edges and
one open margin to establish their reading order. Do not hide the transitions;
decide which compartment the eye must enter first.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="02" title="Turn a seam into a claim">
Make one horizontal or vertical cut between two images at different scales. Treat it
as a display shelf, rail, or page break rather than a natural horizon, and test how
the meaning changes if you remove it.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="03" title="Let the blank field carry weight">
Build a collage with one large bright or empty region and only two dark or saturated
interruptions. Adjust their spacing until the emptiness reads as an active archival
compartment rather than unused background.
</ExerciseCard>
