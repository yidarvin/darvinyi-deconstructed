# Richard Mosse — source shortlist

Richard Mosse repurposes technologies of surveillance and measurement—infrared film, thermal imaging, and multispectral mapping—to make environmental and humanitarian crises visually strange rather than comfortably consumable. This ten-image set concentrates on the Amazon project *Broken Spectre* and its adjacent photographic works. The National Gallery of Victoria identifies *Broken Spectre* as a 2018–22, four-channel work about Amazon deforestation; Mosse’s public artist site hosts the exact web-display files selected here. The site’s six film stills are uncaptioned, so their file-based identifiers are retained rather than inventing individual work titles.

**Rights basis:** **fair-use** for every selection. No open licence or public-domain dedication is stated. The known creator/rightsholder is Richard Mosse; NGV credits the work © Richard Mosse and identifies Jack Shainman Gallery as the artist’s representative/courtesy credit. Each file below is a publicly served, unauthenticated artist-site display image used by this nonprofit book for transformative criticism, scholarship, and composition teaching. The individual assessments state why the whole frame is necessary and why the web-sized file does not substitute for an original, museum record, book, or licensed reproduction.

## Research context

The [National Gallery of Victoria’s *Broken Spectre* exhibition record](https://www.ngv.vic.gov.au/exhibition/richard-mosse-broken-spectre/) describes Mosse’s 2018–22 Amazon project as a four-channel, 74-minute work that combines multispectral imaging, infrared imagery, and different scales of observation. The [NGV’s earlier *The Enclave* record](https://www.ngv.vic.gov.au/exhibition/richard-mosse/) documents his prior use of Kodak Aerochrome colour infrared film in the Democratic Republic of Congo, while the [Barbican’s *Incoming* record](https://www.barbican.org.uk/whats-on/2017/event/richard-mosse-incoming) explains his use of long-range thermal imaging in the refugee crisis. Together these sources establish that the selected *Broken Spectre* images continue a sustained practice of repurposing technical vision for critical documentary composition.
