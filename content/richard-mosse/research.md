# Richard Mosse — research note

## What the supplied sources establish

The documented source record places the selected files in Richard Mosse's *Broken Spectre* project (2018–22), an Amazon work described by the National Gallery of Victoria as a four-channel, 74-minute installation that combines multispectral and infrared imaging at different scales of observation. The same source trail records a longer practice of repurposing technical vision: Kodak Aerochrome colour-infrared film in *The Enclave* and long-range thermal imaging in *Incoming*. The individual titles and dates that can be supported are preserved in [sources.md](sources.md).

The documentation identifies *Domesticated Palms, Amazonas* (2020) and *Burnt Pantanal I* (2020); the latter is described in the source record as an archival pigment photograph. The six *Broken Spectre* film stills and two additional artist-site display files are not individually captioned by the official page, so this chapter retains the source-file identifiers instead of inventing titles. The supplied file assigned to *Burnt Pantanal I* visibly reads as an aerial image; rather than assuming a title-specific subject or medium from the file name alone, the chapter treats it as the documented display rendition.

## Technique, material, and settings

The sources establish project-level use of multispectral and infrared imaging, not a single capture recipe for every selected frame. They do not establish a camera body, lens, focal length, aperture, shutter speed, ISO, film stock, sensor settings, flight platform, post-production workflow, paper, edition, or print process for the individual supplied images except the stated archival pigment description for *Burnt Pantanal I*. The ingested JPEGs contain no usable camera EXIF. Accordingly, the chapter discusses visible framing and the analyzer's geometry, tone, and palette; it does not convert a false-colour appearance into undocumented technical settings.

## Source and access limits

The ten working files are public artist-site display renditions, used under the per-image fair-use assessments in [sources.md](sources.md) for criticism, scholarship, and composition teaching. They are web-sized display files, not substitutes for Mosse's installations, prints, publications, or licensed reproductions.
