# Miles Aldridge — research notes

## Scope and evidence

This chapter uses eleven 2003–2017 official-archive display files documented individually in [sources.md](sources.md). The source record identifies the works, dates, artist credit, public display URLs, and their limited fair-use basis for criticism and composition teaching. The analysis below is grounded in the ingested pixels, especially the staged figure, prop, aperture, and colour relationships visible in each supplied rendition.

## Technique and visual language

Across this selection, photographic space behaves as a deliberately arranged surface. The images use costume, cosmetics, prepared food, glass, hair, flowers, foliage, vehicle frames, and architectural fixtures as graphic materials. Brightly separated colours and unusually clean tonal fields make an object or body instantly legible, then pose, prop, and enclosure make that clarity psychologically tense. The recurring effect is not reportage but a constructed tableau whose visual order is part of the subject.

The wide pictures make segregation of space especially forceful: the walking pair is held to one side of an empty avenue, five faces are partitioned by car windows, and a figure is broken into layers by neon and glass. The tall pictures instead build pressure by stacking the face, body, object, and frame. The two foliage portraits use controlled surrounding darkness and small bright details to make the face appear placed rather than merely found.

## Printing and camera settings

The supplied official-archive records identify titles, dates, and print medium but do not transcribe a common print process in this source set. They do not establish camera body, lens, film stock, exposure, aperture, ISO, lighting plan, or post-production workflow for these eleven works. The ingested manifest also contains no embedded camera EXIF. Accordingly, this chapter makes no technical-setting claim beyond the documented web display renditions and the visible photographic construction.

## Evidence limits

The source notes remain the authority for title, date, credit, source route, and fair-use rationale. Tonal values, palettes, edges, and overlay geometry quoted in the chapter are deterministic measurements of the project’s normalized files, not claims about an original print or a universal method across Aldridge’s practice.
