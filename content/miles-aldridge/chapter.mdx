Miles Aldridge makes objects, bodies, and interiors feel prearranged before a narrative begins. Here a cigarette enters an egg yolk, a woman sits beside a dog, a meal becomes an accusation, or a face is fractured by glass. The overlays name only the literal scaffolds that keep these scenes readable: a cigarette diagonal, a window aperture, a plate, a road seam, a braid. Their value is not hidden geometry for its own sake. It is that the geometry makes artifice visible. [Source notes](../../content/miles-aldridge/sources.md)

## bright surfaces, hard interruptions

<Plate slug="miles-aldridge" id="01-red-marks-1" label="Red Marks #1, 2003" caption="A pink-tipped cigarette enters the pale, glossy field of fried eggs, making one small diagonal carry the picture’s disturbance." />

*Red Marks #1* is nearly all surface. Its mean luminance is 0.825 and its edge density only 0.003, so the pale white and yolk colours do not compete as a textured scene. The phi grid and overlay instead make the cigarette’s ash point and rising diagonal conspicuous. Orange-yellow #fcba23 and pink #d5667f make an implausibly polished domestic still life; the ash is the small dark fact that prevents it becoming merely decorative.

<Plate slug="miles-aldridge" id="02-like-a-painting-1" label="Like A Painting #1, 2005" caption="A profile, pearl arc, and butterfly break the enclosing ivy field into a precisely arranged portrait surface." />

The title of *Like A Painting #1* is useful because the image does not ask us to forget its construction. Pearl strings contour the hair and butterfly forms puncture the profile’s pale field, while the rest of the frame stays densely dark. The analyzer’s 0.199 edge density is the selection’s highest, consistent with the repeated ivy and bead detail. Yet the measured palette keeps returning to near-black #040a0b and deep green #184540, so those many edges become an enclosure rather than noise.

## rooms, food, and the staged social body

<Plate slug="miles-aldridge" id="03-dog-lady-1" label="Dog Lady #1, 2009" caption="Curtains and bed make a chamber for the unequal sitter-and-dog pair." />

The large curtained bed in *Dog Lady #1* is an internal proscenium. It places the frontal sitter and the dog in the same chamber without making them a natural pair: their size, pose, and attention do not resolve into intimacy. Beige #b7b3a0 and #dededb support the warm, upholstered field, while the 0.122–0.929 tonal range leaves enough contrast for the black lampshade, dog’s face, and bottles to keep troubling the room’s comfort.

<Plate slug="miles-aldridge" id="04-a-precious-glam-2" label="A Precious Glam #2, 2011" caption="Parallel rails, a seated figure, and the red meal build a deliberately frontal hierarchy." />

In *A Precious Glam #2*, the rails make a literal horizontal band through the room, but the food forces the eye back down to the table. The plate ellipse is not a decorative contour: its red mass is the lower counterweight to the face and bouquet. The analyzer records a wide p05-to-p95 span of 0.043–0.937, while #261e12, #814822, and #d0b9ac articulate dark booth, warm room, and pale dress as separate theatrical zones.

<Plate slug="miles-aldridge" id="05-a-family-portrait-1" label="A Family Portrait #1, 2011" caption="A receding avenue leaves the balloon-bearing pair unusually isolated at the right." />

*A Family Portrait #1* makes a family group out of separation rather than togetherness. The tree-row seam at y=0.409 keeps the avenue shallow and level, while the small walking pair at the right has to answer a vast left-hand expanse of shadowed road. Edge density rises to 0.174 through repeated trunks and leaf masses, but dark navy #030721, blue #082e5c, and the light pavement keep the empty corridor legible. The pink balloon is a deliberately absurd accent, not a sentimental release.

<Plate slug="miles-aldridge" id="06-i-only-want-you-to-love-me-1" label="I Only Want You To Love Me #1, 2011" caption="A yellow figure sits above the checkerboard’s sharp perspective and a red debris field." />

The checkerboard in *I Only Want You To Love Me #1* provides an exact, impersonal stage for the yellow-clad body and broken table setting. The overlay’s tile diagonal and convergence make that floor’s recession explicit, while the lower debris band refuses to behave as background. Lime #cccf07, red #8f170e, orange #ce5014, and magenta #c01d68 are held in a relatively low 0.043 edge-density field; colour separation, not photographic busyness, makes the scene feel harsh.

## apertures, glass, and staged fracture

<Plate slug="miles-aldridge" id="07-five-girls-in-a-car-1" label="Five Girls in a Car #1, 2013" caption="Two window apertures pack five faces into a pressured, segmented group portrait." />

The car in *Five Girls in a Car #1* is not merely a setting. Its two windows supply an unusually strict frame-within-frame structure, which forces five differently styled faces into neighboring compartments. The lower door seam lets the green body act as a stabilizing base, while the dark teal #05353b and brighter #148d93 glass make skin, lipstick, and the red drink read as interruptions within one sealed vehicle.

<Plate slug="miles-aldridge" id="08-night-car-1" label="Night Car #1, 2015" caption="Neon tubes and reflected glass divide the seated figure into competing layers." />

In *Night Car #1*, the window is a layer rather than a transparent opening. The blue tube follows a real diagonal across the red field, and an ellipse around the seated figure helps separate the woman from the letters, reflections, and horizontal light bands passing over her. p95 reaches 0.969 against a p05 of 0.125, matching the violent contrast of white neon, #e92312 red, dark #6b0e0a, and cyan #0c8586. The image makes legibility feel intermittent on purpose.

<Plate slug="miles-aldridge" id="09-the-promise" label="The Promise, 2016" caption="A cracked pane turns an immaculate frontal portrait into a radiating interruption." />

*The Promise* is the selection’s cleanest assault on a finished face. The overlay follows the crack impact at x=0.20, y=0.358 and two of its rays rather than treating the break as an incidental texture. The portrait otherwise has an edge density of only 0.013 and a mean luminance of 0.655, so the radial fracture has very little visual competition. Pale #e5e1c3 and blonde #cfbd85 make the crack’s dark, sharp lines feel more invasive.

<Plate slug="miles-aldridge" id="10-ophelia" label="Ophelia, 2016" caption="The floating body is a pale lateral form held in dark water and foreground grasses." />

The allusion in *Ophelia* is reorganized as a graphic relation. An elongated ellipse holds the pale figure, while the traced body length runs laterally toward the face and the dark water break divides the upper field. Here the measurement reports edge density 0.153, much higher than the apparently calm subject suggests, because reeds and water contours keep testing the body’s smoothness. Deep #061b0e and green-blue #0d6d5d make the white dress appear less like a natural highlight than a placed theatrical material.

<Plate slug="miles-aldridge" id="11-in-the-garden" label="In The Garden, 2017" caption="A pearl-banded portrait emerges from a dark foliage field through a plaited diagonal." />

*In The Garden* returns to frontal portraiture but refuses a neutral backdrop. The overlay identifies the face, blue butterfly, and braid, three small structures that make the body feel composed into the foliage. Its mean luminance is only 0.150 and the p05 is 0.000, yet the analyzer finds high edge density, 0.185, in the leaves and floral points. The portrait is therefore less isolated by blank space than carved out of #020407 and #082a33 darkness by skin, pearl, blue satin, and the butterfly.

## Exercises

<ExerciseCard n="01" title="Make the interruption carry the scene">
Photograph a nearly uniform field, such as food, fabric, water, or a painted wall. Introduce one small object with a strong diagonal or colour contrast. Remove every other competing detail, then mark the object and test whether it still changes the field at thumbnail size.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="02" title="Build an aperture before arranging a group">
Use a car window, doorway, mirror, booth, or shelf opening to divide a group portrait. Place at least three faces across the divisions. Make one version in which every face receives equal room and one in which the frames create deliberate crowding.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="03" title="Turn a prop into a counterweight">
Stage a seated portrait with one conspicuous object below the face, such as a plate, bag, lamp, or bouquet. Change its colour, size, and distance from the figure until it directs the eye back toward the face rather than stealing the image outright.
</ExerciseCard>
