# Mishka Henner — research notes

## Documented record

The supplied National Gallery of Art records identify these ten selected works as
part of Mishka Henner's *Eighteen Pumpjacks* (2012), and identify each object as
an inkjet print. The source shortlist documents the portfolio as a typology of
oil-extraction sites assembled from satellite imagery. Henner's biographical
overview, linked in [sources.md](sources.md), places it within a wider practice
using satellite, surveillance, and public-data imagery to examine systems of
vision and power.

The vertical satellite frames make rows, scrub, tracks, cleared pads, and small
pumpjacks comparable as distinct arrangements of texture and access. The titles
retain a geographical index while the repeated format creates a typology.

## Technique and limits

The source records document the 2012 date, series, and inkjet-print medium. They
do not establish a camera body, lens, focal length, aperture, shutter speed,
sensitivity, film or sensor format, satellite platform, or edition-specific print
dimensions. No such setting is inferred here. The available public NGA display
files are 716–722 × 900px and the ingested manifest contains no EXIF metadata.

## Measured evidence

The analyses support systematic variation rather than one fixed formula. The
dark, finely textured agricultural plates 03, 07, 08, and 09 have high edge
density (0.220–0.251), while the near-uniform orange fields in 04 and 05 are
exceptionally sparse (0.002 and 0.010). Mean luminance ranges from 0.336 in 09
to 0.595 in 04. Muted earth-and-vegetation palettes—for example 01's #5b6251,
#7f7662, #bb8f5f and 09's #454e4f and #b49677—make routes and cleared pads read
as changes in material surface before they resolve into infrastructure.

The overlays name observed routes, field edges, cleared pads, and pumpjacks. They
do not mistake detector-proposed horizons or vanishing points for literal aerial
perspective when a cultivated boundary or access path is the more truthful claim.
