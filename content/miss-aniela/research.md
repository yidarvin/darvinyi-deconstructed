# Miss Aniela (Natalie Dybisz) — research note

## Scope and evidence

This chapter considers ten public commercial-display JPEGs made between 2011 and 2016, documented individually in [sources.md](sources.md). The source record identifies Natalie Dybisz as Miss Aniela and records the permitted replacement routes used for each file. It does not rely on the retired direct-download route from the artist's Surreal Fashion gallery.

## Visual method and era

The available record supports a discussion of staged fashion pictures in ornate domestic or institutional rooms, and of their visible collisions among dress, animals, architectural openings, decorative pattern, and art-historical motifs. The dates cluster in the early-to-mid 2010s, but this source set does not establish a universal workflow, the order in which elements were made, or the degree and method of any digital alteration. This chapter therefore treats each supplied image as a finished composition and confines technical claims to what is documented.

## Printing and camera settings

The source record gives no edition details, print process, paper, printer, camera body, lens, focal length, aperture, shutter speed, ISO, lighting plan, or post-production sequence for this ten-work selection. The ingested web-display JPEGs contain no capture EXIF. No camera settings or print method are inferred from their appearance. The Nikon D810 campaign attribution for *White Witch Awakening* identifies a commission context, not a documented capture specification for this file or for the group.

## Evidence limits

Titles, dates, locations where stated, series context, credits, and the public web source are preserved in [sources.md](sources.md). Tonal statistics and palette values in the chapter are measurements of the ingested display JPEGs, not claims about original prints, monitors, or capture files.
