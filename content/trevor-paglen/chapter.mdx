Trevor Paglen photographs systems designed to remain unseen. These pictures refuse the clean revelation of surveillance: a secret satellite becomes a whirl of tracked light, a drone nearly disappears into sky, and an institution is read from roads, pools of light, or the partial evidence of a map. The sources identify the 2010–17 works as prints and image-based works made across surveillance, orbital, cable, and machine-vision projects; the chapter stays with what their frames visibly establish. [Source notes](../../content/trevor-paglen/sources.md)

## distance as evidence

<Plate slug="trevor-paglen" id="01-stss-carson-city" label="STSS-1 and Two Unidentified Spacecraft Over Carson City, 2010" caption="Concentric star trails turn the sky into a measured field whose center of rotation makes surveillance tracking visible as duration." />

The ellipse in this plate follows the visible rotational organization, not an invented spacecraft position. Mean luminance 0.199, edge density 0.130, and the red range from #25030a through #9c150b to #600909 make the dense, luminous tracks read as accumulated time rather than isolated points.

<Plate slug="trevor-paglen" id="04-reaper-drone" label="Untitled (Reaper Drone), 2010" caption="A low dusk horizon leaves almost the entire frame to pale atmospheric variation; the titled drone arrives as a delayed, nearly invisible detail." />

The horizon at y0.722 is the picture's only firm divider. Its exceptional edge density of 0.000 and bright mean luminance of 0.829 leave #d8e4f7, #d5d8ad, and #daca81 to do the work of negative space. The overlay deliberately does not invent a subject marker for the tiny drone: the title changes how patiently the void is read.

<Plate slug="trevor-paglen" id="05-dead-satellite-cosmos-469" label="Dead Satellite with Nuclear Reactor, Eastern Arizona (Cosmos 469), 2011" caption="A desert horizon and a compact monument formation make the broad field feel legible before the title asks it to carry orbital narrative." />

At y0.733, the desert horizon holds an expansive sky over the dark central formation. The image is materially spare, with edge density 0.008, so its #8e8e8e, #242424, and #b0b0b0 palette lets the eye test the relation between title, rock, and distant signal without reducing the landscape to illustration.

<Plate slug="trevor-paglen" id="08-onyx-v-glacier-point" label="ONYX V/HYBRID and Other Spacecraft from Glacier Point, 2015" caption="Slanting satellite trails cross a pale night sky above a low valley horizon and an asymmetrical granite anchor." />

Here the y0.713 valley horizon sets a quiet base for star trails that refuse the stillness of the landscape. The analyzer finds a low edge density of 0.002 but a broad tonal span from 0.078 to 0.808; #d1bebc above #17131a and #322b32 turns the sky into an active field while the granite mass keeps the frame from floating away.

## diagrams of infrastructure

<Plate slug="trevor-paglen" id="02-nsa-tapped-mastic-beach" label="NSA-Tapped Fiber Optic Cable Landing Site, Mastic Beach, New York, United States, 2015" caption="A shoreline divide carries the eye from quiet beach to an informational map, making the paired frame operate as a lateral evidence diagram." />

The y0.620 shoreline is a real visual hinge across both panels. A very high mean luminance of 0.854 and low edge density of 0.032 keep #fbfbf9, #b7a085, and #e8e6dd restrained on the beach, so the chart's dense detail reads as a different but connected kind of observation.

<Plate slug="trevor-paglen" id="07-nsa-tapped-keawaula" label="NSA-Tapped Fiber Optic Cable Landing Site, Keawaula, Hawaii, United States, 2016" caption="Coast, sea, and chart meet at a shallow central band, turning the panorama into a route between visible land and represented network." />

The coast-to-sea divide at y0.504 is measured across a picture that moves from green terrain to white chart ground. Its mean luminance 0.786, edge density 0.058, and palette of #f8f9f9, #81775c, and #d9d9d6 make the geographic and informational halves feel deliberately unequal: place is textured, while infrastructure is indexed.

<Plate slug="trevor-paglen" id="03-national-security-agencies" label="National Security Agency, Ft. Meade, Maryland; National Reconnaissance Office, Chantilly, Virginia; National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency, Springfield, Virginia, 2014" caption="Three distant nocturnal views stack as a serial field, aligned by a central axis and a recurring low night-ground seam." />

The vertical triptych center and y0.655 seam do not claim symmetry within each installation; they name the serial device that makes different sites comparable. Mean luminance 0.291, edge density 0.139, and #0b121a, #454431, and #f9faf8 make the repeating image logic oscillate between darkness, lit road, and administrative glow.

<Plate slug="trevor-paglen" id="09-overhead-nsa" label="Overhead of NSA, 2013" caption="A lit access line and converging compound geometry turn a distant overhead view into an organized pattern of movement and perimeter." />

The overlay's access line is tied to a visible lit route and terminates at the analyzer's measured convergence near x0.546, y0.658. The amber palette of #cc903c, #f1c961, and #905c24, along with mean luminance 0.618 and edge density 0.073, makes roads and parking surfaces look less like neutral illumination than a legible operational diagram.

## when the image becomes an instrument

<Plate slug="trevor-paglen" id="06-fanon-eigenface" label="&quot;Fanon&quot; (Even the Dead Are Not Safe) Eigenface, 2017" caption="A frontal facial axis and interrupted eye-line make a synthetic portrait borrow the address of a face while unsettling its coherence." />

The vertical axis at x0.5 and the eye-line identify a compositional promise of portraiture, even as the face blurs and doubles. The analysis records mean luminance 0.449, no detected hard edges, and #9b9ca1, #424453, #1b1b26: an image that remains facial by distribution of tones rather than by sharp description.

<Plate slug="trevor-paglen" id="10-last-pictures-echostar-xvi" label="The Last Pictures/EchoStar XVI Launch and Preliminary Orbit, 2012" caption="A bright central divide, horizontal seam, and rightward convergence connect a launch trace to a star-filled orbital field." />

The diptych divide at x0.5 is physical, while the y0.495 seam and measured convergence at x0.796, y0.511 direct attention across it. This is the set's darkest plate (mean 0.033) and one of its densest (edge density 0.168); #040304, #070709, and #111215 make a minute launch light and repeated star traces carry the whole technological sequence.

<Plate slug="trevor-paglen" id="11-last-pictures-narbona-predator" label="The Last Pictures (The Narbona Panel / Humans Seen Through a Predator Drone), 2012" caption="A hard vertical split joins terrain photography to a grainy instrumental image, making the difference in visual regimes part of the subject." />

The upper terrain band at y0.134 crosses the pair but means differently on either side: geological contour at left, interface and aerial data at right. Mean luminance 0.447, edge density 0.115, and the gray palette #787878, #a0a0a0, #545454 keep the juxtaposition sober. The point is not that the right panel sees more clearly; it makes the act of seeing look operational.

## Exercises

<ExerciseCard n="01" title="Make an absence do compositional work">
Photograph an open field, such as sky, water, pavement, or wall, so a low horizon or a tiny detail must carry attention. Make one version with the detail marked by title and one without; compare how the frame changes.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="02" title="Pair evidence without making it illustrative">
Place a wide photograph beside a map, diagram, or data view of the same site. Find one horizontal or vertical relation that lets the two panels speak, but keep their visual languages distinct.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="03" title="Read a system through its traces">
From an elevated viewpoint, photograph routes, lights, seams, or repeated enclosures. Before naming the institution or service, describe only paths, convergence, and distribution; let inference follow observation.
</ExerciseCard>
