# Trevor Paglen — source shortlist

This set follows Paglen's photographic strategies for making hidden surveillance infrastructure visible: long-distance observation, orbital tracking, aerial mapping, and deliberately partial machine vision. Eleven complete, public web-display renditions were retrieved from Smithsonian American Art Museum (SAAM), the National Gallery of Art (NGA), and Rollins Museum of Art collection/exhibition pages. Every file opens successfully and has a long edge of at least 640px (the three Rollins files are exactly 640px). The source institutions identify the artist and copyright notices; no open-licensed reproduction was located for these contemporary works.

**Rights basis:** **fair-use** for every selection. Each assessment below is individual and limited. This nonprofit book uses a credited, record-linked, web-sized copy for criticism, scholarship, and transformative composition teaching. The complete published frame is necessary to analyze Paglen's deliberate use of distance, scale, atmospheric interference, orientation, and visual ambiguity; a detail crop would remove the teaching subject. The copies link back to their institutional records and do not replace an original print, museum record, or licensed reproduction.

## Research context

SAAM's *Sites Unseen* exhibition identifies Paglen's photographic project as making surveillance infrastructure apparent through distant views of military installations, satellites, cables, drones, and AI systems: https://americanart.si.edu/exhibitions/paglen. The NGA's artist record confirms the 2015 ONYX and 2010 *Untitled (Reaper Drone)* works in its collection: https://www.nga.gov/artists/47988-trevor-paglen. Paglen's own account of *The Other Night Sky* explains that the project tracks and photographs usually unacknowledged secret satellites: https://paglen.studio/tag/the-other-night-sky/.
