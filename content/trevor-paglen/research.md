# Trevor Paglen — research notes

## Documented record

The institutional records assembled in [sources.md](sources.md) place the
selected works between 2010 and 2017. They identify a deliberately varied set
of photographic and image-based works: C-prints for the long-distance
surveillance, cable-landing, drone, satellite, and overhead views; a
dye-sublimation print for *"Fanon" (Even the Dead Are Not Safe) Eigenface*;
a chromogenic print for *ONYX V/HYBRID and Other Spacecraft from Glacier
Point*; and a gelatin-silver print for *The Last Pictures (The Narbona Panel /
Humans Seen Through a Predator Drone)*. The Mastic Beach and Keawaula works
are also documented as mixed-media works incorporating a C-print. The supplied
record for *The Last Pictures/EchoStar XVI Launch and Preliminary Orbit* does
not specify a printing process.

SAAM describes Paglen's project as making usually hidden surveillance
infrastructure visible through distant views of military installations,
satellites, cables, drones, and AI systems. The artist's account of *The Other
Night Sky*, linked from the source notes, identifies the related satellite work
as tracking and photographing unacknowledged secret satellites. These records
support a chapter about distance, evidence, and the politics of what can barely
be seen; they do not license technical reconstruction of a capture setup.

## Technique and limits

The source records identify the listed dates, titles, institutions, and the
print processes above, but they do not establish camera body, lens, focal
length, aperture, shutter speed, sensitivity, film stock, sensor, telescope,
tracking mount, exposure duration, or post-production workflow. The ingested
manifest contains no retained EXIF metadata. No camera setting is inferred
here.

The works instead establish method at the level documented by their titles and
records: long-distance observation of sites and orbital objects, a paired
map-and-photograph format for the cable-landing works, aerial or overhead
views, and an algorithmically mediated portrait. What is photographically
visible—star trails, a horizon, a map panel, an eye-line, a lit road, or the
seam of a diptych—is distinguished from the apparatus used to produce it.

## Measured evidence

The analyses split the set into sparse fields and dense evidence. The nearly
empty *Untitled (Reaper Drone)* has mean luminance 0.829 and edge density 0.000;
the Eigenface is also measured at edge density 0.000 despite its strong facial
contrast. At the other extreme, *The Last Pictures/EchoStar XVI* has mean
luminance 0.033 and edge density 0.168, while the Narbona/Predator pair reaches
0.115. The overhead NSA image sits between them (mean 0.618, edge density
0.073), where roads and pools of illumination supply the structure.

Palette measurements make the transformation clear: the STSS image is a
compressed red field (#25030a, #9c150b, #600909); the overhead NSA view is
organized by amber (#cc903c, #f1c961, #905c24); the Eigenface resolves through
cool gray, near-black, and pale skin-like values (#9b9ca1, #424453, #1b1b26).
The overlays therefore name only observed geometry—rotation, seam, axis,
horizon, focal formation, and access line—rather than claiming hidden objects
where the image offers only a trace.
