# Wolfgang Tillmans — research note

## What the supplied sources establish

The source set dates this selection from 1992 to 2016 and follows Tillmans through intimate portraiture, astronomical observation, a public interior, still-life and paper-form images, a page-like portrait, and a forest. MoMA's collection record establishes the title and date of *Lutz & Alex sitting in the trees*; the Walker Art Center records establish the titles and dates of the remaining collection works. The source notes also identify *Wald (Briol I)* as a 2008 work by Tillmans under CC BY-SA 4.0. [Source notes](sources.md)

MoMA's survey context, as summarized in the supplied notes, supports the chapter's central comparison: Tillmans places vernacular subjects, serial observation, abstraction, and installation-aware forms in an equal-status visual field. That is useful historical context, but the overlays make narrower claims: they describe visible branches, faces, disc edges, balcony bands, folds, colour divisions, type, sea, and trunks in these particular display reproductions.

## Process, printing, and settings

The available records do not document a camera body, lens, focal length, aperture, shutter speed, sensitivity, film stock, negative format, lighting setup, or a full account of the capture and darkroom workflow for these individual photographs. The ingested web-display JPEGs contain no EXIF. The Walker records do identify media for collection works where stated—for example, *The Cock (kiss)* is an inkjet print on paper and *Venus transit, passage* a color chromogenic print—but those entries do not establish camera settings or a uniform process for the whole selection. The chapter therefore does not infer technical settings from visible grain, colour, blur, or the white margins around reproduced works.

The source notes do identify several images as public collection-display files and describe Tillmans's broader practice as including camera-less abstraction and installation-derived paper forms. Those facts do not establish a single process for every plate. Descriptions of a print border, the visible paper field, or tonal structure refer only to the supplied reproduction.

## Measured display-file observations

The analyzer confirms the range of visual density across the supplied files. *Wald (Briol I)* has the highest edge density in the set (0.1742), while the three *Venus transit* images are exceptionally sparse (0.0064, 0.0068, and 0.0060). *Lutz & Alex sitting in the trees* is also materially dense at 0.1658, consistent with its branch lattice. In the display copies, the Venus plates pair near-black palette representatives with pale lavender-gray values; *Lighter, green/red IV* separates a deep red `#71050d` field from greens such as `#1c482e` and `#275c3d`. These are measurements of web renditions, not claims about original materials or print colour.

## Method for this chapter

The chapter treats the source records as evidence for title, date, collection context, and rights provenance; it treats the overlays as evidence-backed annotations of the intact supplied frames. Where a detector proposes a horizon, vanishing point, or symmetry that does not describe a meaningful photographed structure, the overlay does not repeat that claim. The resulting sequence is about how different subjects receive equal compositional attention, not about reducing the practice to a single camera technique.
