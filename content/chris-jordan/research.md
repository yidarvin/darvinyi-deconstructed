# Chris Jordan — research note

## Working method and chronology

The supplied artist-gallery records place these ten works in Chris Jordan's *Running the Numbers: An American Self-Portrait* (2006–2014). They are photo-based composites: a large image is assembled from thousands of smaller photographic units, so a coherent landscape, scene, or field changes into an inventory as viewing distance closes. The source records identify the counted materials and statistics for bottles, cans, oil barrels, retired phones, prison uniforms, Yukon Denali logos, paper cups, plastic bags, cigarettes, and shipping containers. The dates for this group run from 2006 to 2008.

The supplied records do not establish that these are unmediated documentary photographs. Photographic units are digitally organized into a second, aggregate image. The Griffin Museum record describes large images assembled from smaller photographs, while the Jordan Schnitzer Museum of Art situates the series' consumer-object composites within sustainability, health, and consumerism. The linked artist statement frames the practice around the difficult-to-see scale of mass consumption and a tension between beauty and horror.

## Technical limits

The available records document titles, dates, image subjects, counts, and composite construction. They do **not** document a camera body, lens, focal length, aperture, shutter speed, sensitivity, film or sensor format, lighting setup, print process, paper, edition, edition dimensions, or edition-specific settings. The ingested display JPEGs contain no EXIF data. This chapter therefore describes documented method and visible structure, not undocumented capture or printing specifications.
