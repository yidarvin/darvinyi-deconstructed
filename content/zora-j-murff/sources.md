# Source notes — Zora J Murff

## Scope and source route

This source set concentrates on *At No Point In Between* (2019), Murff's sustained account of North Omaha. The series deliberately makes portraits, topographical views, and archival-inflected images answer one another; this is the clearest compact corpus for teaching his use of sequence, framing, and the political meaning of place. The Museum of Modern Art's [artist record](https://www.moma.org/artists/132455-zora-j-murff) lists 14 collection works from the project, while the [Guardian's credited 2021 gallery](https://www.theguardian.com/artanddesign/gallery/2021/dec/09/i-have-all-the-colours-zora-j-murff-black-neighbourhood) supplies the publicly visible, web-sized display files acquired here. The Guardian captions identify each photograph and credit Zora J Murff; its accompanying text records the series' relation to North Omaha, redlining, the North Freeway, and the multiple forms of anti-Black violence addressed by the work.

All twelve selections are copyrighted, not open-licensed. The use basis for **each** is fair use: (1) this nonprofit educational chapter uses the image for criticism, scholarship, and transformative composition teaching rather than decoration; (2) the complete frame is necessary to analyze Murff's deliberate relation of body, street, architecture, and pictorial space; (3) only a public, web-sized display copy is retained; and (4) the chapter credits the artist, links to the publishing record, and cannot substitute for the original, the gallery's edition, or a licensed reproduction. ## Research notes

- MoMA's [2020 introduction](https://www.moma.org/magazine/articles/432) describes the series as weaving portraits, landscapes, and found images around a community shaped by racist policy and injustice.
- Murff's [2022 MoMA conversation](https://www.moma.org/magazine/articles/706) explains his practice of citation and his concern with how photographic images participate in spectacle, commodification, and racial history.
- Webber Represents' [exhibition record](https://www.webberrepresents.com/exhibitions/at-no-point-in-between-1) provides an independent checklist and describes the project's relation to North Omaha, redlining, police violence, and the image's role in racial hierarchy.
