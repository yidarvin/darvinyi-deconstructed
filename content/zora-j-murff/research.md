# Zora J Murff — research note

## Scope and evidence

This chapter considers twelve display images from *At No Point In Between*.
The Guardian identifies the pictured works as 2019 and reports that the project
was made between 2015 and 2018; it records Murff's account of portraits made
through encounters with people connected to North Omaha. The project joins
portraits, landscape, and archival concerns rather than treating any one
photograph as self-sufficient reportage. Titles, credits, display-source links,
and the image-specific fair-use rationale are retained in
[sources.md](sources.md).

MoMA describes the series as weaving portraits, landscapes, and found images
around a community shaped by racist policy and injustice. Webber Gallery's
exhibition record likewise describes a practice combining archives, portraiture,
and autobiographical accounts; it identifies the project with North Omaha and
its histories of redlining, police violence, and racial hierarchy. Those records
give the chapter its historical frame, while its plate readings remain limited to
what is visible in the supplied frames.

## Process and print information

Webber's checklist identifies *Intersection*, *Terri (talking about the
freeway)*, and *Jerrod and Junior (talking about fatherhood)* as archival pigment
prints. That is a documented exhibition medium for those named works, not a
basis for assigning an identical print process to every image in this selection.
The source packet does not document a camera body, lens, focal length, aperture,
shutter speed, ISO, film stock, sensor, lighting setup, negative format, or
darkroom / digital-printing workflow for these particular exposures. The
ingested web JPEGs contain no usable capture EXIF.

Accordingly, the chapter describes visible choices—close and environmental
portrait framing, shallow or deep focus, tonal compression, color, boards,
fences, street fixtures, and plant forms—without turning those appearances into
claims about undocumented settings. The files are web-sized display copies used
for criticism and do not replace the artist's editions or licensed
reproductions.

## Analysis limits

The image analyzer occasionally proposed horizons, vanishing points, and
symmetry axes in brick courses, fabric, bokeh, or broad tonal transitions. The
overlays retain only visually verified structures: faces, hands, arms, braids,
hood seams, street signals, fence lines, basketball backboards, flowers, and
their contours. A detector's geometric proposal is not treated as evidence of
an actual horizon, perspective system, or symbolic meaning.
