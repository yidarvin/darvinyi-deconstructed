# Martine Syms — source shortlist

This set concentrates on the eight photographic works and the two related installation/video records that Martine Syms presented in and around the 2019 Whitney Biennial. The Whitney Museum of American Art's public collection API serves the ten web-sized JPEGs below directly, without authentication; every file was retrieved from that public response, opened successfully, and preserves the full displayed image. The Whitney identifies the eight individual photographs as 2019 inkjet prints and marks each `© Martine Syms`; it identifies the related installation and video record separately. These are contemporary copyrighted works, so none is treated as open content.

**Rights basis:** **fair-use** for every selection. This is a separate, limited assessment for each image: the book uses a credited, record-linked, web-sized copy for nonprofit criticism, scholarship, and transformative composition teaching about framing, seriality, blockage, and the relation of photographed image to exhibition display. The complete published frame is necessary to analyze each work’s cropping and spatial organization; excerpts would remove the teaching subject. The copies remain small public display renditions and link to the Whitney record, so they do not replace an artwork, a collection record, or a licensed reproduction.

## Research context

The Whitney artist record lists these ten collection works and classifies eight as photographs, while its collection records supply the titles, date, media, credit lines, image descriptions, accession numbers, and copyright notices: https://whitney.org/artists/17568. The Studio Museum in Harlem situates Syms’s practice in the representation of Blackness in the digital age and in the influence of media and technology on the performance of race and gender: https://www.studiomuseum.org/artists/martine-syms. Together, the 2019 series provides a coherent compositional sequence of blocked sightlines, vernacular typography, mundane camera views, and the re-framing of photographs through exhibition space.
