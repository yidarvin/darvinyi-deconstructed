Paul Mpagi Sepuya’s studio photographs make the conditions of portraiture visible. Mirrors do not simply double a sitter: they expose the camera, split a body between physical and reflected space, and make the studio’s screens, lamps, cushions, and props part of the portrait’s social machinery. Across this 2018–2024 selection, the camera is often not an invisible instrument but an object inside the image. The overlays follow that proposition with restrained claims about anchors, floor edges, narrow mirror strips, tripod feet, screens, and optical centers. ## mirrors make a relay

<Plate slug="paul-mpagi-sepuya" id="01-darkroom-mirror-2220232" label="Darkroom Mirror (_2220232), 2018" caption="The camera-hand exchange keeps two bodies and their reflections in one compact relay." />

In *Darkroom Mirror (_2220232)*, the overlay keeps to the camera-hand exchange at the center of the reflection. Rather than force a seam or a continuous diagonal onto the bodies, it lets the camera, hand, and two partially superimposed figures carry the relay of looking. The supplied file is low-key but not monochrome: its measured palette runs from #161515 through red-browns to #ddd0c8, with luminance from p05 0.063 to p95 0.659. That spread gives the camera’s dark bulk and the reflected skin distinct pressures inside the same mirror.

<Plate slug="paul-mpagi-sepuya" id="02-mirror-study-0x5a7387" label="Mirror Study (0X5A7387), 2018" caption="A centered mirror hinge and a low body fragment turn a single object into overlapping image layers." />

*Mirror Study (0X5A7387)* makes its narrow mirror an upright hinge. The vertical axis follows the meeting of its two dark planes, while the lower anchor names the hand-and-body fragment that presses against the frame’s base. An unusually bright measured field, mean luminance 0.711 with #ededed and #cec6be dominant, keeps the black object from reading as a void. Its low edge density (0.019) concentrates attention on the precise split rather than on descriptive detail.

<Plate slug="paul-mpagi-sepuya" id="03-figures-0x5a6168" label="Figures (0X5A6168), 2019" caption="A low floor threshold and a compact figure cluster leave two intertwined bodies exposed in an otherwise open studio." />

In *Figures (0X5A6168)*, the model-and-camera cluster is deliberately small against a large white field. The overlay’s low threshold catches the floor rather than inventing a horizon, while its anchor locates the compact knot of bodies and tripod. The delivered image’s palette, #f2f2f1, #d4c6b9, #7b5846, #a58876, and #30231f, makes the figures’ warm fragments resist the blank field. Its sparse measured edges (0.018) are part of why the compression feels so exposed.

## the studio enters the portrait

<Plate slug="paul-mpagi-sepuya" id="04-studio-0x5a9580" label="Studio (0X5A9580), 2020" caption="An upper seam, a central screen field, and a small tripod foot distribute attention through an intentionally exposed studio." />

The composition of *Studio (0X5A9580)* refuses to treat the studio as neutral backdrop. The upper seam and large screen field give the photograph a sequence of rectangular registers; the small tripod foot at lower left confirms that the recording apparatus has its own place in the arrangement. At p05 0.204 and p95 0.992, this is one of the most tonally extended files in the selection. Whites (#f9f9f9, #e4e0db) make the dark screen, bodies, and equipment read as interruptions rather than as hidden support.

<Plate slug="paul-mpagi-sepuya" id="05-dark-room-studio-mirror-0x5a5515" label="Dark Room Studio Mirror (0X5A5515), 2021" caption="A central mirror axis, red-lit fragment, and lower threshold organize a darkroom scene around the camera tripod." />

In *Dark Room Studio Mirror (0X5A5515)*, the camera tripod occupies the same central route as the blurred reflected figure. The overlay’s mirror axis is a literal vertical passage, while the anchor fixes on the bright lamp-like red fragment at left rather than pretending to locate a face. The image’s palette is correspondingly extreme: #140001 and #70020f are interrupted by #df1632, with pale #daccc4 only as a small counterpoint. A mean luminance of 0.155 and edge density of 0.096 describe the visual pressure of red light against the near-black field, not a claim about a particular darkroom process.

<Plate slug="paul-mpagi-sepuya" id="06-daylight-studio-0x5a4577" label="Daylight Studio (0X5A4577), 2022" caption="A narrow mirror strip doubles the embrace while a low studio base and nearby objects remain fully visible." />

*Daylight Studio (0X5A4577)* reverses the previous image’s enclosure without making the studio disappear. A narrow mirror strip at the far left returns a sliver of the embrace; the black curtain and the warm cushion, basket, and carved stand at right create a competing staged register. The analyzer finds a wide luminance range, p05 0.027 to p95 0.855, and a palette that moves from #100e0e through #885841 to #dbd3cf. The pair remains an event within a deliberately furnished system, not the sole subject.

<Plate slug="paul-mpagi-sepuya" id="07-daylight-studio-model-study-0x5a2323" label="Daylight Studio Model Study (0X5A2323), 2022" caption="A paired-body axis, lower studio plane, and anchor on the model show how a pose is held inside a stage of curtains and props." />

The body in *Daylight Studio Model Study (0X5A2323)* is both present and internally divided by the mirror’s return. The axis tracks that paired construction rather than asserting full bilateral symmetry; the lower line keeps the rug, shoes, pedestal, and wooden block visibly active. The image ranges from #100c0d and #64432b to #f8f7f6, with p05 0.016 and p95 0.996. That contrast lets the pale body meet the black curtain without reducing either to a silhouette.

<Plate slug="paul-mpagi-sepuya" id="08-twilight-studio-x5a6433" label="Twilight Studio (_X5A6433), 2023" caption="An upper studio register, screen base, and wheeled screen identify a temporary stage of lamps, cushions, and a passing body." />

In *Twilight Studio (_X5A6433)*, the frame opens sideways rather than enclosing a single portrait subject. The screen’s base, its tall wheeled screen, and the high studio register make a portable stage; lamps and cushions establish a set of local attention points around the blurred body. Its dark #120e0e and #4c3425 are set against #eeedf0 and muted #b4a59d, while p05 0.024 and p95 0.976 give the twilight scene a very broad tonal reach. The overlay marks the supplied structure, not an inferred lighting setup.

## cameras and second eyes

<Plate slug="paul-mpagi-sepuya" id="09-daylight-studio-mirror-dsf2371" label="Daylight Studio Mirror (_DSF2371), 2023" caption="The mirror’s central axis, low reflection edge, and central camera make the square frame a diagram of its own making." />

*Daylight Studio Mirror (_DSF2371)* is an unusually spare square. The camera on its tripod becomes the anchor; a low reflection edge holds the furniture and floor inside the mirror, while the central axis keeps the reflected room in balance. The high-key palette (#eeeff0, #dad9d9, #b6a38b, #756154, #30231f) and low edge density of 0.011 make the few physical boundaries unusually consequential. Rather than supply a sitter as the picture’s center, Sepuya lets the means of looking occupy it.

<Plate slug="paul-mpagi-sepuya" id="10-self-portrait-tests-gazing-ball" label="Self-portrait Tests in Studio Mirror with Gazing Ball (_DSF1183), 2024" caption="A mirror axis and the highlighted gazing ball establish a second optical center beside the photographer’s camera and reflection." />

The ball in *Self-portrait Tests in Studio Mirror with Gazing Ball (_DSF1183)* is not decorative. The circular outline names it as a second optical center: its convex surface returns a distributed studio view while the camera, tripod, reflected body, and second ball make observation recursive. The near-grayscale palette of #e5e5e5, #5e4933, #232221, #807c79, and #bab7b5 allows reflected highlights to carry much of that relay. With p05 0.122 and p95 0.996, the file retains the chrome’s sharpness without losing the dark clothing and camera.

<Plate slug="paul-mpagi-sepuya" id="11-studio-mirror-arrangement-dsf0714" label="Studio Mirror with Arrangement (_DSF0714), 2024" caption="An upper studio edge and camera tripod show objects and images arranging the conditions of a portrait." />

*Studio Mirror with Arrangement (_DSF0714)* turns the camera tripod, black screens, wooden blocks, mirror, chair, silver ball, and wall photographs into equal agents. The tripod anchor prevents the image from becoming a generic still life; it points to the device through which this arrangement becomes a photograph. The image’s p05 0.051 to p95 0.957 range and palette from #1a191b to #efebe7 hold black screen surfaces against the pale room, with #ac846b and #d9cfc8 linking wood and fabric. The visual claim is modest but decisive: portraiture here is assembled among things.

## Exercises

<ExerciseCard n="01" title="Put the camera in the argument">
Make a self-portrait or collaborative portrait in which the camera, tripod, cable, or reflection visibly changes the composition. Keep only a frame where the apparatus changes how the body is read.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="02" title="Build a second viewpoint">
Use one mirror, glass object, or reflective surface to introduce a second view without making the reflection merely decorative. Check where its boundary creates a new frame or alters a body’s scale.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="03" title="Let props share authorship">
Arrange two studio objects with a figure, then move them until their edges direct attention as forcefully as the pose. Include enough of the room that the setup remains visible rather than becoming a neutral backdrop.
</ExerciseCard>
