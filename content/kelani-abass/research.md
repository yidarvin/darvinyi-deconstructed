# Kelani Abass — research note

Kelani Abass (born 1979) works across painting, photography, archival material,
and printing. The source record for this chapter documents a 2019–21 group of
works built from family photographs, printing matter, color fields, and wooden
frames. It is a study of assembled photographic objects, not a claim that all
the pictured source photographs were made by Abass. [Source notes](sources.md)

## Method and period

The chapter follows three related strategies visible in the supplied 2019–21
works: the compartmented family archive of *Casing History (Sisters 2)*;
the continuous, figure-populated painted fields of *Chronological
Remuneration XI* and *XIII*; and the framed portrait-and-printing-block
assemblages of *Scrap of Evidence* and *Stamping History*. MoMA’s collection
records for the later *Casing History, Spilling Memories* works identify the
type-case/inkjet-print construction and its use of family archive material;
that corroborates the recurring archive-and-type-case method, but does not
establish the exact material specification for every 2019–21 work here.

## Materials, printing, and capture information

The source record establishes publicly displayed, web-sized JPEG derivatives
for critical use, not camera originals. The ingested files contain no EXIF.
Neither the source record nor the cited institutional material supplies a
camera body, lens, focal length, aperture, shutter speed, ISO, film stock,
lighting setup, or capture date for the underlying family photographs. Those
settings are therefore not inferred. Nor does the source record document the
precise printing or mounting process for every individual work. The analysis
can reliably discuss the visible type case, repeated portrait fragments,
printing blocks, textured color grounds, and nested wooden frames.

## Working vocabulary for the chapter

- **Case / compartment:** physical dividers make photographs read as unequal
  units in an archive rather than a single seamless image.
- **Stack / counterweight:** portrait, type, printing block, or medallion is
  placed above, beside, or below another element so that memory is read through
  physical arrangement.
- **Field / interruption:** a broad painted or textured ground receives figures,
  numbers, or objects without becoming a conventional photographic background.

All historical and rights information, individual work titles, dates, credits,
and fair-use rationales are retained in the [source notes](sources.md).
