# Rineke Dijkstra — build research

## What the supplied records establish

The selected Art Institute of Chicago records document twelve works by Rineke Dijkstra: ten *Beach Portraits* made in 1992–93, followed by *Annemiek, February 11, 1997* and *Amit, Golani Brigade, Orev Unit, Elyacim, Israel, May 26, 1999*. The dates and place names are part of the titles recorded in the collection metadata. This makes the selection useful as a study of a repeated portrait situation across different sitters and locations, not as evidence for a single session or a universal account of the artist's practice. [Source notes](sources.md)

The source records identify the holding institution and collection credit for each work. They do not, in the supplied documentation, establish the original print medium, edition, print dimensions, negative format, camera, lens, focal length, aperture, shutter speed, film stock, ISO, lighting setup, or exposure settings. The twelve ingested public display JPEGs also contain empty EXIF fields. The chapter therefore describes only visible framing, posture, spatial bands, and measured tonal/palette structure; it does not infer capture settings or print processes from appearance.

## Working visual evidence

Across the beach photographs, the durable visible structure is a portrait-scale body against broad sky, water, and ground bands. That is a compositional observation from the images, not a technical claim about the camera. The analysis records repeatedly find a near-vertical balance axis and a low horizontal break; their grid fits alternate between phi and thirds, so the chapter treats these as measured aids rather than as a hidden formula. *Annemiek* shifts that method into a close head-and-shoulders crop, while *Amit* retains an upright figure but replaces the beach with a distant ridge and vegetation.

The web display palette is consistently cool around the beach scenes, while red clothing or skin tones provide small warm disruptions. This is a report on the ingested files' sampled palettes, not a statement about the color of an original print. The same distinction matters for tonal range: the analyzer's p05–p95 and edge-density readings describe these 843-pixel study reproductions and support comparisons of broad planes, not conservation or printing analysis.
