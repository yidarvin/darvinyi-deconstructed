# Daniel Gordon — research note

## Documented method and period

The twelve works in this chapter date from 2008 to 2020. Daniel Gordon’s studio
archive identifies the early figure works as C-prints and the later works as
pigment prints (with *Glass Vase With Blue Handles* specified as a pigment print
with UV protectant). The archive’s *Still Lifes, Portraits & Parts* statement
describes Gordon’s process as cutting and pasting internet-sourced image
printouts into three-dimensional tableaux, which he photographs with an 8 × 10
camera; MoMA’s *New Photography 2009* account confirms that the temporary
sculptures are dismantled after being photographed. Sources: [studio
archive](https://danielgordonstudio.com/work/20) and [MoMA](https://www.moma.org/explore/inside_out/2009/11/25/daniel-gordon-in-new-photography-2009/).

This is enough to describe the work as a photographically completed paper
construction, rather than treating its cut surfaces as a Photoshop effect. The
selection’s titles, dates, media, display derivatives, and per-image fair-use
records are in [sources.md](sources.md).

## What is not documented

The consulted records do not establish a camera body beyond the stated 8 × 10
camera, lens, focal length, aperture, shutter speed, ISO/film sensitivity, film
stock, exposure, lighting arrangement, or capture settings for these individual
works. The web-display JPEGs contain no reliable capture EXIF. This chapter
therefore discusses visible frontal lighting, paper edges, color, object
placement, and measured tonal/palette findings without inferring technical
settings.
