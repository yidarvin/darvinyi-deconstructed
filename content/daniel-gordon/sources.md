# Daniel Gordon — source shortlist

This set follows Daniel Gordon’s photographically completed paper constructions from the early composite figure pictures to the later still lifes. All twelve files were obtained from the artist’s publicly accessible studio archive: its `works` API supplies the title, year, medium, and a deliberately reduced “large” JPEG derivative for each work. The downloaded derivatives open successfully and are 750–1,000px on their long edge, comfortably above the project’s 640px source floor while remaining web-display copies rather than production files. The archive does not attach an open licence to the images; each selection is therefore documented as a limited fair-use use of a copyrighted work.

**Rights basis:** **fair-use** for every selection. Daniel Gordon is the artist and rightsholder; where the studio archive does not show a separate copyright line, it is not inferred that the display image is open licensed. Each assessment below applies the four factors: (1) this nonprofit book makes a transformative use for criticism, scholarship, and composition teaching; (2) the underlying work is a highly creative contemporary photograph; (3) the complete published frame is necessary to analyze Gordon’s constructed photograph, not a cropped detail; and (4) the credited 750–1,000px public display copy links to the artist’s record and does not replace a print, the artist’s record, or a licensed reproduction.

## Research context

MoMA identifies Gordon as an artist in *New Photography 2009* and describes these works as photographs of temporary sculptures assembled from internet-sourced printouts, then dismantled after they are photographed: https://www.moma.org/explore/inside_out/2009/11/25/daniel-gordon-in-new-photography-2009/. The artist archive’s 2010–11 *Still Lifes, Portraits & Parts* exhibition statement similarly describes the manual cut-and-paste construction, three-dimensional tableaux, and 8 × 10 camera: https://danielgordonstudio.com/work/20. Those accounts establish why this selection treats paper edge, frontal viewpoint, collage, and objecthood as compositionally inseparable.
