# Pedro Meyer — source record

## Selection and acquisition note

Pedro Meyer is a living photographer and the registry classifies this corpus as
copyrighted. The set combines one museum collection rendition with nine public
artist-archive renders from Meyer's Miramar books. The Miramar files are publicly
served from the artist's own site; their project pages identify the two books used
here: *Colonia Ajusco* and *Virgilio*. The book-page reproductions retain the
photographic frame and, where present, their printed caption. The retained book-page
reproductions may include surrounding quotation or pagination; no photographic
content was altered. All files opened successfully after
acquisition. Their shortest long edge is 1,474px, so every image exceeds the 640px
source floor without upscaling.

All entries below use **fair-use**. Each assessment is specific to the cited work:
this book uses a complete, web-sized frame for criticism, scholarship, and
transformative composition teaching; the complete frame is necessary to analyze its
spatial decisions, rather than to decorate the chapter; and the chapter credits and
links to the public record or artist archive. These reference copies do not replace
the original work, museum record, book, or a licensed reproduction. The works are
creative photographs, so that factor weighs against use; their limited size, analytic
purpose, credit, and source links limit any market substitution.

## Acquired shortlist

1. **El Velo Negro** (1975) — `raw/pedro-meyer/01-el-velo-negro.jpg`
   - **Why it matters:** The veiled figure becomes a dark central mass whose folds,
     highlight, and paneled ground make figure/ground reversal unusually explicit.
   - **Collection/source:** [LACMA object M.90.75.79](https://collections.lacma.org/object/96636); direct public web rendition: `https://collections-images.lacma.org/images/96636/96636-1-primary.webp`.
   - **Use basis:** **fair-use**. LACMA identifies the artist and gives the notice
     **© Pedro Meyer**; its displayed credit is “Los Angeles County Museum of Art,
     Gift of Graham and Susan Nash in honor of the museum's twenty-fifth anniversary,
     photo © Museum Associates/LACMA.” The whole vertical is needed to teach tonal
     hierarchy and concealment; this credited, linked web copy is a limited
     non-substitutive critical use.

2. **Ajusco street and bus** (1974) — `raw/pedro-meyer/02-ajusco-street-and-bus.jpg`
   - **Why it matters:** The bus locks the left edge while pedestrians, utility
     wires, and the descending road distribute attention through a deep civic scene.
   - **Collection/source:** Pedro Meyer, [*Colonia Ajusco* online book](https://pedromeyer.com/onlinemiramar/digital-colonia-ajusco/), page 35 render: `https://pedromeyer.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Colonia-Ajusco-Version-Digital-25.NOV_Pagina_035.jpg`.
   - **Use basis:** **fair-use**. Creator/rightsholder: Pedro Meyer (artist archive).
     The complete documentary frame is required to study depth, entry, and dispersed
     scale; the credited web-sized teaching copy links to the artist's public book and
     does not replace it or a licensed print.

3. **Ajusco card players** (1974) — `raw/pedro-meyer/03-ajusco-card-players.jpg`
   - **Why it matters:** Bent legs, hands, cards, and the tree trunk form a compact
     ring, showing how a low viewpoint can make a social group feel structurally
     closed yet active.
   - **Collection/source:** Pedro Meyer, [*Colonia Ajusco* online book](https://pedromeyer.com/onlinemiramar/digital-colonia-ajusco/), page 71 render: `https://pedromeyer.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Colonia-Ajusco-Version-Digital-25.NOV_Pagina_071.jpg`.
   - **Use basis:** **fair-use**. Creator/rightsholder: Pedro Meyer (artist archive).
     The complete frame is necessary to analyze the circle of bodies and cards; this
     linked, credited web copy is used only for transformative criticism and teaching,
     not as a replacement for the book or a licensed reproduction.

4. **Ajusco girls** (1974) — `raw/pedro-meyer/04-ajusco-girls.jpg`
   - **Why it matters:** The paired portraits alternate direct and oblique gaze,
     making a two-image spread teach repetition, contrast, and the expressive role of
     a narrow dividing gutter.
   - **Collection/source:** Pedro Meyer, [*Colonia Ajusco* online book](https://pedromeyer.com/onlinemiramar/digital-colonia-ajusco/), page 102 render: `https://pedromeyer.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Colonia-Ajusco-Version-Digital-25.NOV_Pagina_102.jpg`.
   - **Use basis:** **fair-use**. Creator/rightsholder: Pedro Meyer (artist archive).
     The two-frame arrangement is the teaching subject, so the complete displayed
     spread is necessary; the credited, linked web copy is limited to composition
     criticism and does not substitute for the source book or licensed prints.
