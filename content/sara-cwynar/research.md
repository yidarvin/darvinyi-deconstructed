# Sara Cwynar — research notes

## Scope and medium

This chapter uses twelve works from Cwynar’s *Flat Death* series, dated 2013–18.
MoMA identifies the selected works as chromogenic prints. The selection moves among
printed ephemera, color calibration, display furniture, books, and staged objects;
it is not evidence for a single uniform production procedure. MoMA’s *Red Film*
interview describes Cwynar’s broader interest in studio staging, reassembled images,
consumer objects, and color as a visual connector. [Source notes](sources.md)

## What the record does and does not establish

The surviving collection records document titles, dates, medium, and MoMA’s public
web-display derivatives. They do not document a camera body, lens, film stock,
aperture, shutter speed, ISO, lighting setup, print date, edition, printer, or
darkroom process for these individual works. The chapter therefore treats the
photographs as constructed chromogenic prints and confines technical claims to what
is visible or what the records support. It does not infer capture settings from
their crispness, shallow space, or calibration imagery.

## Historical context

The 2013–18 dates place *Flat Death* within contemporary image circulation rather
than an analogue-only past. In MoMA’s *Search Engines* context, several *Darkroom
Manuals* works sit alongside concerns with image search, appropriation, rapid digital
circulation, and slowed or obsolete photographic processes. That context supports a
reading of reproduction and mediation; it does not establish the technical history
of every pictured object.
