# Namsa Leuba — research note

## Record and scope

This teaching selection comprises twelve credited web renditions from Namsa Leuba's
official project archive: *Khoisan* (2014), *Weke* (2017), *Zulu Kids* (2014), and
*The Kingdom of Mountains* (2014). The supplied [source record](sources.md)
identifies each title, project context, date, artist credit, direct public source,
and image-specific fair-use rationale. It establishes a selection for critical
study, not a substitute for Leuba's original works, prints, archive, or licence.

The official project descriptions characterize *Khoisan* as staged in South
Africa's Northern Cape; *Weke* as narratives informed by Beninese animist and Vodou
cosmology; *Zulu Kids* as made with Zulu communities in KwaZulu-Natal and Soweto;
and *The Kingdom of Mountains* as concerned with traditions and aesthetics around
Lesotho's Katse Dam. These statements support the project contexts, but not an
universal technical recipe for every photograph.

## Technique, printing, and settings

The selected images visibly use staged portraiture, costume, props, controlled
pose, and deliberately chosen settings. The archive record does not document a
uniform lighting plan, capture format, retouching process, film stock, print
process, paper, edition, printer, or print date, so this chapter does not infer any
of those facts from appearance.

The normalized manifest retains no capture metadata for the nine web files from
*Khoisan*, *Zulu Kids*, and *The Kingdom of Mountains*. Three *Weke* web JPEGs do
retain embedded metadata: *Black JC* records a Nikon D800, 62 mm, f/3.2, 1/125 s,
ISO 5000, dated 19 April 2016; *Azaca* a Nikon D800, 70 mm, f/10, 1/125 s, ISO 200,
dated 8 February 2017; and *Transe A* a Nikon D800, 105 mm, f/11, 1/125 s, ISO 200,
dated 8 February 2017. These values are recorded from delivered web files and are
reported only for those individual works; they do not prove settings for the rest
of the selection or a later print workflow.

## Visual method evidenced by the selection

Leuba treats styling as a structural system. Costumes extend a body into silhouette,
objects turn pose into an action, and walls, sky, rock, or mountains become measured
fields against which the figure can press. The deterministic analyses measure tone,
palette, edge density, and proposed geometry. The overlays retain only claims that
can be located on visible figures, props, contours, and settings; they do not treat
every detector-suggested horizon or vanishing point as a meaning of the photograph.
