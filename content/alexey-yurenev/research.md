# Alexey Yurenev — research note

## Scope and evidence

This chapter considers eleven display images from Alexey Yurenev's *Silent Hero* (2019–ongoing). The artist's project page describes a practice that brings machine learning into contact with family photographs, archives, veteran interviews, and forensic fieldwork around World War II memory. The source packet preserves the project-page links, creator notice `© YURENEV`, and image-specific fair-use assessment for every web-sized display copy in [sources.md](sources.md). The plate readings concern visible structures in the supplied frames, not a claim that a source photograph documents a particular historical event.

The selection moves among winter landscape, monument, motion blur, damaged archive, montage, and matrix. This is not evidence of one fixed medium: it records the visual means available in the eleven frames and the project's stated combination of archival and machine-learning methods.

## Technique, capture, and print information

The record dates *Silent Hero* from 2019 and credits it to Yurenev, but it identifies no camera body, lens, focal length, aperture, shutter speed, ISO, film stock, sensor, lighting setup, negative format, software workflow, or print process for these individual images. The ingested web JPEGs contain no capture EXIF. The chapter accordingly does not infer camera settings or claim that blurred, monochrome, damaged, or grid-like appearances reveal a particular historical process.

The source record gives the direct public display renditions as 1,366 to 8,192 pixels on their long edge. They were normalized to 1,600 pixels for consistent analysis without upscaling. The copies remain web-sized, credited, linked, and used only for critical and compositional teaching.

## Analysis limits

The analyzer reliably measured low horizontal divisions, the tree's central axis, and the stained-glass interior's vertical symmetry. It also proposed false perspective systems in blur, foliage, and repeated cells. The overlays retain only visually checked claims: an archive's torn boundary, staircase, train roof and rail, tree mass, monument, blot, cell matrix, separated fragments, chapel axis, contact-sheet field, and diptych panels. Neither a detector's saliency point nor a broad luminance split is treated as a claim about history, intention, or machine procedure.
