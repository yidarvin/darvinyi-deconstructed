# Richard Misrach: the landscape does not look away

Richard Misrach's photographs make environmental violence legible by refusing the easy image of catastrophe. The selection gathered in the High Museum's *Picturing the South* records holds refineries, floodwater, domestic buildings, historic sites, and a beach at an almost disconcertingly measured distance. The overlays name visible structures in the supplied complete-frame web renditions; titles, dates, credits, access routes, and the per-image fair-use rationale remain in the [source notes](../../content/richard-misrach/sources.md).

## barriers, reflections, and scale

<Plate slug="richard-misrach" id="01-swamp-and-pipeline" label="Swamp and Pipeline, Geismar, Louisiana, 1998" caption="A hard pipeline barrier crosses a pale flooded field, making the industrial interruption impossible to read as scenery." />

In *Swamp and Pipeline*, the rusty pipeline makes a low, hard horizontal against a marsh crowded by dead trunks. The overlay separates that bar from the left tree mass, so the eye moves between mechanical continuity and broken vertical growth. The rendition is unusually open: edge density 0.051, mean luminance 0.620, and p05–p95 from 0.090 to 0.945. Pale gray `#e7ecec`, muted green `#adb7a6`, and near-black `#0b0a09` let the pipe register as a claim on the entire foreground, not an incidental detail.

<Plate slug="richard-misrach" id="02-dead-trees-toxic-waste" label="Dead Trees, Toxic Waste Containment Site, Mississippi River, Dow Chemical Corporation, Plaquemine, Louisiana, 1998" caption="A containment fence divides dead trunks from their reflections, making the surface feel both calm and obstructed." />

The fence in *Dead Trees, Toxic Waste Containment Site* sits across the photograph's central division while the vertical trunks are repeated below in water. The overlay holds the fence and one central dead trunk rather than inventing symmetry where the image is damaged and irregular. Its edge density is only 0.040, but the p05–p95 span of 0.243–0.910 retains the dark trunks against a pale, olive field; `#dee0ce`, `#929372`, and `#43422d` make the reflection read as uneasy continuation rather than a peaceful mirror.

<Plate slug="richard-misrach" id="03-playground-shell-refinery" label="Playground and Shell Refinery, Norco, Louisiana, 1998" caption="A basketball standard holds against the refinery's lateral field, turning ordinary recreation into an impossible adjacency." />

*Playground and Shell Refinery* gives the basketball backboard the frame's most direct vertical assertion, then stretches pipes, wires, and refinery stacks behind it. The overlay marks the backboard joint and its standard against the long refinery band; a local object becomes the scale measure for an immense industrial backdrop. With mean luminance 0.719, p05–p95 of 0.337–0.867, and a low 0.028 edge density, the pale `#d9dbd2` and brown `#756442` palette keeps the scene calm enough that the adjacency arrives slowly.

<Plate slug="richard-misrach" id="04-home-grain-elevator" label="Home and Grain Elevator, Destrehan, Louisiana, 1998" caption="A small house is measured against an elevator that overwhelms its scale without displacing it from the frame." />

The small house in *Home and Grain Elevator* is neither centered as a victim nor cropped away. The overlay frames its roofline beneath a much larger, rectilinear elevator, making domestic scale and industrial scale coexist as the photograph's actual structure. The rendition's p05–p95 range runs from 0.192 to 0.925; pale `#e0e0e2`, grass green `#495c27`, and near-black `#080904` preserve the gap between the apparently benign field and the mass behind it.

## screens, histories, and small human signs

<Plate slug="richard-misrach" id="05-exxon-refinery-state-capitol" label="View of Exxon Refinery, State Capitol, Baton Rouge, Louisiana, 1998" caption="An observation-deck rail points through haze toward a distant refinery, making civic looking part of the composition." />

In *View of Exxon Refinery, State Capitol*, a foreground observation-deck rail sends the eye diagonally into a hazy industrial skyline. The overlay keeps that rail and the distant horizontal band separate: one describes the viewer's controlled perch, the other the diffuse field being surveyed. Edge density is only 0.014, the lowest here, while p05–p95 spans 0.086–0.886. That combination of pale `#e0d9cd`, gray-brown `#5f5a4a`, and dark `#20201d` makes the refinery seem almost to dissolve into the air.

<Plate slug="richard-misrach" id="06-roadside-vegetation-orion" label="Roadside Vegetation and Orion Refining Corporation, Good Hope, Louisiana, 1998" caption="A dense vegetal screen delays the pale industrial horizon behind it, with water repeating the obstruction." />

*Roadside Vegetation and Orion Refining Corporation* arranges three broad registers: a reflected lower field, an opaque screen of leaves, and a thin, pale refinery silhouette. The overlay traces those screens rather than treating the distant plant as a heroic focal point. It is the chapter's darkest large-scale view, with mean luminance 0.475, p05 0.027, and a wide p95 of 0.839. Near-black `#0e110d`, blue `#3e6a83`, and gray-lilac `#d1ccd6` make concealment a tonal fact.

<Plate slug="richard-misrach" id="07-new-housing-sorrento" label="New Housing Development, Sorrento, Louisiana, 2010" caption="Houses form a thin distant line behind flooded trees and their reflections, so development appears as a pressure at the edge of the wetlands." />

*New Housing Development* places detached roofs at a distant threshold behind a flooded foreground of trees. The overlay marks the wetland/housing edge and reflected vegetation; the apparent development remains a narrow, nearly fragile line, which makes its scale less innocent. Mean luminance is 0.674, p05–p95 is 0.318–0.937, and edge density 0.077. Pale `#e2e7e9`, gray `#9d9a90`, and brown `#5e533b` sustain a washed, spacious surface that the reflections complicate.

<Plate slug="richard-misrach" id="08-restored-slave-cabins" label="Restored Slave Cabins, Evergreen Plantation, Edgard, Louisiana, 1998" caption="A gabled nearest cabin and the receding row turn a historical site into a measured structure of enclosure and distance." />

*Restored Slave Cabins* begins with one gabled cabin, then lets its row continue under the live-oak canopy. The overlay frames the nearest building, follows its roof, and continues along the cabins' low recession, making the pictured order feel built and maintained rather than neutral. This is a more textured rendition, with edge density 0.180 and p05–p95 of 0.149–0.718. Dark `#2d3228`, moss `#55503f`, and pale `#e1e2dd` give the scene visual richness without resolving its historical burden.

<Plate slug="richard-misrach" id="09-evergreen-plantation-statuary" label="Evergreen Plantation (garden statuary), Wallace, Louisiana, 1998" caption="A small garden statue is contained by hedge geometry against an open, luminous field." />

The statue in *Evergreen Plantation (garden statuary)* is small enough to be missed, but it is held between hedge enclosure and a distant fence-field band. The overlay identifies that nested scale: ornament is not a respite from the landscape but a carefully placed screen within it. With mean luminance 0.586, p05–p95 from 0.090 to 0.933, and edge density 0.035, the pale `#e9eadd`, brown `#65462c`, and ochre-green `#b5b07d` palette makes the garden's seduction part of the photograph's argument.

<Plate slug="richard-misrach" id="10-nottoway-plantation-leda-swan" label="Nottoway Plantation (Leda and Swan), White Castle, Louisiana, 1998" caption="Two pond edges direct attention through dense foliage toward an isolated white sculpture." />

In *Nottoway Plantation (Leda and Swan)*, the isolated sculpture is held in a narrow clearing, while two sloping pond and bank edges cross the foreground. The overlay boxes the figure and follows those watery margins, so it names the route without pretending the foliage resolves into simple perspective. Edge density reaches 0.240, the chapter's highest, and the p05–p95 interval is 0.051–0.906. Near-black `#171c14`, green `#3e4730`, and pale `#dae2d6` make the white figure a concentrated interruption within a dense field.

<Plate slug="richard-misrach" id="11-hot-lunch-cold-bee" label="“Hot Lunch, cold bee” (Brown's Lunch), Geismar, Louisiana, 1998" caption="A sign and cable reel make the roadside field human-scaled without making it reassuring." />

The sign in *“Hot Lunch, cold bee”* supplies found language, while the cable reel at left supplies a second, circular utilitarian weight. The overlay frames the text, traces the sign's top edge, and contains the reel; these are small, almost comic details held against the unoccupied wet field. Mean luminance is 0.660, p05–p95 is 0.259–0.863, and edge density just 0.033. The muted `#d4d8c9`, `#6f6c46`, and `#453c28` palette lets the words feel more insistent than the scene's low contrast first promises.

## a distant body changes the field

<Plate slug="richard-misrach" id="12-untitled-892-03" label="Untitled #892-03, On the Beach, 2003" caption="A floating figure interrupts the wide shallow division of water and sand." />

*Untitled #892-03* transfers the same measured distance to an aerial beach. A tiny body floats at the shoreline; the overlay locates it against a shallow water tonal band and a gently wavering shore. There is almost no edge density (0.005), and the tonal range is compressed, p05 0.486 to p95 0.776. Blue-green `#75aa92`, pale `#c0c0a9`, and teal `#56867c` let the broad field do most of the work. The figure's bright color makes scale suddenly human, then leaves it exposed.

## Exercises

<ExerciseCard n="01" title="Make the foreground a claim">
Photograph a place with a visible boundary: a rail, fence, hedge, curb, or pipe. Keep the boundary in the lower half and let it divide the frame from a more distant field. Ask whether it merely decorates the view or changes what the view means.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="02" title="Keep the pressure at a distance">
Work from far enough back that a house, factory, monument, or other consequential structure occupies only a small part of the frame. Build the image around the foreground route that makes the distant thing discoverable.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="03" title="Use one small scale cue">
Make a broad field of water, ground, or sky. Place one person, sign, object, or strong color accent inside it without centering it. Check whether the small cue changes the scale of the whole image.
</ExerciseCard>
