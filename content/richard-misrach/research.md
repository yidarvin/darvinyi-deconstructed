# Richard Misrach — research note

## Scope and chronology

The supplied source record documents this twelve-image selection through the High Museum of Art's public collection records and public display JPEGs. Eleven plates belong to the project commissioned in 1998 for the High Museum's *Picturing the South* initiative, tracing the industrial corridor between Baton Rouge and New Orleans; *New Housing Development, Sorrento, Louisiana* is dated 2010. The final plate, *Untitled #892-03* (2003), belongs to *On the Beach*. Titles, places, dates, credits, source routes, and individual four-factor fair-use rationales are preserved in [sources.md](sources.md).

This chapter's grouping is an interpretation of the selected sequence: it moves from floodwater, refineries, homes, and plantation sites to a distant aerial shore. Across those subjects, Misrach's visual method makes infrastructural or historical pressure register through ordinary distances, barriers, and fields rather than through dramatic cropping.

## Technique, printing, and settings

The consulted High Museum records identify the works and, where noted in [sources.md](sources.md), distinguish a 1998 negative from a 2000 print or record a 1998 or 2010 work printed in 2012. They do not establish a camera body, lens, focal length, aperture, shutter speed, ISO, film stock, negative format, print process, paper, edition, or individual exposure settings for this selection.

The ingested public display JPEGs contain inconsistent embedded EXIF: several show 2017 Canon or 2010 Phase One metadata, one names an Epson scanner, and others have none. Those values describe derivative display files or later handling, not the historical photographs, so the chapter does not treat them as capture settings. Its tonal, palette, edge-density, and geometry statements are deterministic measurements of the supplied web renditions, not claims about original prints or exposure.
