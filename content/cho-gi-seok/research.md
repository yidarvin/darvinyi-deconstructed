# Cho Gi-Seok — research notes

## Scope and sources

This chapter uses six public display renditions documented individually in
[sources.md](sources.md): *Flower Study #9* (2020); two 2021 works listed as
*Flower Study* and one 2021 *These days* from *Love & Hate*; *Love These Days*
from *These Days* (2022); and *Daphne's Bad Dream* from *Bad Dream* (2024).
The source record locates the selection within the four series named by
Fotografiska Shanghai's *The Coexistence of Imperfection* exhibition, with the
2020 work additionally linked to Fotografiska Stockholm's *Coexistence* record.
Each supplied rendition is credited © Cho Gi-Seok and is used under the
per-image fair-use rationale recorded there.

## Method and visual language

The available record supports close reading of the displayed pictures' staged
relations: bodies, flowers, makeup, fabricated wire form, and empty ground are
placed as deliberate counterparts. Across the selection, a photograph often
works as a comparison rather than a scene observed in depth: paired profiles,
a flower beside a crouching body, a flower beside a painted portrait, and two
pale clusters separated by white space. This is a description of the visible
arrangements in the documented renditions, not a claim about how every image
in the series was made.

## Technique, printing, and settings

The exhibition and display-page records in this source set do not identify a
camera body, lens, focal length, exposure, aperture, ISO, lighting scheme,
post-production workflow, print process, paper, edition, or installation
specification for these six works. The ingested files contain no retained EXIF
camera fields. Accordingly, the chapter makes no unsupported technical or
printing claim.

## Evidence limits

Titles, dates, series associations, credits, public-display links, and the
fair-use assessment are documented in [sources.md](sources.md). The chapter's
claims about balance, interval, palette, and tonal organization are readings of
the reproduced images, supported by the accompanying composition-analysis
measurements; they are not assertions of the artist's stated intention.
