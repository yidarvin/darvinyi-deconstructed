# Jon Rafman — source shortlist

## Selection and acquisition note

This twelve-image set is drawn from the Guardian's 2012 public presentation of Jon Rafman's *Nine Eyes of Google Street View* (2008–ongoing). The article identifies the project, captions each selected location, and credits every image “Photograph: Courtesy of Jon Rafman.” The artist's [9-Eyes archive](https://9-eyes.com/) identifies the long-running project as *Nine Eyes of Google Street View*, 2008–ongoing; the [Louisiana Museum of Modern Art exhibition record](https://louisiana.dk/udstilling/jon-rafman/) confirms that Rafman's practice is an act of selecting compelling motifs from a vast Street View archive. Together these sources establish both the series and the importance of selection rather than camera operation alone.

The files below are complete-frame public web renditions served directly by the Guardian, with no login, paywall, bypass, capture, or alteration. All opened successfully and measure 760px on the long edge (one is 760 × 428px; the rest are 760 × 475px), meeting the 640px floor. The retained Google navigation fragments and image seams are part of the publicly displayed source frame and were not cropped away.

**Rights basis:** **fair-use** for every selection. The Guardian credits the photographs to Jon Rafman; original automated capture is associated with Google Street View, while copyright ownership beyond that displayed credit is not stated on the gallery. These are creative, copyrighted contemporary works. Each entry applies the four factors: (1) this nonprofit book makes a transformative use for criticism, scholarship, and composition teaching; (2) the source image is creative; (3) the complete web frame is needed to analyze its accidental framing, overlays, glitches, and figure-to-environment relation; and (4) the small, credited, linked display copy does not substitute for Rafman's project, Google Street View, an original print, exhibition, or licensed reproduction.

## Research notes

The source page supplies a wider twenty-image selection, including *630 St Clair Ave W, Toronto*, *8 Rue Valette, Pompertuzat*, *26 Water Street, St Catherine*, *1379 Elgin Avenue West, Winnipeg*, *58 Lungomare 9 Maggio, Bari*, and *Seaworld, San Diego*. The selected twelve retain the strongest set of formal problems for this chapter: vanishing-point interruption, figure-ground concealment, machine stitching, road perspective, involuntary gesture, spectacle, animal rhythm, and the visual language of surveillance interfaces. No canonical work was excluded because of a failed access route; this source set exceeds the registry minimum without limited mode.
