# Campbell Addy — research note

## Record and scope

This chapter uses twelve credited web renditions from Campbell Addy's selected-works
archive, spanning the 2018 *The Cut* cover portrait through editorial work and the
2023 *I ♥ Campbell* exhibition material. The source packet records the individual
titles or contexts, dates where supplied, source URLs, credits, and an image-by-image
fair-use rationale in [sources.md](sources.md). The group is a teaching selection,
not a reproduction of the artist's archive, magazine commissions, monograph, or
exhibition catalogue.

Addy's biography identifies *Feeling Seen: The Photographs of Campbell Addy* (2022)
as his first monograph and identifies *I ♥ Campbell* (180 Studios, 2023) as a body
of newly conceived work reflecting on his roots and inspirations. The exhibition
catalogue describes 36 original pieces across photography, painting, film, and
spatial design. Those are useful historical anchors for the selected works; they do
not establish a uniform technique for every editorial image. See the artist's
[biography](https://campbelladdy.com/about) and the [*I ♥ Campbell* catalogue](https://campbelladdy.com/s/I-3-Campbell-3.pdf).

## Technique, printing, and settings

The supplied record establishes contemporary editorial and exhibition contexts but
does not document a camera body, lens, focal length, aperture, shutter speed,
ISO/sensitivity, capture format, lighting plan, film stock, retouching workflow,
print process, paper, edition, printer, or print date for these twelve photographs.
The normalized manifest contains no retained EXIF. The chapter therefore treats
visible facts—pose, crop, studio or staged setting, tonal field, typography, costume,
and surface—as visible facts rather than as evidence of undocumented production
methods.

## Visual method evidenced by the selection

Across the selection, Addy makes portraiture work through graphic staging: a body can
become a diagonal, a cover masthead can become a frame, a reflective sphere can turn
an arm into a bridge, and a black field can make a small cluster of bodies feel
theatrical. The deterministic analyses supply measured tone, palette, edge density,
and occasional geometric signals; the overlays retain only visual claims that land
on identifiable forms in the individual image. They do not mistake every detected
horizon or convergence for the photograph's argument.
