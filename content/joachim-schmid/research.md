# Joachim Schmid — research note

## Scope and evidence

This chapter is grounded in the twelve credited display files and the work
descriptions collected in [the source record](sources.md). The source page
describes Joachim Schmid's practice as recycling vernacular photography. The
selection moves from found street photographs, through the artist's own
fragmentary *Security Check* photographs, to recirculated webcam, satellite,
and commercial-image material. It is a history of images being selected,
classified, and shown again rather than a history of one camera style.

## Series and material facts

- *Bilder von der Straße* (1982–2012) is documented here as a serial project
  of found pictures. The selected views make the found support, tears, white
  joins, and changed aspect ratios visible alongside the snapshots.
- *Security Check* (1985–1987) is documented as a black-and-white series of
  fragmentary transit/security-screen encounters. The gallery display views
  included here also make the sequencing and individual frames part of the
  visible work.
- *Statics* (1999) is documented as shredded fashion-catalogue photographs
  and printed matter. The two supplied views show the constructed strip
  fields and their white frames.
- *O Campo* (2010) and *X Marks the Spot* (2013) are documented as pigment
  prints; the supplied display images show, respectively, repeated satellite
  football fields and a repeated road-image installation.

## Technical limits

The source record does not identify a camera body, lens, exposure, film stock,
sensor, lighting setup, or original-capture settings for any selected work.
The ingested files have no authoring EXIF. Accordingly, this chapter discusses
the documented materials and the visible display renditions, not invented
technical settings. The analysis JSON files measure the displayed digital
renditions' tonal range, palette, and edge density; they are not evidence about
the original negatives, prints, or source photographs.
