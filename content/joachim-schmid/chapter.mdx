Joachim Schmid treats photographs as material already in circulation: a found snapshot, a blurred security image, a satellite view, or a catalogue page becomes newly legible when its selection, damage, support, sequence, and display are allowed to remain visible. The overlays follow those facts of framing and arrangement rather than pretending each supplied rendition is a transparent window. [Source notes](../../content/joachim-schmid/sources.md)

## found pictures retain their supports

<Plate slug="joachim-schmid" id="01-bilder-von-der-strasse-01" label="Bilder von der Straße, view 01, 1982–2012" caption="A crowd and a lighted gateway share an elephant's monumental architectural enclosure; the visible print itself remains part of the picture." />

The largest animal sits at right, but the overlay correctly gives its anchor to the crowd gathered in front of the bright passage: people, elephant, and architecture make a single uneven scale system. The curving crowd route turns the lower left into an approach rather than dead foreground, while the photographed print edge keeps the image's found condition in view. Its palette of deep #241a26, mauve-gray #574855, and pale #eee2de tracks the collision of dark bodies, masonry, and the high opening; edge density 0.097 is enough to preserve the busy group without making every crack a claim.

<Plate slug="joachim-schmid" id="02-bilder-von-der-strasse-05" label="Bilder von der Straße, view 05, 1982–2012" caption="A child, the foam line, a long cast shadow, and the torn support make a damaged beach snapshot read as a physical object." />

The girl stays near the middle, but nothing about the frame is stable. The waterline slides behind her, the cast shadow escapes down-right, and the torn white loss takes over the left edge. Those are not restoration defects to crop away: they let the source snapshot's body and its damaged support compete for attention. The bright tonal range (p05 0.373 to p95 1.000), sparse edge density 0.011, and pale #fefefd/#dfdad3 palette make the abrasion especially forceful.

<Plate slug="joachim-schmid" id="03-bilder-von-der-strasse-02" label="Bilder von der Straße, view 02, 1982–2012" caption="The white join separates two cropped social snapshots, making their stacked order more important than a seamless scene." />

This is a diptych before it is a portrait. Two photographed moments touch at a white seam; in the lower image, the close pair is held as a local anchor while the upper image establishes a different social address. The dark wine-red #370f22 and #64393a field is interrupted by flesh-colored #ebb29a and a nearly white #fbf3eb, and its 0.094 edge density sustains both the snapshots' detail and the hard division between them. Schmid's serial logic can therefore work through juxtaposition rather than a single decisive image.

<Plate slug="joachim-schmid" id="04-bilder-von-der-strasse-03" label="Bilder von der Straße, view 03, 1982–2012" caption="A torn vertical join keeps two street views discontinuous; the right curb and lamp post organize only their own half." />

The tear is the governing line, not a convenient divider to ignore. On its right, the lamp post and curb give the street a shallow, receding order; on its left, a dark foreground slab resists that order. The white break makes the two photographs answerable to different spatial rules. With edge density only 0.031 and a palette running from #2a2d3b through #727b96 to #fbfaf9, broad pavement and sky matter as much as the few sharp urban marks.

## fragment and sequence

<Plate slug="joachim-schmid" id="05-security-check-01" label="Security Check, view 01, 1985–1987" caption="Eight framed black-and-white fragments form a two-row rhythm inside a broad field of gallery white." />

The supplied view is an installation, so the chapter does not draw an imaginary horizon through its tiny individual photographs. Two rows of framed fragments create a loose cadence, and the large unoccupied wall makes the sequence feel provisional rather than monumental. The analyzer's low edge density, 0.010, and grayscale palette from #1c1c1d to #ececec reinforce that relation between small dense images and expansive white support.

<Plate slug="joachim-schmid" id="06-security-check-04" label="Security Check, view 04, 1985–1987" caption="Curtain folds, a sloping rail, and a pale threshold make an obstructed encounter out of partial architecture." />

The picture withholds a full subject. A rail traverses the upper zone, two folds fall through the dark left mass, and the pale right threshold refuses to resolve into a clear destination. The visible construction is a blurred, black-and-white fragment, consistent with the documented series rather than evidence of a particular camera setting. Its narrow tonal band, p05 0.145 to p95 0.714, and 0.005 edge density make tone and obstruction more decisive than detail.

<Plate slug="joachim-schmid" id="07-security-check-06" label="Security Check, view 06, 1985–1987" caption="A cropped blurred face enters beneath three tilted structural bars, making the apparatus more stable than the body." />

Here the frame catches a face too late and too close. The overlay brackets that cropped entrance while three bars repeat the same diagonal pressure above it. Their recurrence is the picture's real order; the face is the unstable interruption. The high contrast between #090909 and #dedede, with p05 0.027 and p95 0.918, explains why the black voids and pale bars read more clearly than the blurred person.

<Plate slug="joachim-schmid" id="08-security-check-05" label="Security Check, view 05, 1985–1987" caption="A blank threshold presses against a cropped body; a bright interruption and dark strap prevent the fragment from becoming a complete portrait." />

The left half is not empty background. Its bright blankness forces the figure at right into a cropped, unreadable edge, while the shoulder contour and dark strap become the only reliable directional marks. The analyzer records almost no fine structure (edge density 0.001) but an extreme range from #131313 through #f3f3f3; the photograph works by that threshold rather than by descriptive recognition.

## archives become displayed fields

<Plate slug="joachim-schmid" id="09-x-marks-the-spot" label="X Marks the Spot, view 01, 2013" caption="A long row of repeated road pictures, with stepped upper clusters, turns a gallery wall into an archival score." />

The source describes this work as a webcam vantage at Dealey Plaza, but this supplied view asks first to be read as a sparse installation. A nearly continuous central sequence is punctuated by three stepped clusters above and below; repetition makes the individual road image a unit of memory rather than a standalone landscape. At edge density 0.010, the gallery wall's white field and the small gray photographs dominate the composition, matching a restrained palette from #5f5f5f to #ebebec.

<Plate slug="joachim-schmid" id="10-o-campo" label="O Campo, view 01, 2010" caption="Four white-framed satellite football fields repeat across the wall, letting the orange rectangles confront dense urban texture." />

The display makes comparison unavoidable. Each field is framed as its own image, yet all four repeat the same orange rectangle amid differently packed city fabric. The overlay names the four display units rather than claiming one central vanishing point. The pale #ededed gallery field, orange-beige #beac9b playing surfaces, and dark #414145 urban edges support that read; edge density 0.017 keeps the images legible as small mapped objects.

<Plate slug="joachim-schmid" id="11-statics-womens-fashion" label="Statics (women's fashion catalogue), 1999" caption="A white frame contains a dense field of horizontal shredded catalogue strips; repeated bands make fragmentation its subject." />

The individual fashion photograph cannot reassemble itself. Long tan, gray, and image-bearing strips continually start and stop across the framed raster, with faces and body fragments appearing only as flashes. Three representative bands make the governing horizontal grammar visible without competing with it. The analyzer's comparatively high 0.107 edge density and palette of #dededd, #97877c, #73615f, and #41373b describe a dense but muted surface rather than a centered figure.

<Plate slug="joachim-schmid" id="12-statics-mens-fashion" label="Statics (men's fashion catalogue), 1999" caption="Cooler, darker horizontal fragments and staggered seams make the paired catalogue work a second raster rather than a conventional picture." />

The paired *Statics* view retains the same horizontal construction while changing its tonal weather. Darker black-blue strips and white text fragments create a cooler, sharper mesh; occasional warm skin-colored interruptions never recover a complete commercial pose. The white containing frame stays visibly outside the raster. Its #dddedf, #bec2c6, #36343f, and #5b5964 palette, together with edge density 0.102, supports the comparison as a shift in material rhythm rather than a gendered portrait claim.

## Exercises

<ExerciseCard n="01" title="Keep the support visible">
Photograph a found or discarded image without cropping away its tear, fold, border, or mounting surface. Make one overlay claim about the pictured scene and one about the support; revise until neither reads as an afterthought.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="02" title="Sequence fragments rather than complete scenes">
Make six small images of one threshold, queue, or corridor. Arrange them as a row or a loose two-row field. Use spacing and repeated frame size to create rhythm before choosing any single image as the focal point.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="03" title="Turn a source image into a raster">
Cut or mask duplicate printed imagery into horizontal strips, then arrange the strips inside a visible border. Preserve enough fragments for recognition to flicker without allowing one intact figure to take over the field.
</ExerciseCard>
