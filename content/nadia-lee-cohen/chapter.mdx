Nadia Lee Cohen’s *HELLO My Name Is* (2021) treats the badge as a compact fiction engine. Dazed identifies the larger project as 33 self-portraits made from collected badge names and presents the twelve images here as paired persona portraits and still lifes. The pairings matter: a costume and a held pose make the name feel inhabited; a set of purchases, food, printed matter, and small tools makes an absent person feel imaginable. In both cases, the photographic frame is doing the scripting. The full, credited web renditions and their image-specific fair-use rationale are documented in the [source notes](../../content/nadia-lee-cohen/sources.md).

## the portrait is an arrangement, not a confession

<Plate slug="nadia-lee-cohen" id="01-lilian-ressler" label="Lilian Ressler, from *HELLO My Name Is*, 2021" caption="A vertical balance line, a large figure ellipse, and a costume-contrast anchor show how frontal stillness turns a name badge into a performed persona." />

*Lilian Ressler* begins with a familiar portrait promise: seated body, direct face, plain backdrop. It then makes the promise visibly artificial. The overlay’s vertical balance axis at x0.60 and its enclosing figure ellipse keep the body from escaping the studio field; the small anchor at x0.760, y0.508 identifies the striped costume contrast rather than pretending that the face alone owns attention. The warm palette moves from tan `#c29774` and pale peach `#e2b58d` to deep brown `#43241f`. A broad p05–p95 range of 0.141–0.871 preserves a dark costume against the pale ground, while the modest 0.064 edge density keeps the pose legible as one designed mass.

<Plate slug="nadia-lee-cohen" id="03-kat" label="Kat, from *HELLO My Name Is*, 2021" caption="A measured near-symmetry axis and head ellipse hold Kat’s glasses, wig, and blue coat inside a severe vertical studio rectangle." />

In *Kat*, the same recipe changes character because the costume is more graphic and the face is visibly framed by hair and glasses. The analyzer finds especially strong vertical symmetry (0.891) about x0.575; the overlay uses that axis with a face anchor at x0.467, y0.194, a head-and-hair ellipse, and the jacket edge descending along the left. The darker mean luminance of 0.435 is the set’s clearest departure from the beige portraits, with brown `#644534`, warm tan `#dcaf7a`, and blue `#166777` allowing the coat to read as a charged block rather than a naturalistic garment.

<Plate slug="nadia-lee-cohen" id="05-jeff" label="Jeff, from *HELLO My Name Is*, 2021" caption="Near-symmetry, the cowboy-hat oval, and a uniform-detail anchor make a social type readable through a deliberately economical costume mass." />

*Jeff* is almost diagrammatically centered. Its measured symmetry axis sits at x0.5125, and the phi grid keeps the hat, face, name badge, and light suit working as a stacked costume mass rather than a loose character study. The mean luminance is 0.648, p05 is still a comparatively bright 0.333, and edge density drops to 0.019, one of the quietest frames here. That low descriptive noise is why a hat rim, badge, and the single sleeve-to-leg contour can carry such disproportionate narrative force.

<Plate slug="nadia-lee-cohen" id="07-diane" label="Diane, from *HELLO My Name Is*, 2021" caption="The wig-and-face oval and upright balance axis reduce Diane’s persona to hair, striped shirt, seated legs, and a few crisp graphic accents." />

*Diane* makes the constructedness more elastic. The overlay’s x0.5375 balance axis and large wig-and-face oval give the figure a stable top; the left leg contour then opens the pose into the lower field. The analyzer’s palette of `#c69d7b`, `#a98262`, and `#ddb692` stays close to the warm ground, so the nearly black hair and striped shirt must create the separation. Its tonal span, 0.133 to 0.769, supplies enough contrast for that silhouette while the 0.037 edge density prevents the picture from becoming a catalog of details.

<Plate slug="nadia-lee-cohen" id="09-mrs-fischer" label="Mrs Fischer, from *HELLO My Name Is*, 2021" caption="An outer figure ellipse, costume-balance axis, and patterned-skirt anchor show how texture turns a persona into an icon before it becomes an individual." />

The pink blouse and patterned skirt in *Mrs Fischer* make surface itself do the characterization. The overlay uses the measured x0.6125 vertical balance, enclosing head ellipse, and lower skirt-edge anchor rather than treating the portrait as a spontaneous encounter. Dark `#110f17`, red-pink `#aa444f`, and warm `#e1ac87` give the web rendition a tighter chromatic argument than the beige ground suggests. The 0.574 mean and 0.039 edge density leave the decorated torso and skirt as the controlled disruptions inside an otherwise quiet field.

<Plate slug="nadia-lee-cohen" id="11-michael" label="Michael, from *HELLO My Name Is*, 2021" caption="A central seated axis and head oval make the striped shirt, face, and crossed pose read as a concise social script against open ground." />

*Michael* returns the operation to its barest form: centered stool, striped shirt, face, jeans, and a room of warm nothing around them. The x0.50 symmetry axis is exact, while the anchor at x0.592, y0.351 rests on the face-and-shirt zone that gives the pose its charge. The supplied JPEG ranges from p05 0.153 to p95 0.843 with edge density 0.034; pale peach `#ecc8a3` and denim-adjacent `#5c6264` separate ground from clothing without producing an elaborate setting. The persona is therefore assembled from a very small vocabulary.

## things carry the second half of the fiction

<Plate slug="nadia-lee-cohen" id="02-lilian-ressler-still-life" label="Lilian Ressler still life, from *HELLO My Name Is*, 2021" caption="The plate, lower-left prop anchor, and diagonal clue sequence show how separated objects ask for an absent sitter to be inferred." />

The still lifes do not explain the portraits; they stage a parallel act of inference. *Lilian Ressler still life* leaves an immense amount of beige around a plate, cards, food, phone, and packaging. The thirds grid catches the distribution without falsely making it a room, while the anchor at x0.214, y0.711 and the staggered clue path organize the objects as a reading sequence. Its edge density is only 0.029, and its p05–p95 span of 0.267–0.773 is narrow enough that red-brown `#86492e` and small dark accents become the decisive punctuation.

<Plate slug="nadia-lee-cohen" id="04-kat-still-life" label="Kat still life, from *HELLO My Name Is*, 2021" caption="The grandma sign, lower-left receipt cluster, and telephone-to-coupon path turn commercial fragments into a graphic surrogate portrait." />

*Kat still life* spreads its signs, phone, coupons, tickets, and printed scraps over the same uninflected ground. The overlay isolates the large “World’s Greatest Grandma” sign and traces a path from the receipt cluster through the telephone and coupons, so the objects become a deliberately irregular rhythm rather than evidence accidentally left on a table. The image is bright (mean 0.670; p95 0.882), with pale `#e0dbcf` and warm `#dbb187` making blue `#2c6d7e` and orange `#ce6d46` read as abrupt commercial signals.

<Plate slug="nadia-lee-cohen" id="06-jeff-still-life" label="Jeff still life, from *HELLO My Name Is*, 2021" caption="An anchor at the central object cluster and a scattered-possession path show how evenly spaced clues make a backstory spatial." />

For *Jeff still life*, the third-grid structure is less important than the held distances between objects. The anchor at x0.602, y0.679 catches the central cluster, while the line from bottle through printed matter to the lower objects makes a readable but nonliteral possession sequence. The JPEG is bright and restrained: mean 0.683, edge density 0.032, with peach `#eabb89`, orange-brown `#a35c3b`, and dark `#532d1f` keeping the sparse marks visible against the field.

<Plate slug="nadia-lee-cohen" id="08-diane-still-life" label="Diane still life, from *HELLO My Name Is*, 2021" caption="The book-and-apple ellipse and upper-object rhythm convert color accents and small intervals into an indirect portrait." />

*Diane still life* is especially explicit about the difference between a collection and a composition. The ellipse around the book-and-apple group gives a lower weight to the frame, and the upper polyline reads the telephone, tube, and small objects as a counter-rhythm. The analyzer reports a restrained 0.021 edge density, p05 0.180, p95 0.765, and a palette that jumps from peach `#e2b688` to deep brown `#56372a` and red `#a03821`. A few isolated colors are enough to make the gaps feel intentional.

<Plate slug="nadia-lee-cohen" id="10-mrs-fischer-still-life" label="Mrs Fischer still life, from *HELLO My Name Is*, 2021" caption="The name-book ellipse, map-and-puzzle anchor, and clue sequence show biography being designed through selective grouping rather than narration." />

In *Mrs Fischer still life*, a name book, map, puzzle pieces, a bird, and an old image become legible as a system only because the open ground keeps them separate. The overlay connects that sequence without imposing a fictional chronology on it. Mean luminance is 0.683 and edge density 0.040; the warm trio `#e4b787`, `#cea174`, and `#ad6f49` lets darker objects operate as punctuation inside the pale field.

<Plate slug="nadia-lee-cohen" id="12-michael-still-life" label="Michael still life, from *HELLO My Name Is*, 2021" caption="A photo-strip anchor, baseball-cap oval, and cross-frame object sequence make an oblique portrait from separate commercial clues." />

The final still life retains that disciplined incompleteness. Its thirds grid keeps the spread from collapsing to the center; a photo-strip anchor at x0.1935, y0.4766, baseball-cap oval, and object sequence describe how the viewer travels across the field. Its bright mean of 0.673, p05–p95 range of 0.259–0.816, and low 0.028 edge density leave the cap, magazine, egg, and label-like objects suspended in enough air to become prompts rather than answers.

## Exercises

<ExerciseCard n="01" title="Build a character from a small visual vocabulary">
Use one plain background, one costume or color system, one prop, and a fixed pose. Remove everything else. Make the picture work from the relation between the figure’s silhouette and the empty field, not from added decoration.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="02" title="Make a still life act like a portrait">
Arrange six to ten objects that could imply a person without showing one. Leave a distinct gap around each important clue. Then move one object at a time until the eye can travel through the arrangement without needing a caption.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="03" title="Let a badge become a compositional anchor">
Photograph a subject with a small patch, label, name badge, or logo. Keep it readable but do not center it by default. Use posture, costume, and background to decide whether that small signal confirms the figure or quietly unsettles it.
</ExerciseCard>
