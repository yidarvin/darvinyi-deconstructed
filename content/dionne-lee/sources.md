# Dionne Lee — source shortlist

All twelve selected works are contemporary, copyrighted works. No source page
asserts an open licence, so every acquisition below is documented as
`fair-use`. The copies are direct, publicly served display files; none required
authentication, payment, a restricted endpoint, or a screen capture. The
rights notice for the Aperture and Strange Fire displays is © Dionne Lee; NOMA
identifies the works as “Courtesy of the artist.”

For each item, this project makes a good-faith, image-specific U.S. fair-use
assessment: (1) the use is transformative criticism, scholarship, and
composition teaching rather than decoration; (2) the underlying work is a
published, highly creative photograph/collage; (3) the complete frame is used
only where it is necessary to teach its spatial structure, in a web-sized copy;
and (4) the chapter credits and links to the public record below and cannot
substitute for the original, a museum record, or a licensed reproduction.

1. **Breaking Wave** (2018) — [Aperture interview and display image](https://aperture.org/editorial/dionne-lee-is-reinventing-the-landscape-photograph/). The torn and rephotographed wave makes the seam and the two hands a lesson in how a split can redirect an image’s movement. **Use basis: fair-use.** © Dionne Lee; published by Aperture. The complete vertical frame is needed to analyze the hand-to-wave interruption under the four-factor assessment above.

2. **Untitled (Wound)** (2020) — [Aperture interview and display image](https://aperture.org/editorial/dionne-lee-is-reinventing-the-landscape-photograph/). Its scar-like central break turns a photographic surface into a compositional fault line. **Use basis: fair-use.** © Dionne Lee; published by Aperture. The whole frame, at web size, is necessary to compare the rupture with the surrounding tonal field under the four-factor assessment above.

3. **True North** (2019) — [Aperture interview and display image](https://aperture.org/editorial/dionne-lee-is-reinventing-the-landscape-photograph/); [MoMA collection record](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/407676). The raised hands function as a bodily compass, locating a directional gesture inside a fragmented field. **Use basis: fair-use.** © Dionne Lee; Aperture display and MoMA record. The complete frame is required to study the alignment from arms to sky-facing hands under the four-factor assessment above.

4. **Untitled Rock Drawing III** (2023) — [Aperture interview and display image](https://aperture.org/editorial/dionne-lee-is-reinventing-the-landscape-photograph/). A drawn spiral makes the rock’s surface act as both ground and diagram, concentrating attention inward. **Use basis: fair-use.** © Dionne Lee; published by Aperture. The entire image is needed to teach the relation between the enclosing stone and its central mark under the four-factor assessment above.

5. **Fire Bed** (2019) — [Aperture interview and display image](https://aperture.org/editorial/dionne-lee-is-reinventing-the-landscape-photograph/); [NOMA discussion](https://noma.org/photographs-by-dionne-lee/). The body and constructed fire site place survival knowledge at the picture’s center. **Use basis: fair-use.** © Dionne Lee; Aperture display, with NOMA crediting it “Courtesy of the artist.” The complete frame is necessary to analyze the body-to-ground geometry under the four-factor assessment above.

6. **trespass is the most beautiful word** (2018) — [Aperture interview and display image](https://aperture.org/editorial/dionne-lee-is-reinventing-the-landscape-photograph/). This work makes a charged textual/photographic encounter useful for studying how a phrase changes the reading of a landscape. **Use basis: fair-use.** © Dionne Lee; published by Aperture. The complete frame is necessary to analyze the text’s placement within the photograph under the four-factor assessment above.

7. **a plot that also grounds** (2016) — [Aperture interview and display image](https://aperture.org/editorial/dionne-lee-is-reinventing-the-landscape-photograph/). The altered circular patch in the found landscape turns subtraction into a focused exercise in figure, ground, and land claim. **Use basis: fair-use.** © Dionne Lee; published by Aperture. The complete frame is needed to understand the circle against its surrounding field under the four-factor assessment above.

8. **Lapse** (2019) — [Aperture interview and display image](https://aperture.org/editorial/dionne-lee-is-reinventing-the-landscape-photograph/). The work is a key example of Lee’s layered darkroom construction, in which image fragments make time and surface visible. **Use basis: fair-use.** © Dionne Lee; published by Aperture. The full composition is required to teach the relation among its layered elements under the four-factor assessment above.

9. **Contact (A Muscle Memory)** (2020) — [Aperture interview and display image](https://aperture.org/editorial/dionne-lee-is-reinventing-the-landscape-photograph/). The title and image foreground touch as an organizing photographic gesture rather than a merely depicted subject. **Use basis: fair-use.** © Dionne Lee; published by Aperture. The whole web-sized frame is needed to analyze contact and surrounding negative space under the four-factor assessment above.

10. **Netting** (2019) — [New Orleans Museum of Art source page and display image](https://noma.org/photographs-by-dionne-lee/). NOMA identifies an inverted lean-to shelter at the center, while graphite and the woven pattern push its structure beyond the photograph’s edge. **Use basis: fair-use.** Courtesy of the artist, per NOMA. The complete frame is necessary to teach inversion, extension, and the shelter’s central placement under the four-factor assessment above.

11. **A Test for 40 Acres** (2016) — [New Orleans Museum of Art source page and display image](https://noma.org/photographs-by-dionne-lee/). The mylar blanket’s large, reflective square makes an aerial field that directly joins land, scale, and the unfulfilled Reconstruction promise. **Use basis: fair-use.** Courtesy of the artist, per NOMA. The whole frame is required to analyze the square’s proportion and relationship to the hillside under the four-factor assessment above. This direct source is 720 × 576 px; it is above the normal 640 px long-edge floor and remains compositionally legible.

12. **Broken Signal** (2019) — [Strange Fire artist Q&A and display image](https://www.strangefirecollective.com/qa-dionne-lee). The darkroom-made disruption is a useful study in how marks interrupt photographic continuity and produce directional tension. **Use basis: fair-use.** © Dionne Lee; displayed in the Strange Fire Q&A. The complete frame is necessary to analyze its interruption and tonal structure under the four-factor assessment above.
