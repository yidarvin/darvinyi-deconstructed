# Dionne Lee — research note

## Documented record

This chapter considers twelve public display copies of works made from 2016 to
2023. Lee is an American artist born in 1988. The source packet retains the
title, date, display link, credit, and image-specific fair-use assessment for
each work in [sources.md](sources.md).

The documented process is materially varied. MoMA identifies *True North*
(2019) as a collage of gelatin silver prints with graphite. Its collection text
also identifies scanning, solarization, and graphite scrawling across print
surfaces as processes Lee experiments with. The New Orleans Museum of Art
identifies *Fire Bed* as a gelatin silver print; *Netting* as a gelatin silver
print with graphite; *A Test for 40 Acres* as a pigmented ink print; and *a
plot that also grounds* as an archival inkjet print made by manipulating and
re-photographing found landscape material. NOMA describes Lee's survival-skills
practice and records that *Fire Bed* followed a fire-making class. It also
records the stitched mylar survival blankets used to make *A Test for 40 Acres*.

The artist's accounts support a reading of collage, photography, drawing, and
fieldwork as connected but distinct methods. In a 2026 Aperture interview, Lee
describes beginning collage by collecting physical things and making a picture
from the pieces; she describes photographing more selectively and making
impermanent rock drawings with water. In the same interview she says she has not
used a large-format camera. A 2020 Strange Fire interview records her discussion
of darkroom double-exposure “stutters” and names *True North* and *Broken
Signal* as gelatin silver prints. These are process and medium records, not
grounds for assigning one process to every plate.

## Technical limits

No source documents a camera body, lens, focal length, aperture, shutter speed,
ISO, film stock, sensor, lighting setup, or exposure workflow for the selected
frames. The ingested display JPEGs carry no capture EXIF. The chapter therefore
describes documented print and collage media, plus visible framing, surface,
tonal range, palette, and contour; it does not infer camera settings from the
look of a picture.

## Analysis limits

The deterministic analyzer found a very dark, low-key *Breaking Wave* (mean
luminance 0.063; palette beginning #010206, #16171b) and a relatively sparse,
pale *Netting* (mean 0.655; edge density 0.029). It found especially broad tonal
spreads in *Untitled (Wound)* (p05 0.039, p95 1.0) and *A Test for 40 Acres*
(p05 0.024, p95 0.969). Those measurements describe the supplied web copies,
not original prints.

Several automatic proposals mistook collage seams, graphite traces, repeated
eyes, or tonal changes for literal horizons and perspective systems. The final
overlays retain only visually verified claims: held fragments, hands, a drawn
spiral, a prepared ground patch, a central photographic insert, repetition,
and the mylar sheet. They are compositional readings, not claims that Lee used
a particular camera geometry or intended a single symbolic meaning.
