# Corinne Vionnet — research note

## Scope and documented process

The artist portfolio identifies *Photo Opportunities* as a project begun in 2005, with its longer project account dating the body of work 2005–2014. Vionnet finds and layers numerous tourist photographs of celebrated sites, turning the recurrence of a familiar viewpoint into the material of the final image. The twelve supplied display JPEGs are artist-portfolio renditions; their descriptive landmark names, source routes, credits, and per-image fair-use reasoning are preserved in [sources.md](sources.md).

The photographs in this chapter are therefore not single exposures of a site. They are composites made from crowdsourced tourist views. The chapter discusses the visible alignment, blur, and repeated framing of those finished images, not undocumented decisions made in the contributing snapshots.

## Technique, material, and settings

The consulted artist portfolio documents the collecting-and-layering method but does not state a camera body, lens, focal length, aperture, shutter speed, ISO, film stock, sensor format, paper, print process, edition, or individual workflow settings for these plates. The ingested JPEGs contain no usable capture EXIF. No technical setting is inferred here.

The rendering record does establish that every supplied plate is an unaltered 800-pixel-long-edge public display JPEG. Its analyzer values—geometry, luminance, palette, and edge density—describe that web rendition only. They do not establish the characteristics of an exhibition print or of any individual contributing image.

## Era and access limits

*Photo Opportunities* belongs to the networked-image era: its subject is both the landmark and the habitual, widely shared view of it. The source set is composed of contemporary copyrighted works by Corinne Vionnet. Each complete but web-sized frame is reproduced under the documented, image-specific fair-use rationale for criticism, scholarship, and composition teaching; the chapter credits and links the artist portfolio and is not a substitute for the work, monograph, or a licensed reproduction.
