# Kate Steciw — source shortlist

This twelve-work set follows Kate Steciw from the early photograph-object experiment *The Strangeness of This Idea* through the hanging *Construction* works and recent canvas collages. It concentrates on the compositional question that recurs throughout the practice: how a screen-born image is made to behave as a physical, discontinuous object. Eleven files were retrieved from Steciw’s publicly accessible portfolio, which supplies the captions, dates, media, and full-frame display images. *Armchair* was retrieved from the International Center of Photography’s public exhibition record, which identifies the work, date, copyright, and installation photographer. Every saved file opens successfully and is at least 629px on the long edge; the sole 629px short edge is immaterial because the required measure is the long edge (800px).

**Rights basis:** **fair-use** for every selection. These are copyrighted contemporary works, and neither the artist portfolio nor ICP’s exhibition page grants an open licence. Each assessment applies all four factors individually: (1) this nonprofit book uses the image transformatively for criticism, scholarship, and composition teaching; (2) the work is highly creative; (3) the complete published frame is necessary to analyze the work’s framing, cuts, overlays, material supports, and spatial cues, rather than merely illustrating it; and (4) the credited web-sized copy links to the artist or museum record and does not replace the original, a gallery record, or a licensed reproduction. The direct image URLs below are public display responses only; they are not asserted to be permissioned production files.

## Research context

ICP included Steciw in *What Is a Photograph?* (2014), an exhibition that frames the contemporary medium through experiments with light, color, composition, materiality, subject, and digital/analog hybrid forms: https://www.icp.org/exhibitions/what-is-a-photograph. Galerie Christophe Gaillard describes the *Construction* series as works that construct and render art from essentially immaterial digital images, with image cut-outs, overlays, and physical extensions emphasizing the gap between image and object: https://galeriegaillard.com/en/artists/8232-kate-steciw/overview/. Higher Pictures likewise identifies the stock image as the formal point of departure and notes the artist’s cutting, layering, building, and tinted-Plexiglas interventions: https://higherpictures.com/press/kate-steciw/.
