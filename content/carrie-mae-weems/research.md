# Carrie Mae Weems — research note

## What the records support

*Kitchen Table Series* was made in 1990. The National Gallery of Art describes it as a staged fictional drama in which Carrie Mae Weems performs the central role; a small room, kitchen table, and single hanging lamp stay fixed while lovers, friends, and a daughter alter the situation. The National Gallery's 2003 portfolio comprises twenty platinum/palladium prints and fourteen screenprints/text panels, so the twelve photographs selected here are a study set rather than the whole work. [National Gallery of Art](https://www.nga.gov/artworks/209288-kitchen-table-series)

The individual print record matters. Cleveland identifies its 1990 *Untitled (Man Smoking)* as a platinum print, while MoMA identifies its *Untitled (Man Smoking)* and a 2010 printing of *Untitled (Woman and Daughter with Makeup)* as gelatin silver prints. These holdings do not justify assigning one print process to every image or every realization of the series. [Cleveland Museum of Art](https://www.clevelandart.org/art/2008.116.2) · [MoMA: *Man Smoking*](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/51324) · [MoMA: *Woman and Daughter with Makeup*](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/146596)

## Visual method

The square frame is a repeatable stage. A receding tabletop makes a physical foreground; the lamp concentrates the upper field; a door, wall picture, chairs, cage, mirror, bottles, cards, and people can be redistributed inside this same grammar. That recurrence makes a change of body orientation or prop legible as a change in relation rather than a new setting. The series is staged, but its visual account is not reducible to plot: the photographs show how a domestic scene can allocate attention, access, distance, and self-presentation.

The ingested public-display files are grayscale and preserve no EXIF. The selected institutional and artist sources do not provide a camera body, lens, film stock, focal length, aperture, shutter speed, ISO, exposure, or lighting setup for these plates. MoMA associates *Man Smoking* with a medium-format camera, but that does not establish settings or authorize a generalization to the whole selection. This chapter therefore discusses visible staging, framing, and analyzer-measured tonal structure only.
