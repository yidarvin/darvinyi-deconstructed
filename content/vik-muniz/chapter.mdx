Vik Muniz makes photographs that ask the eye to change scale. From a distance, wire,
sugar, chocolate, thread, food, or refuse resolves into a figure, a portrait, a
canonical picture, or a landscape. Up close, the apparent image gives way to the
fact of its construction. These overlays name visible structure while the source
records identify the materials and series. [Source notes](../../content/vik-muniz/sources.md)

## recognition before material

<Plate slug="vik-muniz" id="01-wind" label="Wind, 1995" caption="A wire form occupies a near-empty field; its small central gesture is held above a low tonal break." />

In *Wind*, the wire form makes the surrounding pale paper active rather than blank.
The anchor catches its central crossing, the gesture follows the right-hand upright,
and y0.594 marks a low change in the almost continuous ground. Mean luminance is
0.954 and edge density 0.005; #f3f4f3 and #b7bab8 explain why so few dark marks can
hold the frame.

<Plate slug="vik-muniz" id="02-jacinthe-loves-orange-juice" label="Jacinthe Loves Orange Juice, from The Sugar Children, 1996" caption="A nearly frontal face and a close vertical axis let granular sugar cohere into a direct portrait." />

*Jacinthe Loves Orange Juice* depends on a frontal read. The portrait axis at x0.475
passes through the face, while the ellipse limits the claim to the visible granular
head. The range from p05 0.227 to p95 0.906 and the sequence from #31312c to
#dddfdb turn deposits into eyes, mouth, and hair without letting sugar disappear.

<Plate slug="vik-muniz" id="03-11-eggs" label="11 Eggs, 1997" caption="A compact stack of pale ovals interrupts a dark, debris-like field and turns repetition into a concentrated cluster." />

In *11 Eggs*, the ellipse and short polyline isolate the pile rather than inventing
perspective around it. The vertical midline gives the small arrangement a stable
field, but repeated ovals touching and overlapping supply the pressure. Edge density
is 0.248, with p05 0.004 and p95 0.918: bright eggs become rhythm against #080806,
#292924, and broken gray detail.

<Plate slug="vik-muniz" id="04-self-portrait" label="Self-Portrait, 1997" caption="A face resolves from a dense mark field through a near-center axis, an eye anchor, and an enclosing head-and-shoulders oval." />

The overlay stays with visible geometry: the axis at x0.488 and eye anchor at x0.416,
y0.242 make the face legible before one inventories its fragments. This is the
set's highest edge density, 0.329; #131313 through #bbbbbb makes the likeness feel
assembled from competing marks rather than smoothed into continuous tone.

## quotation and hard materials

<Plate slug="vik-muniz" id="05-double-elvis" label="Double Elvis, 1999" caption="A centered Elvis figure, an enclosing oval, and a diagonal through guitar and arm turn chocolate syrup into a high-contrast quotation." />

The Art Institute record identifies *Double Elvis* as Muniz's chocolate-syrup
reworking of Warhol. The central axis and large figure ellipse make the borrowed icon
read immediately; the guitar-and-arm diagonal prevents the stance from becoming a
flat emblem. Its p05–p95 span is 0.114–0.937, with #212428 and #e6e6e4 giving brown
syrup contours graphic force.

<Plate slug="vik-muniz" id="06-pillow-ii-after-durer" label="Pillow II (after Dürer), 1999" caption="A diagonal fold contour and a low edge break make hard nailed marks describe a soft, reclining volume." />

*Pillow II (after Dürer)* turns contour into contradiction. The diagonal fold enters
the dense right-hand crossing, while the low edge break stabilizes the floating form.
Mean luminance 0.895 and the #4d4d4d, #787878, #f1f1f1 palette let clusters of
documented nails describe compression and softness without ceasing to look hard.

<Plate slug="vik-muniz" id="07-11000-yards-helmingham-dell" label="11,000 Yards, The Helmingham Dell, after Constable, from Pictures of Thread, 1999" caption="A low receding route and a tree-mass contour direct the eye through a thread-built landscape." />

A leading bank rises from the lower-left toward the measured vanishing point at
x0.511, y0.656; the tree-mass contour interrupts that recession. Edge density 0.160
keeps the strand-like surface present, while p95 0.965 opens a luminous exit among
#403934, #5d514b, #8a7e79, and #f1eeec. The quoted landscape reads as atmosphere
and as a route made of thread.

## figures made of matter

<Plate slug="vik-muniz" id="08-medusa-marinara" label="Medusa Marinara, 1997" caption="A plate-sized oval, a central mask axis, and a sauce crown hold the spaghetti-and-sauce Medusa in frontal symmetry." />

*Medusa Marinara* makes a mask out of a dinner plate. Its x0.5 axis and enclosing
ellipse state the frontal structure; the crown names the upper sauce pattern rather
than mistaking it for depth. #7c1d0d and #b1551d against #cfd0d1 and #070404 turn
edible disorder into a readable art-historical head.

<Plate slug="vik-muniz" id="09-pictures-of-garbage-the-gipsy-magna" label="Pictures of Garbage: The Gipsy (Magna), 2008" caption="Face, dress silhouette, and a tilted material break let a monumental figure emerge from a surrounding field of refuse." />

In *The Gipsy (Magna)*, the face anchor at x0.549, y0.336 is the switch that makes
the surrounding material read as a person. The polyline follows the dress, while the
tilted break at y0.561 separates its large mass from the lower field. At edge density
0.068, the #5e382d-to-#eae1db palette coheres through value and contour rather than
a high-frequency description of every object.

<Plate slug="vik-muniz" id="10-pictures-of-garbage-mother-and-children" label="Pictures of Garbage: Mother and Children (Suellen), 2008" caption="Crossed diagonals connect the mother's face to the children and surrounding refuse without reducing the group to a single silhouette." />

*Mother and Children (Suellen)* is a structure of care rather than a single
silhouette. The mother's face is anchored at x0.520, y0.258; opposing diagonals
connect children, arms, and refuse without collapsing their separate faces. Edge
density 0.165 and #48231d, #774b3a, #a5795e, #d6b496, #f5e1c5 preserve both dense
surround and pale portrait field. Distance makes the family legible; closer looking
makes the physical residue and labor of reconstruction impossible to ignore.

## Exercises

<ExerciseCard n="01" title="Make recognition arrive late">
Arrange thread, receipts, leaves, food, or packaging into a simple figure that
resolves only from a chosen distance. Keep the photograph where the material remains
visible after the figure appears.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="02" title="Build a portrait with an axis">
Make a frontal portrait from objects rather than continuous shading. Place eyes, nose,
and mouth so a near-center vertical axis helps them read together, then shift the
axis and compare how direct the address remains.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="03" title="Let material make the composition">
Choose a silhouette and construct it from objects with directional character. Use one
diagonal for a limb, edge, or route and a contrasting field around it. Check whether
the material supports the composition before it becomes texture alone.
</ExerciseCard>
