# Vik Muniz — source shortlist

Muniz makes temporary drawings, arrangements, and reconstructions from materials whose physical scale contradicts the image seen from a distance, then photographs the result. This working set follows that proposition through sugar, wire, chocolate, thread, photographic quotation, and the landfill portraits of *Pictures of Garbage*. All ten files were retrieved as intact, publicly served display JPEGs and opened successfully. The Art Institute of Chicago IIIF files are 843px on their long edge; the two Google Arts & Culture / MAC Lima files are 1200px high. Both exceed the project's 640px quality floor while remaining unmistakably web-display, not production, reproductions.

**Rights basis:** **fair-use** for every selection. The Art Institute API marks its eight records `is_public_domain: false`, names Vik Muniz as artist, and supplies no further copyright notice; Google Arts & Culture credits the two 2008 works to Vik Muniz / MAC Lima. Each individual assessment below uses a credited, record-linked, web-sized complete frame for nonprofit transformative criticism, scholarship, and composition teaching. The complete frame is needed to analyze the supplied material's shift into an image; the copies do not replace the artist's work, a collection record, or a licensed reproduction.

## Research context

The artist's gallery chronology places *Sugar Children* (1996), *Pictures of Chocolate* (1997), *Pictures of Thread* (1999), and *Pictures of Garbage* (2008) as distinct series: https://vikmuniz.net/gallery. The Art Institute's *Double Elvis* record explains the chocolate-syrup method and its reworking of Warhol, while the Mint Museum identifies *Marat (Sebastião)* in *Pictures of Garbage* as modeled after David's *Death of Marat*: https://www.artic.edu/artworks/154279/double-elvis and https://vikmuniz.net/gallery/mint-museum. This selection retains complete, public display frames so the chapter can teach the central perceptual transition instead of reproducing cropped details.
