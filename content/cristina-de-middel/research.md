# Cristina de Middel — research notes

## Project and era

Cristina de Middel’s *The Afronauts* (2012) is a staged photographic project that reimagines the unsuccessful Zambian space programme of the 1960s. The International Center of Photography describes de Middel’s practice as moving between documentary and conceptual modes, and identifies the series as staged reenactments that challenge familiar depictions of Africa. [ICP profile](https://www.icp.org/users/cristinademiddel)

In her account of the project, de Middel describes researching the programme, drawing on the look of 1960s family albums, and making some photographs at a dump outside Madrid. She also describes borrowing a Russian spacesuit. Those are series-level details, not verified production facts for every plate in this chapter. [The Guardian interview](https://www.theguardian.com/artanddesign/2014/jun/11/my-best-shot-cristina-de-middel-zambia-space-programme)

## Prints and the selected works

Candela Books + Gallery identifies *Umfundi*, *Butungakuna*, *Hamba*, *Yinqaba*, and *Bongo* as 2012 works from *The Afronauts* and as archival pigment prints. The Guardian identifies *Jambo* as a 2012 *Afronauts* work and credits Cristina De Middel, but the available source does not state its print process. [Candela exhibition](https://www.candelagallery.com/fivestories) · [Guardian caption and interview](https://www.theguardian.com/artanddesign/2014/jun/11/my-best-shot-cristina-de-middel-zambia-space-programme)

## Equipment and settings

The available records do not document a camera body, lens, focal length, aperture, shutter speed, film stock, sensitivity, lighting setup, or exposure setting for these individual photographs. The ingested web files contain no camera EXIF. The square format, muted palette, visible costumes, helmet, model rocket, hose, and settings can be discussed as visible pictorial decisions, but not treated as proof of a particular camera process or setting.

## Source scope

This chapter uses six credited, publicly displayed web renditions under the project’s documented per-image fair-use rationale in [the source shortlist](sources.md): full frames are retained because the composition teaching depends on the relation among figures, props, and settings, and the web-sized copies do not substitute for prints, licensed files, or artist publications.
