# Lucas Blalock — research note

## Scope and sources

This chapter concerns ten 2013–2019 works supplied as credited, public display JPEGs by [Galerie Eva Presenhuber](https://www.presenhuber.com/artists/lucas-blalock). The gallery describes Blalock's deliberately awkward, jury-rigged images as constructed with software whose clone stamping and irregular masking are normally hidden. Its individual records supply the titles, dates, media, dimensions, edition information where applicable, and copyright notice. [MoMA's collection account](https://www.moma.org/explore/inside_out/2016/01/08/new-photography-and-momas-collection-part-1/) independently describes his 2013 *Shoe* and *Right Shoe*: he photographed a tabletop arrangement, then used a computer program to add conspicuous tread-like lines. MoMA characterizes these overt edits as a challenge to photographic purity and an assertion of photography's constructedness.

## Technique and era

Blalock's decisive technique is not invisible correction. In this group, cutout contours, cloned or masked passages, superimposed elements, and a photographically legible still life coexist without being reconciled. The resulting images retain the lighting, shallow stage, and object scale of a studio photograph while inviting the viewer to read the file as an assembled surface. That is consistent with the gallery's description and the MoMA account, but the precise sequence of software operations for each work is not documented in the supplied records.

The selected works run from *Blue Bottles* and *The Guitar Player* (2013) through *Reverse Titanic / Hell is in the Air* and *After Animals 2* (2019). The chronology shows a sustained concern with still-life and portrait conventions rather than evidence of one uniform production recipe.

## Print and camera record

The gallery identifies nine works in this set as archival inkjet prints, typically framed. It identifies *Reverse Titanic / Hell is in the Air* (2019) as a dye sublimation print on aluminum. Those are object and print descriptions, not a specification of the camera capture.

No source consulted here gives a camera body, lens, focal length, aperture, shutter speed, ISO, capture date, or lighting setup for these ten photographs. The ingested public-display files contain no usable EXIF. The chapter therefore makes no camera-setting claims. Its tonal, palette, and geometry values are deterministic measurements of these supplied web renditions, not claims about the original prints.
