# Karl Ohiri — research notes

## Scope and record

Karl Ohiri (born 1983) is represented by three connected source groups. MoMA identifies *The Archive of Becoming* (2015–ongoing) as a reworking of discarded Lagos studio negatives in which chemical deterioration becomes part of the image. Autograph identifies *Equation for Humanity* (2020) as a lockdown project: Ohiri collected, inscribed, and photographed stones to make the proposition `I + U = US`. The six portraits credited to Lagos Studio Archives are circa-1990s studio photographs; the archive's source account describes its preservation of discarded negatives documenting Lagos portraiture from the 1970s onward. Titles, dates, credits, public display links, and image-specific fair-use assessments are recorded in [sources.md](sources.md).

## Method and visual conditions

These groups make an archive active in different ways. *The Archive of Becoming* treats the damaged negative as an image whose stained, yellow-green surface competes with the sitter. *Equation for Humanity* gives a hand-inscribed found stone a spare black field, making scale, placement, and lettering do the work of syntax. The Lagos portraits retain studio backdrops, costume, props, and pose as co-authors of the frame. Statements about arrangement, line, symmetry, palette, tonal range, and edge density in this chapter come from inspection and deterministic analysis of these specific supplied JPEGs; they do not authenticate an original negative or print.

## Technique, printing, and settings: what the record establishes

The source record establishes Ohiri's recontextualisation of deteriorated negatives and his collecting, inscribing, and photographing stones. It does not document camera body, lens, focal length, aperture, shutter speed, ISO, lighting setup, capture format, editing workflow, paper, print process, edition, or the original photographers of the Lagos Studio Archive portraits. The normalized manifest retains no EXIF settings for any supplied file. This chapter therefore makes no claims about camera settings or printing processes, and does not infer them from the JPEGs.
