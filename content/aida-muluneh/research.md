# Aïda Muluneh — research note

## What the source record establishes

This chapter studies ten credited public display JPEGs from Aïda Muluneh’s 2018 *Water Life* and 2016 *The World is 9*. The artist archive identifies titles and dates. Its *Water Life* statement connects the commission to water access, women’s liberation, health, sanitation, education, and production in Dallol, Afar, Ethiopia. The source record describes *The World is 9* through African body art, time, space, life, love, and history. These materials support discussing staged symbolic tableaux; per-image credits and fair-use rationales are in [sources.md](sources.md).

## Visual method and era

Made in 2016 and 2018, these are contemporary color photographs whose visible method is deliberate staging: painted faces and bodies, saturated red, blue, and yellow clothing or props, expansive ground, and isolated figures. The sources support this description of the finished photographs and stated themes. They do not identify precise lighting, set-building, retouching, compositing, or post-production procedures for individual plates, so none is inferred here.

## Printing and camera settings

The artist archive and supplied display JPEGs do not document a print process, paper, edition, printer, camera body, lens, focal length, aperture, shutter speed, ISO, or lighting setup. The normalized web files contain no capture EXIF. The chapter therefore does not attribute a camera or printing method to the series.

## Evidence limits

The tonal values, edge densities, palette swatches, and geometric detections used here measure the web-display JPEGs; they do not establish the technical conditions of the original artworks. Credits, titles, dates, source links, and the per-image fair-use rationale remain in [sources.md](sources.md).
