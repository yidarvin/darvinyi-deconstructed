# Boris Mikhailov — research note

## Scope and sources

Eleven image records are credited to Boris Mikhailov through the Kharkiv School
of Photography (KSP) project pages, which provide the series titles and date
spans used in this chapter. A twelfth supplied file is instead a credited
picture-alliance/dpa installation photograph, discussed only as that documentary
view. KSP's artist overview places Mikhailov within the Kharkiv School and
documents the progression represented here: *Red Series*, *Overlays*, *Luriki*,
*Unfinished Dissertation*, *I Am Not I*, *At Dusk*, *Case History*, *Tea Coffee
Cappuccino*, *Industrial Zone*, and *Temptation of Death*. MoMA describes *Case
History* (1997–98) as an extended project concerning people made homeless in
post-Soviet Kharkiv; that context matters for the chapter's discussion of direct
address without resolving the photographs into simple evidence. See
[sources.md](sources.md) for the exact records, credits, and fair-use rationales.

## Method and material evidence

The supplied records substantiate several visibly different working methods:
serial color emphasis in *Red Series* (1965–78); superimposition in *Overlays*
(1968–81); hand coloring in *Luriki* (1971–85); self-performance in *I Am Not
I* (1992); and chemically toned black-and-white images using color-toning stains
and defects in *At Dusk* (1993); and late diptychs in *Temptation of Death*
(2019), combining crematorium and pre-existing-image fields. They do not document a camera body, lens,
focal length, aperture, shutter speed, film stock, negative format, lighting
setup, or original printing process for these individual reproductions. The
normalized display files contain no capture EXIF. This chapter therefore reads
visible framing, sequence, color, tone, surface, and the measured overlay
geometry, and does not infer historical settings from web files.

One supplied file, `06-by-the-ground-01.jpg`, is an installation photograph of
Mikhailov before a wall of photographs, visibly credited `© picture-alliance/dpa`.
It is not a work from *By the Ground*; the corrected source note records its
documentary provenance, and the chapter analyzes only what this supplied
reproduction visibly contains.
