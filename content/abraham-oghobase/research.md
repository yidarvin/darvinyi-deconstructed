# Abraham Oghobase — research note

## Documented record

Abraham Oghobase (born 1979) is a Nigerian artist whose photography, performance, and image construction address the social, historical, and economic geography of place. The retained source record for this chapter places the 2018 *Anatomy of Landscape – Jos* photographs on the Jos Plateau and identifies their relation to the residual landscape of British colonial tin mining. KADIST identifies the four selected Jos works; MoMA places Oghobase in *New Photography 2023* and records later work in *Constructed Realities* (2019–22). The Art Institute of Chicago describes the related 2018 *Rock Anatomy* as layering mineral specimens onto self-portraits.

The available record does not establish a print process for the four retained Jos plates. It also does not establish a single process for the group merely from their shared concerns with landscape, body, archival material, and extraction.

## Camera and settings limits

No collection or artist record supplied for these four works verifies a camera body, lens, focal length, aperture, shutter speed, ISO, lighting setup, capture format, or post-production workflow. Three supplied web JPEGs—*Anatomy of Landscape – Jos 16*, *Jos 21*, and *Jos 25*—retain embedded file metadata naming a Canon EOS 550D and EF24–70mm f/2.8L USM lens, with dates in December 2016 and respectively 35mm at f/8, 1/640s, ISO 100; 70mm at f/8, 1/320s, ISO 100; and 50mm at f/8, 1/1000s, ISO 200. This metadata is retained in [`manifest.json`](manifest.json), but it is not corroborated by the artist or a collection record as production history for the finished 2018 works. It therefore does not justify a general claim about the series, its lighting, or the remaining plate. The first supplied file contains no capture EXIF.

## Analysis limits

The normalized KADIST web renditions are airy, shallow landscape fields with local interruptions of tree, ruin, figure, rail, and industrial distance. The analyzer measured *Anatomy of Landscape – Jos 16* as the most edge-dense retained view (0.096), while *Jos 13* has the lightest mean luminance (0.618). These are measurements of supplied web-sized copies, not of an original print or an exposure.

The analyzer sometimes proposes horizons, symmetry axes, or saliency points that are only tonal or incidental artifacts. Final overlays retain only claims visibly grounded in the photographs: tree and ridge, ruin wall and aperture, the ledge supporting a reclining figure, and rails with industrial distance. They do not treat automatic detections as claims about Oghobase's intention.
