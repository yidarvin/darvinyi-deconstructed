# Abraham Oghobase — research note

## Documented record

Abraham Oghobase (born 1979) is a Nigerian artist whose photography, performance,
and image construction address the social, historical, and economic geography of
place. The source record for this chapter places the 2018 *Anatomy of Landscape –
Jos* photographs on the Jos Plateau and identifies their relation to the residual
landscape of British colonial tin mining. KADIST identifies the four selected Jos
works; MoMA places Oghobase in *New Photography 2023* and records later work in
*Constructed Realities* (2019–22). The Art Institute of Chicago describes the
related 2018 *Rock Anatomy* as layering mineral specimens onto self-portraits.

The selected photographs span 2018–19. The *Alloiosis* works are identified in
the source packet as inkjet photographs. The record does not establish a print
process for the selected Jos, *Metallurgical Practice*, or *Colonial Self-Portrait*
plates; it also does not establish a single process for the group merely from
their shared concerns with landscape, body, archival material, and extraction.

## Camera and settings limits

No collection or artist record supplied for these eleven works verifies a camera
body, lens, focal length, aperture, shutter speed, ISO, lighting setup, capture
format, or post-production workflow. Three supplied web JPEGs—*Anatomy of
Landscape – Jos 16*, *Jos 21*, and *Jos 25*—retain embedded file metadata naming
a Canon EOS 550D and EF24–70mm f/2.8L USM lens, with dates in December 2016 and
respectively 35mm at f/8, 1/640s, ISO 100; 70mm at f/8, 1/320s, ISO 100; and
50mm at f/8, 1/1000s, ISO 200. This metadata is retained in
[`manifest.json`](manifest.json), but it is not corroborated by the artist or a
collection record as production history for the finished 2018 works. It therefore
does not justify a general claim about the series, its lighting, or the other
eight plates. The remaining supplied files contain no capture EXIF.

## Analysis limits

The normalized web renditions move between airy landscape, dense technical
superimposition, and a low-key archival portrait. The analyzer measured
*Anatomy of Landscape – Jos 16* as the most edge-dense Jos view (0.096) and
*Metallurgical Practice: Landscape 04* as exceptionally sparse (0.015), while
the three *Alloiosis* files remain dark and materially dense (mean luminance
0.187–0.300; edge density 0.079–0.111). These are measurements of supplied
web-sized copies, not of an original print or an exposure.

The analyzer sometimes proposed horizons, symmetry axes, or saliency points that
were only tonal or technical-drawing artifacts. Final overlays retain claims that
remain visibly defensible: tree and ridge, ruin wall and aperture, rails and
industrial distance, printed architecture, the layered landscape/drawing field,
and frontal portrait structure. They do not treat automatic detections as claims
about Oghobase's intention.
