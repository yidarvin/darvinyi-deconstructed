# Abraham Oghobase: landscape as a measuring device

Abraham Oghobase’s photographs do not offer the Jos Plateau as a neutral view. Across these 2018–19 images, a tree, ruin, rail track, body, technical drawing, or archival portrait turns land and history into things that can be measured against one another. The source record connects the Jos works to the residues of British colonial tin mining; the overlays keep that history grounded in visible structure. The supplied web copies are used for criticism and composition teaching under the individual fair-use rationales in the [source notes](../../content/abraham-oghobase/sources.md).

## a place held open

<Plate slug="abraham-oghobase" id="01-anatomy-of-landscape-jos-13" label="Anatomy of Landscape – Jos 13, 2018" caption="A solitary tree rises from a low ridge, making the sky an active field rather than empty background." />

The ridge is held at y0.883, almost at the bottom edge, and the tree trunk begins at the measured anchor before dividing upward. That small base is why the broad sky does not dissolve into blankness: it measures the tree’s precarious vertical claim. A mean luminance of 0.618 and muted blue-gray and earth palette, from #a9adb5 through #393416, make the open field light but not empty.

<Plate slug="abraham-oghobase" id="02-anatomy-of-landscape-jos-16" label="Anatomy of Landscape – Jos 16, 2018" caption="A ruined wall rises through the frame, turning a derelict building into a long directional event." />

Here the wall is the dominant action. Its broken top and window aperture make a shallow diagonal, while the low terrain edge prevents the ruin from becoming a silhouette detached from the hillside. The overlay names an actual opening rather than turning it into a symbolic frame. With edge density 0.096, the busiest of the Jos group, the close grayscale rendition lets cracks, stone, wire, and the broken contour keep the image materially alert.

<Plate slug="abraham-oghobase" id="03-anatomy-of-landscape-jos-21" label="Anatomy of Landscape – Jos 21, 2018" caption="A sign, shuttered opening, and reclining figure are held apart across a long wall." />

The *JOS* sign and resting figure never resolve into a single anecdote. The shuttered opening establishes a hard left-hand block; the raised ledge carries the eye through the wall to the white-clad figure at right. The analyzer finds a p05–p95 span of 0.145–0.835, which fits the pressure between yellow masonry, dark base course, and the abrupt pale body.

<Plate slug="abraham-oghobase" id="04-anatomy-of-landscape-jos-25" label="Anatomy of Landscape – Jos 25, 2018" caption="Rail tracks lead from rusted vessels toward sheds and a distant tree line." />

The track begins near the lower edge and rises toward the station-like middle distance; the shed roof counters that route, while rusted vessels interrupt it before it can read as progress. The p05 of 0.114 and p95 of 0.988 describe the harsh range between white sky and dry foreground. The anchor circles the large rusted tank: it is a physical obstruction, not a proxy for a human subject.

## bodies inside systems

<Plate slug="abraham-oghobase" id="05-alloiosis-08" label="Alloiosis 08, 2019" caption="A seated hooded body interrupts a luminous engineering diagram, making technical drawing and figure share one unstable surface." />

*Alloiosis 08* does not simply place text over portraiture. Its drawn diagonal enters from the upper left and terminates at a visible joint, while the engineering field crosses behind the hood. Mean luminance 0.300, p05 0.106, p95 0.584, and edge density 0.079 describe an orange-and-near-black range in which white technical marks flash without freeing themselves from the body beneath.

<Plate slug="abraham-oghobase" id="06-alloiosis-06" label="Alloiosis 06, 2019" caption="A centered hooded figure breaks the symmetry of a drawn architectural structure." />

The drawing’s roof line gives the central body an architectural shell, but uneven contours and buttons stop the image from becoming a tidy diagram. The overlay’s vertical axis names a visual balance, not literal mirroring. This is the darkest and most edge-dense *Alloiosis* plate here: mean luminance 0.187 and edge density 0.111, with orange lines and hood held against a #251809 ground.

<Plate slug="abraham-oghobase" id="07-alloiosis-07" label="Alloiosis 07, 2019" caption="A hooded silhouette interrupts an overlaid field of pipes, arcs, and handwritten technical notation." />

In *Alloiosis 07*, the crucial event is obstruction. A printed arc rises at left and a drawn baseline stops before the hood, setting a counter-rhythm to the body’s vertical silhouette. The web rendition remains low-key (mean 0.283; p05 0.129; p95 0.490), so drawing and figure do not separate into clean layers; they visibly contaminate one another.

## extraction as an image system

<Plate slug="abraham-oghobase" id="08-metallurgical-practice-landscape-01" label="Metallurgical Practice: Landscape 01, 2019" caption="An outsized machinery drawing occupies the sky above a low horizon and makes the landscape read as an industrial record." />

The machinery drawing is deliberately too large for the place it overlays. Its bounded field takes over the left side while the distant horizon remains at y0.418 beneath it; the small van at right becomes a countermeasure to the diagram’s scale. The frame runs from p05 0.075 to p95 1.000, yet has edge density only 0.033: white sky and dark ground let technical lines exert the pressure.

<Plate slug="abraham-oghobase" id="09-metallurgical-practice-landscape-04" label="Metallurgical Practice: Landscape 04, 2019" caption="A cyanide-plant drawing and specimen panel turn the landscape into a layered technical field." />

The left drawing and right specimen panel are not captions applied after the fact: their unequal rectangles establish the picture’s real balance. The anchor falls within the specimen panel, where cylindrical forms and a circular sample counter the long pipe diagram. Edge density is only 0.015 and mean luminance 0.720, so the sparse pale field makes each black technical mark unusually insistent.

<Plate slug="abraham-oghobase" id="10-metallurgical-practice-landscape-03" label="Metallurgical Practice: Landscape 03, 2019" caption="A radial diagram meets electrical infrastructure, linking a hill, power lines, and a drawn center of distribution." />

The radial drawing turns a utility site into a system of distribution. Its pivot sits just above the equipment, while a real power line carries that center toward the tower at right; the hill ridge remains a low counter-line at y0.672. The palette is essentially grayscale (#343434 through #d3d3d3), and edge density 0.049 keeps towers, wires, and diagram legible as one network rather than separate illustrations.

## the authority of an archival field

<Plate slug="abraham-oghobase" id="11-colonial-self-portrait" label="Colonial Self-Portrait, 2018" caption="A frontal face, decorated uniform, and seated pose put archival authority into an explicitly constructed portrait field." />

The final image tightens the earlier relation between body and system. A face meets the camera within a broad archival portrait field; the uniform waistline supplies a hard lower division, while medals and hands make authority a material construction rather than a neutral costume. Mean luminance is 0.256, with p05 0.031 and p95 0.557; the narrow brown palette, from #1e1004 to #978b7d, keeps surface and insignia present without making historical charge a simple visual effect.

## Exercises

<ExerciseCard n="01" title="Make scale answer to a field">
Photograph one small, distinct form against a large ground: a tree, tank, person, or sign. Leave enough surrounding space to make its scale legible. Then test a second frame in which the ground is reduced; compare what disappears.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="02" title="Let a diagram interrupt a photograph">
Work from a photograph of a site with visible infrastructure. Add a found or self-made technical drawing as a transparent layer. Keep one real edge and one drawn edge legible, but let them disagree about depth.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="03" title="Treat evidence as a counterweight">
Make a two-part frame in which a broad descriptive image is countered by a small object, document, or diagram. Give each part a distinct boundary and adjust their sizes until the smaller part changes how the larger field is read.
</ExerciseCard>
