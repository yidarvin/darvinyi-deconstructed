# Abraham Oghobase: landscape as a measuring device

Abraham Oghobase’s photographs do not offer the Jos Plateau as a neutral view. Across these 2018 images, a tree, ruin, rail track, and body turn land and history into things that can be measured against one another. The source record connects the Jos works to the residues of British colonial tin mining; the overlays keep that history grounded in visible structure. The supplied KADIST web copies are used for criticism and composition teaching under the individual fair-use rationales in the [source notes](../../content/abraham-oghobase/sources.md).

## a place held open

<Plate slug="abraham-oghobase" id="01-anatomy-of-landscape-jos-13" label="Anatomy of Landscape – Jos 13, 2018" caption="A solitary tree rises from a low ridge, making the sky an active field rather than empty background." />

The ridge is held at y0.883, almost at the bottom edge, and the tree trunk begins at the measured anchor before dividing upward. That small base is why the broad sky does not dissolve into blankness: it measures the tree’s precarious vertical claim. A mean luminance of 0.618 and muted blue-gray and earth palette, from #a9adb5 through #393416, make the open field light but not empty.

<Plate slug="abraham-oghobase" id="02-anatomy-of-landscape-jos-16" label="Anatomy of Landscape – Jos 16, 2018" caption="A ruined wall rises through the frame, turning a derelict building into a long directional event." />

Here the wall is the dominant action. Its broken top and window aperture make a shallow diagonal, while the low terrain edge prevents the ruin from becoming a silhouette detached from the hillside. The overlay names an actual opening rather than turning it into a symbolic frame. With edge density 0.096, the busiest of the retained group, the close grayscale rendition lets cracks, stone, wire, and the broken contour keep the image materially alert.

<Plate slug="abraham-oghobase" id="03-anatomy-of-landscape-jos-21" label="Anatomy of Landscape – Jos 21, 2018" caption="A sign, shuttered opening, and reclining figure are held apart across a long wall." />

The *JOS* sign and resting figure never resolve into a single anecdote. The shuttered opening establishes a hard left-hand block; the ledge directly beneath the figure carries the eye across the wall to the white-clad body at right. The analyzer finds a p05–p95 span of 0.145–0.835, which fits the pressure between yellow masonry, dark base course, and the abrupt pale body.

<Plate slug="abraham-oghobase" id="04-anatomy-of-landscape-jos-25" label="Anatomy of Landscape – Jos 25, 2018" caption="Rail tracks lead from rusted vessels toward sheds and a distant tree line." />

The track begins near the lower edge and rises toward the station-like middle distance; the shed roof counters that route, while rusted vessels interrupt it before it can read as progress. The p05 of 0.114 and p95 of 0.988 describe the harsh range between white sky and dry foreground. The anchor circles the large rusted tank: it is a physical obstruction, not a proxy for a human subject.

## Exercises

<ExerciseCard n="01" title="Make scale answer to a field">
Photograph one small, distinct form against a large ground: a tree, tank, person, or sign. Leave enough surrounding space to make its scale legible. Then test a second frame in which the ground is reduced; compare what disappears.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="02" title="Use a built edge to direct attention">
Work at a wall, platform, track, or curb with one figure in the frame. Make one real supporting edge carry the eye toward the figure without making that edge a symbolic substitute for the person.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="03" title="Treat interruption as evidence">
Make a broad landscape frame in which one built form interrupts the route through the image. Adjust your position until the interruption changes how the distant field is read without obscuring it.
</ExerciseCard>
