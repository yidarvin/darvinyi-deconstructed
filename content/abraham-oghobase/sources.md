# Abraham Oghobase — source shortlist

This source set follows Oghobase's sustained study of the Jos Plateau: landscape photographs, bodily interventions, and layered images that make extraction and colonial history legible through the frame. Eleven complete-frame JPEGs were retrieved from the artist's publicly accessible portfolio display and KADIST's public collection page, opened successfully, and retained at their supplied web dimensions (1000–2974px on the long edge). The artist portfolio explicitly identifies its images as “Copyright © All rights reserved”; KADIST credits Abraham Oghobase but does not state an open licence. No public-domain or open-licensed rendition was located, so every selection below is documented as a limited fair-use use.

**Rights basis:** **fair-use** for every selection. Abraham Oghobase is the identified artist/rightsholder; the artist portfolio reserves all rights, and KADIST's collection page credits him. Each assessment is individually limited and transformative: this nonprofit book uses a credited, publicly displayed, web-sized copy for criticism, scholarship, and composition teaching. The complete frame is needed to analyze the particular relationship of body, geological material, horizon, scale, and layered photographic surface; a crop would defeat that visual argument. Each copy links to and credits its public artist or collection record and does not replace the original, a museum record, print, or licensed reproduction.

## Research context

- MoMA's [artist record](https://www.moma.org/artists/134873-abraham-oghobase) identifies eleven online works from *Constructed Realities* (2019–22) and locates Oghobase in *New Photography 2023*.
- KADIST's [collection page](https://kadist.org/people/abraham-oghobase/) identifies the four *Anatomy of Landscape – Jos* works and frames the practice around socio-economic and historical geographies.
- The [Lahore Biennale Foundation exhibition record](https://www.lahorebiennale.org/lb03-artists/abraham-onoriode-oghobase/) describes the Jos works' relation to residual British colonial tin mining and the artist's use of his body within those landscapes.
- The [Art Institute of Chicago record for *Rock Anatomy*](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/251294/rock-anatomy) independently describes the 2018 work's photographic layering of mineral specimens onto self-portraits and the mining history behind it.
