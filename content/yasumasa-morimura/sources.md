# Yasumasa Morimura — source record

## Selection and acquisition note

Morimura's elaborate staged self-portraits are copyrighted contemporary works.
The four-image set below comes from public Art Institute of Chicago collection
records and its public IIIF delivery service. Each downloaded JPEG opens
successfully and has a long edge of 1,070–1,333px; no file has been enlarged,
retouched, or otherwise altered. The deliberately compact group concentrates on
the artist's 1996–97 actress/persona studies and one recent art-historical
recasting. Together they make the chapter's teaching premise concrete: costume,
makeup, backdrop, prop, and pose are compositional apparatuses rather than
transparent portraiture.

All four uses below are individual **fair-use** assessments for criticism,
scholarship, and transformative composition teaching. Each is a creative,
copyrighted photograph (factor two weighs against use). The complete frame is
necessary to analyze its staging and the relation among figure, costume, props,
and background (factor three); a detail would defeat that purpose. The copies
are only the museum's web-sized public renditions, are credited and linked to
the collection record, and do not replace the original, the collection record,
or a licensed reproduction (factor four).

## Acquired shortlist

### 01 — *M Holding a Cigarette* (1997)

- **Why it matters:** The upright, near-frontal figure sits within a deliberately
  exposed black-paper set; the cigarette, hand, and face make a compact diagonal
  relay while the visible support turns theatrical fabrication into part of the
  image's structure.
- **Collection/source:** [Art Institute of Chicago, 198278](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/198278/m-holding-a-cigarette); direct public rendition: `https://www.artic.edu/iiif/2/9be1d66e-3987-5f8e-78cd-6d4f47f44390/full/full/0/default.jpg`.
- **File:** `raw/yasumasa-morimura/01-m-holding-a-cigarette-1997.jpg` (843 × 1092px).
- **Use basis: fair-use.** Creator and record notice: Yasumasa Morimura; © 1997
  Yasumasa Morimura. The complete frame is needed to teach the pictured
  figure/set boundary, pose, and diagonal hand-to-face rhythm; this web-sized,
  credited, linked copy supports transformative analysis rather than a market
  substitute.

### 02 — *M Wearing a Military Cap 2* (1997)

- **Why it matters:** A dark cap and high-contrast face consolidate the top of
  the vertical frame, while the costume's small emphases make identity read as
  an assembled hierarchy of signs rather than a naturalistic likeness.
- **Collection/source:** [Art Institute of Chicago, 198279](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/198279/m-wearing-a-military-cap-2); direct public rendition: `https://www.artic.edu/iiif/2/8017e810-b40b-3b73-c82a-d5aa1fa123f8/full/full/0/default.jpg`.
- **File:** `raw/yasumasa-morimura/02-m-wearing-a-military-cap-2-1997.jpg` (843 × 1070px).
- **Use basis: fair-use.** Creator and record notice: Yasumasa Morimura; © 1997
  Yasumasa Morimura. The complete vertical is required to analyze the cap,
  face, and costume hierarchy; the linked public web rendition is a limited,
  transformative scholarly use and does not replace the artist's work or a
  licensed reproduction.

### 03 — *Self-portrait (Actress) after Greta Garbo 1* (1996)

- **Why it matters:** This full-height persona study uses coiffure, gaze, and
  the narrow portrait format to turn a film-star reference into an exercise in
  silhouette, frontal pressure, and controlled theatrical distance.
- **Collection/source:** [Art Institute of Chicago, 236499](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/236499/self-portrait-actress-after-greta-garbo-1); direct public rendition: `https://www.artic.edu/iiif/2/b95d54bb-2b6d-9476-42b3-dd467bf68679/full/full/0/default.jpg`.

- **Use basis: fair-use.** Creator: Yasumasa Morimura; the collection record
  names the artist but supplies no separate rightsholder notice. The complete
  frame is essential for criticism of the figure's vertical silhouette and
  staged gaze; this small, credited, linked copy is transformative composition
  teaching and does not substitute for the collection object or its licensing
  market.

### 04 — *Mysterious Illusion of KAINOSHO* (2023/24)

- **Why it matters:** The contemporary work extends Morimura's art-historical
  inhabitation into a tall, layered figure image, offering a clear comparison
  between the compact Polaroid persona studies and a more elaborate vertical
  staging of costume, body, and surrounding space.
- **Collection/source:** [Art Institute of Chicago, 273772](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/273772/mysterious-illusion-of-kainosho); direct public rendition: `https://www.artic.edu/iiif/2/fc7ba08d-ec54-47fb-0447-66ed0d1d27d2/full/full/0/default.jpg`.

## Canonical research leads checked

These authoritative catalogue and exhibition records guided the selection and
give the builder a wider 10-work map of the oeuvre. They were not acquired in
this unit because the four direct, public Art Institute renditions already meet
the readiness threshold and form a coherent source set; no access control was
bypassed.

1. *Portrait (Van Gogh)* (1985) — identified by the [National Museum of Art,
   Osaka](https://www.nmao.go.jp/archive/en/exhibition/2016/the_self-portraits_of_yasumasa_morimura_my_art_my_story_my_art_history.html) as the breakthrough early self-portrait.
2. *Portrait (Futago)* (1988/89) — [Mori Art Museum collection record](https://www.mori.art.museum/en/collection/5084/index.html), Morimura's intervention in Manet's *Olympia*.
3. *Daughter of Art History, Theater A* (1989) — [Getty collection record](https://www.getty.edu/art/collection/object/109D6T), the paired *Bar at the Folies-Bergère* re-staging.
4. *Daughter of Art History, Theater B* (1989) — [Getty collection record](https://www.getty.edu/art/collection/object/109D6V), its companion panel.
5. *Portrait of Rembrandt's Mother* (1994) — [SFMOMA artist holdings](https://www.sfmoma.org/artist/Yasumasa_Morimura/).
6. *Unfinished Self-Portrait 1660* (1994) — [SFMOMA artist holdings](https://www.sfmoma.org/artist/Yasumasa_Morimura/).
7. *Self-Portrait (Actress) / After Red Marilyn* (1996) — [SFMOMA artist holdings](https://www.sfmoma.org/artist/Yasumasa_Morimura/).
8. *Self-Portrait / After Audrey Hepburn 1* (1996) — [SFMOMA artist holdings](https://www.sfmoma.org/artist/Yasumasa_Morimura/).
9. *Self-Portrait (b&w) / Vivien Holding Camera* (1996–97) — [LACMA collection record](https://collections.lacma.org/object/97496).
10. *An Inner Dialogue with Frida Kahlo (Collar of Thorns)* (2001) — [SFMOMA artist holdings](https://www.sfmoma.org/artist/Yasumasa_Morimura/).

The public Getty record for *Daughter of Art History, Theater B* identifies the
work as © Yasumasa Morimura and labels its image rights In Copyright; SFMOMA
directs users seeking high-resolution scholarly reproduction to its copyright
office. Those records confirm the historical importance of the omitted works
while also supporting a conservative choice to retain the directly delivered,
web-sized Art Institute group for this sourcing pass.
