Yasumasa Morimura does not use a self-portrait to stabilize a self. In this compact sequence, identity is visibly made from costume, makeup, pose, quotation, prop, and the image's own support. The small 1996–97 persona studies and the much later *Mysterious Illusion of KAINOSHO* all make performance legible as a compositional operation: a body is positioned, its signs are distributed, and the frame tests whether the viewer will mistake that construction for a natural likeness. [Source notes](../../content/yasumasa-morimura/sources.md)

## the persona and its support

<Plate slug="yasumasa-morimura" id="01-m-holding-a-cigarette-1997" label="M Holding a Cigarette, 1997" caption="The small staged figure is held inside a dark Polaroid field; its printed border and signature keep the objecthood of the image in view." />

In *M Holding a Cigarette*, the overlay's internal frame follows the dark image
field, while the centered set axis makes the support part of the proposition
rather than a neutral margin. Inside that field, the circled figure and the
cigarette-hand-to-face path keep the pose from settling into a frontal emblem.
The hip, flower, and violet trim extend that unstable relay. The analyzer finds a
low edge density of 0.0206 and values from p05 0.216 to p95 0.933: charcoal
#393a3b gives the pale face, floral costume, and purple trim room to perform
against a broad, almost unarticulated black stage. The Art Institute records
this small work as an internal dye diffusion transfer print, a material fact
that makes the visible border especially consequential.

<Plate slug="yasumasa-morimura" id="02-m-wearing-a-military-cap-2-1997" label="M Wearing a Military Cap 2, 1997" caption="A cap and blue mask interrupt an over-the-shoulder figure, while the chair and split wall make the studio arrangement visible." />

*M Wearing a Military Cap 2* uses the same small dye-diffusion-transfer format
to make hierarchy unstable. The overlay isolates the Polaroid field, the
cap-and-mask cluster, and the bare shoulder where the measured anchor lands.
The eye moves from that exposed shoulder to the masked cap and back to the
turned face, then down to the chair and discarded fur: costume is not decoration
but a chain of competing signs. Its 0.0136 edge
density is even lower than the first plate's, and the #e7ebe8, #3f3b38,
#594d3c, and muted gold #816e4a palette lets broad wall light carry the
arrangement instead of crisp contour.

## quotation as a constructed stage

<Plate slug="yasumasa-morimura" id="03-self-portrait-actress-after-greta-garbo-1-1996" label="Self-portrait (Actress) after Greta Garbo 1, 1996" caption="The theatrical costume holds a frontal figure within intersecting red drapery, flowers, and a deliberately crowded studio field." />

The Garbo after-image refuses a simple centered portrait. The overlay locates a
drapery division at y0.438, a measured convergence at x0.351, y0.550, the
costume center, and the two drapery sweeps that cut across the field. These
marks do not turn the studio into a believable room; they show how fabric,
flowers, sword-like props, and the costume keep redirecting attention around a
face that nevertheless meets the camera. The palette is correspondingly hot:
#8d0a18, #450e10, #b43917, #dc7319, and #ecbf7c dominate a dark mean luminance
of 0.258. At edge density 0.0567, the picture has more surface articulation
than the small persona studies, but its 120 × 95 cm chromogenic-print record
reminds us that the dense theatrical field was made to operate at a markedly
larger scale.

<Plate slug="yasumasa-morimura" id="04-mysterious-illusion-of-kainosho-2023-24" label="Mysterious Illusion of KAINOSHO, 2023/24" caption="Near symmetry organizes the coiffure and lowered face before diagonal hands and a red flower pull the portrait off balance." />

The 2023/24 portrait compresses the staging into a closer, more ceremonial
format. The overlay's portrait axis, coiffure ellipse, kimono collar, and
folded-hands-to-flower path identify a provisional equilibrium: the head and
ornaments gather the upper frame, while the folded hands lead diagonally toward
the flower. Brown-black #3a3531, mauve-brown #906750, and
pale #c0a99a work within a moderate p05–p95 range of 0.184–0.682, so the small
red accent in the collar and flower is a controlled interruption rather than a
general wash of color. The Art Institute records this 120 × 75 cm work as an
inkjet print. It is a different recorded medium from the earlier transfer and
chromogenic prints, but the visual method remains legible: persona emerges from
the tension between a nearly formal axis and signs that refuse to stay still.

## Exercises

<ExerciseCard n="01" title="Make the support part of the picture">
Photograph a small print, card, screen, or page so its border and any inscription
remain visible. Then arrange a figure within the image field. Make the relation
between object and figure do more work than a close crop would.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="02" title="Build a persona from competing signs">
Use a costume, one prop, a chair or wall, and one exposed body area. Keep the
items from forming a single costume story: position them so the eye must move
between them before it arrives at the face.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="03" title="Let symmetry almost hold">
Set a portrait on a near-central axis, then break it with hands, a flower, or a
tilted head. The image should retain ceremony while making the imbalance visible.
</ExerciseCard>
