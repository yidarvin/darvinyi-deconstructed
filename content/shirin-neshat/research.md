# Shirin Neshat — research note

## Scope and evidence

The source record documents seven photographs made between 1993 and 1996: the *Unveiling* work *I Am Its Secret* (1993), *Offered Eyes* (1993), and five works from or associated in the source record with *Women of Allah* (1994–96). Across this selection, the documented visual materials are veiling, Persian calligraphy inscribed on skin, hands, eyes, mouths, and firearms. That is the basis for the chapter's formal claims; it does not establish a translation of the script.

## Technique, process, and printing

The Dayton Art Institute record identifies *Rebellious Silence* as a 1994 silver print. The supplied source records do not establish a camera model, lens, film stock, exposure, sensitivity, darkroom procedure, edition process, or print process for the other six works. The files are collection or exhibition web renditions, not originals or technical records. Embedded iPhone metadata in the downloaded *Rebellious Silence* file is delivery-file metadata, not evidence about Neshat's camera or exposure.

Two supplied renditions visibly show a framed or mounted object rather than an edge-to-edge image alone: *Stories of Martyrdom* and *I Am Its Secret*. The overlay discussion therefore separates the photographed object's inner image field from its surrounding display border; it does not treat that border as a feature of the original negative or print composition.

## Chapter method

The analyzer is most useful here for measured bilateral axes, subject signals, tonal range, palette, and edge density. Its proposed "horizons" and vanishing points in these close portraits are often contrasts, weapon edges, mounts, or script marks rather than spatial horizons or perspective, and are not used as such in the overlays.
