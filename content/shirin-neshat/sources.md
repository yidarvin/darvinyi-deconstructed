# Shirin Neshat — source record

## Selection and acquisition note

Shirin Neshat's photographic practice makes the relation between a body, its
inscription, and the frame unusually explicit. This source pass begins with the
canonical *Unveiling* and *Women of Allah* works, then retains the later
*Book of Kings* and portrait work as useful continuations of her use of text,
gaze, and polarized black/white space. The research shortlist also checked
*Speechless* (1996), *Allegiance with Wakefulness* (1995), *Moon Song* (1995),
*Seeking Martyrdom* (1995), and the 1995 “garden” photographs. The seven works
below are the coherent directly accessible set acquired for composition study.

All uses are **fair-use** assessments. Neshat's works are copyrighted; the
displaying collections identify the creator as Shirin Neshat, and LACMA and
The Broad explicitly give the notice © Shirin Neshat / courtesy the artist and
Gladstone Gallery where applicable. For each image: (1) this book uses the
work for criticism, scholarship, and transformative composition teaching;
(2) it is a creative photographic work, which weighs against use; (3) the
complete web-sized frame is necessary to analyze the pictured relation of
figure, script, weapon, or group and is not used decoratively; and (4) the
credited, linked web copy does not replace the museum record, original work,
or licensed reproduction. No access control was bypassed.

## Acquired shortlist

### 01 — *Rebellious Silence*, from *Women of Allah* (1994)

- **Why it matters:** The rifle forms a strict central axis that splits a direct
  frontal gaze into two matching fields; calligraphy activates the face while
  the chador holds a large, nearly symmetrical black mass.
- **Source:** [Dayton Art Institute collection highlight](https://www.daytonartinstitute.org/do-see/collection-highlights/rebellious-silence-shirin-neshat), direct public display rendition: `https://daytonart.b-cdn.net/files/highlights/2026.111.jpg`.
- **Use basis:** **fair-use** — © Shirin Neshat; Dayton credits the artist and
  identifies the 1994 silver print. The complete frame is required to teach its
  centerline, bilateral gaze, and weapon-as-axis; the 1053 × 1599px credited
  web copy is transformative criticism and does not substitute for the print
  or a licensed reproduction.
- **File:** `raw/shirin-neshat/01-rebellious-silence.jpg` (1053 × 1599px).

### 02 — *Faceless*, from *Women of Allah* (1994)

- **Why it matters:** A covered head becomes an oval field for script and a
  hand-held weapon: the blocked face shifts attention to contour, negative
  space, and the tension between concealment and address.
- **Source:** [Crystal Bridges Museum of American Art, 2022.20](https://crystalbridges.org/artworks/2022-20), direct public display rendition: `https://dam.aweoffice.org/AssetLink/j2r73n75710k5x5a4t5fu3177l8idw5i.jpg`.
- **Use basis:** **fair-use** — © Shirin Neshat; the collection record names
  the artist, the *Women of Allah* series, and Gladstone Gallery provenance.
  The complete frame is needed to analyze the enclosed head, lettering, and
  gun; this 653 × 1080px linked teaching copy is transformative and
  non-substitutive.
- **File:** `raw/shirin-neshat/03-faceless.jpg` (653 × 1080px).

### 03 — *Stories of Martyrdom*, from *Women of Allah* (1994)

- **Why it matters:** Exposed hands become the main visual punctuation in a
  black field; the frame teaches how a close crop can concentrate gesture,
  script, and contrast without a visible face.
- **Source:** [Mathaf / Qatar Museums collection record](https://collections.qm.org.qa/en/objects/stories-of-martyrdom-from-the-series-women-of-allah-mat20081536), direct public display rendition: `https://dgcollectionprod.blob.core.windows.net/media/images/MAT.2008.15.36.width-1200.jpg`.
- **Use basis:** **fair-use** — © Shirin Neshat (creator/rightsholder known);
  Mathaf publicly displays and catalogs this 1994 work. The full crop is
  necessary to explain its hand-to-script hierarchy; the 1200 × 896px credited
  web copy supports criticism and does not replace the collection work or a
  licensed reproduction.
- **File:** `raw/shirin-neshat/04-stories-of-martyrdom.jpg` (1200 × 896px).

### 04 — *I Am Its Secret*, from *Unveiling* (1993)

- **Why it matters:** The picture establishes the pivotal early device that
  later work elaborates: calligraphy crosses the exposed face while the veil
  creates a dense surround, turning a portrait into a figure/ground argument.
- **Source:** [Google Arts & Culture public object display](https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/i-am-its-secret-from-unveiling-series-shirin-neshat/8QHE3YC8-8bJdA), with the work identified there as a 1993 *Unveiling* series
  photograph held by Museo Madre; direct public 1200px rendition acquired.
- **Use basis:** **fair-use** — © Shirin Neshat. The full portrait is essential
  to discuss how script, face, and veil share one field; this 800 × 1200px
  credited and linked web copy is a transformative scholarly use, not a
  replacement for the original or a licensed reproduction.
- **File:** `raw/shirin-neshat/05-i-am-its-secret.jpg` (800 × 1200px).

### 05 — *Offered Eyes* (1993)

- **Why it matters:** The early portrait makes the eyes a small high-contrast
  anchor inside a tall enclosing black form, a compact lesson in visual weight
  and the pressure of a centered gaze.
- **Source:** [The Broad, *I Will Greet the Sun Again* exhibition](https://www.thebroad.org/shirinneshat), direct public display rendition: `https://www.thebroad.org/sites/default/files/styles/broad_pages_scale_500px_width/public/media-files/Shirin%20Neshat%2C%20Offered%20Eyes%2C%201993.jpg.webp?itok=vDjCSrj4`.
- **Use basis:** **fair-use** — © Shirin Neshat; The Broad identifies the work
  as 1993. The complete vertical is needed to teach the image's tight gaze and
  enclosing silhouette; the 500 × 692px credited web copy is transformative
  criticism and does not replace a museum or licensed rendition.
- **File:** `raw/shirin-neshat/06-offered-eyes.jpg` (500 × 692px; converted
  without resizing from The Broad's public WebP rendition).

### 06 — *Bonding* (1995)

- **Why it matters:** The low, wide arrangement of closely grouped figures
  makes contact and repetition read as a horizontal rhythm rather than a
  conventional individual portrait.
- **Source:** [The Broad, *I Will Greet the Sun Again* exhibition](https://www.thebroad.org/shirinneshat), direct public display rendition: `https://www.thebroad.org/sites/default/files/styles/broad_pages_scale_500px_width/public/media-files/Shirin%20Neshat%2C%20Bonding%2C%201995.jpg.webp?itok=RYcPgYgv`.
- **Use basis:** **fair-use** — © Shirin Neshat; The Broad identifies the work
  as 1995. The entire horizontal frame is necessary to analyze its group
  spacing and repeated gestures; the credited 500 × 325px public web copy is
  used only as transformative criticism and does not substitute for a licensed
  reproduction. This is a documented 480–639px long-edge exception: the
  figures and horizontal rhythm remain legible, and no larger public rendition
  was offered on the exhibition page.
- **File:** `raw/shirin-neshat/07-bonding.jpg` (500 × 325px; converted without
  resizing from The Broad's public WebP rendition).

### 07 — *Untitled*, from *Women of Allah* (1996)

- **Why it matters:** A late series portrait combines the direct, calligraphed
  face with a tall vertical frame, allowing the builder to compare how Neshat
  redistributes visual weight when text and veil do not meet a central rifle.
- **Source:** [The Broad, *I Will Greet the Sun Again* exhibition](https://www.thebroad.org/shirinneshat), direct public display rendition: `https://www.thebroad.org/sites/default/files/styles/broad_pages_scale_500px_width/public/media-files/Shirin%20Neshat%2C%20Untitled%20%28Women%20of%20Allah%29%2C%201996.jpg.webp?itok=CX92h0AJ`.
- **Use basis:** **fair-use** — © Shirin Neshat; The Broad identifies the work
  as 1996. The complete tall frame is needed to teach face/script/veil balance;
  its 500 × 722px credited web copy is a linked, transformative teaching use
  and does not replace the work, museum record, or a licensed reproduction.
- **File:** `raw/shirin-neshat/08-untitled-women-of-allah.jpg` (500 × 722px;
  converted without resizing from The Broad's public WebP rendition).

## Acquisition verification

companions preserve The Broad's unmodified public delivery for the three files
whose public rendition was WebP; the corresponding `.jpg` files are format-only

registry readiness threshold of four images, so no limited-source fallback is
used.
