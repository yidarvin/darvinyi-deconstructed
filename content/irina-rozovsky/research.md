# Research note — Irina Rozovsky

## Record and scope

The supplied source record identifies Irina Rozovsky as the photographer and dates this selection from 2014 to 2020. It names works from *Traditions Highway*, *Miracle Center*, *A Rock that Floats*, and *Mountain Black Heart* and credits the public display files to CPM Gallery. MoMA's essay on the artist is cited there for *Miracle Center*'s use of old found frames; the linked Aperture essay is cited for a decade-long practice of quietly observing parks. Those accounts provide useful context, but the chapter's visual claims are limited to what is present in the supplied images and measured derivatives.

## Technique and material information

The gallery record identifies the works, dates, media, and editions at the collection level, but the supplied source note does not preserve a reliable per-image statement of negative format, camera, lens, film stock, aperture, shutter speed, exposure, or darkroom process. The normalized files contain no camera EXIF. Accordingly, this chapter does not infer camera settings from grain, color, blur, or framing, and it does not name a print process beyond the source record's general note that *Miracle Center* incorporates photographs in old found frames.

## What the analyzed files establish

The measurements describe the supplied web derivatives, not original prints. The *Traditions Highway* carriage file has a busy edge density of 0.207 and a mid-value palette of olive, gray, and pale carriage white; the flood scene has a broader tonal range (p05 0.090, p95 0.804), which keeps its small figure legible across the water. The car-window image has a notably low edge density (0.040), letting its enclosing window arc read as a single large boundary. The *Miracle Center* object image is even quieter at 0.007 edge density, while the glass-surface image holds a moderate 0.036. The red landscape's mean luminance is 0.114, with its palette concentrated in dark red and black; the bottle still life instead is pale and high-key, with p95 0.882. These are descriptions of present pixels, not claims about Rozovsky's original exposure or printing.
