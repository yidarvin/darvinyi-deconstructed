# Image sources — Irina Rozovsky

## Source and rights note

Irina Rozovsky's own site identifies her as the photographer and states, “All
images Copyright © Irina Rozovsky 2023.” It lists CPM, Baltimore as a gallery;
CPM's public artist page supplies the direct, unwatermarked display files and
identifies the works, dates, media, and editions. No open licence or
public-domain dedication is offered. Every acquisition below is therefore a
**fair-use** reproduction, not an open image. The selected web files are direct
public responses from the artist's listed gallery, with no login or access
control bypassed.

For each numbered item, the four-factor assessment is: (1) this book's use is
noncommercial criticism, scholarship, and transformative composition teaching;
(2) the underlying work is a creative photograph, which weighs against use but
is necessary to analyze; (3) the complete frame is reproduced only at the
gallery's web-display size because cropping would defeat the compositional
lesson; and (4) the page credits and links to the gallery record and does not
replace a print, original, or licensed reproduction. The selection is limited
to frames that materially support the chapter's account of found structures,
quiet gesture, and photographic memory.

## Verification

All eleven files opened successfully after download and each has a long edge of
at least 960px (well above the 640px normal floor). The MoMA essay, [“Irina
Rozovsky”](https://www.moma.org/magazine/articles/427), independently identifies
*Miracle Center* and describes its use of the artist’s photographs in old found
frames; [Aperture’s *In Plain Air* essay](https://aperture.org/editorial/irina-rozovsky-majestic-photographs-of-prospect-park/)
confirms the artist’s decade-long practice of making quiet, ethically alert park
photographs. Those sources guide the shortlisting but no Aperture or MoMA image
file was downloaded.
