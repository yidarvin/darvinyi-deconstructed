# Tom Chambers — research notes

## Record-supported process

Chambers describes these works as photomontages: he sketches a concept, photographs the separate elements, matches their light intensity and direction, and composites selected material in Photoshop. His process statement identifies a Nikon D800 as the camera body used for this work and says a single montage can take a month or more. The statement also describes final output as archival pigment inks on cotton rag paper. These are artist-level process claims, not evidence that every selected web image has one uniform physical print specification. Source: [Tom Chambers biography and process](https://www.tomchambersphoto.com/biography).

The artist's portfolio says that each element in the *Declaration* series was photographed or hand-drawn by Chambers. It places that recent series after the resumption of international travel in 2022 and describes an Italian Renaissance influence. Source: [*Declaration*](https://www.tomchambersphoto.com/new-gallery-76/). The portfolio identifies the selected titles and series; the detailed item-level source links and rights rationale are in `sources.md`.

## Era and terminology

Chambers has exhibited photographic-series photomontages since 1998. The selected images cross several mostly undated series. Two firm series anchors are *The Ties That Bind* (2021) and *Declaration* (post-2022); the chapter therefore retains `undated` for the other individual works rather than assigning dates from visual style. His own account frames the constructed scenes as narrative magic realism: believable-looking pictures made improbable through association and scale. Sources: [artist biography](https://www.tomchambersphoto.com/biography) and [artist portfolio](https://www.tomchambersphoto.com/).

## What the record does not establish

The cited artist material does not document a lens, focal length, aperture, shutter speed, ISO, lighting equipment, sensor crop, capture date, edition size, or physical dimensions for these twelve individual images. The files' web metadata is not evidence of original camera settings. The chapter therefore discusses visible staging, compositing, tonal structure, palette, and measured spatial relations rather than inventing technical settings.
