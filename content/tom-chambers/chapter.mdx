Tom Chambers makes an impossible scene read before it can be explained. A bird occupies a chair, a deer stands over a laid table, a child runs uphill toward a village through fog. The plausibility comes from photographic particulars; the unease comes from how their scales, thresholds, and routes have been edited into a single decisive sentence. Chambers describes this process as photomontage: he sketches an idea, photographs separate elements, matches their light, and composites selected material. His *Declaration* series specifically adds photographed-or-hand-drawn elements. The overlays are deliberately literal about what the finished image gives us: a rail, a table edge, an arch edge, a hand, a shoreline. They do not mistake collage texture for perspective.

## thresholds that make a story

<Plate slug="tom-chambers" id="01-caging-the-songbird" label="Caging The Songbird, undated" caption="A doorway edge, bird on a chair, and the girl's direction of attention divide the room into two adjoining but irreconcilable spaces." />

In *Caging The Songbird*, the narrow doorway is the work's first cut. It holds a complete warm room at left while the chair, bird, and girl occupy the larger, damaged wall at right. The attention line is interpretive rather than an asserted physical wire: she is holding an empty cage and looking toward the bird. The analyzer's earthy palette of deep brown, black-brown, ochre, and worn tan fits the image's material logic, while a 0.099 edge density keeps the chair, tiled floor, and peeling wall alive enough for the stillness to feel staged rather than blank.

<Plate slug="tom-chambers" id="02-glass-flower" label="Glass Flower, undated" caption="The table edge gives the deer-and-flower group a route from the seated girl into an intentionally asymmetric domestic tableau." />

*Glass Flower* makes the central animal both guest and obstacle. The ellipse around the deer and flower names the hinge; the smaller ellipse around the seated girl prevents the image from becoming a one-subject novelty. Its dark umber palette and mean luminance of 0.270 keep the room heavy, while the 0.122 edge density records how much patterned furniture, crockery, and lace must be organized before the improbable figure can feel calm.

<Plate slug="tom-chambers" id="03-a-view-from-the-bridge" label="A View From The Bridge, undated" caption="A figure on the balustrade is held above a narrowing canal, with the rail making the improbable shift in scale legible." />

Here the rail is a stage edge. It gives the large pink-skirted figure a place to stand, then hands the eye to the two canal edges as they narrow between the palazzi. The composition refuses a clean real-world scale: pigeons, figure, water, and buildings are all crisp enough to be believed separately. The measured palette moves from brown and tan to a sudden pink-red accent, and the 0.078 edge density leaves the cloud field broad enough to make the architecture and dress feel cut forward.

## a prop can steer the whole frame

<Plate slug="tom-chambers" id="04-one-oar-out" label="One Oar Out, undated" caption="A tilted distant ice edge, the boat hull, and one upright oar give a small human figure an exposed vertical task." />

In *One Oar Out*, the measured ice edge at y=0.584 is not a generic horizon; it is the boundary against which the boat's slight tilt can register. The oar runs upward through the pale field, turning a calm blue-grey image into a diagram of precarious balance. Its mean luminance of 0.630 and very low 0.0166 edge density make the reflected water and sky unusually spacious; the boat, figure, and oar must carry nearly all the pressure.

<Plate slug="tom-chambers" id="05-black-from-blue" label="Black From Blue, undated" caption="The fox's rising contour and a low grassy ridge make the dead raven and airborne birds part of one uneasy vertical relation." />

The fox does not merely fill the frame: its back takes the eye from the low raven toward the sky. The broad ellipse names that body as a mass rather than pretending the animal is a point. A grass ridge near y=0.607 keeps the foreground carrion and animal legs on a tilted base. This is the chapter's more graphic tonal construction: p05/p95 run from 0.086 to 0.827, while 0.156 edge density belongs to fur, feathers, grass, and cloud edges rather than to a dense setting.

<Plate slug="tom-chambers" id="06-late-for-dinner" label="Late For Dinner, undated" caption="The running girl and the narrow path make the hilltop village a visible destination rather than background scenery." />

The path in *Late For Dinner* is a physical line and a narrative promise. It begins beneath the girl and climbs through fog to the village ellipse at the summit; the child is not placed on a detector's abstract vanishing point. With mean luminance 0.471, p05/p95 of 0.212/0.733, and only 0.0114 edge density, Chambers gives the misty field very little local distraction. The figure's white dress and the path must therefore do the work of direction.

## a container can become an argument

<Plate slug="tom-chambers" id="07-the-goatherd" label="The Goatherd, undated" caption="A book, a reaching hand, and three goat heads create a small unstable triangle of shared attention." />

The image’s claim is not pastoral repose. The open book is the center of a close, hungry arrangement: the keeper's hand arrives from above while three goats crowd its pages from three sides. The overlay uses an interpretive triangle rather than falsely calling horn contours a vanishing system. The subdued brown-black palette, mean luminance 0.212, and 0.129 edge density leave the table, hair, horns, and print as a tightly knit surface.

<Plate slug="tom-chambers" id="08-springs-landfall" label="Spring's Landfall, undated" caption="The boat's white hull edge contains the blindfolded figure while a branch and distant tree line set opposing directions." />

*Spring's Landfall* turns a broken boat into a container and a threshold. Its visible white hull edge curves around the seated figure; the branch across her lap interrupts that enclosure. The level tree line at y≈0.36 holds the field still while dress, flowers, and white hull create the busy local event. The analyzer records a wide p05/p95 range of 0.071/0.816 and edge density 0.129, an apt measure of the collision between clothing, vegetation, and improvised vessel.

<Plate slug="tom-chambers" id="09-aground" label="Aground, undated" caption="A boy, boat rim, stump, and distant shoreline make a vertical stack out of a stranded incident." />

*Aground* makes a simple vertical relation extraordinary: water rises to stump, stump lifts boat, boat lifts boy. The distant shoreline at y=0.424 gives that stack an open horizontal counterweight. Its cool blue palette is unusually bright at the upper end (p95 0.792), but the p05 of 0.039 keeps stump, boat rim, and storm bands emphatic. The 0.104 edge density is reserved for moving water, birds, and wood grain, not a false perspective grid.

## frontal myths, made from components

<Plate slug="tom-chambers" id="10-hide-your-eyes" label="Hide Your Eyes, undated" caption="The large arch and a central body axis give the blindfolded girl an icon-like stage, offset by the bird on her arm." />

The large arch in *Hide Your Eyes* makes direct address especially strange: the girl faces us while her eyes are bound. The central axis follows the body, not a claim that the figure and falcon are mirror images; the bird's weight is the designed imbalance. The overlay traces the arch's actual outer edge, and the shoreline at y=0.609 anchors the enormous interior figure to a small coastal world. Mean luminance 0.710, p95 1.000, and only 0.0223 edge density make this one of the chapter's most high-key, least cluttered constructions.

<Plate slug="tom-chambers" id="11-buffalo-gal" label="Buffalo Gal, undated (*The Ties That Bind* series, 2021)" caption="Foreground boots, a compact buffalo herd, and the mesa ridge make bodily scale the subject of the image." />

In *Buffalo Gal*, Chambers gives the figure no head or torso, only an outsized yellow skirt and boots. The large boot ellipse makes that cropping intentional; the herd ellipse restores the actual scale that the foreground has distorted. The ridge is a useful lateral divider, not a horizon invented from snow texture. The palette's ochre, brown, blue, and pale snow tones, plus p05/p95 of 0.184/0.922, let the image hold both broad Western space and close costume detail.

<Plate slug="tom-chambers" id="12-genesis" label="Genesis, undated, post-2022 series" caption="A central fire, three-figure triangle, axis, and stone wall make an asymmetrical scene borrow the gravity of an altarpiece." />

*Genesis* is frontal without being mechanically symmetrical. The central girl's face, metal halo, and held fire align with the organizing axis; the flanking figures, tree, and costume differences keep the structure alive. A stone wall supplies the low horizontal base for the three-figure tableau. Its palette moves from near-black and brown through gold to white, and the p05/p95 span from 0.047 to 1.000 is the chapter's widest: a controlled mixture of deep costume shadows and the held fire's impossible light.

## Exercises

<ExerciseCard n="01" title="Build a believable impossibility">
Photograph three elements separately: a figure, a prop, and a location. Before combining them, decide which literal edge will connect them: a table, rail, path, or shoreline. If you cannot name that edge in the composite, the story will have no route through the frame.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="02" title="Make scale do narrative work">
Make two versions of a composite with the same figure and background. In one, let the figure establish normal scale. In the other, crop or enlarge one element so that a small distant subject becomes newly charged. Keep one stable horizontal or enclosing frame to make the distortion readable.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="03" title="Audit the claim, not just the look">
Draw only three annotation claims on a finished frame. For each, ask whether it follows an actual pictured feature, such as a rim, branch, body contour, or architectural edge. Remove any line that exists only because the software found a texture break.
</ExerciseCard>
