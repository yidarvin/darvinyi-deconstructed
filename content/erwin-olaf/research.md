# Erwin Olaf — research notes

## Record and scope

The source record identifies twelve photographs in four Erwin Olaf series:
*Royal Blood* (2000), *Rain* (2004), *Hope* (2005), and *Grief* (2007).
The supplied images are unaltered public web-display JPEGs from the [Erwin Olaf
Estate](https://www.erwinolaf.com/art); titles, series, dates, credit, and the
fair-use basis are documented image by image in [source notes](sources.md).

The record supports a discussion of staged interiors, frontal portraiture,
colour, visible light, spatial arrangement, and tableau-like stillness. It does
not document a common print process, lighting plan, digital workflow,
post-production method, lens, aperture, shutter speed, or ISO for the four
series as a whole. This chapter therefore does not infer any of those facts
from the finished appearance of the pictures.

## Retained technical metadata

Only two supplied display files retain usable camera metadata. That metadata is
about those delivered web files, not an Estate statement of the artist's
process, and is reported without generalizing it:

- *Caroline*, *Grief* (2007): Phase One P 45; ISO 100; file timestamp
  2007-01-10 16:35:44.
- *Troy*, *Grief* (2007): Phase One P 45; ISO 100; file timestamp
  2007-01-25 20:15:59.

The ten other ingested files contain no usable EXIF settings. None of the
supplied sources documents how any of these photographs was printed, so no
printing claim is made.

## Visual method evidenced by the selection

Across the selected work, rooms act as measured containers: counters, floor
seams, doors, windows, court markings, tables, ropes, and curtains make figures
feel placed rather than caught. *Rain* uses muted greens, browns, and pinks to
make ordinary social spaces exacting and strangely inert. *Hope* presses its
figures through corridors, rings, and fixtures; its measured palettes are the
darkest of the selection. *Grief* opens a far brighter field around a seated or
kneeling body. *Royal Blood* reduces the field to a near-white portrait ground,
where a local disturbance carries disproportionate force.

The composition analyses record that visible construction in modest terms:
dominant horizontal bands, constrained symmetry where it is measured, and
specific lines or frames that can be inspected in each photograph. Their palette
and tonal statistics describe the delivered display files, not the colour or
density of an original print.
