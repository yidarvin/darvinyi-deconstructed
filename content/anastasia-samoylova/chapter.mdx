Anastasia Samoylova photographs an environment already busy with images: resort colors, pools, real-estate promises, shop signs, reflections, and gigantic screens. In this selection from *FloodZone*, *Floridas*, and *Image Cities*, climate risk is not illustrated from a safe distance. It arrives as a changed relation between surface and depth. Water reflects an architecture that is no longer secure; a fence becomes both an obstruction and a picture; an advertisement becomes another inhabited room. The overlays make only those visible relations explicit. They draw attention to railings, waterlines, panel seams, and picture-within-picture structures rather than treating the photographs as neutral documents. Titles, years, credits, portfolio links, and image-specific fair-use rationales are preserved in the [source notes](../../content/anastasia-samoylova/sources.md).

## water makes a surface unreliable

<Plate slug="anastasia-samoylova" id="01-gator" label="Gator, 2017" caption="A high waterline and separated submerged silhouettes turn the green field into a difficult figure-ground." />

In *Gator*, the dark forms suspended under green water are made to feel both close and hard to parse. The overlay's **WATERLINE** crosses high in the frame, leaving a large lower field with little conventional depth. The marked **SUBMERGED FIGURE** and **SUSPENDED LIMB** do not produce a stable portrait; they ask the eye to move between separate dark fragments. That uncertainty is formal, not an invented narrative about the scene. The analyzer's very low edge density of 0.011 helps explain the effect: broad greens `#507523`, `#a5d15d`, and `#78a33e` give the web rendition a nearly continuous field, while the p05–p95 tonal range of 0.059–0.757 lets the silhouettes emerge without fully resolving.

<Plate slug="anastasia-samoylova" id="02-fountain" label="Fountain, 2017" caption="Tile edges converge through a flooded interior, while shuttered architecture and reflections keep the room from reading as stable space." />

*Fountain* is an argument between hard construction and water. The **POOL RIM** and **TILE EDGE** converge at the marked **TILE CORNER**, but the shallow water repeats light, wall, and drain hardware until those directions lose their firmness. The **SHUTTERED WALL** is a real internal screen, not a decorative border: it makes the right side feel closed while the reflected pool keeps opening below. Its edge density rises to 0.074 and its p95 reaches 0.898, so bright reflections and dark openings coexist in a relatively broad tonal span. The subdued gray, tan, and near-black palette is a measurement of this supplied copy, not a claim about its print.

<Plate slug="anastasia-samoylova" id="03-fallen-trees-after-hurricane-irma" label="Fallen Trees on Collins Ave, Miami Beach Sidewalk after Hurricane Irma, 2017" caption="A curb recession and toppled palms turn a clean pedestrian axis into a diagonal obstruction." />

The force of *Fallen Trees on Collins Ave* comes from its refusal to make storm damage picturesque disorder. The white buildings establish a crisp, repeating street grid. Against it, the **FALLEN PALM** runs diagonally across the route while the **CURB** still insists on recession toward the marked **CURB RECESSION**. The overlay separates those two claims: municipal order remains visible, but it is physically crossed by the tree. The rendition is unusually bright: p95 is 0.976 and mean luminance 0.670, so its pale facades, pink pavement, and brown trunks are held as distinct planes rather than dissolved into dramatic darkness.

<Plate slug="anastasia-samoylova" id="04-pool-after-hurricane" label="Pool after Hurricane, 2017" caption="A handrail crosses a leaf-filled pool, turning a recreational surface into an uneven water field." />

*Pool after Hurricane* reduces a large event to a specific mismatch of surfaces. The immaculate-looking **POOL DECK** stays above the frame's wet, leaf-strewn lower portion; the diagonal **HANDRAIL** is the hard element that crosses between them. The broad **WATER FIELD** is intentionally a descriptive bound rather than a false geometric object, while **FLOATING LEAVES** identifies the small repeated matter that changes the pool's function. With edge density 0.118, this is one of the more textural plates. Blue `#91c0c7`, green `#4a6437`, and pale stone values remain legible across a p05–p95 range of 0.322–0.867.

<Plate slug="anastasia-samoylova" id="05-flooded-garage" label="Flooded Garage, 2017" caption="A wading figure stands at a flooded threshold between the paving in front and a deep, dark interior." />

In *Flooded Garage*, the photograph makes a boundary visible precisely because it has failed. The **BRICK THRESHOLD** holds near the lower edge, the marked **WADING FIGURE** interrupts its order, and the **FLOODWATER** is a horizontal reflective condition rather than background. The square grid supports the image's architectural regularity while the figure faces into a space whose interior cannot be measured by sight. The supplied rendition is much darker than the adjacent pool images: mean luminance is 0.133, p05 is 0.020, and the palette runs from `#0e0e0e` to `#b5b5b5`. The isolated bright reflections therefore read as small openings inside a nearly black field.

## florida's image economy is also a material surface

<Plate slug="anastasia-samoylova" id="06-construction-normandy-shores" label="Construction in Normandy Shores, 2017" caption="Printed pool imagery and real chain-link mesh occupy the same plane, with diagonal shadows repeatedly cutting across both." />

*Construction in Normandy Shores* is the clearest collision of image and site in the chapter. A printed luxury-pool scene sits behind actual construction mesh; its **PRINTED FENCE** frame makes the rectangle visible as a temporary surface rather than a view. The paired **MESH DIAGONAL** lines follow repeated shadows and links that drag attention from the worker and machinery above into the rendered pool below. They are not a claim that the pool itself recedes. The edge density of 0.136 is the selection's highest, consistent with the work's mesh, lettering, machinery, poles, and image seams. Cool blue `#2c779a` and pale `#ced3d2` are interrupted by the orange machinery and dark fence pattern.

<Plate slug="anastasia-samoylova" id="07-staircase-at-king-tide" label="Staircase at King Tide, Hollywood, 2019" caption="The railing and stair edge aim toward a flooded terminus, so an escape structure ends as a waterline." />

The side-on view in *Staircase at King Tide* is economical and severe. A rail descends from the upper left to the marked **STAIR TERMINUS**, while the **STAIR EDGE** makes the wooden flight read as one long declining wedge. Below it, the **WATERLINE** begins the reflected half of the frame. The overlay does not call the reflection symmetry; it visibly ripples and breaks, but it lets the rail's purposeful line meet the flood at its most pointed location. Pale pinks `#f0bcba` and `#d39e9e` dominate the rendition against dark brown `#160a08`; the broad p05–p95 span of 0.043–0.843 keeps wall, shadow, rail, and water distinct.

<Plate slug="anastasia-samoylova" id="08-car-reflection" label="Car Reflection, Miami Beach, 2018" caption="A windshield edge and reflected stripes transfer a graphic wall pattern onto the car's glass and paint." />

*Car Reflection* refuses a clean separation between what is photographed and what is reflected. The **WINDSHIELD EDGE** makes the car's own geometry legible, but the **REFLECTED STRIPES** travel over glass and bodywork, while the **STRIPED REFLECTION** anchor identifies their sharpest visible recurrence. The low **CAR ROOF EDGE** prevents the image from floating entirely into abstraction. This is a measured, not metaphorical, layering of planes: the reflections really do occupy the car surfaces. Mean luminance is 0.367, with a wide p05–p95 range of 0.102–0.878; deep teal `#10514f`, charcoal `#1e242b`, and pale reflected whites make the stripe system unusually crisp.

<Plate slug="anastasia-samoylova" id="09-gun-shop-port-orange" label="Gun Shop, Port Orange, 2019" caption="Weapon silhouettes become the dominant graphic weights on a long mint-colored facade." />

In *Gun Shop, Port Orange*, a wall that might otherwise be read as a modest one-story building is converted into a sign field. The two overlay paths identify the **LARGE GUN SIGN** and **SECOND GUN SIGN** without pretending every smaller silhouette is equally important. The **ROOF EAVE** cuts diagonally across the upper portion, while the **MINT FACADE** names the broad plane on which the signs operate. Low edge density (0.032) makes that emphasis feel deliberate: the big shapes have little competition from texture. The web rendition's soft gray sky and pale green wall sit beside very dark sign shapes, a palette relationship summarized by `#d3d6d7`, `#85938b`, and `#1d2017`.

<Plate slug="anastasia-samoylova" id="10-road-destroyed-by-hurricanes" label="Road twice destroyed by hurricanes, Alligator Point, 2021" caption="Road centerline and edge converge beside the sea, making repair, erosion, and forward movement share one narrow corridor." />

The black-and-white *Road twice destroyed by hurricanes* uses a familiar road structure but denies its promise of continuity. The **CENTERLINE** and **ROAD EDGE** set up a directional pull toward **ROAD RECESSION**; at left, the **SEA HORIZON** stays flat and indifferent to that route. The title gives the history of destruction, but the frame makes its vulnerability visible without any added illustration: road, water, rocks, utility poles, and exposed shoulder are compressed together. The analyzer records edge density 0.071 and a p05–p95 spread of 0.125–0.863, enough separation for the bright sea to become an active counterweight to the dark road and foreground pole.

## advertising changes the scale of the city

<Plate slug="anastasia-samoylova" id="11-real-estate-advertisement-moscow" label="Real Estate Advertisement, Moscow, 2021" caption="A luxury interior is shown as a billboard on wheels, divided by its panel seam and undercut by the pavement below." />

*Real Estate Advertisement, Moscow* photographs an advertisement without allowing it to disappear into the thing it advertises. The **BILLBOARD IMAGE** frame contains the walking figure, ornate railing, and drawn interior; the horizontal **PANEL SEAM** and vertical **ADVERTISEMENT DIVIDE** insist that this image is made of joined physical panels. The wheeled base then gives the promise an ordinary street-level support. The vertical axis is a measured structural divide, not a claim of perfect bilateral symmetry in the pictured room. Mean luminance is 0.494 and edge density 0.074; gray `#7a787a`, pale `#ccd4cf`, and black `#222122` make the division between polished image and support plane especially legible.

<Plate slug="anastasia-samoylova" id="12-female-lead-times-square" label="Female Lead, Times Square, 2022" caption="A billboard-scale portrait contains pedestrians and an escalator, while the rail cuts diagonally beneath the enormous advertising body." />

*Female Lead, Times Square* gives the ad image a body so large that real pedestrians appear to occupy its lower torso. The **AD COLLAR** anchor identifies the sharply lit hardware that joins face, body, and screen; below it, the **ESCALATOR RAIL** draws a strong diagonal over the passers-by. The marked **ESCALATOR EXIT** is a measured convergence, while **STREET LEVEL** grounds the elaborate image in a narrow real strip at the bottom. The rendition is low-key, with mean luminance 0.297 and edge density 0.028. Blue-black `#151518`, `#353c46`, and `#586476` reserve the brightest attention for skin, metal, and the screen's illuminated surface.

## Exercises

<ExerciseCard n="01" title="Photograph a failed boundary">
Find a threshold that water, reflection, shadow, or transparent material makes uncertain: a garage entrance, pool rim, window, doorway, or curb. Compose so one hard architectural line and one unstable surface are both readable. Let neither one become a generic background.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="02" title="Make a reflected surface do double duty">
Work with a car window, shop glass, polished metal, or water. Start by identifying the physical edge of the reflecting surface, then arrange the reflection so it alters the thing beneath it. Make one overlay or contact-sheet note that distinguishes actual object edge from reflected pattern.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="03" title="Keep the support in the frame">
Photograph an advertisement, sign, screen, or printed image while retaining at least one support, seam, curb, wheel, railing, or frame edge. The goal is not to expose an illusion as fake; it is to make the image and its physical situation visible at once.
</ExerciseCard>
