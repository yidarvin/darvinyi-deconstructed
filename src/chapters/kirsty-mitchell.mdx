Kirsty Mitchell's *Wonderland* pictures do not use fantasy as an escape from structure. They make structure theatrical: a constructed figure is fitted into a field, an ivy wall, a carriage, or a forest route until costume and place become one designed event. The official record dates the 74-picture project to 2009–2014 and documents hand-made costumes and props assembled at true scale in the English landscape. These eleven credited, public display images are used here for composition criticism under the image-by-image fair-use rationale in the [source notes](../../content/kirsty-mitchell/sources.md).

## a figure can be a clearing

<Plate slug="kirsty-mitchell" id="01-secret-locked-roots-kingdom-2014" label="The Secret Locked in the Roots of a Kingdom, 2014" caption="An ivy enclosure and a flower-to-face path make the figure appear gradually rather than all at once." />

In *The Secret Locked in the Roots of a Kingdom*, concealment is not simply a theme; it is the sequence by which the eye reads the frame. The overlay's ivy enclosure describes the packed field that nearly absorbs the figure, while the flower-to-face path moves down through hair and blooms to the low face. The phi grid helps account for the delayed reveal rather than imposing a central portrait logic. The analyzer finds a dark, high-detail image: mean luminance 0.341 and edge density 0.247, with leaf black-green #151707 and olive #3d3e1e punctuated by pale #cfc6bf and flower pinks. The available record documents the finished artwork and its edition context, not its camera or print process.

<Plate slug="kirsty-mitchell" id="02-gammelyns-daughter-waking-dream-2012" label="Gammelyn’s Daughter a Waking Dream, 2012" caption="The reclining figure becomes a long pale route through heather, with the face held at the far right." />

The waking dream is organized laterally. A long body ellipse and the route running from hand through torso to face make the figure read as a shallow horizontal band, while the heather repeatedly crosses it rather than supplying a clean background. This is a useful way to make a reclining body feel embedded rather than displayed. The thirds grid registers that band, and the palette stays within a dense mauve family: #864d6f, #59343c, #b47a9d, and #d9b7cf. At 0.159 edge density, foreground stems soften the descriptive boundaries without dissolving the body.

<Plate slug="kirsty-mitchell" id="03-gammelyns-daughter-2012" label="Gammelyn’s Daughter, 2012" caption="A seated figure and a broad fabric spread turn the heather into a protected, radial clearing." />

The companion image reverses that horizontal ease. The seated figure is held in one ellipse, but the much larger dress ellipse makes the lower frame fan outward into an enclosure. The fabric arc is not a decorative outline; it connects the separated pale folds across the heather and keeps the central stillness from collapsing into a conventional posed portrait. The analyzer records the selection's highest edge density, 0.304, against a palette that runs from black-purple #1f0d16 through #7b486e to pale pink #e6c7dd. The busy flower texture therefore presses constantly against the comparatively quiet costume volume.

## constructed depth, held in place

<Plate slug="kirsty-mitchell" id="04-last-dance-flowers-2014" label="The Last Dance of the Flowers, 2014" caption="The flowered figure occupies the left edge while trunks reserve a corridor of depth at right." />

*The Last Dance of the Flowers* gives its protagonist only one side of the frame. The figure ellipse makes that left-side mass legible, while the woodland-corridor frame reserves open space and receding trunks at right. The small flower trail links foreground to the figure without pretending to be a literal road. Dark browns #140f09 and #3c2f26 establish the compressed border; pale #e4dccb appears as the distant clearing, so depth reads first as a change in brightness and only then as a line of trees. Its 0.260 edge density keeps foliage and costume equally insistent.

<Plate slug="kirsty-mitchell" id="05-shell-wait-shadows-summer-2013" label="She’ll Wait For You in the Shadows of Summer, 2013" caption="A carriage and a figure form two bright anchors joined by tangled branches and roots." />

Here the bright yellow is distributed, not concentrated. The carriage ellipse on the left and the figure ellipse on the right establish two independently scaled masses; the branch-and-root relay makes them share a floor. That separation matters because it lets the forest remain active instead of becoming a theatrical backdrop. The analyzer's palette of black-green #211d0f, olive #4a4129, ochre #a29266, and yellowed highlight #d3c594 echoes the picture's selective illumination. The historical record verifies the artwork and date but does not supply exposure, lighting, or printing details.

<Plate slug="kirsty-mitchell" id="06-pure-blood-blossom-2014" label="The Pure Blood of a Blossom, 2014" caption="A direct face is pressed into an irregular crown of flowers and falling ivy." />

The close portrait turns framing into pressure. The face ellipse is deliberately large enough to include the direct gaze and white makeup; the flower-crown ellipse and falling-ivy line show how the image interrupts that clean oval with small, irregular forms. It is not a neutral floral border: ivy repeatedly crosses the face and prevents the image from becoming a smooth beauty portrait. The analyzer records a bright mean luminance of 0.587 but a low 0.067 edge density, fitting the broad pale skin and large flower planes. White #e1e4e6 is set against leaf #32361c, with pinks and creams occurring as concentrated accents.

## yellow as a stage direction

<Plate slug="kirsty-mitchell" id="07-gaias-spell-2013" label="Gaia’s Spell, 2013" caption="The gown and ship are separate anchors, made theatrical by their repeated yellow against field and sky." />

*Gaia’s Spell* makes an outdoor field read like a stage set by repeating one artificial colour across different scales. The gown ellipse fixes the figure at left, while the internal ship frame holds the prop at right; the bow line pushes the eye back across the vessel instead of allowing the composition to break into two unrelated halves. The thirds grid fits the broad landscape, and the yellow #e5ba0e and #ddc768 are unusually emphatic against sky blue #d2e1ea and dark foliage #333c14. The image's low 0.068 edge density helps the scene read first as big coloured forms, then as a collection of crafted details.

<Plate slug="kirsty-mitchell" id="08-arrival-gaia-2013" label="The Arrival of Gaia, 2013" caption="Foreground fabric sweeps upward into a yellow figure set against the tree and distant field." />

The companion *Arrival* changes the movement from lateral staging to an approach from the camera. The giant foreground-fabric ellipse refuses to behave like a neutral foreground; its sweep moves toward the figure and then up the tree, so the subject is both a destination and a continuation of the cloth. The measured palette remains yellow-led, with #d69006 and #f3d749 against dark green #27270b and pale blue #d0dceb. With edge density 0.142, the individual ruffles stay legible enough to make the sweep a physical, constructed thing.

<Plate slug="kirsty-mitchell" id="09-let-your-heart-be-map-2013" label="Let Your Heart Be The Map, 2013" caption="A leaf hood fixes the face while windblown hair sends loose vectors out into dark space." />

The portrait's centre is stable, but its perimeter is not. The direct-face ellipse and leaf-hood ellipse establish a compact enclosure; the hair polyline shows the breakouts that drag attention across the otherwise black field. That contrast makes the face feel calm without making the picture static. The analyzer finds very dark #100803, brown #4f3623, leaf gold #c49a6c, and pale hair/skin #e4cab3, with 0.138 edge density. Mitchell's exact capture or printing method for this plate is not identified in the supplied sources.

## a path is an instruction to look

<Plate slug="kirsty-mitchell" id="10-guidance-stray-souls-2013" label="The Guidance of Stray Souls, 2013" caption="A leaf trail enters from the foreground and makes the frontal yellow figure its terminating anchor." />

In *The Guidance of Stray Souls*, the yellow leaves are a route before they are a costume echo. The polyline follows their turn from the lower foreground into the leaf-cloaked figure; the broad autumn-wood frame prevents the picture from narrowing prematurely to a portrait. The phi grid supports the deliberately frontal figure rather than a symmetrical forest. The analyzer measures 0.215 edge density, with brown #5c3f37 and #2f1d18 receiving a sharp yellow-orange interruption at #c18424. The route makes that colour contrast active: it tells the eye where to go.

<Plate slug="kirsty-mitchell" id="11-journey-home-2013" label="The Journey Home, 2013" caption="A winding leaf path passes through the wood before returning attention to the figure at lower right." />

*The Journey Home* makes a more complicated use of the same material. The low-figure ellipse prevents the small yellow costume from disappearing, but the path does not point straight to it: it enters at the bottom, recedes up the central wood, then returns the eye toward the lower-right figure. The enclosing trunks frame that detour as a real spatial interval. Brown #5b3e30 and #2a1d14 dominate the analyzer's palette, while #b87c1f gives the leaves their instrumental visibility; at 0.273 edge density, the trees have enough texture to compete with the trail. The work demonstrates how an off-centre subject can be made structurally necessary by a path that delays arrival.

## Exercises

<ExerciseCard n="01" title="Make costume continue a place">
Build or find one material that can occur both on a subject and in the setting: leaves, fabric, flowers, paper, or branches. Compose it so the eye can trace a deliberate route from one to the other.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="02" title="Give a prop equal weight">
Photograph a person with a prop large enough to be a second anchor. Keep them separate in scale or position, then use one visible relay, such as colour, branch, shadow, gesture, or edge, to make them belong to the same frame.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="03" title="Make a path delay its destination">
Place a subject off-centre in a wooded or built space. Add a route that begins in the foreground, recedes away from the subject, and only then returns attention to it. Test whether the detour makes the subject more necessary.
</ExerciseCard>
