Ren Hang's photographs make a body behave less like a private subject than a shape that can be stacked, repeated, interrupted, or placed against a field. This small selection, dated 2010 to 2014, moves between white interiors, vegetation, animals, rooftop space, and tightly cropped skin. Its overlays stay literal: body contours, a roof edge, a braid, an animal silhouette, and local axes rather than invented perspective systems. [Source notes](../../content/ren-hang/sources.md)

The analyzer's measurements describe these credited web JPEGs rather than original prints. The layered blue body study has exceptionally low edge density (0.0033), while the horse-and-branches image is much denser (0.0707). Pale gray-blue, skin, dark green, and red-brown dominate the measured palette set. That spread clarifies an important constant: even when the setting changes, a few controlled shapes carry the picture.

## bodies become a rhythm

*Untitled 16* makes two kisses and the spaces between them read as one vertical sequence. The upper and lower body contours bend into the points of contact, so the white field is not merely empty background. It is what lets each cropped shoulder and face register as a separate beat.

<Plate slug="ren-hang" id="01-untitled-16" label="Untitled 16, 2011" caption="Two points of contact and cropped body contours make a vertical sequence." />

In *Untitled 27*, arms descend as a repeated set of near-parallel bars while faces interrupt the row. The third-grid overlay is a measuring aid, not a claim of perfect order: the figures are deliberately irregular enough to keep the repetition bodily rather than mechanical.

<Plate slug="ren-hang" id="02-untitled-27" label="Untitled 27, 2012" caption="Repeated limbs establish a lateral rhythm while faces break the sequence." />

The low edge density of *Untitled 46* makes its overlapping contours unusually legible. Separate backs merge into three broad ridges. Instead of identifying a false horizon, the overlay follows those actual body lines, showing how cropping turns individual figures into a shallow landscape.

<Plate slug="ren-hang" id="03-untitled-46" label="Untitled 46, 2012" caption="Three body ridges turn overlapping forms into a shallow landscape." />

## field, interruption, and contour

*Untitled 50* puts a curled figure between the pale, granular ground and a dense mass of lotus leaves. A fallen stem steers upward toward the body; the spine contour then sends the eye back into the leaf enclosure. The green-black palette and hard flash keep the relation direct: figure, ground, foliage.

<Plate slug="ren-hang" id="04-untitled-50" label="Untitled 50, 2010" caption="A fallen lotus stem and the curved body bind ground, figure, and foliage." />

In *Untitled 54*, the frontal figure is made almost diagrammatic by the white field and the fish's dark transverse silhouette. The vertical axis is approximate, not a mirror claim. It lets the gaze, fish, and falling dark liquid read as a compact sequence of interruptions.

<Plate slug="ren-hang" id="05-untitled-54" label="Untitled 54, 2012" caption="A frontal axis meets the fish's dark transverse weight and falling liquid." />

*Untitled 66* reverses that relation. The horse is a large, dark foreground plane; the profile, red earring, branches, and shoulder contour make several small directional paths across it. Its higher measured edge density belongs to this busy crossing of organic lines, not to a deeper photographic space.

<Plate slug="ren-hang" id="06-untitled-66" label="Untitled 66, 2014" caption="Profile, branches, and horse contour create several intersecting directions." />

The back-and-dove picture is much stricter. The baseboard supplies a horizontal divide, the seated body supplies an approximate central axis, and the bird becomes a tiny, high counterweight. In the web file, that economy produces one of the set's lowest edge densities.

<Plate slug="ren-hang" id="07-untitled-01" label="Untitled 01, 2012" caption="A small dove offsets the centered mass of a seated back." />

## a staged direction

The blue dress in *Untitled 20* is held against a powder burst and a sideways sweep of hair. The overlay treats the powder as a visible event, not a precise geometric ray. Hair, face, dress edge, and burst form a chain that moves from upper left toward the right-hand hand.

<Plate slug="ren-hang" id="08-untitled-20" label="Untitled 20, 2012" caption="Hair, face, blue dress, and powder create a staged diagonal sequence." />

On the rooftop, the parapet's two edges meet at a near corner beneath the crouching body. That concrete convergence makes the long hair feel even more improbable: it rises against a pale sky while the small city grid presses across the middle distance.

<Plate slug="ren-hang" id="09-untitled-22" label="Untitled 22, 2012" caption="The rooftop corner concentrates the frame beneath a crouched figure and rising hair." />

In *Untitled 45*, a braid, flower crown, and shoulder line organize a back that is not perfectly symmetrical. The centered braid offers a stable route; uneven circular marks on the skin prevent that route from becoming a diagram. The pale blue field is among the brightest measured web-file grounds in this group.

<Plate slug="ren-hang" id="10-untitled-45" label="Untitled 45, 2012" caption="A braid gives the flower-crowned back a central route through an open field." />

The final crop removes setting almost entirely. Red-painted hands press from both sides, creating bilateral tension around the chest axis without actually mirroring one another. The palette's red-brown accents make touch read first as a graphic rhythm, then as a bodily gesture.

## Exercises

<ExerciseCard n="01" title="Build a body rhythm">
Stage three or more figures so that shoulders, arms, or heads repeat across the frame. Make one contact point and one blank interval do the most compositional work. Test a version with the figures evenly spaced, then disturb one interval.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="02" title="Use one interruption">
Photograph a figure against a pale field. Add one dark object, flower, bird, or colored hand. Move it until it shifts the whole frame without becoming a separate story.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="03" title="Make a contour lead">
Find a roof edge, branch, stem, animal back, or body contour that guides the eye. Compose so it reaches a figure or gesture, then make a second frame in which it misses. Compare the resulting attention paths.
</ExerciseCard>
