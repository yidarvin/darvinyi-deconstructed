# Alexey Yurenev: memory does not stay in one image

Alexey Yurenev's *Silent Hero* (2019–ongoing) treats World War II memory less as a recovered picture than as a changing image system. In this selection, a family portrait can be a torn object in snow, fragments can hover over a meadow, a landscape can be interrupted by opaque marks, and a scene can arrive as a grid of near-unreadable cells. The project page describes work with family photographs, archives, interviews, fieldwork, and machine learning; the visual lesson is therefore about relation: how one image is separated, scaled, repeated, or made to face another. [Source notes](../../content/alexey-yurenev/sources.md)

## distance makes an archive physical

<Plate slug="alexey-yurenev" id="01-silent-hero-01" label="Silent Hero, untitled archival portrait in winter field, 2019–ongoing" caption="A torn double portrait becomes a small, dark event beneath a distant tree line." />

The first image makes absence measurable. **ARCHIVAL PORTRAIT** and **TORN IMAGE EDGE** contain a small dark rectangle at lower left, while the **DISTANT TREE LINE** barely interrupts the snow. Its mean luminance is 0.955 and edge density only 0.0034: there is almost no competing detail. The portrait's small scale lets the empty surround describe the precarious condition of the object.

<Plate slug="alexey-yurenev" id="02-silent-hero-02" label="Silent Hero, Motherland monument in snow, 2019–ongoing" caption="A procession uses the rising steps as a narrow diagonal approach to the monument." />

The monument image reverses that proportion. **ASCENDING STEPS** pull from the small **PROCESSION** toward the figure above, but the low **SNOW RIDGE** keeps the sky broad and unclaimed. The palette, #d9e7eb, #bbd2dd, and #98bbcd, reduces monument, weather, and snow to closely related blue-gray values. Scale is carried by the staircase, not by a heroic central close-up.

<Plate slug="alexey-yurenev" id="05-silent-hero-05" label="Silent Hero, distant monument at lower right, 2019–ongoing" caption="The monument is held at the edge, so the large unoccupied upper field becomes active." />

In the later monument frame, the **MARGINAL MONUMENT** is literally boxed at the lower-right edge. The raised sword supplies the only strong upward vector; the sloping **TREE LINE** holds it down. With p95 at 0.992 and mean luminance 0.922, the white upper field is not blank background but most of the image's available visual space. The off-center arrangement refuses the promise that a commemorative figure should command the frame.

## evidence can blur, split, and obstruct

<Plate slug="alexey-yurenev" id="03-silent-hero-03" label="Silent Hero, passing train and tracks, 2019–ongoing" caption="A blurred train roof and a receding rail turn railway perspective into velocity." />

In the train image, **BLURRED ROOF EDGE** is a dark diagonal sweep, while the lower **RECEDING RAIL** keeps a second, slower geometry. The tonal range is severe, from p05 0.063 to p95 1.000, and the blurred edges do not ask us to reconstruct a clean train. They make the speed of the encounter visible: the horizontal band is pressure across the picture rather than a stable object.

<Plate slug="alexey-yurenev" id="06-silent-hero-06" label="Silent Hero, obscured landscape, 2019–ongoing" caption="A central blot and a dark horizon band make obstruction part of the scene's structure." />

The blurred landscape does not let its damage disappear into atmosphere. One **OBSCURING BLOT** is held against the low **LANDSCAPE DIVIDE**, while the **DARK HORIZON BAND** makes an interrupted horizontal. Its luminance standard deviation is 0.345 and p05–p95 span is 0.110–0.965, supporting the visible swing between nearly white passages and deep marks. The overlay does not diagnose a process; it makes clear that occlusion organizes what can be seen.

<Plate slug="alexey-yurenev" id="08-silent-hero-08" label="Silent Hero, floating family-photograph fragments, 2019–ongoing" caption="The largest portrait fragment and two smaller pieces remain visibly apart above the meadow." />

The green meadow makes separation literal. **TREE LINE** gives a low support, but the **SEPARATED ARCHIVE** path crosses only air between fragments. The **LARGEST PORTRAIT FRAGMENT** explains the asymmetric weight: this is not a continuous diptych but pieces that cannot occupy one surface. The palette shifts to #171a0b, #363921, and #9ca9af, a dark green-gray counterpoint to winter white.

<Plate slug="alexey-yurenev" id="09-silent-hero-09" label="Silent Hero, damaged portrait before stained glass, 2019–ongoing" caption="A centered archival portrait interrupts the chapel's symmetrical stained-glass architecture." />

The stained-glass image is the set's clearest collision of formats. **CHAPEL AXIS** and **CENTRAL APSE** establish a symmetrical setting, but the damaged **ARCHIVAL PORTRAIT** covers its center. Deep blue-black dominates the palette (#060a14, #222c35, #1d608a), while edge density rises to 0.216, the selection's highest. The surrounding detail does not restore the portrait; it makes the insert's rough boundary and lost portions more conspicuous.

## when pictures become a dataset

<Plate slug="alexey-yurenev" id="07-silent-hero-07" label="Silent Hero, machine-vision grid, 2019–ongoing" caption="A bounded cell matrix and its vertical center turn the picture into a regular field." />

The first matrix is nearly empty at its edges. Naming only the **CELL MATRIX**, **MATRIX CENTER**, and one **REPEATED ROW** shows how a photographic field is reorganized as a regular array. Its very low edge density (0.0114) comes chiefly from the small central grid; the white margins keep the cells from reading as a conventional full-frame scene.

<Plate slug="alexey-yurenev" id="10-silent-hero-10" label="Silent Hero, contact-sheet-like image grid, 2019–ongoing" caption="The sheet's center and a repeated row make comparison, rather than a single image, the unit of attention." />

The larger **CONTACT-SHEET FIELD** changes the same operation through density. **MATRIX CENTER** and **REPEATED ROW** keep the viewer from mistaking the sheet for one blurry landscape: attention moves from cell to cell, comparing recurrence and interruption. Its tonal spread is nearly total (p05 0.086, p95 1.000), distributed among many miniature frames instead of one subject.

<Plate slug="alexey-yurenev" id="11-silent-hero-11" label="Silent Hero, archival diptych of figure and landscape, 2019–ongoing" caption="The two panels share a page but remain separated by a narrow, active gap." />

The final diptych declines to fuse its two panels. **FIGURE PANEL** and **LANDSCAPE PANEL** retain their own frames, and the thin **DIPTYCH GAP** becomes the condition of their adjacency. At left, a body bears another body; at right, a horizon and tracks recede. The wide tonal range (p05 0.122, p95 1.000) belongs to both panels, but their unequal narrative weights keep the pair from becoming one illustration.

<Plate slug="alexey-yurenev" id="04-silent-hero-04" label="Silent Hero, solitary winter tree, 2019–ongoing" caption="A near-central branching mass tests symmetry against the blank winter field." />

The solitary tree returns the sequence to one object, but not simple stability. **TREE'S CENTRAL BALANCE** and **BRANCHING MASS** reveal an almost symmetrical center that breaks into uneven limbs. A low field line cuts through the tree rather than sitting below it. Here a measured axis is not a claim of perfect order; it is the structure that makes irregularity legible.

## Exercises

<ExerciseCard n="01" title="Make absence structural">
Photograph one small object in a large, quiet field. Identify the one line or boundary that stops the empty space from becoming generic. Keep the object small enough that distance is part of the subject.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="02" title="Separate an archive without illustrating loss">
Work with two or three photographs of the same person or place. Arrange them in a scene without letting them touch, then mark only the spatial relation that makes their separation legible. Do not add a symbolic line the image does not support.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="03" title="Turn one image into a field">
Make a grid of at least twenty small crops or near-duplicates. Change one row or cell so comparison becomes active. Then remove every annotation except the boundary, a center, and one repeated path through the grid.
</ExerciseCard>
