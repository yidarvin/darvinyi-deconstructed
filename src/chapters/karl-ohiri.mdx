# Karl Ohiri: the archive does not stay still

Karl Ohiri works with photographic memory as material rather than backdrop. In *The Archive of Becoming*, a damaged Lagos studio negative does not merely survive its chemical change: yellow-green staining, dark loss, and the pictured sitter share the frame. In *Equation for Humanity*, a stone becomes a word by being isolated, inscribed, and photographed. The six Lagos Studio Archives portraits extend the argument back into the studio, where clothing, painted scenery, flowers, a banister, and a pose make identity collectively rather than privately. The credited web renditions appear here for criticism, scholarship, and composition teaching under the documented per-image fair-use assessments in the [source notes](../../content/karl-ohiri/sources.md).

The portrait's center is a negotiation, not a clean reveal. The overlay's vertical axis at x0.562 runs through the sitter while the labelled yellow veil crosses from upper left toward the patterned shirt; its anchor at x0.592, y0.416 stays with that contested surface. A broad tonal span (p05 0.082, p95 0.875) and the palette's near-black #261f19 beside yellows #e5df76 and #c1c058 make the damage structurally loud. It is not an effect pasted over a stable portrait: it changes how the face, shirt, and background are ranked.

<Plate slug="karl-ohiri" id="05-equation-equals-2020" label="=, from Equation for Humanity, 2020" caption="Two incised strokes interrupt a centered stone with a small internal rhythm." />

<Plate slug="karl-ohiri" id="06-equation-us-2020" label="US, from Equation for Humanity, 2020" caption="Joined letters turn one modest object into a collective proposition." />

The *Equation* pictures are sparing without being neutral. Each overlay uses the strong centered field around the stone: the `I` anchor lies at x0.558, y0.633; the `+`, `U`, `=`, and `US` overlays each place the irregular stone inside a measured silhouette, while their vertical axes keep the objects from drifting in the black field. Across all five JPEGs, mean luminance is only 0.023–0.029 and edge density 0.017–0.019. Near-black #000000 dominates, with greys rather than decorative color carrying the object. That extreme reserve lets the marks behave sequentially: an `I`, then a plus, then `U`, then an equals, then `US`.

## the studio furnishes the portrait

<Plate slug="karl-ohiri" id="07-blazer-boy-with-phone-c1990s" label="Blazer Boy with Phone, c. 1990s" caption="A child's patterned blazer, flowers, and handset build a deliberate studio presence." />

The child is held near the portrait axis at x0.537, while the overlay's anchor at x0.525, y0.308 falls on the blazer and tie, the dense middle through which the handset and floral arrangement acquire their counterweight. The measured floor edge at y0.872 gives the full-length pose a firm base. The olive greens #bbba77 and #9fa06e meet dark purple #261145, and the moderate edge density of 0.0455 keeps the patterned jacket legible without making the scene noisy.

<Plate slug="karl-ohiri" id="08-two-girls-in-native-c1990s" label="Two Girls in Native, c. 1990s" caption="Paired sitters use drapery, touch, and unequal stance to make balance dynamic." />

The two figures do not mirror each other exactly. The x0.450 balance axis runs between them, the right figure's red drapery is anchored at x0.832, y0.436, and a textile diagonal carries the eye from the lower left wrap into the central embrace. This is the most articulated Lagos portrait here after the banister scene (edge density 0.1132): red #ba5047, violet #370d46, blue-grey #66556d, and orange #de9d71 make repetition and difference visible at once.

<Plate slug="karl-ohiri" id="09-cape-c1990s" label="Cape, c. 1990s" caption="An open cloak turns frontal pose into a broad, near-symmetrical silhouette." />

Here an anchor at x0.525, y0.071 catches the head and the cloak opens around a true central axis. The detected studio-floor edge, y0.864 at −2.4°, keeps the spreading garment from floating. Its low edge density (0.0168) makes the silhouette unusually clear; dark brown #362734 and #694b4a press against a yellow field of #c1b261 and #dbd166.

<Plate slug="karl-ohiri" id="10-new-york-daydream-c1990s" label="New York Daydream, c. 1990s" caption="A portrait figure meets a painted skyline and a tabletop threshold." />

The painted city is not background scenery alone. Its central axis sits at x0.500, the tabletop threshold cuts at y0.620, and the anchor at x0.756, y0.658 records the figure's right-side visual weight. Pale #b3b076 and yellow #dfd07d make the skyline bright, while #271f41 and #524854 give the figure and architectural details enough density to resist it. The p05–p95 range of 0.114–0.835 lets imagined city and local studio occupy the same tonal world.

<Plate slug="karl-ohiri" id="11-suit-and-banister-c1990s" label="Suit and Banister, c. 1990s" caption="A diagonal banister presses a formal sitter into an unusually busy studio surround." />

The suited sitter's anchor at x0.550, y0.257 fixes the bow tie and shirt front, while the overlay follows the banister rail down from the right. That rail supplies the counter-direction to the plant and painted backdrop; it stops the figure from reading as a centered cut-out. At 0.1986, this image's edge density is by far the set's highest. Near-black #150e24 and #352e3b give the suit and banister density, while #97866d and #c3b98c keep the backdrop and floor in play.

<Plate slug="karl-ohiri" id="12-untitled-c1990s" label="Untitled, c. 1990s" caption="Two yellow-clad figures, a bouquet, and a painted field make paired portraiture into an arrangement." />

The final portrait makes a pair, not a singular frontal monument. Its balance axis is x0.512; an upper-right dark surface blemish at x0.756, y0.119 pulls the eye above and to the right, slightly unsettling the portrait's centered balance. Yellow #e4ca64 is offset by violet #37314f and mauve #887a59, with edge density 0.1094 preserving the bouquet, fabric texture, and painted setting as active equals.

## Exercises

<ExerciseCard n="01" title="Let an altered surface change hierarchy">
Make a portrait in which one stain, reflection, projection, or transparent material crosses the figure. Decide exactly where that layer should compete with the face rather than treating it as an accidental obstruction.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="02" title="Make a small object behave like a word">
Place one ordinary object in a broad, quiet field. Add a single mark or change its orientation, then test whether that minimal change reorganizes the entire image.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="03" title="Build a studio with three supports">
Photograph a sitter with a backdrop, one prop, and one piece of clothing that creates a clear line or silhouette. Move each element until none of them reads as mere decoration.
</ExerciseCard>
