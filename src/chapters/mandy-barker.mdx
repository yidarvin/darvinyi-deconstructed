Mandy Barker’s *SOUP* photographs use beauty as a delay, not an excuse. The artist’s gallery identifies each dark-field arrangement with the particular marine plastic from which it was made: toys, pellets, fishing line, cup fragments, flowers, and the remains of an albatross chick’s diet. Across the 2011 series, that knowledge changes the visible logic of the pictures. What first reads as a star field, bouquet, specimen tray, or jewel-like abstraction slowly becomes an inventory of persistence. The overlays name only visible clusters, rings, intervals, and paths; they do not turn scattered debris into false horizons or perspective systems. ## fields that ask the eye to count

<Plate slug="mandy-barker" id="01-soup-turtle" label="SOUP: Turtle, 2011" caption="Blue bath toys form a tall, uneven swarm whose repeated silhouette is held together by black intervals." />

In *SOUP: Turtle*, repetition is both the image’s pleasure and its pressure. The **UPPER TOY SWARM** and **LOWER TOY SWARM** are not two subjects; their broad, overlapping bounds describe one vertical accumulation that thins into black at the sides. The **VERTICAL ACCUMULATION** path keeps the eye moving between the dense blue bodies rather than allowing one toy to become a mascot. The analyzer finds a very dark mean luminance of 0.094 and an edge density of 0.210; blue #145392 and #6591b2 flare against near-black #020305, making the field feel abundant before the source account of the *Evergreen Ever Laurel* loss reframes it.

<Plate slug="mandy-barker" id="02-soup-translucent" label="SOUP: Translucent, 2011" caption="Pale fragments make an off-center cloud whose loose right side prevents the dark field from settling." />

*Translucent* withholds a central monument. The **LUMINOUS DEBRIS CLOUD** gathers around crumpled bags, bottles, and small bright pieces, while the **SCATTERED COUNTERWEIGHT** keeps the right side from becoming empty. The gentle **RISING DRIFT** is a reading of density rather than a literal current: the objects retain their distinct edges even as they begin to read as atmosphere. A mean luminance of 0.107 and edge density of 0.266 explain why that atmosphere is not smooth. The gray ladder from #040404 through #a2a3a4 makes small value differences do the work of color.

<Plate slug="mandy-barker" id="03-soup-alphabet" label="SOUP: Alphabet, 2011" caption="Lettered fragments turn an all-over field into a sequence of interrupted acts of reading." />

In *Alphabet*, the **LETTERED DEBRIS FIELD** does not offer a sentence to be read cleanly. Large printed forms, a blue rectangle, and small colored fragments punctuate a densely granular ground, while the **DENSE LOWER-RIGHT CLUSTER** gives the scan a weightier destination. The **SCANNING DIAGONAL** is deliberately broad: it follows the way isolated signs pull attention across the frame rather than claiming a physical line in the debris. This is the selection’s busiest measured surface, with edge density 0.309, a p05–p95 tonal range from 0.004 to 0.612, and pale #b5b1ad and bright local colors repeatedly breaking #070607.

## clusters, knots, and the refusal of landscape

<Plate slug="mandy-barker" id="04-soup-burnt" label="SOUP: Burnt, 2011" caption="A low scatter of damaged fragments lets the frame read briefly as shallow terrain without becoming a landscape." />

The **CHARRED DEBRIS BAND** in *Burnt* is held low enough to suggest ground, but it is made from separate, differently scaled fragments rather than a continuous horizon. The **LEFT FLARE** gives the broad group an early visual stop; the **LOW ACCUMULATION** path then lets isolated pieces keep the eye moving laterally. The title’s damage is not made picturesque by the overlay: it remains a fact of material and tone. Mean luminance is only 0.073, while p95 reaches 0.514; pale #b3b2ab, rusted #7e6b5a, and a small blue #2475ab rise sharply from #030303.

<Plate slug="mandy-barker" id="05-soup-nurdles" label="SOUP: Nurdles, 2011" caption="A pellet core dissolves into a larger constellation, making industrial feedstock look cosmically dispersed." />

*Nurdles* makes a countable material class appear astronomical. The **PELLET CORE** is a bright, packed center; the larger **DISPERSED CONSTELLATION** shows how rapidly that density leaks into isolated points. The **DENSITY DRIFT** path is not a trajectory for any one pellet but a way to see the image relax from its center toward its peripheral scatter. The source identifies pellets from six beaches; the picture’s structure makes that distributed collection palpable. Its vertical and horizontal symmetry measures are both high (0.873 and 0.871), but the visible scatter remains loose rather than mechanically mirrored. With mean luminance 0.072 and palette #010101, #575349, #88846f, and #bbba9a, the black void is an active part of the count.

<Plate slug="mandy-barker" id="06-soup-birds-nest" label="SOUP: Bird’s Nest, 2011" caption="A central fishing-line knot gathers loose lateral debris into a tense, open field." />

The source identifies discarded fishing line compacted by tidal movement; in the picture that history appears as a **FISHING-LINE KNOT** that resists becoming an emblem. It is held within a larger **PERIPHERAL DEBRIS FIELD**, and the **LATERAL SCATTER** names the uneven, low exchange of objects around it. This is less a centered still life than a knot under pressure from its edges. Mean luminance drops to 0.050 and p95 only reaches 0.369; brown #6b4837 and blue #456770 are therefore small, tactile events inside #010001 rather than a full palette.

<Plate slug="mandy-barker" id="07-soup-fragmented-cups" label="SOUP: Fragmented Cups, 2012" caption="Pale cup shards make a thick, shallow field whose repetitions deny a single focal object." />

The repeated crescents in *Fragmented Cups* offer a visual rhythm without a stable unit. The **CUP-SHARD FIELD** holds the work as a broad carpet of pieces, while the **LOWER WHITE CLUSTER** makes the accumulation feel slightly heavier below center. The **FRAGMENT DRIFT** path is a loose visual route through changes of size and brightness, not a claim that the pieces literally align. This nearly monochrome picture is more detailed than it first seems: edge density is 0.292, p95 is 0.569, and #c3c0bc repeatedly opens against #070606 through intermediary browns and grays.

## color makes the warning hard to resist

<Plate slug="mandy-barker" id="08-soup-tomato" label="SOUP: Tomato, 2011" caption="A warm fragment mass and its rightward spill make discarded plastic seem briefly, uneasily alive." />

*Tomato* concentrates its appeal in a restricted red family. The **RED FRAGMENT MASS** gathers bits of packaging, loops, and rounded objects into a hovering body; the **RIGHTWARD SPILL** keeps that body porous. The **WARM-DENSITY DRIFT** shows how the red pieces fade outward into black rather than being enclosed by a crisp contour. Mean luminance is 0.078 and p95 0.361, so #64262c, #9b434d, and #be818f carry the image with little help from brightness. Color supplies the organic illusion; the fragment edges and gaps undo it.

<Plate slug="mandy-barker" id="09-soup-refused" label="SOUP: Refused, 2011" caption="A compressed lower cluster gives chewed and broken plastic the uneasy scale of a damaged body." />

The **DAMAGED PLASTIC BODY** in *Refused* is an interpretive grouping, not a figure hiding in the image. Its lower concentration, marked as **LOWER COMPRESSION**, makes many jagged forms cohere long enough to feel anatomical; the **JAGGED LOWER ARC** then breaks that coherence into individual shards. The source context of rejected plastic makes that oscillation particularly hard to ignore. The analyzer records mean luminance 0.110, p95 0.533, and edge density 0.267. Blue #385e7a, brown #785747, and pale #a5a3a5 keep puncturing #050506, making every apparent body unstable.

<Plate slug="mandy-barker" id="10-soup-ruinous-remembrance" label="SOUP: Ruinous Remembrance, 2011" caption="Flowers, line, and small plastic fragments build an asymmetrical memorial against a deep black field." />

*Ruinous Remembrance* resists a single botanical silhouette. The **UPPER-LEFT FLOWER CLUSTER**, **UPPER-RIGHT MIXED CLUSTER**, **RIGHT-SIDE FLOWER CLUSTER**, and **LOWER BOTANICAL CLUSTER** identify separate visible anchors rather than enclosing an imagined central bouquet. Black intervals keep these groupings distinct, so the eye moves between individual flowers, leaves, feathers, bones, and line instead of resolving them into one memorial form. The source identifies material recovered from a one metre square area of shoreline on Spurn Point Nature Reserve, England; the frame makes that local collection feel both intimate and excessive. Mean luminance is 0.064, edge density 0.129, and the muted gray-beige palette (#252522, #514d45, #817a6e, #adaeb2) gives the few flower colors unusual force.

<Plate slug="mandy-barker" id="11-soup-500-plus" label="SOUP: 500+, 2011" caption="More than five hundred pieces form a bright ring whose central absence makes the accumulation impossible to treat as decoration." />

*500+* makes enclosure do the ethical work. The **PACKED PLASTIC RING** is regular enough to be legible at once, but its individual pieces remain differentiated by color, scale, and orientation. The small **CENTRAL VOID** turns that packed abundance into a circular pressure; the **RADIAL PULL** is the eye’s return from rim to absence and back again. The source identifies the material as more than 500 pieces from an albatross chick’s digestive tract. Mean luminance remains low at 0.095 despite the ring’s brightness, while p95 reaches 0.580 and edge density is only 0.099; #ab6246, #b4afa2, and #2f6590 read as a mosaic suspended in #010101.

## Exercises

<ExerciseCard n="01" title="Make repetition change scale">
Gather many instances of one ordinary material. Photograph them first as a loose scatter and then as a dense field. Keep the frame in which the interval between things is as important as the things themselves.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="02" title="Let the background count">
Place a small material grouping against one simple, dark or light ground. Move pieces outward until the empty space begins to describe the group’s pressure, drift, or fragility. Do not fill the frame merely to make it busy.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="03" title="Use color, then question it">
Photograph a restricted-color collection so its palette first feels attractive or coherent. Make a second exposure that preserves irregular edges, breaks, and unwanted pieces. Compare which frame lets beauty and material history remain in productive tension.
</ExerciseCard>
