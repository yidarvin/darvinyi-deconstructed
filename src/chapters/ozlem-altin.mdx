Özlem Altın uses photographs as material with a memory of having been images already. The selected works do not ask a body, a face, or a view to resolve into one stable scene. Reproduced fragments, painted passages, cut edges, and gaps remain visible as separate acts of attention. The result is an open narrative: the eye moves between citations, gestures, and supports without being offered a final order. [Source notes](../../content/ozlem-altin/sources.md)

## a figure made of intervals

<Plate slug="ozlem-altin" id="01-echo" label="Echo, 2025" caption="A nearly bilateral painted field leaves a small dark knot as its interruption." />

In *Echo*, the approximate vertical balance does not make the panel calm. The overlay's axis measures the near-bilateral field, while its small anchor lands on the folded hand-like knot that breaks that balance low in the picture. A pale blue-gray field (#93b0b3) holds darker plum-brown and charcoal passages; the normalized rendition has a low edge density of 0.012 and a p05–p95 tonal span of 0.204–0.965. Those measurements describe a work that can feel both spare and unsettled: the figure is assembled through soft changes in value, then arrested by one dense, local mark.

<Plate slug="ozlem-altin" id="02-fountain" label="Fountain, 2025" caption="A pale head and dark lower cluster create a vertical relay through the panel." />

*Fountain* turns a high pale figure and a lower dark cluster into linked but unequal events. The measured middle seam gives the panel a horizontal hinge; the central axis and lower anchor make the eye travel down rather than simply reading a portrait. Near-white #f1f0ef, muted rose-beige #ceb8ac, and dark charcoal #1f262a are separated by a broad p05–p95 span of 0.188–0.969. The source record describes Altın's use of cut, concealed, reproduced, and painted-over photographs; here the visible discontinuity is the point, not a flaw to be smoothed away.

<Plate slug="ozlem-altin" id="03-mirror" label="Mirror, 2025" caption="An upper seam and central cut turn repetition into a hinge." />

In *Mirror*, the upper collage seam and near-central mirror axis make repetition legible without making it identical. A yellow hand-like fragment is the local anchor, caught where green, black, and red-brown pieces meet. The available web image has mean luminance 0.742 and edge density 0.023; pale gray-beige (#f0efee), mossy #a2a092, and dark brown-black (#2c2826) maintain separations that let each fragment retain its own source-like identity.

## seams that keep images apart

<Plate slug="ozlem-altin" id="04-mosaic" label="Mosaic, 2025" caption="A stacked blue figure is held between a high seam and a lower blue fragment." />

*Mosaic* makes its cuts unusually explicit. The upper seam, a vertical stacked axis, and the lower blue anchor do not turn its fragments into one coherent body; they show how each segment borrows force from the others. The normalized palette moves from pale gray (#ebebeb) through dusty rose (#b88f89) to strong blue (#384178), with a restrained edge density of 0.027. The apparent coherence is therefore carried as much by repeated color and vertical stacking as by contour.

<Plate slug="ozlem-altin" id="05-untitled-alignment-blessing" label="Untitled (alignment, blessing), 2025" caption="A wide orange field joins separated dark fragments into a lateral sequence." />

The panoramic *Untitled (alignment, blessing)* makes interval the subject. The overlay's central hinge lands on a black fragment, but the more consequential enclosure is the orange painted field extending nearly across the full width. Vivid orange-red #e23c06, pale warm white #f7f3ee, and nearly black brown #291611 produce the group’s broadest lateral contrast, with p05/p95 at 0.051/1.000. Instead of a conventional scene with foreground and distance, the work stages a sequence of interruptions that must be read side to side.

<Plate slug="ozlem-altin" id="06-our-light-is-a-voice-i" label="Our light is a voice I, 2025" caption="A violet field is interrupted by a lower dark figure and a middle break." />

The first *Our light is a voice* panel is almost whisper-quiet in its web reproduction: its edge density is only 0.001, and pale lavender #eae9ee, blue-violet #aca8d7, and deep indigo #302e57 form a narrow, soft field. The middle break and lower dark anchor keep that softness from becoming empty. A reproduced body appears as a trace through pigment, so the composition makes attention pause at an interruption rather than settle into a photographic likeness.

<Plate slug="ozlem-altin" id="07-our-light-is-a-voice-ii" label="Our light is a voice II, 2025" caption="Two pale figures divide the panel before the darker lower-right figure." />

The companion panel makes recurrence less like duplication than a changed relation. Its middle break divides the field, while the anchor on the lower-right dark figure makes that side visually heavier. The low edge density (0.002) and palette of lavender #c5c1dd, pale pink-gray #eae7eb, mauve #916387, and dark plum #311d34 preserve the pair's soft continuity. What changes is the direction of the pause: the eye ends at the dark right-hand figure instead of returning to a single center.

## support, scale, and public space

<Plate slug="ozlem-altin" id="08-alignment-expansion-ii" label="Alignment (expansion) II, 2025" caption="An upper blue fragment and central stack keep the tall panel visibly open." />

*Alignment (expansion) II* uses the height of the panel to postpone closure. A slightly sloping upper image edge and a strong vertical stack organize the field, but the small upper blue fragment opens it again. Pale gray #e9e8e7, muted mauve #a48084, deep violet #433a5f, and blue #5567c0 create a measured tonal span of 0.247–0.957. The overlay names the visible alignments rather than claiming a traditional perspectival depth that the layered work does not supply.

<Plate slug="ozlem-altin" id="09-pulse-and-grief-encapsulated-reactivated" label="Pulse and Grief (encapsulated, reactivated), 2022" caption="A blue trace remains distinct inside a wide red painted field." />

Istanbul Modern identifies *Pulse and Grief (encapsulated, reactivated)* as a photo print on canvas with ink and oil. Its public 640px-long-edge rendition is enough to read the large relation: a central blue trace remains active within a long red field and a sloping lower boundary. Red #da3d35, blue #345484, deep navy #111c46, and charcoal-red #772a2b make color a structural binder rather than a decorative addition. Its 0.105 edge density is higher than the softer 2025 panels, which fits the work’s insistent segmentation while not revealing undocumented original print or camera details.

<Plate slug="ozlem-altin" id="10-psyche-grow-deep-installation" label="Psyche (grow deep), 2018, installation view" caption="Courtyard edges converge toward the distant gallery setting." />

The 2018 *Psyche (grow deep)* installation view moves Altın's method out into a courtyard. The overlay follows the measured convergence of paving and building edges rather than inventing a horizon; the right roof corner is a small counterweight against the long approach. Cool pale blue #c1d6f7, concrete gray #c9c9c5, olive #9e9b81, and dark architectural gray #353530 describe the normalized documentation's exterior light. This is a photograph by Timo Ohler, credited in the source record, so its analysis concerns how the installation is framed in the published view, not an undocumented claim about the original work's fabrication.

## Exercises

<ExerciseCard n="01" title="Make a seam active">
Place two photographs or fragments so that the join stays visible. Test whether the seam makes the images compare, interrupt, or conceal one another.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="02" title="Use paint as a relay">
Add one painted or drawn passage across a photographic fragment. Let it direct attention between two existing image areas without trying to erase their separate histories.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="03" title="Build a lateral sentence">
Work in a wide format with at least four separated elements. Make the spaces between them do as much compositional work as the elements themselves.
</ExerciseCard>
