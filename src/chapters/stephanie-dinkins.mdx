# Stephanie Dinkins: who gets a face

Stephanie Dinkins’s *Conversations with Bina48* and *Not the Only One* (N’TOO)
ask portraiture to do more than preserve a likeness. In the first project, a
human artist and social robot sustain a filmed exchange. In the second, a
conversational AI is shaped by three generations of family oral history. Across
the selected display views, a face is never merely a subject: it is a site of
relation, repetition, and the terms by which a system remembers. These six
credited web-sized images are reproduced for composition criticism under the
documented fair-use rationale in the [source notes](../../content/stephanie-dinkins/sources.md).

## an encounter is built from an interval

<Plate slug="stephanie-dinkins" id="01-conversations-bina48-close-heads" label="Conversations with Bina48, 2014–present" caption="Two closely facing profiles turn the interval between their eyes into the image’s active centre." />

The paired profiles make looking reciprocal rather than one-directional. The
overlay’s two portrait ellipses and short shared eye-line stop at the gap: they
describe a relation without pretending the figures fuse into one. The display
rendition’s p05–p95 range of 0.086–0.835 separates Bina48’s gray-brown face,
Dinkins’s dark hair, and the pale room; the low 0.0191 edge density keeps the
interpersonal interval unusually quiet.

<Plate slug="stephanie-dinkins" id="04-bina48-close-heads-white" label="Conversations with Bina48, 2014–present, white-ground view" caption="Two white silhouettes face across a narrow gray interval, making contour and figure–ground carry the encounter." />

On the white-ground view, context is deliberately withheld. The silhouette
ellipses are broad enough to name two bodies but leave the facing contours
themselves visible; that narrow gray wedge does the work of dialogue. The
analysis is nearly high-key, p05 0.592 and p95 1.000, with only 0.0063 edge
density, so contour rather than texture establishes the encounter.

## the portrait becomes an installation

<Plate slug="stephanie-dinkins" id="02-bina48-fragments-7-6-5-2-icp" label="Conversations with Bina48: Fragments 7, 6, 5, 2, ICP installation view, 2018" caption="Four monitors repeat the face-to-face exchange in a compact grid while a headphone-wearing visitor turns viewing into a second encounter." />

The four monitor frames make repetition legible as a structure of both
similarity and delay. Each screen preserves the same exchange, while the gaps,
stands, and listening visitor extend it into the gallery. The bright display
view has a mean luminance of 0.741 and a p05–p95 span of 0.165–0.961; its sparse
0.0256 edge density lets the black monitor borders and the visitor’s dark figure
hold the composition together.

<Plate slug="stephanie-dinkins" id="03-bina48-fragments-17-19-20-mocp" label="Conversations with Bina48: Fragments 17, 19 &amp; 20, display image, 2020" caption="A lateral row of screens, cables, and a floor seam lets a talking portrait read as a physical gallery architecture." />

Here the three screen frames spread the conversation laterally. The overlay
keeps the black floor/wall seam as a material base and marks the portrait on the
right monitor rather than inventing a vanishing point from the cables. At p05
0.275 and p95 0.949, light monitor fields stand sharply apart from the muted
gallery palette (#97837a, #e5f0fc, #6f564d, and #1f1a1d), while the 0.0263 edge
density leaves spacing and scale easier to read than surface detail.

## composite memory, made visible

<Plate slug="stephanie-dinkins" id="05-ntoo-3d-avatar-becoming" label="Not the Only One (N’TOO), “Becoming” 3D avatar sketch, 2018–present" caption="A composite face is centred within an enormous field of hair-like marks, so individual likeness and aggregation remain in tension." />

The N’TOO avatar starts with a frontal portrait axis, then refuses the clean
outline such an axis usually promises. The face ellipse is stable and small;
the larger hair-field ellipse is ragged, uneven, and materially overwhelming.
The rendition’s very dark mean, 0.151, and p05 of 0.000 make the orange face
appear from black, while its unusually high 0.1686 edge density records the
restless gray mark field rather than a smooth photographic background.

<Plate slug="stephanie-dinkins" id="06-ntoo-making-process" label="Not the Only One (N’TOO), making/process diagram, 2018–present" caption="A starting prompt and repeated horizontal text rows make learning, interviews, testing, and revision visible as an ordered process." />

The process diagram gives an otherwise invisible system a reading order. Its
marked title, list frame, and repeated instruction rows hold the eye in a
top-to-bottom sequence; the phi grid fits this asymmetrical text block better
than a centered portrait grid. Its p05–p95 range of 0.012–0.475 and #050504,
#88837d, #4c4843 palette create a low-key field in which gray type, not a face,
becomes the active graphic material.

## Exercises

<ExerciseCard n="01" title="Make looking reciprocal">
Photograph two people in profile at close range. Keep both eyes and the space
between them visible. Make one version with a busy room and another with an
almost empty ground; compare where the relation becomes most legible.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="02" title="Repeat without flattening">
Place one short moving portrait on three or four separate screens. Change the
screen spacing and the viewer’s position, but not the video. Ask whether the
gaps create rhythm, delay, hierarchy, or a second act of looking.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="03" title="Draw a system as an image">
Turn a process you know into a single dark-field diagram. Give it one clear
starting point and a repeated visual rule for each step. Then remove any line
or box that does not actually clarify an order, dependency, or return loop.
</ExerciseCard>
