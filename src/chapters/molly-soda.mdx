# Molly Soda: a room becomes the interface

Molly Soda’s *Should I Send This?* (2015) makes a self-portrait out of the
conditions that usually disappear behind one: a dirty mirror, an Apple-logo phone,
a pink wall, a plant, a pile of clothes, a bathroom sill, a table spread with
products. The seven published frames are not a neutral survey of a room. Each
uses a device or reflective surface to turn the route of taking the photograph
into visible subject matter. The group is read here through the credited,
web-sized Dazed reproductions for criticism and composition teaching under the
documented fair-use rationale in the [source notes](../../content/molly-soda/sources.md).

## the camera stays in the picture

<Plate slug="molly-soda" id="01-should-i-send-this-01" label="Should I Send This?, frame 01, 2015" caption="Phone, hands, and a near-central mirror axis make the act of photographing the dominant event." />

The phone and clasped hands sit at the analyzer’s salient point (x0.397,
y0.433), while the overlay’s vertical centerline keeps the reflected torso from
escaping into either pink field. A hand-and-arm route breaks that otherwise
frontal address. This is an unusually low-detail image; edge density is 0.0021, and
its high-key range from p05 0.404 to p95 0.988 lets pale pink (#f4eaf6), rose
(#dcb7d6), and the bright fuchsia patch (#f76ec9) carry the setting without
competing with the phone.

<Plate slug="molly-soda" id="02-should-i-send-this-02" label="Should I Send This?, frame 02, 2015" caption="A cropped body and a screen of palm leaves divide the tall mirror frame into exposed and obstructed zones." />

Frame 02 withholds the face entirely. The torso and legs pull down the left
half, but the foreground palm leaves claim the analyzer’s salient far-right point
and repeatedly cross the body’s space. The marked plant stem is a measured
diagonal, not an invented perspective system. Its 0.0300 edge density is much
higher than the soft pink selfies: leaf edges, floorboards, and the mirror’s
scuffs make the body share attention with the room. Olive (#82693e), dark green
(#3a4d1c), wood brown (#b09472), and turquoise (#74b39e) keep that obstruction
material rather than atmospheric.

## reflection is not a clean window

<Plate slug="molly-soda" id="03-should-i-send-this-03" label="Should I Send This?, frame 03, 2015" caption="A small reflected phone gesture survives above a clothing pile that blocks a clear view of the body." />

The mirror in frame 03 is a literal inner field, yet it refuses the clean,
full-body self-portrait promised by its size. The reflected phone at x0.245,
y0.555 is small and partly masked; the lower clothing pile forms a physical
barrier before the viewer reaches the reclining figure. A p05 of 0.122 and p95
of 0.957 preserve deep dark clothing against the washed pink wall, and the
palette’s pale pink (#f1e2ec), rose (#e2a6ce), and dark plum (#2f1b23) turns the
room into a layered, not empty, backdrop.

<Plate slug="molly-soda" id="04-should-i-send-this-04" label="Should I Send This?, frame 04, 2015" caption="The cropped torso is held against the abrupt dark waistband break near the bottom of the frame." />

Frame 04 narrows the selfie to crop, garment, and room. The analyzer’s dominant
horizontal at y0.776 coincides with the start of the dark lower garment, a hard
break underneath the warm pink underwear and pale torso. The contour overlay is
not a claim that the body is geometrically symmetrical; it simply follows the
large curved mass that the cropping produces. With p05 0.110, p95 0.941, and
only 0.0115 edge density, the image keeps its transitions broad: near-white
(#e8e2e2), muted green (#777c48), turquoise-gray (#8b9e97), and deep blue-black
(#152532) work as surfaces and interruptions.

<Plate slug="molly-soda" id="05-should-i-send-this-05" label="Should I Send This?, frame 05, 2015" caption="A frontal mirror address is interrupted by the phone and by two hands converging at the face." />

The clearest direct address arrives with a precise obstruction. A central axis
runs through the phone, face, and torso, but neither the camera nor the hands
allows the face to settle into a conventional frontal portrait. The horizontal at
y0.801 is the lower mirror sill, not a horizon. This is another smooth image;
edge density is 0.0052, so the ocher yellow (#dbd09f), pink-brown (#9c5b3a), pale wall
(#e7eeea), and blue-gray (#6b87b2) divide the frame into large color zones.

## domestic objects return the gaze

<Plate slug="molly-soda" id="06-should-i-send-this-06" label="Should I Send This?, frame 06, 2015" caption="A raised arm, cropped phone, and narrowing torso turn the mirror selfie into an intimate, off-balance field." />

Frame 06 pushes the phone to the top edge and lets a raised arm cut down from
the left. The near-center mirror line remains useful as a reference, but the
actual energy comes from the arm’s arc and the torso’s curved narrowing beneath
it. That imbalance is legible despite the soft rendering: edge density is just
0.0039, while p95 reaches 0.996. Pale pink (#f7e8f1), rose (#e7b2d0), mauve
(#b18c9a), and dark violet (#443243) supply the figure/room contrast without
requiring an undocumented lighting explanation.

<Plate slug="molly-soda" id="07-should-i-send-this-07" label="Should I Send This?, frame 07, 2015" caption="A round tabletop mirror nests the selfie inside a converging domestic still life of products, books, and objects." />

The final image steps back from the body to make the room’s surfaces carry the
portrait. The circular mirror is a frame within the frame; its reflection holds
the phone while table seams lead toward the measured convergence at x0.328,
y0.275. The tabletop still life is materially busier than the pink interiors:
edge density reaches 0.0415, the chapter’s highest. Light gray (#eae6e5),
pink-beige (#cdb8b3), brown-gray (#655c59), and near-black (#312d29) keep the
books, chair, mirror, and display objects distinct, so the camera gesture reads
as one item among an intentionally crowded domestic inventory.

## Exercises

<ExerciseCard n="01" title="Keep the device visible">
Make a self-portrait in a mirror without removing the camera or phone. Move it
through three positions: dead center, over the face, and near one edge. Compare
which position makes the act of taking the photograph most visible.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="02" title="Let the room interrupt">
Choose one ordinary room element: plant, clothes, furniture, or a counter, and
place it so that it partially blocks or competes with the body. Keep the
obstruction; refine the crop until it changes the path through the image.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="03" title="Nest a portrait">
Use a small reflective object on a table or desk. Arrange a few everyday objects
around it, then make a self-portrait only through that reflection. Make the
surrounding still life describe the space rather than serving as neutral decor.
</ExerciseCard>
