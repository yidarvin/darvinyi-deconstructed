Boris Mikhailov keeps refusing the reassuring boundary between a photograph that records
and one that visibly makes its world. Across this selection, a propaganda panel, a
hand-colored portrait, a mounted page, a studio performance, a close face, an
industrial print field, and a split late image are all constructions with different
degrees of friction. The overlays do not settle their meanings; they locate the
visible devices: edge, axis, enclosure, sequence, and interruption, by which each
image controls attention. [Source notes](../../content/boris-mikhailov/sources.md)

## color as pressure

<Plate slug="boris-mikhailov" id="01-red-series-01" label="Untitled, from Red Series, 1965–1978" caption="A red Lenin panel and its plinth dominate the frame; the distant pedestrian keeps the monument answerable to ordinary scale." />

The first *Red Series* frame makes red behave less like a local detail than an
architectural force. The overlay follows the plinth edge at y0.704 and names the
red Lenin panel instead of inventing perspective out of the pale sky. The analyzer
finds a very low edge density, 0.008, and a broad tonal spread from p05 0.071 to
p95 0.941: pale gray #e5e7eb gives the panel and dark earth room to insist. The
pedestrian to the monument’s right is not a decorative dot; it makes the
object's propaganda scale visibly disproportionate.

<Plate slug="boris-mikhailov" id="02-red-series-10" label="Untitled, from Red Series, 1965–1978" caption="A banner line, central face, and narrowing procession organize the crowded political display." />

The second frame reverses that spareness. Its banner line at y0.381 crosses faces,
flags, and portrait placards, while the measured convergence at x0.411, y0.429
marks a real recession through the procession. Red #a33d18 and dark #17130f
concentrate against a near-white #ebece6 sky; p05 0.051 to p95 0.957 explains why
the sashes and flags arrive as shocks rather than as a uniform red wash. The
overlay's face anchor does not declare a hero. It shows how the picture uses one
near face to hold an impersonal display at the camera.

## pictures that disclose their making

<Plate slug="boris-mikhailov" id="03-overlays-01" label="Untitled, from Overlays, 1968–1981" caption="A flame-like figure rises across an overlaid ridge while two foreground bodies remain at incompatible scale." />

*Overlays* makes collision its subject. A ridge-like horizontal at y0.331 is a
visible meeting of layers, not a stable landscape horizon, and the line from the
left foreground figure to the flame figure registers the forced connection between
separate image fields. The plate is dark (mean 0.297) and unusually articulated
(edge density 0.122); green #43855a, charcoal #1e2525, and brown #765c4c keep the
superimposition material rather than atmospheric. The overlay is deliberately
spare because its job is to clarify the clash, not to pretend the layers resolve.

<Plate slug="boris-mikhailov" id="04-luriki-01" label="Untitled, from Luriki, 1971–1985" caption="A hand-colored print field encloses three sitters, with the right sitter held against the postcard’s central axis." />

In *Luriki*, the internal frame is part of the image's argument. The overlay
separates the hand-colored print field from the pale surround, then keeps the
vertical axis as a broad organizer rather than a claim of literal symmetry. The
right sitter's face is the measured anchor, yet the pink and yellow dresses keep
attention distributed across the group. The modest edge density, 0.012, and palette
of blue #96bbc2, rose-brown #846e63, and gray #cccaca make surface and tint carry
more force than contour.

<Plate slug="boris-mikhailov" id="05-unfinished-dissertation-01" label="Untitled, from Unfinished Dissertation, 1984" caption="A page couples an office photograph to a television image; handwriting makes their vertical hinge a sequence, not a single scene." />

The mounted page in *Unfinished Dissertation* is already an edited object. Two
internal frames hold the upper office image and the lower television image, while
the short hinge line marks the passage between them rather than a fictive depth.
The warm paper ground #e3d2ab reaches p95 0.894, but its p05 of 0.133 preserves
the dark screen and office shadows. This is why the surrounding handwriting cannot
be treated as margin: it changes the pace at which the paired images are read.

## bodies, distance, and address

<Plate slug="boris-mikhailov" id="06-by-the-ground-01" label="Boris Mikhailov before a photograph display, supplied installation photograph" caption="The supplied reproduction shows a photograph wall: a rail and receding display organize an installation view around Mikhailov’s body." />

The source note identifies this file as a supplied installation photograph, visibly
credited `© picture-alliance/dpa`, rather than as a work from *By the Ground*.
The chapter therefore does not claim a street composition for it. The overlay names
the photograph wall, the rail at y0.553, and the display
receding to the right. Its edge density of 0.081 and p05–p95 span of 0.129–0.973
make white mounts, dark frames, and the figure remain sharply separate: here the
sequence is a physical wall before it is an image sequence.

<Plate slug="boris-mikhailov" id="07-i-am-not-i-10" label="Untitled, from I Am Not I, 1992" caption="A reclining body, isolated face, and floor break turn the studio into a dark stage for a deliberately awkward gesture." />

*I Am Not I* puts a body at odds with the empty black field. The floor break at
y0.636 holds the stool and reclining figure down, while the body polyline rises
toward the hand and the anchor holds the face. Mean luminance is only 0.130 and
p05 0.051; black #111315 allows the skin colors #cebebb and #a18b87 to read as
a lit proposition rather than as naturalistic room light. The raised finger matters
because it denies the ease of a reclining pose.

<Plate slug="boris-mikhailov" id="08-at-dusk-10" label="Untitled, from At Dusk, 1993" caption="A blue print field contains a small foreground pair beneath a long, tilted urban register." />

The blue-toned print turns a winter street into a constrained horizontal field.
Its frame-in-frame names the visible print boundary, and the upper band at y0.151
keeps the skyline from being mistaken for neutral background. The foreground pair
forms a small triangular event against a much longer urban span. Blue #185276 and
pale #adcac8 dominate a mid-bright tonal range (mean 0.597, p05 0.239, p95 0.847),
so the image feels washed and provisional without losing its figures.

<Plate slug="boris-mikhailov" id="09-case-history-09" label="Untitled, from Case History, 1997–1998" caption="An extreme face turns eyes, mouth, and beard into a direct but unstable field of address." />

The directness of *Case History* is physical: a face fills the frame until beard,
mouth, and nose become competing masses. The overlay's facial axis and anchor
provide only a light orientation; the large beard ellipse is what makes the crop
read as an enclosing field. This is the set's highest edge density, 0.135, despite
a mean luminance of 0.387, because each hair and crease remains articulate.
Near-black #1d2220, warm skin #f2e4d8, and brown #886c5a prevent the encounter
from becoming a clean, easily consumable portrait.

## late constructions

<Plate slug="boris-mikhailov" id="10-tea-coffee-cappuccino-01" label="Untitled, from Tea Coffee Cappuccino, 2000–2010" caption="Linked arms and a central figure press three men toward the camera while a pale street opens at right." />

The three men in *Tea Coffee Cappuccino* make a compact front plane against an
available street. The overlay follows the linked arms rather than treating the tram
tracks as the picture's only route; their bodies are the more immediate structure.
The slight measured street edge tilt, -1.9 degrees,
keeps the public backdrop unsettled. Mean luminance 0.569 spans p05 0.071 to p95
0.957, and the pale #ebebec field makes black clothing #1a1b24 and denim-blue
accents feel especially near.

<Plate slug="boris-mikhailov" id="11-industrial-zone-01" label="Untitled, from Industrial Zone, 2011" caption="An oval picture field encloses steel beams whose converging directions create depth without making the industrial scene orderly." />

*Industrial Zone* makes its own format visible. The ellipse is not an imposed
vignette: it follows the photographed oval field, while the central axis and
measured convergence at x0.478, y0.394 give the steel an unstable depth. The
high contrast is structural: p05 0.071 and p95 0.910, with #171718 and #303135
beams cutting across blue #839fc5 sky. At edge density 0.038, the picture avoids
technical inventory: a few heavy beams do the compositional work.

<Plate slug="boris-mikhailov" id="12-temptation-of-death-01" label="Untitled, from Temptation of Death, 2017–2019" caption="A quiet seascape and a trench-bound body remain separate panels, joined only by the right panel’s curving trench edge." />

The last plate withholds a single unified scene. Two frame-in-frame marks preserve
the seascape and trench as separate panels; the right-hand polyline follows the
trench edge toward the body rather than aestheticizing the junction. The left
panel's muted blue-gray #56667d and the pale surround #d2d4dd belong to the
same restrained tonal range as the darker right panel (p05 0.196, p95 0.843).
That restraint makes the discontinuity more severe: the picture asks the eye to
hold incompatible distances side by side.

## Exercises

<ExerciseCard n="01" title="Make one color carry the argument">
Photograph an ordinary scene containing one repeated or isolated color. Do not
increase saturation afterward. Move until that color changes the hierarchy of the
whole frame, then test whether the image still works when the color is masked.

</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="02" title="Build a picture from an already-made page">
Photograph or scan a page, screen, display wall, or found image with visible
writing or borders. Keep at least two image fields and one textual or material
field in the frame. Make the joins, not any individual image, establish the pace.

</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="03" title="Keep the seam visible">
Make a two-part image in which the halves refuse to become one continuous space.
Use framing, tone, or a material edge to retain the seam. The finished image should
make the relation between the panels more active than either panel alone.

</ExerciseCard>
