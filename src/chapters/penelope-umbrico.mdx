Penelope Umbrico makes the network's repeated picture templates visible as composition. A collected sunset, a camera-app effect, a gallery wall, or an obsolete television does not remain a singular scene: it becomes one unit in a field whose repetitions, seams, and deviations direct attention. The overlays name those visible structures without mistaking a detector's incidental contrast for a landscape horizon or a technical fact. [Source notes](../../content/penelope-umbrico/sources.md)

## abundance makes a field

<Plate slug="penelope-umbrico" id="01-suns-from-sunsets-2023" label="49,317,520 Suns from Sunsets from Flickr (Partial) 03/13/2023, 2023" caption="A framed matrix and its central cadence turn many centered sunsets into one luminous field." />

The square is strict enough to make variation legible. A frame holds the serial print, a central column gives the array a measured spine, and a single row names the rhythm that keeps one glowing cell from becoming a conventional focal point. The analyzer's broad range, p05 0.227 to p95 0.922, matches the pressure between pale discs and red-orange ground; its palette moves through #d6462e, #e88831, and #ad1123 rather than resolving the picture into one sunset color. The source identifies an aggregate of Flickr sunsets, so the chapter reads the visible collection and not an unverified method of printing or capture.

<Plate slug="penelope-umbrico" id="02-everyones-photos-any-license" label="Everyone's Photos Any License, 2019" caption="Five unequal panels use their top edges and a shared baseline to turn many moon images into an archive-like cadence." />

Here the moons are not arranged as a neutral catalogue. The high, uneven skyline and low shared baseline make five panels behave like one long score, while their internal clusters continually change scale. With edge density 0.150, this is the set's most articulated serial field: white borders and small dark rectangles keep the many circles from melting together. The palette's #fdfdfc and #090a0d extremes explain the graphic force of the repeated lunar discs. The source names the 2019 work but provides no camera or printing data; the reading concerns its displayed serial spacing.

<Plate slug="penelope-umbrico" id="03-suns-from-sunsets-2018" label="39,019,893 Suns from Sunsets from Flickr (Partial) 11/12/18, 2018" caption="Row and column seams make the supplied sunset mosaic read as an all-over, equal-unit field." />

This rendition dispenses with a privileged center. The overlay follows two horizontal seams and a central vertical division, enough to show that the image's force comes from equal cells rather than a receding vista. Orange #f16d1f, yellow #f7b63b, red-brown #b03824, and pale #f9f3d5 scatter warmth across the whole field; the modest 0.027 edge density keeps it optically broad. The source describes a 2018 installation view, but the analysis stays with the reproduced mosaic visible here rather than claiming a specific wall size or display process.

<Plate slug="penelope-umbrico" id="04-suns-out-takes-birds-blue" label="Suns from Sunsets from Flickr: Out-takes/Birds (Blue), 2018" caption="A blue twelve-image matrix makes tiny bird silhouettes and the empty intervals around them equally active." />

The teal row seam and central division are factual grid relations, not a false horizon and axis. A short path across the upper silhouettes identifies the small interruptions without pretending they form one continuous flight. At edge density 0.005, almost all of the work's energy lies in broad blue transitions: #315675, #4f7e92, #6f9aba, and #95b0ae. The black birds arrive as rare punctuation. The source calls this an out-take/birds work; it does not establish exposure or original-source settings.

## the preset becomes a frame

<Plate slug="penelope-umbrico" id="05-range-masters-of-photography" label="Range: Masters of Photography, 2017" caption="Gallery corner, floor-wall edge, and receding framed works make a serial landscape installation read as a procession." />

The source view makes installation geometry part of the argument. Two measured wall edges converge toward the gallery corner, while the polyline steps through the framed works so that the nearest large landscapes dominate the approach. Its phi grid best-fit is less a rule than a useful way to notice the offset pacing of that procession. The muted palette, #bebbb9, #dadadb, #705f5d, #9f8b86, and #1e242a, lets the colored pictures emerge from a quiet room. The record identifies *Range* and its date, but gives no physical print medium or camera information.

<Plate slug="penelope-umbrico" id="06-weston-calotype-filters" label="Weston with Calotype SepiaPlate Oyster2 GrungeScratch SplitScreen and LightLeak Camera App Filters (IMG_2189), 2014" caption="A narrow pane, white gutter, and broad filtered pane make the title's named app effects read as picture-plane structure." />

The white vertical gutter divides the reproduction before its image can settle as transparent scenery. The overlay keeps that split and the broad right-hand pane visible, then follows the mountain-like contour that crosses their color flare. Low edge density, 0.009, explains why the spectral band rather than fine detail controls the view; dark #3b3833, pale #eaefee, and blue-violet #6564ee remain broad blocks. The title documents named camera-app filters, not the original photograph's technical settings or a particular device.

<Plate slug="penelope-umbrico" id="07-swiss-alps-duotone-filters" label="Swiss Alps with Duotone6 Rusty and Burn Camera App Filters (IMG_6388), 2014" caption="A hard filter seam, ridge profile, and bright descending glacier separate blank color field from mountain mass." />

The overlay's horizontal seam is a visible switch from pale upper field to mountain register, not a natural horizon claim. Below it, the ridge and diagonal glacial fall make the eye descend through an image whose texture is deliberately shallow. #d5e5c5, #735c88, #8b8aa3, and #452b61 turn a conventional alpine depth into layered color. Edge density is only 0.006, supporting the reading of the work as broad treatment rather than detailed topography. The source's title verifies the named filter effects only.

<Plate slug="penelope-umbrico" id="08-adams-splitscreen-filters" label="Adams with SplitScreen BleachCalm and LightLeak Camera App Filters (IMG_6432), 2014" caption="Two vertical seams isolate an orange exposure strip between a pale image field and catalogue-like text." />

This narrow reproduction makes a vertical edit out of its three strips. The overlay traces the two actual dividers and brackets the orange middle field; it does not claim that the central mountain creates a balanced landscape. The very bright tonal range (p05 0.627, p95 0.992) and #f2f1ef, #dedad6, #ef9d60 palette keep the page-like whites and orange intervention dominant. The supplied display rendition is a documented 600px-long-edge exception, selected because this division remains legible; no original capture settings are known.

<Plate slug="penelope-umbrico" id="09-swiss-alps-multiexposure-filters" label="Swiss Alps with NightBokeh MultiExposure Quad and LightLeak Camera App Filters (IMG_8949), 2014" caption="Two lower tiles, a bright orb, and a descending light path make repeated exposures into a broken grid." />

The lower rectangles do not simply support a landscape: their uneven seam makes segmentation part of the composition. An ellipse catches the brightest upper orb and the polyline follows a short cascade of light down and left through the central tiles, connecting repetitions without inventing a vanishing-point system. This image is unusually dense for the set (edge density 0.096), with #37373c, #65625f, #948e82, #c4bcaa, and #f2efe0 sustaining a noisy night surface. The long title documents the named multi-exposure and light-leak effects, not the original source image's camera record.

The line at y0.231 separates a pale upper band from the mountain field, and the vertical division at the right keeps the work's material boundary active while the profile falls through the lower half. At edge density 0.002, the picture is nearly all broad tone: #c98e5e, #c45350, #ba2c41, #f9b576, and #fce3a3 push the mountain toward abstraction. ## support and obsolete screens retain the image's route

<Plate slug="penelope-umbrico" id="11-not-easy-canvases-kinkos" label="(Not) Easy Canvases (Kinkos), 2013–2015" caption="An installation field, row axis, and repeated diagonal light direction make support and spacing part of the composition." />

The installation refuses to make its panels disappear. A broad frame keeps the mounted field intact, the row axis makes the uneven top and lower arrangements comparable, and one repeated beam direction shows how each small photograph echoes across the wall. Its 0.059 edge density is enough to preserve the beams, window grids, and mounting edges; #f0f0f0 and #1d1b1d set the white wall sharply against the dark works. The source describes the visible edge, seam, and commercial surface as important but does not identify a printing medium.

<Plate slug="penelope-umbrico" id="12-tvs-from-craigslist" label="TVs from Craigslist, 2009–2011" caption="A wide screen array uses a lower-row seam and scattered highlight path to keep a dark archive from becoming uniform." />

The work turns defunct-screen photographs into a panoramic rhythm. The enclosing frame establishes the overall array, the lower seam distinguishes the bottom row, and the light path identifies how a few glare points make the eye cross otherwise dark domestic rectangles. The palette, #2c2c2e, #47474e, #696971, #9e9da1, and #f2f2f2, moves from near-black interiors to white reflections, while the 0.043 edge density preserves the gaps between sets. The source supplies the 2009 to 2011 date range, not settings or a material specification.

## Exercises

<ExerciseCard n="01" title="Let repetition carry variation">
Make a grid of at least twelve small photographs of the same bright or circular motif. Keep the cell size fixed, then change only distance, surrounding tone, or local color. Test whether the array reads as one field before any individual cell becomes a picture.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="02" title="Treat an interface as a frame">
Photograph a scene through one visible division: a window mullion, reflected screen, page seam, or two-pane layout. Do not crop the division away. Revise until it changes the picture's hierarchy instead of merely occupying its edge.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="03" title="Sequence dark rectangles">
Arrange a broad strip of dark images, objects, or screens with a few uneven highlights. Use gaps and row changes to create a route for the eye. Remove one bright point at a time and note when the field loses its rhythm.
</ExerciseCard>
