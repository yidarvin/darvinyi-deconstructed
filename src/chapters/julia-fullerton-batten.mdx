Julia Fullerton-Batten's *Teenage Stories* makes a world where a girl can be taller than a house but emotionally no larger than the scene permits. The source record dates these twelve works to 2005 and identifies model villages, street-cast girls, location work, and static direction as the project's conditions. The pictures do not depend on a seamless illusion. Their power comes from making the mismatch legible: paving, roads, roofs, water, and miniature vehicles remain stubbornly measurable beside bodies that disobey their scale. The overlays name those visible relays rather than pretending to decode a story. [Source notes](../../content/julia-fullerton-batten/sources.md)

## scale made physical

<Plate slug="julia-fullerton-batten" id="01-marbles" label="Marbles, 2005" caption="Two girls and hundreds of marbles turn a model palace court into a measured field of competing scales." />

In *Marbles*, the palace baseline pins the miniature architecture in place while the two architectural diagonals travel down into the paved court. The figures interrupt that deep recession with different postures: one bends close to the ground; one stands over it. The marbles scatter across the same grid of paving, so a childlike action becomes an extended foreground system. The analyzer finds a dark mean luminance of 0.213, with near-black #020607 and blue-green #1a2c2b against the muted stone colors #575445 and #867f66. That restricted nocturne helps the orange-red vest and the tiny points of marbles feel unusually exposed.

<Plate slug="julia-fullerton-batten" id="02-reflection-in-water" label="Reflection in water, 2005" caption="A crouching figure and her reflection divide a cathedral model and dark pool into linked zones." />

*Reflection in water* turns the model church into a vertical counterpart to the crouching girl. The overlay's reflection break separates the shore from the dark lower pool, while the church-to-water diagonal ties architecture, figure, and reflected image together. Its mean luminance is only 0.135; #031212, #06292b, and #254242 keep the water and rock close to black, leaving the yellow coat as a small, insistent interruption. The image works by withholding a stable scale: the girl can read at once as visitor, giant, and a second shape made by the water.

<Plate slug="julia-fullerton-batten" id="03-po" label="P&amp;O, 2005" caption="A ferry’s horizontal mass counterbalances a lone figure held in open water." />

The far-shore band in *P&amp;O* and the nearly level ferry deck create a long horizontal answer to the figure at right. Smoke or mist partly dissolves the middle distance, so the ship's crisp white geometry has to carry the scene's order. With edge density just 0.056 and a palette of #090f11, #15272e, #25424d, and #415a6d, the analyzer confirms how few hard events the water allows. The woman is not made dramatic through gesture; she is made vulnerable by the amount of unclaimed space around her.

<Plate slug="julia-fullerton-batten" id="04-bike-accident" label="Bike Accident, 2005" caption="A miniature road and steep bank make a fallen figure and bicycle read as a blocked event." />

*Bike Accident* makes the road do more than describe location. Its diagonal climbs from the near edge toward the tunnel, and the opposing bank edge directs attention back toward the fallen figure and bicycle. Those two routes make the tableau feel like a traffic scene even though the human body dominates the miniature infrastructure. The picture is brighter than the night scenes, with mean luminance 0.376 and greens #537547 and #2a582f against gray #7e8383. The cool, ordinary daylight keeps the staged accident matter-of-fact rather than melodramatic.

## miniature routes, giant pauses

<Plate slug="julia-fullerton-batten" id="05-airport" label="Airport, 2005" caption="Runway markings and a cropped traveller make the model airport read as an oversized departure scene." />

The traveller in *Airport* is intentionally incomplete: the frame gives us suitcase, legs, inflatable ring, and a long shadow rather than a face. A runway edge pulls from the left toward the center, while the aircraft approach route carries the eye from the foreground plane to the smaller planes beyond. The analyzer records a broad p05–p95 range of 0.043–0.839, which fits the hard contrast between white aircraft, dark tarmac, and sunlit body. That tonal separation lets the red suitcase operate as a portable focal point inside a much larger field.

<Plate slug="julia-fullerton-batten" id="06-beach-houses" label="Beach Houses, 2005" caption="Two differently posed figures bracket the tiny road and repeated pastel houses." />

*Beach Houses* makes comparison the subject. A standing figure is held in one ellipse, a seated figure in the other, and the road between them gives the model village a small but precise circulation system. Neither woman owns the center; the houses and vehicles keep passing between them. This is the most textured plate in the set, with edge density 0.255. Its dark greens (#071f15, #1a432a) make the yellow, pink, blue, and green houses appear as deliberately placed color blocks rather than natural suburbia.

<Plate slug="julia-fullerton-batten" id="07-broken-eggs" label="Broken Eggs, 2005" caption="A central spread of eggs makes one reclining body and the model street share a fragile event." />

The egg ellipse in *Broken Eggs* identifies the small event around which the larger composition is organized. The house roofline makes a calm horizontal band, while the diagonal garden axis cuts through it toward the figure. Wet dark paths, pale roofs, and the bright shells distribute attention between miniature town and reclining body rather than reducing the scene to a single portrait. The analyzer's edge density of 0.225 matches that busy exchange of paving, greenery, roofs, limbs, carton, and shells.

<Plate slug="julia-fullerton-batten" id="08-red-dress-in-city" label="Red Dress in City, 2005" caption="A red-dressed figure stands above a miniature commercial street whose signs and roads converge below her." />

In *Red Dress in City*, the body is seen from behind and made into a vertical red axis. The shopfront band on the left and the street convergence below send the eye away from her, but the dress returns it to the foreground every time. This plate has the set's highest p95 value, 0.933: white sky and bright signs place a luminous field behind the saturated red. The city is densely named and advertised, yet its human scale has become too small to answer the figure looking over it.

<Plate slug="julia-fullerton-batten" id="09-girls-by-motorway" label="Girls by Motorway, 2005" caption="A close face, a distant standing figure, bridge, and lanes make the motorway a passage between scales." />

*Girls by Motorway* compresses three distances into one frame: a close face at left, a standing figure at right, and the miniature lanes in between. The bridge band prevents the road from escaping upward, while the median route and right-hand lanes converge through the center. Its green-black palette (#081810, #214438) gives the setting a wet, enclosed atmosphere, and the 0.133 edge density keeps the road markings clear without making the whole image visually busy. The still expressions make the infrastructural movement feel oddly suspended.

## enclosed night and open water

<Plate slug="julia-fullerton-batten" id="10-birdcage" label="Birdcage, 2005" caption="A foreground birdcage, seated figure, and lit miniature façades create nested enclosures." />

The birdcage in *Birdcage* is a literal small enclosure inside a larger staged one. Its internal frame sits before the seated figure; lit windows repeat the same enclosing logic behind her. The garden edge and measured convergence make the ground feel less like a natural park than a constructed passage among objects. Mean luminance is 0.221, and the analyzer's #0d1615, #2b3a28, #5d5d3e, and #968b67 palette keeps the white cage from becoming decorative: it reads as a bright, hard boundary.

<Plate slug="julia-fullerton-batten" id="11-torch-with-fox" label="Torch with Fox, 2005" caption="A fox, a torch-bearing figure, and illuminated façade bracket a dark model town." />

*Torch with Fox* uses distance as a charge. The fox-to-torch path crosses the dark foreground toward the woman, and the illuminated façade places both against an orderly model city. The woman is not acting for the camera; the stillness described in the project context lets the torch and the fox do the narrative work. The analyzer records mean luminance 0.158 and a palette beginning #100c0e and #202826, so the white dress and small flashlight appear as limited permissions to see into the scene.

<Plate slug="julia-fullerton-batten" id="12-floating-in-harbour" label="Floating in Harbour, 2005" caption="A floating white-clad body interrupts the harbor wall, crane, docks, and cargo geometry." />

*Floating in Harbour* reverses the series' usual relation between body and ground: the figure becomes an open-water shape while the small port stays rigidly industrial. The harbour wall establishes the upper boundary, the crane boom descends toward the dock convergence, and the floating figure occupies the otherwise empty middle. The plate has modest edge density (0.072) but a wider p05–p95 span of 0.063–0.808; broad gray-blue water (#586573, #35424f) gives way to white ships and dress. The photograph works because the body does not explain the harbor; it makes the harbor's scale system feel suddenly unreliable.

## Exercises

<ExerciseCard n="01" title="Make scale depend on a repeatable unit">
Photograph a person beside an object with an obvious repeated measure: paving blocks, steps, windows, lane markings, or small vehicles. Change only the camera position until that unit makes the person feel unexpectedly large or small. Keep the frame in which the scale relation remains readable without explanation.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="02" title="Let an object relay between two figures">
Place two people at different distances in one scene. Add one object or route between them: a road, path, table, stream, or string of lights. Direct neither person to perform. Revise the placement until the relay, rather than gesture, establishes their relation.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="03" title="Use a bright interruption in a dark field">
Build a low-light composition with one small bright element: a coat, suitcase, cage, window, or flashlight. Make the brightness lead toward a visible structural feature instead of merely calling attention to itself. Compare a version with and without the surrounding dark space.
</ExerciseCard>
