# Daniel Gordon: the photograph after the cutout

Daniel Gordon’s photographs begin before the camera and end after a provisional
sculpture is taken apart. In the artist’s account, internet-sourced printouts
are cut, pasted, and assembled into three-dimensional tableaux, then
photographed with an 8 × 10 camera. That sequence matters: the torn edge, an
impossible profile, and a printed shadow are not defects to be cleaned away.
They are evidence that collage, object, and camera view are sharing one frame.
The source record identifies the 2008–12 works and *Asparagus* (2013) as
C-prints; the documented 2016–20 works are pigment prints. These web-sized display derivatives are used for
criticism and composition teaching under the documented fair-use rationale in
the [source notes](../../content/daniel-gordon/sources.md).

## figures that refuse a single face

<Plate slug="daniel-gordon" id="01-nude-portrait" label="Nude Portrait, 2008" caption="A high face and low bed edge stretch the built figure across the broad frame." />

In *Nude Portrait*, the head sits high while the bed edge makes a hard lower
baseline at y0.855. The body’s long, bent route reaches across the landscape,
but the angular paper joints keep it from becoming a seamless reclining nude.
The analyzer finds a very dark p05 of 0.004 against a p95 of 0.780; black and
brown-violet surroundings (#090707, #3a2e2d, #67585b) intensify the pale,
assembled body. The overlay’s body path is a compositional reading of this
spread, not a claim of anatomical continuity.

<Plate slug="daniel-gordon" id="02-red-headed-woman" label="Red Headed Woman, 2008" caption="A facial collage is held inside a broad, enclosing hair mass." />

*Red Headed Woman* puts the collision exactly where portraiture usually asks for
coherence. The overlay marks a facial-collage anchor and the outer hair mass;
the horizontal cut near y0.466 is a visible seam, not a horizon. Blue
background (#265796) and browns (#8f6e61, #56382c) divide the image into
distinct printed materials. Its 0.088 edge density is high enough to keep the
many joins active without turning every cut into equal noise.

<Plate slug="daniel-gordon" id="03-reclining-nude" label="Reclining Nude, 2009" caption="Two low diagonals push a reclining construction into an upright, shallow frame." />

The sideways pose in *Reclining Nude* argues against its vertical container.
The near leg and the reclining edge both drive diagonally up toward the torso,
while a bed-plane break at y0.560 stops those vectors from opening into deep
space. Gordon makes the figure feel staged rather than naturally posed: the
right shoulder rose, white paper body, and blue floor remain discrete layers.
A 0.098 edge density and the palette’s dark blue (#16375d), white-gray
(#c4c7d2), and muted rose (#a3969d) describe that busy but controlled joinery.

<Plate slug="daniel-gordon" id="04-portrait" label="Portrait, 2010" caption="A displaced forehead cutout and low shoulder break make the face read as assembled material." />

*Portrait* withholds a stable frontal face even as its format promises one. The
forehead cutout sits well left of the head’s larger silhouette, and the shoulder
break at y0.655 divides the portrait into incompatible scales. The overlay
follows the head’s material contour instead of inventing a perspective system
from its seams. Pale paper (#c6b4b1), navy (#103a69), and red-brown (#884c35)
are separated by a wide p05–p95 span of 0.035–0.812.

## color becomes construction

<Plate slug="daniel-gordon" id="05-red-eyed-woman" label="Red Eyed Woman, 2011" caption="A cut-paper face and red lower shoulder build a deliberately uneven portrait." />

*Red Eyed Woman* is organized around a cut-paper face rather than a stable,
symmetric portrait. A dark and red assembled face crowds its left side, while the
red shoulder below pulls attention outward. The overlay traces the facial cut
seam and the visible paper contour instead of converting its asymmetry into a
false axis. Deep plum (#14090e), flesh tones (#c4a8a6), and blue (#17475e) make
the collage feel lit as an object rather than smoothed into a digital composite.

<Plate slug="daniel-gordon" id="06-portrait-in-ruby-and-blue" label="Portrait in Ruby and Blue, 2012" caption="A central blue profile and color-blocked planes keep the portrait from resolving into one face." />

*Portrait in Ruby and Blue* makes color do the work of cutting. The pale blue
profile stands in front of a ruby profile and dark blue shadow, while a lightly
tilted paper top edge at y0.122 reveals the tabletop construction. The measured
color blocks offset one another rather than establishing an axis. Its palette is unusually explicit: navy
#0f1c3a, ruby #7d2426, sky blue #97ccf2, and saturated blue #154ea5; its
lower 0.050 edge density lets the larger color planes carry the argument.

<Plate slug="daniel-gordon" id="07-tropical-still-life" label="Tropical Still Life, 2012" caption="Diagonal leaves and patterned table edges turn a still life into an all-over constructed field." />

*Tropical Still Life* expands the collage logic from face to table. A low edge
at y0.765 anchors the foreground; a tight pineapple leaf fan rises from the
fruit pile while the patterned cloth supplies the long diagonal route. The
analyzer’s 0.122 edge density is the group’s strongest, fitting the picture’s
many patterned intervals. Green (#1e2b1e, #7b9783), warm brown (#7d4532), and
yellow (#daae1e) make the table a field of competing surfaces, not a neutral
support.

<Plate slug="daniel-gordon" id="08-asparagus" label="Asparagus, 2013" caption="Parallel spears cross torn color fields, replacing tabletop depth with stacked paper bands." />

In *Asparagus*, four spears run across blue, green, yellow, and red paper
planes. The overlay traces the sloping upper spear and the ragged torn red-blue
paper seam; neither claim invokes natural recession. The
composition is flatter and quieter than the fruit tableau, with edge density 0.041,
which makes the white tears and individual spear shadows more conspicuous.
Color blocks (#0439ad, #9e1111, #08361b, #c4b334) become the still life’s
actual architecture.

## the still life as a provisional stage

<Plate slug="daniel-gordon" id="09-screen-selection-7" label="Screen Selection 7, 2016" caption="A literal frame and repeated orange-white marks turn the work into a measured field." />

*Screen Selection 7* makes repetition unusually spare. The literal black frame
and its central axis hold orange and white forms against a pink ground; the
line at y0.707 marks a lower repeat rather than pretending the work has a
horizon. With edge density only 0.008 and mean luminance 0.686, the work has
room to make each interruption read as a separate event. The pale pink,
yellow-orange, white, and black palette is structural: it sorts recurrence from
absence.

<Plate slug="daniel-gordon" id="10-glass-vase-with-blue-handles" label="Glass Vase With Blue Handles, 2018" caption="Near-symmetry and an oval vessel body make the paper construction briefly credible." />

The vessel in *Glass Vase With Blue Handles* is a test of belief. Its measured
axis at x0.512, the tabletop back edge at y0.560, and the broad oval body all
support the familiar silhouette, but paper joins at the base and sides undo any
illusion of glass. The proof uses the phi grid that best fits its measured
features. It is one of the smoothest images here (edge density 0.010), and its
warm pink, rust, and beige palette helps the blue handles register as graphic,
not merely decorative, interruptions.

<Plate slug="daniel-gordon" id="11-blue-still-life-with-white-peonies-eggs-and-onions" label="Blue Still Life with White Peonies, Eggs, and Onions, 2019" caption="White blooms, a bowl of eggs, and blue planes assemble a tightly staged color rhythm." />

The title’s objects are less a catalog than a set of recurring shapes. White
flowers interrupt the blue shelf system, the stems send a visible diagonal into
the bouquet, and the bowl makes a low oval counterweight. The overlay follows
those three claims and leaves the analyzer’s false perspective proposal aside.
Dark indigo (#191936), blue-violet (#484a7f), and cyan (#1083b4) stage the
pale flowers and eggs; p05 0.075 and p95 0.796 keep their value contrast
legible.

<Plate slug="daniel-gordon" id="12-peonies-and-pomegranate" label="Peonies and Pomegranate, 2020" caption="A central bloom starts a stacked still-life column that keeps exposing its paper joins." />

*Peonies and Pomegranate* closes with a tall, deliberately unstable stack. A
central peony begins the column, which passes through blue vase, striped shelf,
bananas, and split pomegranate to a cabinet below. The y0.231 background seam
is a material break, not scenic depth. Here the color system is green, cyan,
magenta, orange, and red (#4e5b18, #1382b9, #bc4d69, #ce6b17); p95 reaches
0.835, giving the white blossoms and striped support an emphatic brightness.

## Exercises

<ExerciseCard n="01" title="Build a face that will not settle">
Print fragments from several portraits at incompatible scales. Build a head from
them without hiding torn edges. Photograph it frontally, then move only one
fragment. Keep the version in which the shift changes how the viewer tries and
fails to make one face.

</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="02" title="Use color as a cut">
Make a still life from objects or printouts with four emphatically different
colors. Place color boundaries where a conventional composition would place
depth cues. Photograph from one fixed point and ask whether color planes, rather
than perspective, now organize the image.

</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="03" title="Let the support remain visible">
Build a small arrangement on a table or paper field. Do not crop out seams,
tape, overlaps, or the edge of the support. Make a frontal photograph in which
those construction traces clarify the picture’s geometry instead of reading as
accidental mess.
</ExerciseCard>
