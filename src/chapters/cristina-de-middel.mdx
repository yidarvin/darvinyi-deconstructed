Cristina de Middel’s *The Afronauts* (2012) takes a historical Zambian space-programme ambition as the occasion for a deliberately staged photographic fiction. ICP describes the project as staged reenactment; the photographs here make their construction legible through props, costume, square framing, and settings that are never permitted to become neutral background. The overlays stay literal: face, rocket, elephant, dune, helmet, ridge, and hose, so the visual argument does not need to pretend that a tonal split is a horizon or a detector’s corner signal is a subject. ## evidence made visible

<Plate slug="cristina-de-middel" id="01-umfundi" label="Umfundi, from The Afronauts, 2012" caption="A resting face, lifted hand, and tabletop model rocket set private reverie against a field of handmade stars." />

In *Umfundi*, the face leans into the hand while the toy rocket rises separately from the lower left. That triangular relay makes the dream feel composed rather than observed: a face, a gesture, and an object are all given equal pictorial force. The image has a broad p05–p95 luminance span of 0.086–0.984 but low edge density (0.048), so the weathered wall and star shapes read as a few large, quiet tonal fields. The analyzer’s palette moves from brown-black #2a1f1e through gray #97a3a3 to near-white #f9f9f8; the red shirt and rocket punctuate that restrained range.

<Plate slug="cristina-de-middel" id="02-butungakuna" label="Butungakuna, from The Afronauts, 2012" caption="An elephant’s cropped head meets a helmeted, patterned figure at close range; scale and costume turn contact into a story." />

*Butungakuna* makes the elephant’s left-side mass and the curved helmet answer each other. The trunk-to-suit contact is more important than a conventional portrait center: it joins two incompatible scales without smoothing their difference. Its 0.081 edge density is higher than the other close-up portraits here, because textile pattern, skin, trunk, and helmet all remain legible. Yet the measured palette stays muted: #26211b, #4d453b, #787367, #aeb4b2, and #fbfcfb, letting patterned fabric register as a material interruption rather than mere decoration.

## the expedition as a small figure

<Plate slug="cristina-de-middel" id="03-hamba" label="Hamba, from The Afronauts, 2012" caption="A climbing figure interrupts the dune’s long diagonal, making a broad sky feel like an intentionally sparse stage." />

In *Hamba*, the dune ridge rises toward the right while the lifted leg and pack interrupt it near the lower-left third. The overlay names that actual slope rather than calling it a horizon. The emptiness above is active: mean luminance is 0.516 and edge density only 0.033, so the pale sky becomes a field against which the compact patterned figure can appear improbable and determined. The neutral palette, from #3f362a and #675b4a to #a1a6af and #fefefd, keeps the scene close to an album-like, faded register without making any unverified claim about how it was photographed.

<Plate slug="cristina-de-middel" id="04-yinqaba" label="Yinqaba, from The Afronauts, 2012" caption="A reflective helmet gathers gloved hands and a reflected head into one compressed, unstable frontal image." />

*Yinqaba* nearly turns a face into a camera obscura. The large reflective helmet contains the reflected head while the two gloves close in from the sides; the composition is frontal but not mechanically symmetrical. With p05–p95 at 0.125–0.996 and edge density at just 0.029, smooth highlight and shadow carry more of the image than fine detail. Its palette shifts from #3d2d2a to #f7f9f7 through pale greened neutrals, making reflection feel both bodily and metallic.

## night portrait, built portrait

<Plate slug="cristina-de-middel" id="05-bongo" label="Bongo, from The Afronauts, 2012" caption="A small animal silhouette holds an irregular ridge beneath a sparse field of lights and points." />

The small silhouette in *Bongo* would disappear without the ridge. The overlay traces that uneven edge and the animal’s back-and-head contour rather than claiming a neat horizon or identifying the bright points as astronomical stars. This is the darkest plate: mean luminance is 0.199, p05 is 0.039, and edge density only 0.010. Against a palette anchored in #100c0e and #2a2a2f, the scattered white lights become isolated marks, leaving the animal as the picture’s fragile hinge.

<Plate slug="cristina-de-middel" id="06-jambo" label="Jambo, from The Afronauts, 2012" caption="A helmet aperture frames the face while hose, raffia collar, retaining wall, and scrap mound declare the heroic portrait assembled." />

*Jambo* is a frontal portrait with visible interruptions. The helmet’s opening holds the face, the breathing hose curves downward and across the body, and the scrap mound rises behind the head; together they refuse the seamless authority of a conventional astronaut image. This is the chapter’s most texturally active image after *Butungakuna* (edge density 0.066), with a large tonal span from p05 0.059 to p95 0.992. Browns (#2c1d10 and #634e34), pale blue-green #b4bdba, and #eff1f1 let the white helmet stand against the rusted background without erasing the photographed materials.

## Exercises

<ExerciseCard n="01" title="Make a prop do narrative work">
Photograph a person with one ordinary object that changes the picture’s implied story. Arrange the object so it forms a visible relation (diagonal, echo, or interruption) with the face or body; do not rely on a caption to make it meaningful.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="02" title="Use an empty field">
Place a compact figure against a large open area of sky, wall, or ground. Move the figure until the blank field produces pressure rather than simply unused space, then identify the real edge or slope that lets the figure hold the frame.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="03" title="Let the construction show">
Make a portrait using costume, cable, fabric, or a reflective surface. Keep at least one join, material edge, or prop visibly in the frame so the image can be fantastical without asking the viewer to mistake it for transparent evidence.
</ExerciseCard>
