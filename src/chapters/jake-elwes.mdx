Jake Elwes's photographs and screen works make machine vision answerable to a frame. A generated or transformed face is never presented here as frictionless output: it is made to occupy a repeated tile, an outdoor screen, a gallery route, or a paced performance strip. That insistence on a visible support is what makes the work useful for photographic study. Composition does not merely beautify a synthetic image; it reveals the categories, intervals, and exclusions through which that image is made to appear. The overlays name only structures visible in the supplied whole-frame renditions. Project dates, credits, access routes, and image-by-image fair-use rationale remain in the [source notes](../../content/jake-elwes/sources.md).

## a dataset becomes a field of comparisons

<Plate slug="jake-elwes" id="01-zizi-queering-dataset-prints" label="Zizi - Queering the Dataset, print series, 2019–ongoing" caption="Twenty-four synthetic portraits turn a regular field into a way of comparing face, gaze, colour, and deformation." />

The print field's strength is its refusal to let a single face settle into the authority of a conventional portrait. The overlay's field boundary and last-row baseline make the grid's serial logic explicit: each square is independent enough to differ, but close enough to make difference compare itself. The image spans p05 0.059 to p95 1.000, with pale `#f4f4f3`, near-black `#1f1a1d`, warm `#c1775d`, and rose-brown `#6d4e4d` distributed across the faces. Its 0.086 edge density is restrained for so many images, which leaves colour, makeup, and distortion as the main variables rather than a mass of texture.

## CUSP makes an image plane visible

<Plate slug="jake-elwes" id="05-cusp-generated-bird-01" label="CUSP, generated-bird still I, 2019" caption="A pale bird image sits inside a bounded projection plane, held against the dark horizontal bands of the Essex marsh." />

The first *CUSP* still is built from a productive mismatch of scales. The `IMAGE PLANE` is compact and centered, but its bird is much brighter and more descriptive than the surrounding marsh. The symmetry axis makes that placement emphatic; the tidal band at y0.571 then runs through both site and projection. Gray-black palette values from `#1c1b1d` to `#646061`, a p05–p95 range of 0.086–0.494, and a relatively active 0.160 edge density keep the ground materially present instead of treating it as an empty backdrop.

<Plate slug="jake-elwes" id="06-cusp-generated-bird-02" label="CUSP, generated-bird still II, 2019" caption="The screen's hard top edge and centered bird contrast a bright inserted image with a nearly even tidal ground." />

The second still is even more economical. The screen's upper boundary at y0.332 and vertical center isolate an intact bird within a dark, uneven expanse. The bird sits at the analyzer's anchor near x0.554, y0.540, so the slight displacement inside the centered plane keeps it from reading as a static emblem. Here p05 0.063 to p95 0.612 separates light projection from charcoal `#161615` and gray `#474749`; the 0.085 edge density lets the rectangular intervention do most of the work.

<Plate slug="jake-elwes" id="07-cusp-generated-bird-03" label="CUSP, generated-bird four-panel image, 2019" caption="A four-panel division makes intact and degraded bird information legible as adjacent, unequal states." />

The four-panel image asks recognition to move between panels rather than resolve in one. The horizontal and vertical seams divide a clean bird, a blurred body, a cropped beak, and a degraded reflection-like figure; the anchor on the right-panel bird marks the point where ordinary description is most intact. Its values are unusually light and compressed: p05 0.412, p95 0.812, with a minute 0.008 edge density. That softness is not a lack of structure: it makes the panel division, rather than sharp detail, carry the comparison.

<Plate slug="jake-elwes" id="08-cusp-generated-bird-04" label="CUSP, Essex-marsh working view, 2019" caption="Operator, light, and angled white screen disclose the apparatus that inserts the bird image into the marsh." />

The working view gives the project a necessary reverse angle. The ellipse gathers operator and screen while the anchor identifies the light fixture; together they make the produced image inseparable from its physical setup. The broad mud shelf at y0.296 sets a horizontal counterweight to the tilted white board. The palette runs from near-black `#262524` and rock-gray `#514c46` to pale `#dcdcd6`, with a p05–p95 span of 0.133–0.831. At 0.093 edge density, clothing, tripod, board, and cracked mud retain distinct roles without overwhelming the scene.

## performance distributes a body across space and time

The nearest performer is not merely larger than the later screens; the floor curve joins its luminous lower edge to a gallery convergence at x0.407, y0.567. The screen band is therefore an architectural route, not a neutral display rail. Dark `#1b1818`, brown `#523b2d`, and blue `#1f2e57` hold a p05–p95 range of 0.047–0.639; the low 0.052 edge density preserves the portrait's glow against the dark room.

<Plate slug="jake-elwes" id="10-the-zizi-show-va-still-02" label="The Zizi Show, V&A installation still II, 2023" caption="A small serial screen bank and a large projected trio share one room through ceiling convergence and an open floor." />

The second V&A view splits attention across two incompatible scales: a tight bank of individual faces at left and a trio of bodies projected at right. This overview makes the performance read as a three-part stage. ## Exercises

<ExerciseCard n="01" title="Make repetition compare rather than decorate">
Photograph or collect six to twelve similarly framed subjects. Keep one variable stable, such as distance, background, or format, while allowing another to vary. Arrange them as a field and ask what each image makes visible about the others.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="02" title="Show the support, not only the image">
Place a photograph, projection, or phone screen in a materially different setting. Include enough of the support, surrounding surface, and light source that the image reads as an intervention in a place rather than a floating file.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="03" title="Build a sequence without a single climax">
Make a horizontal strip of five or more related poses or actions. Use repeated gesture, directional change, or changing scale to create a path for the eye. Resist choosing one image as the sole winner; let the interval itself carry the meaning.
</ExerciseCard>
