James Coupe's image systems never arrive as transparent windows. A gallery wall, façade, cage, screen border, subtitle strip, or receding run of paintings keeps declaring the means by which a person becomes a signal. Across surveillance panoramas, platform-work recordings, and deepfake installations, the decisive compositional move is to make a classifier's support visible. The overlays therefore identify only pictured structures in the supplied web renditions; project histories, credits, and fair-use rationale are in the [source notes](../../content/james-coupe/sources.md).

## surveillance becomes a sequence

<Plate slug="james-coupe" id="01-panoptic-panorama-1" label="Panoptic Panorama #1, installation view, 2012" caption="A visitor's gaze joins a receding monitor array, exposing a supposedly empty surveillance panorama as a staged viewing event." />

In *Panoptic Panorama #1*, the visitor's `GAZE TOWARD SCREENS` and the `MONITOR ARRAY` put an interpersonal vector into an automated view. The artist describes five cameras and movement filtering that produce an apparently empty 360-degree panorama from different recent intervals. The documentation does not picture that system as seamless: the receding screens and the viewer's profile keep the camera's selective absence embedded in a room. The supplied rendition is relatively spare at 0.020 edge density, yet spans p05 0.020 to p95 0.824; blue `#62a0de` screens read as small bright events against charcoal `#0a0f10` and gallery gray.

<Plate slug="james-coupe" id="02-panoptic-panorama-2" label="Panoptic Panorama #2: Five People in a Room, installation view, 2013" caption="The shallow panorama turns faces and a continuous subtitle strip into a synthetic lateral narrative." />

The five-panel strip of *Panoptic Panorama #2* makes montage feel like a single field. `DISTRIBUTED FACES` crosses the `FIVE-SCREEN PANORAMA`, while the `SUBTITLE BAND` supplies a low, continuous counterline. Coupe's record describes a system that admits one face per screen and uses age/gender profiles to select Facebook-status subtitles. Here that technical structure is legible as pacing: separate encounters are held in a common horizontal duration. The file is dark: mean luminance 0.101, p05 0.000, p95 0.361; its 0.112 edge density retains faces, screens, and lettering. Near-black `#070607` and gray `#444347` let the pale blue `#bdcee9` display fragments register as interruptions.

<Plate slug="james-coupe" id="03-sanctum" label="Sanctum, with Juan Pampin, façade installation documentation, 2013" caption="A receding forecourt leads to an illuminated, windowed monitor bay where public movement is made into a classified display." />

*Sanctum* puts a façade-scale grid behind an individual encounter. The `CONCRETE CANOPY EDGE` sets a hard architectural division, and the `FORECOURT RAILING EDGE` steers the eye toward the `WINDOWED PROJECTION BAY`. Coupe and Pampin's system tracked approaching people, classified age and gender, and joined screen imagery with social-media material; the Henry Art Gallery confirms the project's 2013–15 façade context. The composition does not reduce that system to a face close-up: the passer-by, building, and multiple screens remain inseparable. A mean luminance of 0.480 and p05–p95 range of 0.180–0.824 preserve both warm window light and darker brick, while 0.066 edge density makes the grid readable without turning it into visual noise.

<Plate slug="james-coupe" id="04-swarm" label="Swarm, installation view, 2013" caption="A central mast and a hovering ring of monitors arrange several viewers into an explicit field of observation." />

The `CENTRAL MAST` gives *Swarm* an almost diagrammatic center, but the `SUSPENDED MONITOR RING` prevents that center from becoming stable. The two gaze paths record viewers looking up from opposite sides, so the work's classifier is perceived as an overhead arrangement rather than an invisible process. Coupe describes stereo cameras that profile and locate people before assembling groups and panoramas; the photograph makes that aggregation spatial through repetition, suspension, and distance. Its warm palette, `#c29d7c`, `#e7c2a1`, and dark brown `#22130d`, is broad in value (p05 0.043, p95 0.827), while a low 0.025 edge density keeps attention on the sparse monitor crown and bodies.

## labor appears through a frame

<Plate slug="james-coupe" id="05-general-intellect" label="General Intellect, video still, 2015" caption="Fence, driveway, and stair rail turn an otherwise ordinary camera view into a visibly constrained passage." />

The strength of this *General Intellect* still is its refusal to deliver a centered subject. `RECEDING CHAIN-LINK FENCE`, `DRIVEWAY EDGE`, and `STAIR RAIL` all aim into a narrow middle distance, making the image's empty foreground an interval to cross. Coupe's project used hourly worker-made recordings with captions and metadata, queried from an expanding database. In that context, an unremarkable exterior is not an establishing shot: it is a unit selected, captioned, and placed inside a labor system. The broad pale pavement produces mean luminance 0.678 and p95 0.898; subdued `#dfdcd4`, `#8a8779`, and `#676358` hold edge density to 0.034, so the few directional boundaries do the compositional work.

<Plate slug="james-coupe" id="06-watchtower" label="Watchtower, video still, 2017" caption="A hand-drawn border and shop façade make an apparently simple dream narrative visibly mediated." />

Here the `DRAWN DREAM PANEL` is an image inside an image. Its crude outline contains two stick figures and the `SHOP FACADE`; the roof line gives the small architecture a direction before subtitles interrupt the lower edge. Coupe's *Watchtower* organized thousands of Mechanical Turk uploads through a tower and a sixteen-monitor display, so the drawn scene matters precisely as an account delivered through an interface rather than direct access to a person. The rendition's palette, gray `#8c8a80`, `#78776d`, blue-gray `#363c3f`, and pale `#cfd2d7`, is modest, with p05 0.278, p95 0.761, and edge density 0.044. The panel boundary, rather than tonal drama, holds the scene together.

<Plate slug="james-coupe" id="07-i-am-not-a-robot" label="I am not a robot, installation documentation, 2019" caption="A suspended gripper, orange guard, and wire enclosure give an algorithmic workplace literal industrial geometry." />

*I am not a robot* makes coercion architectural. The `SUSPENDED GRIPPER` hangs in a blank field before the `WIRE ENCLOSURE`; `CAGE CROSSBAR`, `DIAGONAL BRACE`, and `ORANGE SAFETY GUARD` then convert the right half into a mechanical threshold. Coupe describes a worker cage and a personality-test screen associated with employment and Cambridge Analytica contexts. The pictured machinery does not stand in for an abstract metaphor: the frame makes its separation, barrier, and scale physically readable. White `#f3f6f5`, steel blue `#2e3b4d`, and orange `#dd5f1a` produce a high-key range (p05 0.196, p95 1.000) with an active 0.096 edge density. The supplied documentation file has Hasselblad H6D metadata, but that identifies the documentation photograph only, not the installation's technical specification.

## deepfake images retain their seams

<Plate slug="james-coupe" id="08-warriors-generative-adversarial-network" label="Warriors: Generative Adversarial Network, installation still, 2020" caption="A close face, hair mass, and zipper compress a face-swapped figure into an intimate vertical portrait." />

The close view of *Warriors: Generative Adversarial Network* uses `FACE EMERGING FROM SHADOW` as its main boundary, with the `HAIR MASS` and `JACKET ZIPPER` closing the frame around it. The artist describes customized Deepfakes and classification by gender-related markers, and notes that the substitutions may vary in speed and resolution. That variability is crucial here: the portrait is intimate but not settled, an appearance maintained by a dark, enclosing field. Mean luminance is only 0.066 and p95 0.227; near-blacks `#040202`, `#0f0b0a`, and `#1e1819` make the visible face feel less like a stable character than a precarious emergence. Edge density 0.082 preserves the zipper as a material countercurve.

<Plate slug="james-coupe" id="09-warriors-war" label="Warriors: War, installation still, 2020" caption="A carriage partition and four-face rhythm make altered identities compare across one shared screen frame." />

In *Warriors: War*, the `CENTRAL PARTITION` and `REAR WINDOW` split the carriage into two pairs, while `FOUR-FACE RHYTHM` makes the heads rise and fall slightly across the width. Coupe's work applies classifiers using demographic, economic, and occupational markers; the photographed frame turns that sorting into a confrontational side-by-side comparison rather than an invisible operation. The color range is restrained (p05 0.012, p95 0.561, mean 0.262), with brown-black `#100d0c` and `#3c302b` holding the group together while gray `#a29ca1` lifts faces and carriage surfaces. At 0.089 edge density, seams, clothing, and window divisions remain legible.

<Plate slug="james-coupe" id="10-warriors-sixty-thousand-soldiers" label="Warriors: Sixty Thousand Soldiers, installation still, 2020" caption="Two faces carry unequal weight across a compressed, low-key crowd frame." />

The panoramic compression of *Sixty Thousand Soldiers* does not give every figure equal access to recognition. `LEFT PORTRAIT` and `RIGHT PORTRAIT` remain distinct while `TWO-FACE AXIS` crosses the field between them; the brighter right face becomes the image's visual pressure point. Coupe describes grouping faces into gangs through classificatory markers, and this long frame makes grouping look like a problem of distribution and unequal emphasis. It is the darkest supplied rendition: mean 0.051, p05 0.004, p95 0.165, with black `#030202` and blue-black `#465464`. The relatively high 0.148 edge density comes from crowd texture and contour, not from a brightly articulated scene.

<Plate slug="james-coupe" id="11-the-chinese-rooms" label="The Chinese Rooms, installation documentation, 2020" caption="A nearest painting begins a receding gallery route of framed faces and commissioned image labor." />

*The Chinese Rooms* turns repetition into an argument about production. The `NEAREST PAINTING` claims the left foreground, while the `FLOOR-WALL EDGE` and `RECEDING PAINTINGS` turn the rest of the gallery into a paced procession. Coupe's record identifies ten oil-on-canvas works made through Instapainting.com with named painters and links the project to the Chinese Room thought experiment. The installation view lets the commissioned portraits read neither as isolated paintings nor as a frictionless image stream: their frames, intervals, and recession keep human production in view. The bright gallery rendition has mean luminance 0.667, p05 0.157, p95 0.976, and just 0.014 edge density. White `#eeece8` and `#cec9c2` leave the blue `#1f2d3c` and brown `#785537` paintings as measured interruptions. Its iPhone X metadata belongs only to the supplied documentation photograph.

## Exercises

<ExerciseCard n="01" title="Make the apparatus part of the frame">
Photograph a screen, sensor, or interface together with enough of its room, mount, cables, or viewer to show how it organizes attention. Identify one boundary that makes the image system visible.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="02" title="Turn a sequence into an argument">
Make a lateral sequence of four to six portraits or found images. Add one continuous element, such as a caption band, ledge, rail, or shared background, that makes comparison unfold across the whole strip.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="03" title="Use a frame to show unequal visibility">
Compose two figures or faces at different scales or brightnesses within one wide frame. Keep both present, but let the surrounding architecture or darkness make one command more attention. Describe what created the imbalance.
</ExerciseCard>
