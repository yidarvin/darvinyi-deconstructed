Idris Khan does not make a sequence settle into a representative image. He makes its repetitions remain present at once. Turner postcards turn into weather, the Brown Sisters into a collective body, Bach into a field of pressure, and the Bechers into architectures that tremble because their repeated views will not agree perfectly. The overlays make only the visible terms of that transformation: axis, frame, band, mass, and interval, rather than inventing a stable camera space where Khan has deliberately made one collapse. ## accumulation turns scene into field

<Plate slug="idris-khan" id="01-every-william-turner-postcard" label="every…William Turner Postcard from Tate Britain, 2004" caption="A dissolving dark tree band crosses the lower field while a bright accumulated atmosphere holds above it." />

In *every…William Turner Postcard from Tate Britain*, the line at y 0.609 is not a dependable landscape horizon. It is the dark, repeatedly impressed tree band that divides an otherwise uncertain atmospheric field. The overlay’s broad central ellipse names the luminous accumulation, and its short path follows one surviving vertical trace rather than pretending that the whole image has retained discrete trees. The normalized derivative is exceptionally spare, with edge density 0.0013, a low mean luminance of 0.320, and a range from p05 0.024 to p95 0.643. Brown-black #1f0601, olive #5f5d33, and muted yellow-green #aba25d make the work feel less like a view than like a memory of many views held in one weather system.

<Plate slug="idris-khan" id="02-every-nicholas-nixon-brown-sisters" label="every…Nicholas Nixon’s Brown Sisters, 2004" caption="Repeated figures fuse around a central group spine; the left and right contours retain the pressure of individual bodies." />

The Brown Sisters have been overlaid until portrait likeness becomes a problem of collective silhouette. A vertical spine at x 0.525 runs through the group, but the work is not literally symmetrical: the two contour paths preserve the way bodies leak outward on either side of the central mass. The large ellipse is intentionally a mass, not a claim that a single face can still be isolated. The tonal span is wide, p05 0.012 to p95 0.800, while edge density stays modest at 0.021. In the grayscale palette of near-black #0e0e0e, mid-gray #5a5a5a, and pale #d2d2d2, repetition makes the sitters hover rather than resolve.

## pages and typologies become architecture

<Plate slug="idris-khan" id="03-bach-six-suites-solo-cello" label="Bach… Six Suites for the Solo Cello, 2006" caption="The accumulated page field is organized as horizontal notation registers around a central score-block axis." />

The Bach work refuses the analyzer’s tempting diagonals: those are collisions of notation, not a depicted one-point space. Instead, the overlay keeps to the bounded score field, the centered axis, and two literal horizontal registers whose repeated marks make the page feel architectural. This is the chapter’s densest edge field at 0.215, with p05 0.169 and p95 0.886. Dark #353438, middle grays #5e5d61 and #848287, and pale #dad9dc register changes in deposited notation as rhythm. The source identifies the work as a 2006 Lambda digital C-print; it does not document the capture settings of any source material or the technical settings of its production.

<Plate slug="idris-khan" id="04-every-becher-gasholder" label="Every… Bernd and Hiller Becher Gasholder, 2008" caption="A centered print field and its vertical axis hold a trembling dome against an expansive pale surround." />

The 2008 gasholder is nearly the inverse of Bach: very little contour is needed to make its form insist. The overlay distinguishes the actual central print field from the pale outer surround, then puts the dome and its vertical axis inside that small enclosure. The measured base at y 0.687 belongs to the lower edge of that image field, not to a city horizon. Mean luminance reaches 0.836 and edge density falls to 0.0010; pale #ece7e6 and #dfd9d1 leave the gray-brown dome #726c6b as a faint but inescapable event. The Government Art Collection record calls this work a lithograph, a distinct medium that the chapter preserves rather than absorbing into a generic notion of photographic print.

<Plate slug="idris-khan" id="05-every-becher-prison-gasholder" label="every…Bernd and Hilla Becher Prison Type Gasholder, 2004" caption="A central axis, tall print field, and dark accumulated core make the gasholder a compressed vertical mass." />

In the prison-type gasholder, the industrial typology has become a dense upright core whose edges cannot stop vibrating. The tall field and its central axis are visible organizers; the ellipse names the accumulated mass, while the upper band at y 0.106 marks surviving scaffolding rather than a landscape line. This derivative has a much larger tonal and edge range than the pale 2008 work: mean 0.399, p05 0.122, p95 0.875, and edge density 0.138. Charcoal #28282b and #464547 make the dark center heavy; #d8d9d8 and #a9a6a9 let the peripheral layers stay legible as a halo. The cited Phillips record identifies it as a chromogenic print.

<Plate slug="idris-khan" id="06-every-becher-spherical-gasholders" label="EVERY … BERND & HILLA BECHER SPHERICAL TYPE GAS HOLDERS, 2004" caption="The centered ellipse follows the hovering spherical accumulation while the axis makes the archive’s unstable symmetry visible." />

The spherical gasholders compress a whole typology into an almost planetary form. The ellipse is not a decorative graphic: it follows the actual boundary where repeated framework densifies into a sphere, while the axis at x 0.500 registers the compositional pull toward centeredness. The upper structural band at y 0.134 is retained as a thin surviving register above the sphere. A mean luminance of 0.384 and p05–p95 range of 0.055–0.796 give the work a deep but not closed tonal scale; #191419 and #3d393f sit against gray-lilac #686569 and #c4c3bb. Sotheby’s identifies the finished work as a pigment print.

## the late work makes the support visible

<Plate slug="idris-khan" id="07-lost-happiness" label="Lost Happiness, 2019" caption="The framed blue field, low frame edge, and centered mark field make accumulation read as a contained but active surface." />

*Lost Happiness* presents a decisive frame within the installation view: the overlay follows that blue interior field and its lower edge instead of treating gallery wall and floor as part of Khan’s image. Within it, the broad ellipse names the concentrated mark field. The source identifies the work as a 2019 digital C-print. Its derivative is extreme in tonal span, from p05 0.031 to p95 0.980, with a mean of 0.564 and edge density 0.042. The bright #f6f5f6 surround, nearly black #0f1523, and blue #283d5f/#4d75b6 let the dense accumulation appear to radiate without requiring any fictional perspective.

<Plate slug="idris-khan" id="08-blue-rhythms" label="Blue Rhythms, 2019" caption="A two-row eighteen-panel field sits on the installation axis above the wall-to-floor seam, turning separate works into a single cadence." />

*Blue Rhythms* is the chapter’s clearest statement that the unit of accumulation can remain visibly plural. The overlay’s frame contains the two-row panel field; a short horizontal path marks the rhythm of the upper row, while the level line at y 0.806 records the actual wall-to-floor seam. These are installation facts, not a claim that the individual dark panels describe one continuous view. The normalized installation image has mean luminance 0.636, p05 0.102, p95 0.890, and edge density 0.038; #1e1e24 panels are made emphatic by the pale #d9d9db wall and the muted floor colors #887d72 and #514e4f. The source identifies the 2019 work as gesso and oil stick on eighteen black-and-white fibre prints.

The source records document the listed dates and several distinct finished media: digital C-print, Lambda digital C-print, lithograph, chromogenic print, pigment print, and a fibre-print assemblage. ## Exercises

<ExerciseCard n="01" title="Turn a sequence into one field">
Make a sequence of at least twelve views of the same subject with a fixed frame. Layer them digitally without trying to preserve every individual image. Then make one version where the accumulation keeps a stable central axis and one where it drifts; compare what each version makes visible about repetition.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="02" title="Let notation become structure">
Work from a public-domain page of text, a score, or your own handwritten marks. Repeat it until the information begins to read as bands, density, and margin. Do not add a false vanishing point: identify only the actual registers and boundaries that survive the accumulation.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="03" title="Make support part of the rhythm">
Arrange a small grid or row of prints on a wall. Photograph the installation without cropping away the wall-to-floor seam, then decide whether the supports and intervals make the group read as one work or as a sequence of separate images.
</ExerciseCard>
