# Zhong Lin: portraiture by interruption

Zhong Lin’s pictures give a face something to negotiate. A flower blinds it, a
butterfly becomes its smallest interruption, a braid joins it to another body,
or an object breaks its frontal address. Across the 2020 *Project 365* selection
and the 2022 editorial images, the recurring method is not depth for its own
sake but a staged collision of skin, prop, colour, and framing. These twelve
credited, web-sized photographs are reproduced for composition criticism under
the documented fair-use rationale in the [source notes](../../content/zhong-lin/sources.md).

## faces that cannot settle

<Plate slug="zhong-lin" id="01-project-365-tears" label="Untitled [Tears], Project 365, published 2020" caption="An upside-down close face and a row of dangling gel drops turn a portrait into a problem of rotation and repeated accents." />

The *Tears* overlay names the inverted face and the gel-drop rhythm rather than
mistaking the tonal change for a horizon. The lower p05 (0.067) against p95
(0.773) separates dark hair and the blue-green ground from lit skin, while the
low edge density (0.0258) lets the few drops read as discrete punctuation.

<Plate slug="zhong-lin" id="02-project-365-butterfly" label="Untitled [Butterfly], Project 365, published 2020" caption="A reclining body arcs across a deep dark field; a small butterfly interrupts its route at the face." />

The body arc is a contour, not a synthetic perspective line. The butterfly
interrupts it at the face with a compact black silhouette, and the image’s broad
p05–p95 span (0.012–0.957) explains the pressure between the nearly black field
and pale skin. The palette’s #080905 and #f8e8d1 make the scale difference feel
even sharper.

<Plate slug="zhong-lin" id="03-project-365-bubblegum-profile" label="Untitled [Bubblegum Profile], Project 365, published 2020" caption="A leftward profile contour presses into open pale space, with one small bubble at the mouth." />

Here the profile is an extended boundary that pushes toward the left edge; the
small bubble answers it without turning the head into a generalized geometric
form. This is the quietest surface in the first group: edge density is only
0.0070 and the mean is 0.575, so the pale field gives the mouth and bubble their
unusually precise isolation.

<Plate slug="zhong-lin" id="04-project-365-flower-visor" label="Untitled [Flower Visor], Project 365, published 2020" caption="An orange flower acts as an opaque visor across the eyes, while the chin lifts into the empty yellow-green field." />

The flower visor suppresses ordinary facial recognition and leaves the chin’s
upward path to carry the pose. Low edge density (0.0088) keeps the yellow-green
ground nearly continuous; #aaa54c and #c76a1f sharpen the hot flower/cooler
field contrast without requiring an invented lighting explanation.

## bodies made from fields

<Plate slug="zhong-lin" id="05-project-365-pebble-bed" label="Untitled [Pebble Bed], Project 365, published 2020" caption="A legible face appears inside a dense pebble field, where dark hair and large stones tighten the neck into a rough collar." />

The face remains distinct, but only after the stone collar and hair make its
boundary intermittently difficult to find. This is a notably busy surface:
edge density reaches 0.1320, far above the smooth portraits around it. The dark
#0c0b0b hair, gray stones, and warm skin keep figure and ground in active
comparison rather than a complete reversal.

<Plate slug="zhong-lin" id="06-project-365-lily-pair" label="Untitled [Lily Pair], Project 365, published 2020" caption="Two faces approach through an overlapping knot of lilies; petals and stems deliberately interrupt any clean pair portrait." />

The two face ellipses are deliberately broad because the flowers take over the
centre. The overlay’s crossing lily contour explains how occlusion, not
perspective, creates intimacy. Its p05–p95 range of 0.259–0.847 stays high-key,
and the pale pinks, oranges, and teal (#d8c2b8, #c7895c, #264c3d) keep the
fragmented faces readable without a hard outline.

<Plate slug="zhong-lin" id="07-project-365-three-portrait" label="Untitled [Three Portraits], Project 365, published 2020" caption="Three heads form a compact triangle, while overlapping arms send the cluster forward toward the bottom edge." />

The three-head triangle makes a stable centre that the reaching-arm path breaks
open. Their distinct looks prevent this from becoming a single shared gaze; the
structure is physical overlap and depth. The salmon palette (#d9826d,
#eba193) and deep #110f0c clothing place faces and hands over a restrained
range from p05 0.047 to p95 0.745.

<Plate slug="zhong-lin" id="08-project-365-braided-pair" label="Untitled [Braided Pair], Project 365, published 2020" caption="Two unequal portrait masses are literally connected by a looping network of braids across the upper half." />

The braid network is an irregular drawing laid onto a paired portrait, not a
grid or a claim of symmetry. The two head ellipses keep the comparison readable
while the black line routes across their empty background. Its tonal range,
0.086–0.863, gives both the near-black braids and the pale ground enough room to
keep their separate roles.

## colour as physical pressure

<Plate slug="zhong-lin" id="09-untitled-pink-matter" label="Untitled [Pink Matter], published 2022" caption="A crouched body, falling pink material, and red-orange field combine as one dense yet still differentiated surface." />

The crouched-body contour runs from face to hip, while the falling material
continues the downward pull at left. The image is not monochrome: #bb4633,
#db868e, skin, and small dark gaps keep the body from disappearing into the
field. Its narrower p05–p95 range (0.353–0.733) supports that intentionally
compressed color pressure.

<Plate slug="zhong-lin" id="10-untitled-red-fabric-multiplicity" label="Untitled [Red Fabric Multiplicity], published 2022" caption="Two broad fabric sweeps carry repeated instances of one figure across a rock-bound vertical scene." />

The upper and lower fabric paths show a field that changes direction rather
than a single clean leading line. The repeated-figures ellipse names a shared
visual zone, not a chronological sequence. Mean luminance is only 0.243 and p95
only 0.502, so the red cloth accumulates as a dark mass; the small near-white
#f9f8f8 flare and the rocky black ground prevent it from becoming flat.

<Plate slug="zhong-lin" id="11-untitled-tidal-pool" label="Untitled [Tidal Pool], published 2022" caption="A small face and gold garment surface within an irregular pool, while rocks make a broken enclosure around the pale sand and water." />

The overlay follows the gold garment back from the face and marks the upper
rock enclosure without pretending the fractured shoreline is a geometric frame.
At edge density 0.1205, rocks, sand, and water are highly active; the gold
garment acts as the warm counterweight against charcoal #2b2a28, gray #43433f,
and sand #b8a692.

<Plate slug="zhong-lin" id="12-untitled-ice-pop-portrait" label="Untitled [Ice-Pop Portrait], published 2022" caption="A near-bilateral portrait is organized around a vertical ice pop, stick, and hand that interrupt the face while joining its central axis." />

The measured portrait axis lands on the hair part and ice pop, but it does not
claim strict symmetry: hand, loose hair, and uneven light undo it. The inner
frame identifies the pop as an object containing its own colored fragments.
With mean luminance 0.424, p05 0.145, p95 0.773, and a palette from gray
#565759 to skin-toned #d8b9a4, the object’s translucency can stay visible while
the face continues to look directly through it.

## Exercises

<ExerciseCard n="01" title="Interrupt a face">
Make a close portrait with one prop that blocks or redirects a recognisable
feature. Keep the prop structurally necessary: it should change the path through
the image, not merely decorate the subject.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="02" title="Build a field">
Place a figure against one continuous color or texture. Make two versions: in
one, let the figure separate cleanly; in the other, let one edge deliberately
merge. Compare where the eye finds the body.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="03" title="Repeat without sequencing">
Photograph or composite several appearances of a body in one frame. Use cloth,
shadow, or terrain to connect them, but avoid implying a literal timeline unless
the image can actually support one.
</ExerciseCard>
