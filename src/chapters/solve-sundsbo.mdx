+Sølve Sundsbø makes fashion photography feel less like a window onto a finished look than a place where a body is repeatedly rebuilt. This selection moves from a 1999 wireframe-like figure to recent editorial portraits, but the constant is not a single effect. It is a way of making crop, surface, colour, gesture, and obstruction compete for the viewer's attention. The overlays name only structures visible in the supplied whole-frame reproductions; work titles, credits, source routes, and image-specific fair-use rationale remain in the [source notes](../../content/solve-sundsbo/sources.md).

## a face can direct the whole frame

<Plate slug="solve-sundsbo" id="01-out-of-order-aw-2017" label="Out of Order, A/W 2017, selected frame" caption="A turned face sends its gaze out of frame while the shoulder makes a slow counter-diagonal below." />

The off-frame gaze in *Out of Order* makes the leftward pale field consequential. The overlay follows the eye's exit, the face's large oval, and the shoulder line that prevents the portrait from becoming a simple profile. Light does not merely reveal the sitter; it divides the face into pale forehead, dark eye socket, and near-black hair and dress. The analyzer's very low edge density, 0.0190, supports that economy: the palette is essentially black and grays from `#040404` to `#b0b0b0`, with a mean luminance of 0.289. A small earring punctuates the dark side, but the larger drama is the refusal to let the gaze resolve inside the frame.

<Plate slug="solve-sundsbo" id="02-v-magazine-135" label="V Magazine 135, selected frame" caption="A hand rises into a wet-lit close portrait while hair falls as a counterweight at the opposite edge." />

The *V Magazine* close-up is fuller and more tactile. The raised hand begins at the lower edge, reaches to the jaw, and makes the skin, rings, wet hair, and earrings read as competing reflective surfaces. The overlay keeps this as a three-part proposition rather than inventing a false symmetry: tilted face, hand ascent, hair counterweight. Its p05–p95 range of 0.039–0.961 is broad, and the 0.0758 edge density is much higher than the first portrait's; highlights on skin and jewellery are not isolated accents but a dense visual texture.

## surfaces can interrupt bodies

<Plate slug="solve-sundsbo" id="03-perfect-2" label="Perfect 2, selected frame" caption="A reclining blue body moves diagonally under a warm translucent colour veil." />

In the supplied *Perfect* 2 frame, the body is never quite a stable contour. A warm translucent plane crosses the blue reclining figure, while the screen-like texture makes the image feel mediated even before one asks how it was made. The overlay's body diagonal and colour-veil line identify the two motions that organize the picture; the small head ellipse keeps the figure from dissolving entirely into the field. The analyzer finds a restrained mean luminance of 0.303 and a low 0.0156 edge density, with rust `#6f3320`, deep blue-green `#162b2c`, and blue `#456c91` among the palette representatives. The effect is slow rather than sharp: colour, rather than outline, carries the body across the frame.

<Plate slug="solve-sundsbo" id="06-vogue-italia-795" label="Vogue Italia 795, November 2016, selected frame" caption="A blue-painted face holds a provisional axis behind a tied transparent layer and foliage shadows." />

The *Vogue Italia* portrait makes transparency into a structural interruption. A blue-painted face looks through a wrap that forms an internal screen; a tied band crosses the lower face and foliage shadows press in from the right. The dashed portrait axis is not a claim of total bilateral symmetry. It shows how the eyes hold a provisional center while the translucent folds and shadows pull the image away from it. With p05–p95 values of 0.051–0.949 and palette representatives including pale cream `#f1e4cf`, dark plum `#27111f`, and muted blue `#68919b`, the normalized reproduction carries both a wide tonal range and a notably low edge density of 0.0302. It is a portrait designed as layered screen space.

## colour can become a cast of characters

<Plate slug="solve-sundsbo" id="04-v-magazine-133" label="The Surreal World, V Magazine 133, Winter 2021, selected frame" caption="Black and red figures are held in opposed clusters by cyan bodies across a white studio." />

*The Surreal World* is an unusually explicit lesson in colour hierarchy. On the left, bare-toned attendants frame a feathered black figure. On the right, cyan bodies curve around a red garment. The white gap does not reconcile these groups; it makes their difference more legible. The overlay isolates the two central figures and the two enclosing routes, so the image reads as a staged distribution of colour and silhouette rather than a crowded set. Its palette confirms the stark organization: white `#e9e9e8`, near-black `#221719`, red `#a91e0f`, and cyan-blue `#548999`; the surprisingly low 0.0203 edge density reminds us that large, clean masses do most of the work.

<Plate slug="solve-sundsbo" id="10-face-off" label="Face Off, V Man 45, Winter 2020–Spring 2021, selected frame" caption="A gold mask, blue hair, and inflated red sleeve stack above a hard yellow tabletop." />

*Face Off* compresses the same approach into one shallow figure. The gold mask is small but dense; the pale-blue hair sweeps left into darkness; the huge red sleeve overtakes the body. The tabletop at y0.737 is the decisive stabilizer: without it the three masses might float as a collage, while with it they become a deliberately theatrical still life. The reproduction's mean luminance is only 0.293, yet red `#b71e1b` and yellow `#d59a16` strike out of dark values such as `#160805`. At 0.0512 edge density, gloss and seams give the red sleeve its physical pressure without turning the frame into mere texture.

## the close crop can turn a person into an axis

<Plate slug="solve-sundsbo" id="07-beauty-papers-3" label="Beauty Papers 3, S/S 2017, selected frame" caption="Paired blue eye fields are held by the central nasal ridge inside a deliberately uncompromising crop." />

The close crop in *Beauty Papers* has almost no room for anecdote. The vertical nose bridge and two blue eye fields create an uneasy bilateral structure, while the red mouth below changes the relation from neutral symmetry into a stylized face. The overlay separates the left and right painted fields rather than pretending they are one undifferentiated mask. The analyzer confirms strong vertical correlation at the nose-bridge axis and reports a low edge density of 0.0202; pale blue-gray `#ccd8df`, navy `#12111c`, and muted blue `#92adbc` turn colour and skin texture into the frame's main event.

<Plate slug="solve-sundsbo" id="08-vogue-nippon-november-2011" label="Vogue Nippon, November 2011, selected frame" caption="Open mouth, orchid stem, flower chain, and shoulder contour make a soft beauty crop directional." />

The *Vogue Nippon* frame makes an orchid do compositional work normally assigned to architecture. Its green stem travels from the shoulder area toward the bloom, while mouth and petals form a short upper chain. The shoulder counter-curve keeps that route from becoming a one-way arrow; the eye can return through skin and lip. This is the sequence's lightest normalized reproduction, with mean luminance 0.768 and edge density only 0.0074. Cream-pink `#fae3da`, pale yellow `#f3cf96`, and warm browns make the image almost weightless, so the stem's directional role matters all the more.

## figure and graphic can occupy the same body

<Plate slug="solve-sundsbo" id="09-dazed-and-confused-1999" label="Dazed and Confused, 1999, selected frame" caption="A mesh-like head meets a hand interruption and horizontal trailing contours in a white field." />

The 1999 *Dazed and Confused* image makes the photographed body look already translated into an information graphic. A wireframe crown and large head are interrupted by a darker hand form; the contours then trail horizontally beyond the head. The overlay keeps those four visible facts distinct, because the image's force comes from their collision rather than from an imagined perspective system. Its mean luminance of 0.862 and p95 of 1.000 describe the broad white field, while the 0.0680 edge density records the mesh's unusual density. The technical effect is historically early in this selection, but the compositional method is recognizably continuous: a figure is made unstable by treating its surface as a designed field.

<Plate slug="solve-sundsbo" id="05-v-man-47" label="V Man, selected frame" caption="A low-angle figure rises through pleats, a fan sleeve, and a fabric relay against clouded sky." />

The *V Man* frame makes clothing act as a vertical instrument. A low-angle face sits above a pleated front, while a fan sleeve and the left fabric relay push energy upward through the figure. The distant ground line is spare but important: it separates the white fashion mass from the sky and gives the extreme low angle a place to stand. The reproduction runs from near-black `#141414` to pale gray `#d6d6d6`, with a moderate 0.0501 edge density. Rather than giving us a clean fashion silhouette, it makes pleat, cuff, cloud, and body trade places as the image's active form.

## Exercises

<ExerciseCard n="01" title="Give a portrait an exit">
Make a close portrait in which the subject looks beyond one edge of the frame. Add one counter-direction, such as a shoulder, hand, garment seam, or shadow, that keeps the eye from simply following the gaze out. Remove anything that does not clarify the tension between those two routes.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="02" title="Make a body into a colour field">
Use one translucent, reflective, or patterned foreground material. Photograph a body through it, but decide in advance whether the body should remain legible as a figure or be reduced to a diagonal, a mass, or a palette relation. Check the result in monochrome as well as colour.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="03" title="Stage three unequal masses">
On a plain ground, arrange three unlike colour or material masses: a small dense detail, a flowing line, and a large soft volume. Add a table edge, floor seam, or other horizontal only if it makes the arrangement feel intentionally shallow rather than merely flat.
</ExerciseCard>
