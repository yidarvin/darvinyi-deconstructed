# Dionne Lee: landscape as a made relation

Dionne Lee's photographs and collages refuse the fantasy that landscape is a
neutral view. In these twelve works, a hand holds a torn seascape, graphite
crosses a support, a body measures the sky, a spiral is drawn on stone, and a
survival blanket occupies a hillside. The overlays name only visible structures:
fragments, marks, inserts, hands, fields, and repetition. [Source
notes](../../content/dionne-lee/sources.md)

## hands, fragments, and orientation

<Plate slug="dionne-lee" id="01-breaking-wave" label="Breaking Wave, 2018" caption="Two hands hold apart torn wave fragments, turning the break between them into the picture's central interval." />

*Breaking Wave* places two small seascapes in a nearly black surround. The
held-fragment frame and seam trace make the gap legible before the wave itself
resolves. Mean luminance is only 0.063, and #010206 and #16171b lead the
palette, so the bright paper fragments and hands carry the available light.

<Plate slug="dionne-lee" id="02-untitled-wound" label="Untitled (Wound), 2020" caption="A dark collage field holds red and white interruptions in an uneasy, nearly mirrored balance." />

Ragged white pieces, red areas, and black surround make *Untitled (Wound)* an
assembled surface rather than a window. The assembled-field frame and upper red
floral insert identify distinct interruptions without claiming a literal wound
geometry. Its web copy
spans p05 0.039 to p95 1.000, which keeps the red accents abrupt.

<Plate slug="dionne-lee" id="03-true-north" label="True North, 2019" caption="Raised hands sit across the upper pair of a six-panel collage, making bodily measurement the work's directional event." />

MoMA identifies *True North* as a collage of gelatin silver prints with
graphite. The six-panel frame and central axis reveal the deliberately composed
support; the short hand trace identifies the actual upper-pair gesture rather
than imposing a vanishing point on collage seams. MoMA describes this gesture as
one used in the northern hemisphere to locate the North Star.

<Plate slug="dionne-lee" id="04-untitled-rock-drawing-iii" label="Untitled Rock Drawing III, 2023" caption="A hand-drawn spiral concentrates the rough stone surface into an enclosing diagram." />

The enclosing frame, ellipse, and traced curve show the made spiral in *Untitled
Rock Drawing III* without turning every crack into a perspective line. Lee has
described making impermanent water drawings on rocks; here the visible mark makes
a small act of drawing carry the field.

## survival as picture structure

<Plate slug="dionne-lee" id="05-fire-bed" label="Fire Bed, 2019" caption="A reaching arm enters a leaf-litter field and points toward the prepared central ground." />

NOMA identifies *Fire Bed* as a gelatin silver print made after a fire-making
class. The arm directs attention into a dense field (edge density 0.266), while
the ellipse marks the visibly prepared ground rather than an invented optical
device.

<Plate slug="dionne-lee" id="06-trespass-is-the-most-beautiful-word" label="trespass is the most beautiful word, 2018" caption="Two hands enclose a floral photographic insert within a deep black surround." />

Paired hand contours enclose a small floral image. The central ellipse marks the
floral insert the hands make visible, not a claim of symmetry. Mean luminance is
0.146; the #030506-to-gray palette makes the floral insert a charged opening.

<Plate slug="dionne-lee" id="07-a-plot-that-also-grounds" label="a plot that also grounds, 2016" caption="A pointing finger reaches toward a torn dark opening in a dense flowering field." />

NOMA records that Lee manipulated and re-photographed found landscape material
for *a plot that also grounds*. The overlay follows the pointing finger and the
torn opening that interrupts the flowering field. Brown-green ground colors
(#1f140c, #423525, #6a5e44) make the break precise.

<Plate slug="dionne-lee" id="08-lapse" label="Lapse, 2019" caption="A tight grid of repeated eyes turns photographic time into a visibly accumulated surface." />

*Lapse* changes scale from land to contact-sheet-like repetition. Its frame and
two repetition bands name the actual grid rather than a false horizon. Both
thirds and phi score zero in the analyzer, confirming that no single cell should
govern the matrix.

## supports, inserts, and signals

<Plate slug="dionne-lee" id="09-contact-a-muscle-memory" label="Contact (A Muscle Memory), 2020" caption="A white hand held between reflective strips makes touch a constructed spatial event." />

The extended white hand reads as a cut shape against the darker collage. The
support frame and strip trace show how touch becomes an interruption in the
surface. The p05–p95 range of 0.051–1.000 describes the sharp black-white
pressure, not an undocumented lighting setup.

<Plate slug="dionne-lee" id="10-netting" label="Netting, 2019" caption="A central photographic insert is held inside a pale field of graphite-like woven traces." />

NOMA identifies *Netting* as a gelatin silver print with graphite and describes
an inverted lean-to shelter at its center. The overlay marks the insert, one
visible trace, and near-central axis; it does not pretend that every graphite
mark converges optically. This is the palest, sparsest web copy (mean 0.655;
edge density 0.029), so the small dark insert gains force from open support.

<Plate slug="dionne-lee" id="11-a-test-for-40-acres" label="A Test for 40 Acres, 2016" caption="A stitched reflective mylar sheet interrupts the hillside below a flat tree-line horizon." />

NOMA records that Lee stitched mylar survival blankets into a huge square and
spread them on a hillside. The overlay bounds the bright sheet and follows its
lower edge, separating it from the tree line. The 720 × 576 source remains
legible; p05 0.024 and p95 0.969 make mylar a hard tonal interruption.

<Plate slug="dionne-lee" id="12-broken-signal" label="Broken Signal, 2019" caption="Six panels, five marked by luminous flares, repeat within a dark, nearly balanced signal field." />

*Broken Signal* closes with repetition that does not settle into calm. The
overlay frames the six-panel field, locates the upper row, and keeps the measured
central balance while rejecting detector diagonals with no visible signal. Dark
olive-grays (#181915, #343531, #61615e) make the five pale flares feel like a
damaged transmission rather than a decorative highlight.

<Callout>
MoMA documents *True North* as a collage of gelatin silver prints with graphite.
NOMA documents distinct media and making contexts for *Fire Bed*, *Netting*, *A
Test for 40 Acres*, and *a plot that also grounds*. No source gives camera, lens,
focal length, exposure, film, sensor, or lighting settings; the display JPEGs
have no capture EXIF. This chapter therefore limits its technical claims to
documented process and visible measured structure. [Research
note](../../content/dionne-lee/research.md)
</Callout>

## Exercises

<ExerciseCard n="01" title="Make the support visible">
Photograph or scan a printed image, then introduce one physically visible
operation: a tear, fold, graphite line, cut, or second image that changes the
viewer's route through the frame.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="02" title="Use a hand as an instrument">
Make a picture where a hand measures, holds apart, shields, or points. Compose
so the gesture changes structure rather than merely illustrating an action.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="03" title="Interrupt a field">
Find a broad ground: leaf litter, a hillside, a dark support, or a repeated grid.
Mark only the interruption and the structure that makes its scale legible.
</ExerciseCard>
