+Philip-Lorca diCorcia photographs people and places as though an ordinary encounter has just acquired a lighting cue. Across a courtyard, a bathroom, a city crossing, a pole stage, or a supermarket parking lot, the frame gives a real situation the pressure of a set. The overlays stay literal: they name the openings, rims, façades, and receding streets that make that pressure visible rather than claiming to reconstruct unseen direction. ## thresholds, rooms, and withheld narratives

<Plate slug="philip-lorca-dicorcia" id="01-naples-1995" label="Naples, 1995" caption="A dark threshold withholds most of a courtyard while a birdcage and seated figure make the visible portion feel like a private scene." />

In *Naples*, large leaf shapes and a dark central jamb hold back the left half of the frame. The courtyard opening begins near the phi grid's x=0.618 line; within it, the birdcage and the seated figure form two small, competing anchors. The analyzer's low mean luminance of 0.224 and palette of near-black `#0b0f15`, blue-gray `#2e3438`, and pale wall color `#b9a694` explain why the lit courtyard reads as a fragment rather than an open view.

<Plate slug="philip-lorca-dicorcia" id="02-hartford-1978" label="Hartford, 1978" caption="A large blue field, narrow pantry opening, and low cat make a domestic room feel both spare and alert." />

*Hartford* makes emptiness active. The pantry is a narrow vertical interruption at the right, while the cat crouches just above the measured floor threshold at y=0.906. The dominant blue-green palette of `#3a6b71`, `#638183`, and `#0f4d57`, plus a low edge density of 0.022, allow the small white animal and the black shelving to carry disproportionate attention.

<Plate slug="philip-lorca-dicorcia" id="03-salonika-1980" label="Salonika, 1980" caption="A rebar screen and rising platform turn a construction site into a layered, partially inaccessible crowd scene." />

The rebar grid in *Salonika* is not decorative foreground texture: it is the first plane the eye must cross. Behind it, the platform edge sits at the analyzer's measured y=0.462, and the scaffold rises toward the crowded upper-right. Its high edge density of 0.121 and tonal span from p05 0.059 to p95 0.741 make the lattice, workers, sky, and dark excavation read as separate, insistently legible levels.

<Plate slug="philip-lorca-dicorcia" id="04-new-haven-1978" label="New Haven, 1978" caption="The counter edge and the face at right hold a pale interior in a tense diagonal relation." />

In *New Haven*, the visible rear tub rim begins at left and sends the eye to the face near x=0.819, y=0.542. The tub is a practical object, but its narrow visible boundary makes the figure look carefully placed inside an enclosure. This is the set's sparing display reproduction: edge density is 0.010 and the palette stays close to gray `#949391`, `#757173`, and pale `#acaeaa`, so the face, dark hair, and warm wooden counter become concentrated departures.

## street scenes as arranged intervals

<Plate slug="philip-lorca-dicorcia" id="05-tokyo-1998" label="Tokyo, 1998" caption="Billboards and the crossing organize a crowd into overlapping currents rather than a single public scene." />

*Tokyo* uses its billboards as an upper field and its crossing as the lower stage. The overlay's pedestrian path follows the staggered feet and bodies that pass through the crossing at y=0.751; no single person controls the frame for long. The display JPEG has the selection's highest edge density, 0.111, while near-black `#0b0e13` and pale `#d5d2c9` keep signage, clothing, and pavement distinct without flattening the crowd.

<Plate slug="philip-lorca-dicorcia" id="06-hartford-1979" label="Hartford, 1979" caption="A lit window and roofline set one man against an extensive evening darkness." />

The warm window in *Hartford* (1979) makes the man a figure in a box, while the roofline at left gives the house a larger, dark counterweight. The man at the window occupies the right-side third; his small scale makes the illumination feel selected rather than ambient. Mean luminance is only 0.174, with `#171415`, `#2a2c34`, and `#404957` surrounding the warm `#bc967b` highlight, so the image's drama comes from a narrow zone of visibility.

<Plate slug="philip-lorca-dicorcia" id="07-head-9-2000" label="Head #9, 2000" caption="A face and shoulder line give a passing stranger the isolation and weight of a studio portrait." />

*Head #9* denies the city the chance to explain its sitter. The face is set near the phi grid's center-right intersection, and the oval of head and glasses rises out of a dark, defocused field. A palette that moves from `#1d1b22` through blue-gray `#97a3b2` to skin-toned `#c8bab8`, plus p05/p95 values of 0.086/0.702, supports the sharp separation of lit face from background without requiring a claim about the equipment that produced it.

<Plate slug="philip-lorca-dicorcia" id="08-hannah-2004" label="Hannah, 2004" caption="A pole, the figure's extended body, and a low dark room make the vertical frame feel like a theatrical apparatus." />

In *Hannah*, the pole divides the tall frame while the illuminated face and extended body pull across it. The overlay marks the pole rather than inventing a vanishing point from the dark room. The normalized reproduction is exceptionally dark, with mean luminance 0.127 and p95 0.220, and `#151514`, `#21201d`, and warm `#d1b393` let the body and the wood floor register as staged pools of light.

<Plate slug="philip-lorca-dicorcia" id="09-havana-1999" label="Havana, 1999" caption="A solitary man, building cornice, and receding street hold a public crowd at a deliberate distance." />

*Havana* separates its man from the group behind him through the broad column and open pavement. The building cornice at y=0.254 creates an upper, bright band, while the curb at lower left and the crowd occupy a compressed middle distance. Its p05/p95 range of 0.114–0.933 is the selection's broadest; pale `#e3e7e7` and gray-green `#b2b4a7` keep the daylight field open enough for the darker street figures to read as a compressed group.

<Plate slug="philip-lorca-dicorcia" id="10-ike-cole-hustlers-1990-92" label="Ike Cole, 38 Years Old, Los Angeles, California, $25, from Hustlers, 1990–92" caption="A market façade and two foreground cars place Ike Cole in a public frame that is also a deliberately constructed portrait." />

The *Hustlers* title records a named, paid encounter; the photograph makes its public setting part of the portrait's argument. The market façade makes a broad illuminated band, while the two foreground cars form a low barrier that places Ike Cole between commerce and traffic. The Art Institute identifies this work as a chromogenic print. In the supplied display file, deep `#202322` cars, cream `#dcd5c8` signage, and the yellow-green market field create a p05/p95 span of 0.082–0.902. Those are observations of the reproduction, not a substitute for the original print's color.

## Exercises

<ExerciseCard n="01" title="Make a threshold do the staging">
Photograph a room or courtyard from behind one literal obstruction: a door frame, plant, window, or column. Keep most of the subject visible, but let the obstruction withhold enough information that the scene reads as a discovered interval rather than an open description.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="02" title="Light one zone of an ordinary place">
At dusk or in a room with mixed light, make one version in which the important figure is in the brightest zone and another in which they are just outside it. Compare whether the surrounding darkness feels like atmosphere, narrative pressure, or simply lost information.
</ExerciseCard>

<ExerciseCard n="03" title="Use public architecture as a portrait frame">
Make a portrait in front of a storefront, window, crossing, or parked vehicle. Mark the façade line and a foreground barrier afterward. Ask whether those structures give the sitter presence, isolate them, or make the setting compete with them.
</ExerciseCard>
